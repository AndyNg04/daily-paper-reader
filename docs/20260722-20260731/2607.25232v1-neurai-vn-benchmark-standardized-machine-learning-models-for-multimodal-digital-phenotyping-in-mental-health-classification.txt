## ABSTRACT 

Digital phenotyping (DP) using smartphones and wearable devices has shown considerable potential for mental health monitoring. However, progress remains difficult to evaluate due to heterogeneous datasets, inconsistent preprocessing pipelines. In this study, we present a reproducible benchmark built upon the N EURAI -VN dataset, a high-resolution, multimodal dataset comprising passive sensing and active assessment from wearable and smartphone devices, collected from 100 Vietnamese adults over two weeks. The benchmark defines four clinically relevant binary classification tasks evaluated using standardized subject-wise cross-validation. Representative linear, tree-based, and neural baseline models are evaluated across predefined feature configurations. Mean subject-level F1 scores across five cross-validation folds reached 0.71 for Healthy Control vs. Depression and Healthy Control vs. Clinical, while Healthy Control vs. Anxiety and Depression vs. Anxiety achieved 0.69 and 0.56, respectively. These benchmark results provide reproducible baselines for future research on multimodal DP for mental health classification tasks. 

## 1 Introduction 

Digital phenotyping (DP) transforms heterogeneous sensing data, collected across individuals and time, into behavioral representations that can be mapped onto clinical outcomes such as depression and anxiety [ 1]. Smartphones and wearable devices make this feasible at scale: passive streams such as mobility dynamics, screen interaction, sleep, and cardiac activity, together with active self-report prompts, capture day-to-day variation that periodic clinical assessment is poorly positioned to observe, given that it is administered weeks apart and relies on retrospective recall [ 2 , 3]. The clinical premise is therefore straightforward: if routinely collected behavioral signals track symptom severity, they can support earlier detection and continuous monitoring at negligible marginal cost to the patient. A decade of empirical work has established that such associations exist. Early studies linked GPS-derived mobility features to depressive symptom severity [ 4 , 5], and longitudinal campus deployments related passive sensing to mental health and academic outcomes [ 6, 7]. More recent studies report similar findings across a widening range of signals: depressive symptoms have been associated with screen time [ 8 , 9 ], phone unlocks [ 10 ], and social media exhaustion arXiv:2607.25232v1 [cs.LG] 28 Jul 2026 A PREPRINT - J ULY 29, 2026 [11 ], while anxiety disorders have been consistently linked to reduced heart rate variability (HRV) [ 12 ] and abnormal sleep durations [ 13 ]. Several public resources have supported this line of work, including StudentLife [ 6], CrossCheck [ 14 ], Tesserae [ 15 ], LifeSnaps [ 16 ], and GLOBEM [ 17 ]. Cross-study comparison nevertheless remains difficult, for three distinct reasons. First, datasets differ in population, sensing modality, and sampling regime, so a model reported on one cohort is rarely applicable to another without redesign. Second, feature extraction and preprocessing are typically task-specific and only partially documented, which limits reproducibility [ 18 ]. Third, and most consequentially, evaluation protocols are not aligned: record-wise cross-validation, which allows observations from the same participant to appear in both training and test folds, yields substantially optimistic estimates relative to subject-wise validation [19 ], and studies further differ in how continuous symptom scales are binarized into class labels. The practical result is that reported performance figures are not comparable across studies, and that models generalize considerably less well across cohorts and collection periods than single-dataset results suggest [ 17 ]. A further limitation is demographic: existing resources are drawn overwhelmingly from Western, English-speaking, and predominantly university-affiliated samples [ 20 ], leaving it unresolved whether reported behavior–symptom associations hold in other linguistic, cultural, and socioeconomic settings. We address these limitations with N EURAI -VN [ 21 ], a benchmark dataset for mental health classification collected from a free-living Vietnamese cohort of 100 participants. The dataset comprises 17 modalities including 13 passive sensing and 4 active assessments, acquired over a two-week monitoring period and supports clinical diagnostic annotations. Our main contribution is the establishment of a reproducible benchmark for multimodal mental health classification, including clinically motivated prediction tasks, standardized feature configurations, representative baseline models, and a unified evaluation protocol for consistent and reproducible method comparison. This paper complements the accompanying dataset descriptor by reporting the first benchmark evaluation on the proposed dataset. We evaluate representative machine learning models across multiple clinically motivated binary classification tasks using standardized feature configurations and a unified subject-wise cross-validation protocol. The resulting baseline results serve as reference performance for future methodological development and comparison.. 

## 2 NEURAI -VN Dataset 

2.1 Description 

We present an observational cohort collected under IRB-approved protocols from 100 Vietnamese adults over a two-week period under realistic condition. The data includes wearable and smartphone multimodal data with multiple temporal scales, along with momentary and periodic self-report entries. Participants were classified by psychiatrists into four groups: depression (Dep), anxiety (Anx), healthy controls (HC), and other psychiatric conditions (OPC) based on clinical assessments conducted by verified psychiatrists. The data collection was supported by a custom-built mobile platform on both iOS and Android system.

f (X) = [mean( X), min( X), max( X), skew( X), std( X)] . (2)

3.3 Benchmark Design Benchmark Configurations. For evaluation, the two smartphone modalities, event-based sensing ( Pe) and continuous sensing ( Pc), are merged into a unified smartphone feature group ( P ), while the weekly self-report modality ( Sw) is excluded because its temporal resolution is not aligned with the remaining modalities. Benchmark configurations are constructed from the feature space M to evaluate the effect of modality integration. Specifically, we consider predefined single-modality, two-modality, three-modality, and four-modality feature combina-tions, resulting in 15 configurations evaluated consistently across all baseline models and classification tasks. Let P = Pe ∪ Pc, the benchmark configuration space consists of four feature groups as follows: 

M = {Wm, W s, S d, P }, (4) We exhaustively evaluate all possible non-empty combinations of these feature groups, yielding 15 benchmark configu-rations. These configurations are grouped according to the number of feature groups included: • Single-group (4): Wm, Ws, Sd, and P .• Two-group (6): Wm + Ws, Wm + Sd, Wm + P , Ws + Sd, Ws + P , and Sd + P .• Three-group (4): Wm + Ws + Sd, Wm + Ws + P , Wm + Sd + P , and Ws + Sd + P .• All-group (1): Wm + Ws + Sd + P .

Benchmark Tasks. To evaluate the utility of the proposed dataset under different clinical screening scenarios, we define four binary classification tasks with increasing diagnostic difficulty: • Task B1 : Healthy Control (HC) vs. Depression (Dep) • Task B2 : Healthy Control (HC) vs. Anxiety (Anx) • Task B3 : Depression (Dep) vs. Anxiety (Anx) • Task B4 : Healthy Control (HC) vs. Clinical (Dep + Anx + OPC) The Clinical group comprises all participants diagnosed with a psychiatric disorder (i.e., Depression, Anxiety, and Other Psychiatric Conditions). All benchmark tasks are evaluated using the same feature configurations, baseline models, and subject-wise cross-validation protocol to ensure fair comparison. 4A PREPRINT - J ULY 29, 2026

4.1 Experimental Setup Baseline Models. We benchmark four machine learning models: Logistic Regression (LR), Random Forest (RF), XGBoost (XGB), and a Multi-Layer Perceptron (MLP). All models are trained using the same feature representations for each benchmark condition without model-specific feature engineering. Hyperparameter settings are summarized in Table 4. Baseline models were implemented using the scikit-learn and the XGBoost library. Unless otherwise specified, default hyperparameter settings were used; the XGBoost classifier was configured with a learning rate of 0.05 and 300 estimators. A fixed random seed (42) was used to ensure reproducibility. 

Evaluation Metrics. We use subject-level macro F1-score ( mF1 ) as the primary evaluation metric: 

mF 1 = 2P R P + R ,

where P and R denote precision and recall, respectively. Predictions are evaluated at the subject level, and performance is reported as the mean ± standard deviation across five subject-wise cross-validation folds. F1-score is used to account for potential class imbalance in the classification tasks. 

Evaluation Protocol. All experiments are conducted using five-fold subject-wise Group K-Fold cross-validation, where samples from the same participant are restricted to a single fold to prevent subject-level information leakage. The same fold partition is used across all models, feature configurations, and classification tasks to ensure fair comparison. Each defined benchmark configuration defined is independently evaluated using all baseline models under the same subject-wise cross-validation protocol. This results in a complete benchmark matrix spanning all classification tasks, feature configurations, and baseline models. All experiments are implemented in Python, using NVIDIA GeForce RTX 5080 GPU. 5A PREPRINT - J ULY 29, 2026
