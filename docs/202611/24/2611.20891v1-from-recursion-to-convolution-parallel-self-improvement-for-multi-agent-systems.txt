## Abstract 

Multi-agent self-improvement enables agents to improve through interaction and feedback. However, sequential dependencies across agents and refinement rounds can propagate errors, increase compu-tational costs, and limit parallelism. Moreover, refining individual features or agents in isolation can overlook their dependencies and restrict collective coordination. To address these challenges, we propose 

ConvRSI , a framework for parallelizing multi-agent self-improvement through convolution. ConvRSI 

couples intra-agent convolution, which jointly refines related agent features, with inter-agent graph convolution, which aggregates messages according to the collaboration topology. Both stages operate in latent space, enabling parallel updates without repeatedly decoding and re-encoding intermediate mes-sages. Experiments on MATH-500 show that ConvRSI achieves 5.04% performance improvement while reducing 17.27% inference cost, and 45.11% latency compared with RecursiveMAS. It also supports diverse collaboration patterns by representing their topologies as adjacency matrices. These results demonstrate the potential of convolutional co-evolution for efficient multi-agent self-improvement. 

Keywords: Recursive Self-Improvement, Parallel Multi-Agent Systems, Graph Convolutional Net-works, Large Language Models 

## 1. Introduction 

Multi-agent systems (MAS) tackle complex tasks by coordinating agents with complementary roles, knowledge, and reasoning capabilities. Beyond collaboration among fixed agents, recent research explores how agents can improve through their interactions and experience. Prior work advances MAS through role-specific training feedback [Motwani et al., 2025] and the co-evolution of question generation, solving, and judging through self-play [Chen et al., 2026]. Recursive self-improvement (RSI) extends this direction by feeding improvements back into the system, enabling the updated system to guide subsequent rounds of improvement. For example, RecursiveMAS [Zou et al., 2026] iteratively refines shared latent states and jointly optimizes agents at the system level, while MASS [Lee et al., 2026] alternates workflow evolution with model training on self-generated trajectories. Despite this progress, scaling self-improvement to more agents and deeper iterations faces inherent challenges in optimization stability, computational efficiency, and the scope of collaboration, as discussed below. 

First, deeper recursion propagates error. Each recursive round builds on earlier outputs or updates, allowing mistakes to influence subsequent decisions and become harder to correct. RecursiveMAS [Zou et al., 2026] jointly optimizes agents across rounds but retains the dependency chain through which errors can spread. Similar risks arise in other forms of agent RSI: inaccurate self-evaluation in MASS [Lee et al., 2026] can introduce flawed trajectories into subsequent training, while mistaken memory updates in Recuris [Yu et al., 2026] can affect future task execution. This raises a key question: can comparable improvement be achieved with fewer sequential updates? arXiv:2611.20891v1 [cs.AI] 16 Nov 2026 From Recursion to Convolution: Parallel Self-Improvement for Multi-Agent Systems 

Second, sequential multi-agent computation limits efficiency and parallelism. Multi-agent RSI repeatedly executes agents, exchanges information, and evaluates updates, increasing computational costs as agent count and iteration depth increase. Chain-like dependencies force later updates to wait for earlier results, limiting the use of available parallel computing resources. Such dependencies arise across recursive collaboration links in RecursiveMAS [Zou et al., 2026], the generation–verification–refinement pipeline in MALT [Motwani et al., 2025], and successive harness evolution cycles in ModularRSI [Wu et al., 2026]. This motivates restructuring agent updates to reduce the time and cost required to reach a target level of performance. 

Third, individual feature updates and chain-like evolution limit global coordination. Each agent’s behavior depends on related features, including task information, environmental observations, roles, experience, prompts, and messages. Refining one feature in isolation can overlook its dependencies on others. Existing methods address different aspects of improvement: Recuris [Yu et al., 2026] evolves memory, ModularRSI [Wu et al., 2026] evolves and integrates harness modules, MASS [Lee et al., 2026] improves workflows with model capabilities, and RecursiveMAS [Zou et al., 2026] refines hidden states across agents. These leave room for a complementary design that jointly models related features within each agent and aggregates information across agents to support global evolution. In particular, chain-like evolution passes information from one agent to the next, making distant agents interact through intermediate updates. A broader approach would aggregate agents for collective, rather than agent-by-agent evolution [Yu et al., 2026; Lee et al., 2026]. To address these challenges, we propose ConvRSI , a framework for parallelizing multi-agent self-improvement through convolution . The idea is to replace portions of sequential recursive computation with convolutional updates over agent features and multi-agent collaboration graphs. This introduces a 

space–time scaling perspective : improvement can be pursued not only by increasing temporal iteration depth, but also by expanding spatial computation within each iteration. Rather than simply adding more independent agents, ConvRSI uses structured convolutional operations to coordinate parallel updates within and across agents. 

ConvRSI comprises two coupled stages of improvement. Intra-agent convolution applies one-dimensional convolution to hidden-state sequences representing an agent’s task, role, experience, prompt, and messages. It jointly models local dependencies among these features, enabling each agent to independently refine its message state through the convolutional interactions. The features are 

initialized once and subsequently refined through convolution. Inter-agent convolution then applies graph convolution to the updated agent messages according to the collaboration topology. Through parallel matrix operations, it aggregates information across connected agents. The resulting messages are fed into the next intra-agent stage, coupling individual refinement with collective evolution. Both stages operate on latent representations, enabling efficient convolutional updates without repeatedly decoding messages into text and re-encoding them. This matrix-based design exposes parallelism across feature positions and agents throughout evolution. Moreover, ConvRSI supports diverse multi-agent collaboration patterns by representing their topologies as adjacency matrices for inter-agent graph convolution. Empirically, we evaluate ConvRSI on 2 benchmarks spanning math and coding, using diverse model families and multi-agent collaboration patterns, including sequential style and mixture style. Compared with representative multi-agent and recursive baselines, ConvRSI improves task performance while reducing the cost of self-improvement. In particular, it achieves a 5.04% performance improvement, a 17.27% reduction in inference cost, and a 45.11% inference speedup. At comparable performance, 

ConvRSI achieves these gains with 1.82 × and 1.80 × inference speedups and generates 17.3% and

Our main contributions are: • We introduce a space–time perspective on multi-agent self-improvement , exploring how spatially parallel computation can reduce reliance on deep sequential updates. • We propose ConvRSI , which couples intra-agent convolution over related agent features with inter-agent graph convolution over collaboration topologies. Together, these stages support iterative individual refinement and collective evolution. • We evaluate ConvRSI across diverse benchmarks, model families, and collaboration patterns, measuring performance gains under matched time and computational costs, as well as reductions in time and cost at comparable performance. 

## 2. Related Work 

Recursive Self-Improvement of Agents. Recursive self-improvement enables agents to improve their capabilities through feedback and accumulated experience. Existing work explores different targets of improvement, including model behavior, agent workflows, harness components, and memory. Specifically, RecursiveMAS [Zou et al., 2026] extends recursive computation to multi-agent collaboration by iteratively refining shared latent states and jointly optimizing agents through an inner–outer learning procedure. MASS [Lee et al., 2026] alternates multi-agent workflow optimization with model training on self-generated trajectories, using collective behavior as a source of self-supervision. MetaRSI [Tan et al., 2026] further explores meta-level self-improvement by coordinating data, harness, and model updates and adapting the strategy that schedules these updates. Beyond model-level improvement, ModularRSI [Wu et al., 2026] evolves agent harness modules using execution experience, Recuris [Yu et al., 2026] updates agent memory based on feedback, and Dream-RSI [Zheng et al., 2026] reuses discovery histories to improve exploration policies. Building on RSI, ConvRSI reorganizes the improvement process through convolutional updates, coupling individual agent refinement with collective evolution while reducing reliance on sequential recursive steps. 

Multi-Agent Training and Co-Evolution. Multi-agent training methods improve collective perfor-mance by encouraging agents to develop complementary capabilities and learn from their interactions. MALT [Motwani et al., 2025] trains specialized agents for generation, verification, and refinement, while MAPoRL [Park et al., 2025] applies multi-agent reinforcement learning to improve collaboration among language models. Multi-Agent Evolve [Chen et al., 2026] uses interacting roles to generate training data and feedback for model self-improvement. Related approaches explore the evolution of agent populations, as in EvoAgent [Yuan et al., 2025], or the automatic optimization of agentic workflows, as in AFlow [Zhang et al., 2025]. ConvRSI jointly refines features within each agent and uses a graph to model collaboration among agents, enabling collective co-evolution. 

Parallel and Efficient Multi-Agent Collaboration. Multi-agent systems improve performance through collaboration, but additional agents increase compute costs, while sequential interaction rounds increase latency and limit parallelism. Recent work [Wunderlich et al., 2026] compares the accuracy–compute trade-offs of self-consistency, self-refinement, multi-agent debate, and agent aggregation. Parallel self-refinement [Wang et al., 2026] generates multiple candidate solutions concurrently and syn-thesizes them into an improved response. Studies of multi-agent debate [Smit et al., 2024] further highlight the importance of comparison with strong alternatives under matched computational budgets. RecursiveMAS [Zou et al., 2026] improves the efficiency of recursive collaboration through latent-state communication. Complementing existing approaches to efficient collaboration, ConvRSI uses

3.1. Hidden States in Autoregressive Text Generation 

An autoregressive language model generates a response one token at a time. Given an input context 

x and the previously generated tokens y<t, a causal Transformer [Vaswani et al., 2017] produces a contextual hidden state ht−1 ∈ RH. An output head maps this state to the distribution of the next token: 

pθ (yt | x, y<t) = softmax (︀ Wout ht−1 + bout )︀ yt . (1) The hidden state is a continuous representation of the preceding context, whereas the output distribution is defined over discrete vocabulary tokens. During teacher forcing, the model conditions on a reference prefix y⋆<t and causal masking allows all reference positions in a given sequence to be processed in one forward pass. During free generation, the model instead conditions on its own preceding outputs. Hidden states also provide a communication medium for a multi-agent system. Rather than decoding every intermediate thought into text, an agent can transform its hidden states into continuous input embeddings for another agent. Because agents may have different hidden widths and embedding spaces, these representations require learned mappings before they can be consumed by the receiving model.

## 5. Experiments 

5.1. Experimental Setup Datasets and metrics. We use the Sequential-Math and Sequential-Code training datasets released with RecursiveMAS [Zou et al., 2026] for mathematical reasoning and code generation, respectively. We evaluate mathematical reasoning on MATH-500 [Lightman et al., 2024; HuggingFaceH4, 2024] using final-answer accuracy and code generation on MBPP+ [Liu et al., 2023] using pass@1, the fraction of problems for which a single generated solution passes all benchmark tests. We report both metrics as percentages averaged over four random seeds. 

Baselines and backbones. We compare ConvRSI with five baselines. The first two use the Sequential-Light configuration of RecursiveMAS [Zou et al., 2026]: Recursive-TextMAS exchanges intermediate outputs as text, while RecursiveMAS passes latent states between agents. Both use Qwen3-1.7B as the planner, Llama-3.2-1B-Instruct as the critic, and Qwen2.5-Math-1.5B-Instruct as the solver. The remaining baselines are ModularRSI [Wu et al., 2026], Hermes+ [Xue et al., 2026], and MASS [Lee et al., 2026]. We adapt all three to use the Sequential-Light solver backbone, Qwen2.5-Math-1.5B-Instruct [Yang et al., 2025b], in place of the DeepSeek backbones used by ModularRSI and Hermes+ and the 27B Qwen backbone used by MASS. 

Inference and evaluation. We evaluate ConvRSI , Recursive-TextMAS, and RecursiveMAS at recursion depths r ∈ { 1, 2, 3 }. Following RecursiveMAS [Zou et al., 2026], we use top-p sampling with p = 0.95 

on both benchmarks. For MATH-500, we set the temperature to 0.6 and the maximum generation length to 2,000 tokens. For MBPP+, we use a temperature of 0.2 and a maximum of 4,000 tokens. Within each benchmark, all methods receive the same problem statement in the input format required by each method and use the same decoding settings and scoring procedure. We measure inference efficiency on NVIDIA RTX 4090D GPUs and report total runtime (seconds) and the mean number of generated tokens per problem.

5.4. Comparison with Alternative Architectures 

We compare ConvRSI with three alternative approaches to self-improvement. ModularRSI [Wu et al., 2026] uses successful and failed execution trajectories to improve individual modules of an agent’s harness, such as tool use and context management. Hermes+ [Xue et al., 2026] retains and updates memories and procedural skills for reuse on later tasks. MASS [Lee et al., 2026] searches over multi-agent workflows and fine-tunes its model on trajectories generated by those workflows. We also include Recursive-TextMAS [Zou et al., 2026] as a text-based comparison within the Sequential-Light configuration. Table 5 shows that ConvRSI achieves the highest scores among the configurations evaluated with Qwen solvers, reaching 75.0% accuracy on MATH-500 and 32.9% pass@1 on MBPP+. The closest comparison is Recursive-TextMAS: with the same Sequential-Light team and three recursion rounds, 

ConvRSI gains 5.4 percentage points on MATH-500 and 3.8 points on MBPP+. MASS also improves from Level-0 to Level-1 on both benchmarks, gaining 5.9 and 5.2 points, respectively. At Level-1, it trails ConvRSI by 6.3 points on MATH-500 and 4.2 points on MBPP+.

## 6. Conclusion 

We presented ConvRSI , a framework that restructures multi-agent self-improvement through coupled intra-agent and inter-agent convolution. By jointly refining agent features and aggregating messages across a collaboration graph, ConvRSI supports individual refinement and collective evolution in latent space. Its matrix-based operations expose opportunities for parallel computation while accommo-dating different multi-agent collaboration patterns. Experiments show that ConvRSI improves task performance while reducing computational costs and latency across different multi-agent collaboration patterns. Future work will explore more diverse agent collaboration patterns and extend ConvRSI to large-scale agent swarms.
