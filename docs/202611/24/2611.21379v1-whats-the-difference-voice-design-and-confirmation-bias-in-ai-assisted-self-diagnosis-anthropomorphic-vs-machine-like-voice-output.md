## 摘要
随着基于人工智能（AI）的症状检查工具日益成为人们获取健康信息的常见入口，这类工具对非专业用户诊断决策的影响引发了有关信任与偏差的问题。其中尤需关注的是确认偏差，即倾向于偏好支持既有信念的信息，同时忽视与之矛盾的信息。此类系统的一项核心设计选择是语音风格：系统应采用类似人类的拟人化语音，还是明显具有机器特征的异人化语音。本研究考察AI症状检查工具的语音设计是否会调节自我诊断中的确认偏差。在一项被试内在线实验中（N = 81名参与者，共648次试验），参与者先选择诊断并评定信心，随后听取AI生成的建议；该建议由拟人化语音或机器风格语音播报，内容则支持或反驳参与者最初的选择，之后参与者再次作出判断。研究发现，AI的影响显著且取决于建议方向：在AI建议与参与者意见相悖的试验中，参与者有45.7%的情况下修正了诊断；而在建议与其意见一致时，这一比例仅为1.2%（广义线性混合模型，GLMM，p < .001）。受到一致建议时，参与者的信心有所上升；受到相悖建议时，信心几乎没有变化（稳健线性混合模型，p < .001）。相较于一致建议，相悖建议还会延长决策时间（Gamma GLMM，p < .001）。尽管操纵检验证实参与者明显感知到两种语音的差异，但上述效应在两种语音之间并无不同（语音类型或交互项均不显著，最小p值为.282）。研究结果表明，在AI辅助自我诊断中，与确认相关的偏差主要受AI建议的方向影响，而非语音的拟人化程度。

## Abstract
As symptom checkers based on artificial intelligence (AI) are becoming common entry points for health information, their influence on lay users' diagnostic decisions raises questions about trust and bias. Of particular concern is confirmation bias, the tendency to favour information that supports one's existing belief while discounting information that contradicts it. One of the central design choices for such systems is the style of voice: whether they should present themselves with a human-like (anthropomorphic) or a clearly machine-like (xenomorphic) voice. This study investigates whether the voice design of an AI symptom checker modulates confirmation bias in self-diagnosis. In a within-subjects online experiment (N = 81 participants, 648 trials), participants selected a diagnosis and rated their confidence, then heard an AI-generated recommendation that either confirmed or contradicted their initial choice, delivered by an anthropomorphic or a machine-like voice, before deciding again. We observed a strong, direction-dependent influence of the AI: participants revised their diagnosis in 45.7% of trials when the AI contradicted them versus 1.2% when it confirmed them (generalised linear mixed model, GLMM, p < .001); confidence rose under confirmation but barely moved under contradiction (robust linear mixed model, p < .001); and decisions took longer after contradiction (Gamma GLMM, p < .001). This effect did not differ between the two voices (no voice or interaction term was significant, smallest p = .282), even though a manipulation check confirmed the voices were perceived as markedly different. The findings suggest that the direction of an AI recommendation, rather than the anthropomorphism of its voice, drives confirmation-related bias in AI-assisted self-diagnosis.
