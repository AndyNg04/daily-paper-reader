# The Dual-Tier Sentiment Framework: Scalable Multimodal UX Attribution in Web Search 

## Yi Ding ∗, Gaurav Kumar, Rui Min, Isabella Ye, Bhavika Goyal, Emilio Antunez, Huihui Chen, Ke Xu, Xiaowei Sun, Xin Xu, Huijie Feng, Pradyumna Narayana, Golnaz Farhadi

Abstract 

Sustaining user experience (UX) quality in large-scale web search requires scalable evaluation. Traditional surveys are slow and bottleneck rapid A/B testing, while automated met-rics often lack the granularity to diagnose visual-spatial UX anomalies. To address this, we introduce the Dual-Tier Senti-ment Framework, a novel industrial architecture for automated UX attribution. Tier 1 (Macro System) acts as a scalable user sentiment regression detector, utilizing a fine-tuned encoder-decoder LLM (based on Gemini XS) on telemetry features. Through multi-task regularization and contextual feature en-gineering, this tier achieves a +9.8% PR-AUC gain on over 500,000 held-out records, matching baseline recall on all val-idated regressions with around 20% relative precision gain among the 12 held-out experiments. In production, it reduces measurement latency from weeks to hours and cuts survey overhead by over 40%. Tier 2 (Micro System) provides gran-ular diagnostics by employing a Multimodal LLM (Gemini 3.0 Pro) to natively assess visual renderings and spatial layout of web result blocks, quantifying “User Toil” as the estimated effort required to reach a satisfactory result. Enhanced by RL-based automated prompt optimization and an AI judge for win/loss patterns, the MLLM yields a >6.5% F1 score improvement, offering deep insights into content quality and layout friction. Ultimately, this framework enables scalable, accurate, and diagnostic UX attribution, processing O(100k) live experiment queries daily. 

## Introduction 

The continuous evolution of modern web search and ad-vertising platforms is driven by massive, high-frequency A/B experiments (Kohavi, Tang, and Xu 2020). While these rapid release cycles are essential for product innovation, ac-curately capturing how users subjectively experience these changes at scale remains a critical industry challenge. Ex-posing users to localized layout friction or visual degradation erodes platform trust and diminishes long-term engagement. Consequently, developing sophisticated, automated systems to evaluate User Experience (UX) is essential. A central difficulty in this domain is the challenge of scal-able Multi-modal UX Attribution. In a production environ-ment, evaluating search satisfaction presents distinct hurdles compared to traditional offline, text-based relevance scoring:

## Related Work 

Traditional search result quality evaluation has historically relied on implicit behavioral click models and utility metrics, which often miss the complex visual layouts and cognitive friction of modern web navigation (Borisov et al. 2018; Shi, Jayawardena, and Gwizdka 2025). Consequently, recent aca-demic efforts have transitioned to Multimodal Large Lan-guage Models (MLLMs). Architectures such as UGround (Gou et al. 2025) and Aria-UI (Yang et al. 2025) show that grounding raw bounding box coordinates significantly en-hances pixel-level spatial understanding (Chen et al. 2023). However, these models typically evaluate isolated screen-shots in controlled environments and lack mechanisms to capture dynamic pre-click scanning effort or post-click user toil. Moreover, recent benchmarking of MLLMs as UI eval-uators (Luera et al. 2025) reveals that off-the-shelf models align with human judgments on some UI dimensions but break down on others. This validates our Tier 2 design: be-cause base MLLMs struggle with specific UI nuances out-of-the-box, zero-shot inference is insufficient. We overcome this by utilizing RL Mentor-Student tuning and AI Judge align-ment to capture complex visual-spatial dimensions where standard MLLMs typically fail. Industrial high-throughput classifiers often sacrifice inter-pretability for latency. Recent architectures address this by leveraging large teacher models to distill Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning into lightweight student models (Kumar et al. 2026). Advanced multi-task learning paradigms, such as Dual-Head Reasoning Distillation (DHRD), further utilize this CoT rationale generation solely as a train-time regular-izer, boosting accuracy without inference delays (Xu et al. 2025). Additionally, while automated evaluation frameworks like CLEAR efficiently cluster systemic LLM-as-a-judge er-rors (Yehudai et al. 2026), and discrete Reinforcement Learn-ing (RL) methods like RLPrompt optimize black-box model instructions (Deng et al. 2022), these tools are typically benchmarked on deterministic reasoning tasks. They lack the cohesive integration needed to resolve the ambiguity and subjectivity of human UX sentiment. Our proposed frame-work bridges this gap. 

## System Design and Implementation 

To quantify user satisfaction as described in the dual-criteria launch paradigm introduced in Section 1, our architecture evaluates live experiment traffic through two synergistic pipelines. The Macro System (Tier 1) operates on aggre-gate telemetry to provide a high-throughput global safeguard against sentiment degradation. Concurrently, the Micro Sys-tem (Tier 2) leverages multimodal capabilities to diagnose spatial and temporal friction when navigating through web search results. 

Tier 1: Macro Sentiment Detection 

The Macro System serves as the first line of defense, com-puting aggregate user satisfaction scores across control and treatment arms to detect whole-page experience degradation with statistical significance. 

Dual-Head Model Architecture Operating as the frame-work’s primary screening mechanism, Tier 1 must support an inference throughput of O(1 M) queries. By rapidly iden-tifying experiments that cause whole-page sentiment degra-dation, this tier acts as an early-stopping gate—effectively bypassing the need for slow, costly human surveys and avoid-ing computationally intensive visual analysis in Tier 2. To achieve this scale under strict production latency constraints, we designed a hybrid encoder-decoder architecture that pro-cesses only lightweight, text-based modalities. To balance predictive accuracy with interpretability, we separate the tasks across two distinct heads: the encoder functions as a discriminatory classifier predicting binary user sentiment (Regression vs. No Regression), while the decoder is tasked with generating Chain-of-Thought (CoT) rationales. During the training phase, the reasoning targets are dis-tilled from a massive “teacher” model that processes histor-ical human survey responses and rich text features. During live inference, the encoder head directly outputs sentiment predictions while the decoder concurrently synthesizes rea-soning. Crucially, the multi-task learning signal from the decoder’s CoT generation acts as a structural regularizer. This dual-head paradigm improves the encoder’s predictive Precision-Recall Area Under Curve (PR-AUC) by an abso-lute margin of +2.0%. 

Feature Representations To capture the nuanced context of search and advertising, the macro model ingests three distinct categories of sparse telemetry: 

• Lexical Context (TEXT) (Base textual representations, encompassing the search query text, sponsored ad titles, and organic search snippets). 

• Quantitative Metadata (QF) (Query-level structural fea-tures, including ad density, commercial load, match types, and high-level UI layout configurations). 

• Aggregate User Interactions Features (UF) (Aggregate behavioral signals reflecting engagement, such as click-through rates, and post-click abandonment). 

Tier 2: Micro Diagnostic System 

While Tier 1 captures telemetry-driven regressions, it lacks the spatial awareness to diagnose why a visual UI change frustrated a user and how to attribute the root cause on a fine-granular level. The Micro System complements this limitation by quantifying the granular, multimodal fric-tion—termed User Toil —that individuals experience when navigating a Search Results Page (SRP). This is achieved through a structured, four-stage execution pipeline (Figure 2). 1. Sample SRP & Annotate: The pipeline samples vi-sual renderings from live traffic and annotates exact pixel bounding-box coordinates for each distinct result block. 2. Context Enrichment: Raw visual renderings are en-riched with (a) Visual Context via the rendered represen-tation of result blocks; (b) Refined Query Intent (RQI) dis-tilled from aggregate past history; and (c) Result-Specific Context , a semantic summary of its associated Landing Page (LP). 3. Autorate Satisfaction: An MLLM ingests the enriched contexts to evaluate intent alignment, semantic relevance, and visual clarity, assigning each block an ordinal satis-faction rating. 4. Compute Whole-Page User Toil: Finally, the pipeline aggregates these discrete ratings into the holistic User Toil metric, simulating the chronological friction of the user’s browsing journey. In offline stability checks, directional experiment-level Toil movements and Tier 2 launch recommendations remain stable across alternative aggregations of historical penalty weights that preserve ordinal monotonicity. 

Iterative Alignment and Prompt Optimization 

To ensure the Micro System accurately reflects complex hu-man subjectivity, we designed an automated alignment ar-chitecture that continuously refines the MLLM’s behavior without requiring full-parameter fine-tuning. AI-as-a-Judge for Loss Pattern Analysis We imple-mented an auxiliary MLLM (Gemini 3.0 Pro) equipped with toolkits for Google Search and URL context parsing to act as an “AI Judge” for Side-by-Side (SxS) evaluation. By ingest-ing human rating guidelines alongside the outputs of both hu-man experts and our autorater, the AI Judge systematically clusters failure patterns. For instance, the Judge identified that early iterations of our baseline struggled to distinguish boundaries in complex carousels (e.g. UI elements that group multiple individual result cards horizontally). This specific diagnostic insight drove the architectural decision to inject exact pixel bounding boxes into the prompt, yielding an im-mediate +5% F1 improvement against human ground truth. 

RL-Based Prompt Optimization To scale this alignment, we deployed a dual-agent Reinforcement Learning (RL) framework (Sayana, Todi, and Jash 2026) to automate prompt construction (Figure 3).

## Experimental Methodology and Results 

To validate the Dual-Tier Sentiment Framework, we designed an evaluation methodology that mirrors its production de-ployment: Tier 1 is evaluated on its ability to act as a high-throughput, high-recall regression net, while Tier 2 is eval-uated on its diagnostic precision against human-perceived visual friction. 

Tier 1: Macro User Sentiment Detection 

Dataset: Survey-Backed Telemetry Evaluating subjec-tive user friction at a whole-page scope requires proprietary datasets that accurately reflect real-world UI complexity. Ground truth labels for Tier 1 were derived from in-product user surveys triggered after a few minutes of active search session time. To ensure robust generalization, the Macro model was trained on data from 11 distinct A/B experiments. It was rigorously evaluated on a holdout suite of 12 unseen ex-periments collected strictly after the model checkpoint was frozen. This evaluation dataset comprises > 500 , 000 records sampled evenly across control and treatment arms to calcu-late experiment-level precision and recall. 

Ablation and Impact We fine-tuned the Macro Model us-ing PR-AUC as our primary metric to account for class im-balances in sentiment degradation. Extensive ablation studies (Table 1) quantify our architectural decisions. The baseline model, utilizing solely lexical features (TEXT), served as the reference and represents the prior production incumbent de-ployed on the same training data. Introducing the dual-head CoT regularizer brought an immediate +2.0% PR-AUC gain. The sequential integration of Quantitative Metadata (QF) and Aggregated User Interactions Features (UF) signals pushed the peak performance gain to +9.8% over the baseline. 

Translating Metrics to Operational Impact: This +9.8% PR-AUC gain fundamentally alters the A/B testing lifecycle: We define a sentiment regression as a statistically signifi-cant degradation in the aggregated user satisfaction metric (treatment versus control), verified using a required volume of events. Under this definition, Tier 1 successfully yields no missed regressions among the 4 validated positive re-gressions across the 12 held-out experiments. Furthermore, precision is evaluated at the experiment level: at the opera-tional gating threshold, the baseline model flagged 6 exper-iments (yielding a precision of 66.7%, i.e., 4/6), while the final dual-head Macro System flagged 5 experiments, yield-ing a precision of 80% (4/5)—an effective ∼20% relative de-tection precision improvement. Tier 2: Micro Diagnostic System 

While Tier 1 is evaluated on O(100 k) labels, Tier 2 relies on highly precise, multimodal alignment. A common challenge in MLLM evaluation is the scarcity of fine-grained spatial ground truth. To overcome this, we engineered two distinct datasets and leveraged our AI Judge to enforce label quality. 

Bootstrapping the Human-annotated Dataset via AI Judge Rather than blindly training a model on a massive, noisy dataset with high overfitting risk, our paradigm re-lies on a frozen foundational MLLM whose instructions are aligned via prompting. In this paradigm, label fidelity out-weighs label volume. To establish this fidelity, we extracted result blocks from live web renderings and tasked independent human raters with detailed editorial guidelines. Each extracted result block is double-annotated by independent raters on a four-tier or-dinal satisfaction scale followed by expert adjudication to resolve ties. Because Equation (1) truncates toil accumula-tion at idx VS , evaluation operationalizes binary Very Satisfied 

vs. Rest classification on a roughly balanced distribution. We deployed an AI Judge to systematically compare conflicting human labels to investigate the initial high inter-rater dis-agreement rate. This automated loss analysis exposed core ambiguities in how humans perceived boundaries in complex UI carousels. By iteratively refining the evaluation guidelines based on the AI Judge’s insights, we substantially increased dual-rater agreement across calibration rounds and stabilized the human rating process. Because each query encompasses multiple evaluated result blocks across organic and spon-sored slots, sampling O(100) queries for prompt optimiza-tion and a strictly isolated post-freeze holdout split of O(100) 

queries yields several hundred adjudicated result-level labels per split. This process provides over 1,000 expert-validated result labels in total when combined with earlier guideline-calibration batches. 

Validation against First-Result Surveys To prove that our expert labels accurately map to genuine user perception at scale, we deployed a novel “First-Result” user survey. Users were presented with the first viewport of a live SRP and asked to rate and justify their friction for only that specific result. To mitigate the inherent noise of human surveys, UX researchers and product managers established strict guide-lines to filter out objective spam such as nonsensical text or users evaluating the wrong UI element, yielding >5000 high-quality, real-user labels. Crucially, this holdout survey pool was kept completely isolated from the AI Judge. To en-sure the evaluation strictly measures visual-spatial UI fric-tion (User Toil), we denoised this pool by filtering out two specific categories of responses: (1) Invalid execution of the task (e.g., junk responses, evaluating the wrong UI element, or admitted misclicks); and (2) Out-of-scope platform biases (e.g., systemic AI aversion or subjective baseline differences that do not reflect UI layout quality). The final denoised pool retained >60% of the initial labels, ensuring the reported performance metrics evaluate our specific operational target rather than the raw, unfiltered survey distribution. To prevent data cleaning from introducing confirmation bias in favor of the autorater, the denoising classifier inspects only the user’s open-ended element description, rationale, and web result context. The survey cleaning process remains strictly blind to Tier 2’s prompt or predicted rating. 

Alignment Results The Micro System’s baseline prompt was initially optimized using the AI Judge’s win/loss pat-tern analysis. Injecting explicit pixel-coordinate features and applying Human-in-the-Loop prompt refinements yielded a 

+5% F1 improvement by resolving salient structural errors such as carousel boundary bleed. Beyond this plateau, further manual prompt edits exhibited precision-recall oscillation— tightening rubric constraints for specific failure modes im-proved precision on targeted slices while regressing recall overall. Transitioning to the automated Reinforcement Learn-ing (RL) framework overcame this trade-off, further improv-ing global F1 by > 1.5% . Crucially, when evaluating the frozen RL-optimized prompt policy against the completely independent pool of O(1000) denoised real-user survey re-sponses, the model sustained this F1 gain, empirically prov-ing that our prompt-aligned Micro System generalizes across both expert-adjudicated and real-user ground truth at an in-dustrial scale. Deployment Challenges and System Architecture 

Deploying a multimodal evaluation framework at O(100 k)

daily queries introduces unique infrastructural complexities. 

Bridging the Infrastructure Gap : A primary bottleneck was the Visual Rendering Dependency. Initial deployment of Tier 2 was blocked because the efficient infrastructure to render web results for model inference purposes did not exist. To resolve this, we engineered an asynchronous result-rendering pipeline. By heavily parallelizing the log-join and web-content-replay stages and leveraging deployment cell load balancing, we reduced the data processing time for a full A/B experiment cohort from several days to a few hours. 

Inference Cost and Latency Bounds : Deployed for of-fline evaluation, the framework avoids strict real-time latency constraints but requires massive sustained throughput. The computational cost of Tier 2 prevents synchronous, real-time serving. Generating bounding boxes, summarizing landing pages, and running inference through a massive MLLM (with a parameter count orders of magnitude larger than our macro model) for every query is economically prohibitive. There-fore, Tier 1’s lightweight encoder-decoder architecture op-erating at a scale of O(billions ) parameters is necessary to handle the raw scale. The expensive Tier 2 pipeline is in-voked only when fine-granular attribution is required. This asynchronous batch design aligns with its operational role given that A/B launches inherently require accumulating hours of live experiment impressions to achieve statistical power. Therefore, a multi-hour batch turnaround introduces no bottleneck to experiment launch decisions. 

## Lessons Learned & Best Practices 

Our journey from developing a telemetry-based screening tool to a granular, multimodal diagnostic engine yielded crit-ical insights into user behavior and LLM deployment. 

Isolating UX Friction from User Bias: We discovered raw “negative sentiment” in surveys is frequently skewed by factors unrelated to content quality (e.g., inherent ad or AI content aversion). Rigorous data-cleaning and AI Judge adju-dication were essential to isolate genuine spatial-UI friction (User Toil) from platform-level bias. 

Stability of Predicted Magnitude: We attempted to use generative LLMs for Tier 1 initially. While directionally pre-dictive, their predicted magnitude lacked numerical stability to consistently align with historical surveys. Consequently, we adopted the fine-tuned encoder-decoder for reliable re-gression screening. 

The Necessity of Contextual Intent Alignment: Early Micro System iterations often neglected query intent context, rating results as “Satisfied” based purely on visual aesthetics while disregarding deeper search goals of the user. Forcing the model to ingest Refined Query Intent (RQI) signals de-rived from aggregated history data was critical in shifting the model’s focus from mere visual quality to true cognitive friction. 

Automated Optimization vs. Manual Prompt Tuning: 

Finally, we highlight the severe limitations of manual prompt engineering in multimodal UX evaluation. Human engineers naturally over-index on recent, salient failure cases, often regressing overall accuracy when attempting to patch edge cases. By abstracting prompt construction to a dual-agent RL framework and auto prompt adjustment from win/loss patterns, we not only achieved higher F1 score but also elim-inated the human engineering overhead required to maintain the system as search UI layouts continuously evolve. 

## Limitations 

While our framework advances automated UX attribution, several limitations remain. First, the computational cost of multimodal rendering and MLLM inference restricts Tier 2 to asynchronous batch evaluation for A/B launch gating rather than synchronous real-time serving. Second, the empirical benchmarks reported in this work focus on US-based mo-bile traffic; although the underlying multilingual backbones and 2D bounding-box indexing support broader deployment, full validation across multi-column desktop layouts and non-US locales requires device- and region-stratified tscan recal-ibration and localized rater guidelines. Third, calibrating and validating against user surveys requires LLM-assisted denoising to isolate layout toil from platform priors; al-though blind multi-pass consensus mitigates confirmation bias, residual sensitivity to filtering criteria remains. Fi-nally, our current production evaluation scores static page renderings; extending attribution to interactive UI states (e.g., carousels, dynamic filters, and video previews) via interaction-conditioned multi-state rendering remains an ac-tive direction of ongoing work. 

## Ethical Statement 

The development and deployment of the Dual-Tier Senti-ment Framework involve processing aggregate user interac-tion data and visual representations of search results, neces-sitating careful ethical considerations: 1. Data Privacy and Anonymization: All telemetry data used for training and evaluating both Macro and Micro systems is anonymized. Privacy is strictly protected through tiered access controls and rigorous organizational policies. 2. Purpose Limitation: The data and models are used exclu-sively for evaluating and improving search and ads quality and user experience. The analysis focuses on identifying UX frictions such as visual clutter or content quality is-sues. 3. Data Retention: The collected data and associated analy-ses are subject to a strict time-to-live data retention policy, in line with internal data handling policies. By combining rigorous data protection and privacy with user sentiment modeling, we ensure our web results deliver genuine value and meet real information needs.
