## 摘要
评估语言模型给出的产品建议，需要判断推荐是否满足用户需求，并在未满足时找出模型的不足之处。然而，仅将在线回复中提及的产品与推荐进行匹配，无法独立回答这两个问题：可能有多种产品都适合，而记录下来的答案本身也可能是糟糕的建议。在向参与者展示社区列出的备选产品以及模型自行采样给出的汽车推荐后，对于符合条件且意见不一致的情况，六个模型选择备选产品的比例为 1.9% 至 6.5%；无论展示顺序如何，结果都一致，且展示时未透露来源。我们提出一个诊断框架，将用户明确表达的需求、模型添加的假设、候选项生成、筛选和最终选择区分开来，并列出 19 种可能的失败模式及评估这些模式所需的证据。我们将该框架应用于产品建议数据，以及另一个具有外部指定相关性标签的产品选择任务。实验区分了提及社区所列产品与实际选择该产品这两种情况，并展示了答案提取方式和候选列表构建方式如何影响结果。

## Abstract
Evaluating product advice from a language model requires assessing whether the recommendation meets the user's needs and identifying where the model falls short when it does not. However, matching a product mentioned in an online reply cannot answer either question on its own: several products may be suitable, and the recorded answer may itself be poor advice. When shown a community-listed alternative alongside their own sampled car recommendation, six models prefer the alternative in 1.9 to 6.5 percent of eligible disagreements, consistently across both source-blind presentation orders. We propose a diagnostic framework that separates the user's stated needs, the model's added assumptions, candidate generation, filtering, and final choice, with 19 possible failure modes and the evidence needed to assess them. We apply this framework to product-advice data and to a separate product-selection task with externally assigned relevance labels. The experiments distinguish naming a community-listed product from selecting it and show how answer extraction and candidate-list construction affect the outcome.
