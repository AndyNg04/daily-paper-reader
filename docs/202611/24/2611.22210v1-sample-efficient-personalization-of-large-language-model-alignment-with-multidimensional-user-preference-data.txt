Abstract 

Preference alignment has become a central tool to align large language models (LLMs) with human values and desired behavior, and its online variant can further advance their capabilities beyond what static data alone offer by repeatedly generating and labeling new responses with human feedback. But costly , time-consuming annotations limit its scalability, making sample efficiency essential, and existing methods typically seek to improve it by expanding coverage of the response space or selecting pairs with the highest uncertainty for comparison. Although they are effective, these methods quantify uncertainty from the perspective of the LLM, which may differ from the uncertainty perceived by the user: a pair can appear highly uncertain to the LLM even when past comparisons from the user already imply a clear ordering. In this paper, we propose multidimensional preference-guided online direct preference optimization (MPG-DPO), which leverages low-cost multidimensional evaluations from automated verifiers on the user historical comparisons to construct a personalized preference cone guiding response selection for online preference alignment. Specifically, it first discards responses that are confidently dominated under the estimated user preference, then selects two responses that are competitive under the retained preference cone yet exhibit the strongest unresolved disagreement. Theoretical analysis shows that, with high probability, the fraction of queries spent on cone-dominated responses vanishes as learning progresses. Experiments across multiple LLM families show that MPG-DPO outperforms strong contenders under matched query budgets. 

1. Introduction 

Preference alignment has achieved remarkable success in aligning large language models (LLMs) with human preferences and desired behaviors (Christiano et al., 2017; Ouyang et al., 2022). To continually push the boundaries of model capabilities, preference alignment is typically extended to an online process, where models repeatedly generate new responses for human evaluation (Guo et al., 2024; Calandriello et al., 2024; Xie et al., 2025). However, the high cost and long turnaround of the annotations required pose a major barrier to scaling this continuous feedback loop. Therefore, improving sample efficiency in online preference alignment is of critical importance. Existing approaches can be broadly divided into two categories. The first focuses on online exploration to improve policy coverage, increasing the sampling probability of responses that are poorly covered by previous data (Xie et al., 2025; Cen et al., 2025; Chen et al., 2025). The second line of work relies on active preference alignment, which first generates a pool of candidate pairs and then selects the most informative pair for comparison in each interaction (Das et al., 2025; Lin et al., 2026; Belakaria et al., 2025). While effective at optimizing response generation or pair selection, these methods mainly rely on model-perspective information, often querying feedback even when past comparisons made by the user already settle the ordering within a pair. For example, in code generation, previous choices may show that the user is willing to trade some clarity for a sufficiently large gain in accuracy. A new pair may still exhibit high reward uncertainty even when one response offers too little accuracy gain to justify its loss of clarity, making the comparison carry little 

user-perspective information despite appearing uncertain to the model. In a related direction, constitutional AI reduces repeated human supervision via high-level guiding principles (Bai et al., 2022; Huang et al., 2024), but these principles are hand-written and shared across users. To capture personalized trade-offs, specific dimensions like accuracy and clarity can fortunately be evaluated automatically, allowing them to naturally serve as low-cost multidimensional scores. This raises a question: How can low-cost multidimensional scores be leveraged to capture personalized preferences and improve sample efficiency? 

Using such multidimensional information is non-trivial because the scores are heterogeneous and can hardly be directly aggregated. The insightful AMPLe (Oh et al., 2025) uses multi-objective rewards through Bayesian scalarization, but its uncertainty-driven queries may compare responses already dominated by other alternatives. We instead treat each historical comparison as revealing a preferred direction of the user in the sub-objective space, and these directions form a personalized preference Pareto cone (Censor, 1977; Rame et al., 2023; Ma et al., 2026). The key challenge is to estimate this cone from historical data and use it to identify response pairs whose preference remains unresolved. In this paper, we use low-cost multidimensional evaluations to improve sample efficiency in online preference alignment. Each response is scored on attributes such as correctness and generation cost by low-cost or task-specific verifiers that are substantially cheaper than human evaluators. We construct a personalized preference cone from historical user comparisons and leverage its geometry to distinguish comparisons that are already resolved from those that represent genuinely unresolved trade-offs. We then select two competitive responses that exhibit the strongest unresolved disagreement among the retained preference directions . This preserves the exploration of potentially valuable responses while directing costly feedback toward comparisons that provide new information. We propose multidimensional preference-guided online direct preference optimization (MPG-DPO), which uses the user preference cone to guide response selection at each round of online DPO. Specifically, at each round, the current policy generates a pool of on-policy responses, and a ridge-regression estimator predicts their multidimensional score vectors together with confidence bounds. We eliminate responses that are confidently dominated by another response in the sampled pool and select a pair for which different preference directions favor different responses. After obtaining the preference label, we update the preference cone and the policy. Theoretical

4. Experiments 

In this section, we first introduce the experimental setup and then present the experimental results. Our experiments aim to answer the following three questions: Q1 : does the proposed method enhance the policy across different LLM families; Q2 : does the proposed preference-guided sampling make better use of the multidimensional information than other methods with additional access to it; and Q3 : how sensitive is the proposed method to its key parameters. 

4.1 Experimental Setup Prompts. We use the prompt-only split of ultrafeedback-prompt for online training, following the generation and training pipeline of the official XPO implementation in trl 2. All methods share this pipeline. For MPG-DPO, we initialize the preference matrix using comparisons collected from the preference oracle on 

ultrafeedback_binarized .

Models. We evaluate three model families: Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct , Mistral-7B-Instruct-v0.3 ,and OLMo-2-1124-7B-Instruct . Each reference policy is initialized from the corresponding policy check-point and remains frozen throughout training. Unless otherwise specified, MPG-DPO samples N = 8 candidate responses per prompt from the current policy. All methods use the same prompt stream, maximum response length, decoding parameters, optimizer, and preference-query budget. We train with LoRA adapters (Hu et al., 2022) using rank r = 32 and scaling parameter α = 64 , and report results over three random seeds. ϕ is meanwhile implemented as an independent feature extractor, which we fix to use an 86M parameter frozen 

microsoft/deberta-v3-base 3 model on the prompt-response pair. 

Datasets and evaluation metrics. We report four metrics: (i) average reward from a held-out reward model on the IMD B sentiment generation task (Maas et al., 2011), (ii) summarization win rate against human-written summaries on TL;DR (Völske et al., 2017), (iii) length-controlled win rate on A LPACA EVAL 2 (Dubois et al., 2024), and (iv) win rate on A RENA -H ARD (Li et al., 2025).

4.3 Comparison with Alternative Uses of Multidimensional Information 

We compare MPG-DPO with alternative pair-selection strategies that use the same multidimensional feedback. Feedback budgets and training protocol are fixed, with only the selection criterion varied. We construct three information-enhanced contenders: 1. Scalar-Margin selects the pair with the largest predicted scalar reward difference from the pool. 2. APL+MD-Score selects the pair with the largest sum of squared ridge-prediction confidence widths across its two responses, prioritizing uncertainty in the estimated multidimensional scores. 3. APL+MD-Scalar fits a Bradley–Terry model (Bradley and Terry, 1952) to historical five-dimensional score differences and preference labels. Treating the predicted scores as fixed, it selects the pair with the largest approximate information gain about the scalarization weights. These strategies provide comparisons with preference-cone-guided selection based on scalar reward differences, score-estimation uncertainty, and uncertainty in preference weights. All methods share the same prompt stream, predictor architecture, warm-up data, scoring and preference-query budgets, and DPO hyperparameters. Only queried responses receive oracle scores. We evaluate Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct 

and Mistral-7B-Instruct-v0.3 over three independent training seeds. The results in Table 3 support the effectiveness of preference-cone-guided selection relative to these alternative uses of multidimensional feedback, answering Q2 .

5. Related Work 

This section reviews relevant prior work in detail. 

Exploration in online preference alignment. Online preference alignment repeatedly generates responses from the current policy, obtains preference feedback, and updates the policy on the newly collected data (Calan-driello et al., 2024). A fruitful line of research aims to enhance policy-level coverage and generate high-value responses using a scalar implicit reward. XPO adds a principled exploration bonus to online DPO so that the policy can move beyond the support of the initial model and discover responses with higher latent reward (Xie et al., 2025). VPO similarly introduces optimism through value regularization, while recent theoretical work studies when preference-based exploration avoids poor dependence on the reward range (Cen et al., 2025; Chen et al., 2025). These methods primarily modify the DPO loss and can be seamlessly integrated into our algorithm. 

10 MULTIDIMENSIONAL PREFERENCE -GUIDED ONLINE DIRECT PREFERENCE OPTIMIZATION 

Active preference selection. A second line of work assumes a collection of candidate comparisons and actively decides which examples should receive costly preference labels (Das et al., 2025; Lin et al., 2026; Oh et al., 2026). APO formulates RLHF as a contextual preference bandit and allocates queries to statistically important prompt–response comparisons (Das et al., 2025). ActiveDPO derives a selection score from uncertainty in the LLM’s implicit reward difference and uses gradient projection and batch selection to make this criterion practical for nonlinear language models (Lin et al., 2026). Other work designs risk-adjusted active-learning criteria for preference optimization (Belakaria et al., 2025). These approaches rank candidates using scalar reward uncertainty while not exploiting the sub-objective trade-offs considered in our setting. Beyond preference learning, Wang et al. (2023) resolved a fundamental identifiability question through sound and complete orientation rules for local background knowledge in their latent-confounding setting. Their principled active causal discovery framework used accumulated knowledge to guide intervention selection, offering a complementary perspective on our preference-guided response-pair selection. 

Constitutional AI and rule-guided alignment. Constitutional AI replaces repeated human supervision with a written set of high-level principles that guides self-critique, response revision, and AI-generated preference labels (Bai et al., 2022). Lee et al. (2024) further study empirically whether an external language model can provide preference supervision at a quality comparable to human feedback, while collective constitutional AI obtains the constitution through a public deliberation process rather than solely from model developers (Huang et al., 2024). These methods demonstrate that explicit rules can make alignment supervision more scalable and interpretable. However, their constitutions are specified as natural-language principles and are typically shared across interactions. 

Pareto learning. The preference Pareto cone originates from Censor (1977) and has recently been used by Rame et al. (2023) for reward interpolation and by Ma et al. (2026) in multi-objective bandits. We adopt the same geometric abstraction but differ in both construction and use: our cone is estimated online from noisy pairwise comparisons via a consistency check, and is adaptively updated as new comparisons arrive to actively guide response selection. 

6. Conclusion 

In this paper, we proposed MPG-DPO, an online preference alignment method that incorporates historical user preferences into response selection alongside model uncertainty for sample efficiency. Using low-cost multidimensional evaluations, the method constructs a personalized preference cone to filter confidently dominated responses and select pairs exhibiting unresolved trade-offs. This selection mechanism accounts for preference information already implied by past user comparisons. Under the stated assumptions, our analysis suggests that the number of selected cone-dominated responses grows slowly, so such queries make up a vanishing fraction as learning progresses. Experiments across multiple LLM families show improvements over strong baselines under matched preference-query budgets, supporting the effectiveness of preference-cone-guided selection.
