## 摘要
规则帮助人们协调行动、相互合作并实现共同受益。为了与人类及其他 AI 系统共存，AI 模型需要能够成功地与此类规则互动并灵活应对。然而，这种互动要求 AI 系统不仅能够识别某项规则何时允许或禁止特定行为，还要能够识别人们可能对某项行为是否恰当存在合理分歧的情形。本文推出 RULES 基准：该基准基于 300 张描绘纽约市各处张贴标识的图像，并配有 937 个场景，用于测试前沿模型判断拟议行为在社会层面是可接受、不可接受，还是会引发分歧的能力。初步测试发现，前沿模型在该基准上表现稳健，能够在自然的视觉环境中理解由人类撰写的指示，并正确判断各种日常活动在社会层面是否可接受。然而，我们进一步发现，模型在识别某些广泛认可的规则例外（即规则允许的行为）方面存在系统性失误，这表明模型理解能力存在一项关键缺陷。此外，模型难以识别人们对特定规则的适用存在分歧的情形；在这些情况下，模型往往将拟议行为判定为不可接受，而非视为适合引发讨论的对象。我们发现，将一系列不同模型集成起来，开始能够恢复对分歧的识别能力。总体而言，我们的研究揭示了 AI 在理解人类规则方面存在重大局限。

## Abstract
Rules help people coordinate and cooperate for mutual benefit. For AI models to coexist with humans and other AI systems, they will need to interact with and navigate such rules successfully. But such interactions require AI systems to recognize not only when a rule permits or prohibits a particular action, but also cases where people may reasonably disagree about whether a certain action is appropriate. This paper introduces the RULES benchmark---building on a set of 300 images depicting signs posted around New York City, and accompanied by 937 scenarios---to test frontier models' ability to discern whether a proposed action is socially acceptable, unacceptable, or would generate disagreement. At first pass, we find that frontier models exhibit robust performance on the benchmark, successfully engaging with human-written directives in naturalistic visual environments, and correctly determining the social acceptability of a wide range of everyday activities. However, we further find that models systematically fail to recognize certain widely accepted permissible exceptions to a rule, indicating a critical shortcoming in model understanding. Moreover, models struggle to identify instances where humans disagree regarding the application of a given rule; in these instances, models tend to identify proposed actions as unacceptable, rather than as appropriate targets of disagreement. We do find that ensembling a series of diverse models together begins to recover the disagreement signal. Overall, our findings identify significant limitations in AI performance on human rules.
