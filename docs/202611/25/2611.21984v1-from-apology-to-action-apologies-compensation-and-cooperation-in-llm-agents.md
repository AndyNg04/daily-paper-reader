## 摘要
大型语言模型（LLM）能够做出影响其他智能体的决策，但在有害或未成功的交互之后会发生什么，我们仍知之甚少。我们通过受控的囚徒困境实验，研究 LLM 智能体的道歉是否与后续合作行为相关；实验采用四种商业模型（Claude Opus 4.8、GPT-5.5、Gemini 3.1 Pro 和 Mistral Large 3）。在不同收益条件下，我们考察智能体何时道歉、给出何种理由，以及随后采取何种行动。我们还考察了智能体能否获取一轮交互历史对其行为的影响，并在第二项实验中研究智能体是否以及如何在道歉时提供补偿。不同模型的道歉行为以及道歉行为与此前交互结果的关系存在显著差异；言语上表达合作并不总与后续行动一致。值得注意的是，Claude Opus 4.8 即使在双方合作后也会道歉，而 Mistral Large 3 在遭到利用后会道歉，但即便对手选择合作，它仍继续背叛。在一方单方面背叛后，GPT-5.5 和 Gemini 3.1 Pro 带有正向补偿的道歉之后，双方转而合作；Claude 和 Mistral 则没有出现这一结果。我们的研究结果表明，道歉、补偿与策略行为彼此有别：缓和关系的信息之后可能仍是持续背叛，而付出代价的道歉也可能得不到回应。这对机器行为与人机交互研究具有重要启示，因为言语信号未必能可靠反映 AI 系统随后采取的行动。

## Abstract
Large language models (LLMs) can make decisions that affect other agents, but what happens after a harmful or unsuccessful interaction remains poorly understood. We investigate whether apologies by LLM agents are associated with subsequent cooperative behaviour, using controlled Prisoner's Dilemma experiments with four commercial models (Claude Opus 4.8, GPT-5.5, Gemini 3.1 Pro, and Mistral Large 3). Across payoff conditions, we examine when agents apologise, the reasons they provide, and the actions that follow. We also examine how access to one round of interaction history affects behaviour and, in a second experiment, whether and how agents offer compensation alongside their apologies. Apology behaviour differed substantially across models and preceding interaction outcomes, and verbal expressions of cooperation did not always align with subsequent actions. Notably, Claude Opus 4.8 apologised even after mutual cooperation, while Mistral Large 3 apologised after being exploited but continued to defect even when its opponent cooperated. After unilateral defection, positively compensated apologies from GPT-5.5 and Gemini 3.1 Pro were followed by mutual cooperation, unlike those from Claude and Mistral. Our findings suggest that apologies, compensation, and strategic behaviour are distinct: conciliatory messages may precede continued defection, while costly apologies may go unanswered. This has important implications for the study of machine behaviour and human-AI interactions, as verbal signals alone may not reliably reflect an AI system's subsequent actions.
