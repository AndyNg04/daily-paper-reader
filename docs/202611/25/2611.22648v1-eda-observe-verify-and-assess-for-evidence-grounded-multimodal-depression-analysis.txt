Abstract
Existing multimodal depression assessment methods often summarize long recordings into global behavioral descriptions. This leaves the
source and decision relevance of individual observations difficult to inspect. We propose EDA, an evidence-grounded framework
organized around Observe, Verify, and Assess. In Observe, literature-informed acoustic and visual cues are localized into source-
linked Evidence Records with temporal intervals and behavioral measurements. In Verify, frozen multimodal agents examine whether
the cited media support each observation. The simplified Support admission policy retains positively verified records and their review
grounds. In Assess, statistical descriptors and temporal events support reference-based behavioral comparison, while PHQ-9-guided text
analysis provides complementary symptom evidence. Cross-modal disagreement triggers review of the original text before final selection.
Experiments on D-Vlog and LMVD evaluate human-referenced cue agreement and downstream assessment utility separately. AI cue
annotations achieve 91.02% accuracy against human consensus labels on shared source segments. With a frozen 7B backbone, EDA
Support improves F1 over direct prediction by 7.61 and 9.88 percentage points on D-Vlog Leave-Query-Out and LMVD, respectively.
These findings support explicit, source-linked behavioral evidence for inspectable multimodal depression assessment.

Introduction
Video-based depression assessment uses both spoken con-
tent and observable behavior.
Transcripts can describe
symptoms and personal experiences. Vocal expression, fa-
cial activity, and head movement provide additional infor-
mation [1, 5, 18]. In long recordings such as D-Vlog and
LMVD [10, 31], these behaviors vary over time and across
contexts. A useful assessment must identify relevant cues,
locate their source, and explain how they inform the final
judgment.
Current methods address different parts of this task. Fu-
sion models learn acoustic–visual interactions [24, 30],
while symptom-oriented language models organize tran-
script passages by depression-related domains [33, 36]. De-
pressionAgent adds modality-specific extraction, support–
challenge deliberation, and cross-modal reflection [41]. Yet
a recording-level evidence explanation still condenses a
long video into a short narrative (Fig. 1a). It tells readers
what the model observes.
A statement such as frequent
pauses names a behavior, but does not identify its source
intervals. Readers still need to know where/when it occurs
and how it affects the assessment. This leads to three linked
questions. Observe: What behavioral evidence is observ-
able? Acoustic and visual behaviors need reproducible def-
initions and source intervals. Verify: Is the observation
supported by its source? The cited media and measure-
ments must support each candidate before it is retained. As-
sess: How should verified evidence inform assessment?
Behavioral comparison, symptom-related text, and conflict
review must have explicit roles in the final judgment.
We introduce EDA around Observe, Verify, and Assess.
Observe operationalizes five acoustic and four visual cues
from prior literature. Their annotations use no depression
labels and retain source intervals and measurements. Verify
checks these records against the original media. Its Evi-
dence mechanism combines Support verification, Counter
challenge, and cue-level adjudication, retaining the grounds
for admission or exclusion. The optimized Support policy
admits positively verified records directly and forms our fi-
nal model. Assess compares retained behavioral records
with labeled references, using statistical descriptors and

Observe: Fine-Grained Behavioral Cue
Annotation
EDA defines which behaviors the audio-visual workflow
should observe. We derive cue concepts from the litera-
ture, specify observable criteria, and annotate their time in-
tervals. These annotations describe behavior independently
of the final depression label.
3.1
Literature-informed Cue Definitions
Prior behavioral studies guide the cue inventory. Speech
studies inform low loudness variation [7], slow pitch
change [19, 42], slow articulation [2], long pauses [18, 29],
and repeated long pauses [1]. Visual-behavior studies in-
form infrequent lip-corner lowering, frequent lip-corner
tightening, and limited head movement [5], as well as
downward head posture [4].
These studies inform five
acoustic and four visual cue definitions (Fig. 2). We re-
tain the original nonconsecutive visual identifiers for record
consistency.
The same definitions apply to both depression-label groups.
Annotators identify the cue and its temporal extent without
using the recording’s depression label. Labels enter only the
later reference comparison and distribution analysis. This
separates the annotation of a behavior from its interpreta-
tion in depression assessment. The supplement lists the full
criteria and their literature basis.
3.2
Operationalization and Annotation Records
We construct fine-grained behavioral cue annotations
through automatic interval localization and source-media
review.
OpenFace extracts facial action units and head
pose, while FSMN-VAD identifies speech activity inter-
vals.
Cue selection uses pre-segmented 3-second audio
and video windows with cue-specific measurement crite-
ria. Adjacent windows with consistent cue assignments are
merged. Source review checks the corresponding media,
surrounding context, and within-recording baselines. Each
record contains the recording identifier, cue label, onset,

4.2
PHQ-9-guided Textual Evidence Analysis
Text agents first retrieve symptom candidates from the orig-
inal transcript using the nine PHQ-9 domains [11]. Each
passage records its symptom topic, temporal status, and se-
mantic relation. These fields distinguish current personal
experience from history, denial, third-party statements, and
general discussion. For each domain, agents collect sup-
porting passages, counter-evidence, and qualifying con-
text. The assessment role considers these records along-
side personal-status information and the original transcript.
An internal text review checks the initial judgment. The
workflow returns ˆyT, a rationale, and citations to original
passages. It interprets symptom evidence without assigning
questionnaire scores.
The audio-visual workflow runs in parallel.
It receives
the cue annotations from Sec. 3 and checks each candi-
date against the original audio or video. It then computes
a separate behavioral assessment. Neither cue annotation
nor source review receives the recording’s depression label.

5.1
Datasets, Reference Construction, and Metrics
Evaluation protocols.
We evaluate on D-Vlog and
LMVD [10, 31]. D-Vlog test follows the original bench-
mark split. Its reference pool uses labeled training record-
ings only and excludes validation and test recordings. For
LMVD, we sort source identifiers and apply stratified ten-
fold evaluation with shuffle and seed 42.
This follows
the documented BHDD-style construction [14]. Each fold
builds its references and normalization from the other nine
folds. We pool held-out predictions to compute the metrics.
We also evaluate D-Vlog under Leave-Query-Out. The
reference pool uses other eligible same-language recordings
and their labels. It excludes the assessed recording and its
label. This is leave-one-recording-out reference construc-
tion, not leave-one-query-out cross-validation. The model
stays frozen and is not retrained for each recording. Only
reference membership and derived statistics change. This
setting permits labels of other recordings in the assessed
cohort. Benchmark testing permits training labels only. We
report the two settings separately. Consistent with Depres-
sionAgent [41], D-Vlog workflow and ablation tables pri-
oritize Leave-Query-Out. Table 1 and the supplement retain
test comparisons with training references only.

5.2
Observe: Behavioral Cue Characterization
Cue annotations replace a video-level account with named
records and source intervals. Figure 3 shows that cue oc-
currence has different associations with depression across
datasets.
Retained downward head posture (V05) ap-
pears in 92.86% of depression-labeled and 81.97% of non-
depression-labeled D-Vlog recordings. The corresponding
LMVD rates are 21.81% and 21.97%. Limited head move-
ment (V07) also shows a larger label-group difference on
D-Vlog. These differences make cue interpretation dataset-
dependent. EDA preserves each cue and its source interval
for reference comparison, rather than assigning it a fixed
depression meaning. The retained inventory does not itself
measure annotation correctness.
5.3
Verify: Source Grounding and Evidence
Admission
Evidence quality concerns whether a cue has observable
support in its cited source segment. The pooled 91.02%
human-referenced cue accuracy checks this agreement. The
shared segmentation supplies the source anchors, so this
score does not measure independently estimated boundary
accuracy. It also does not establish that one admission pol-
icy produces more reliable evidence than another.
Assessment utility concerns what the evidence and its use
contribute to classification. Table 4 compares Support with
extended Evidence review. Cue Retention is the share of ini-
tial candidate cue intervals retained by a policy. It measures
admission selectivity, not evidence correctness. Support re-
tains 1.26 points more intervals on D-Vlog Leave-Query-
Out and 7.40 points more on LMVD. Its AV F1 gains are
1.37 and 0.08 points, while its AVT F1 gains are 0.12 and
0.25 points. The larger retention change on LMVD accom-
panies the smaller AV gain. Thus, retention and assessment
utility need separate evaluation. Support improves predic-
tion while preserving the shared source-verification mecha-
nism.

The inventory contains five acoustic and four visual cues
from prior behavioral studies.
Table S1 lists each cue’s

The definitions
describe behavior without using the final depression label.

S1.1
Annotation Records and Dataset Variation
Each cue has an observable criterion, a temporal scope,
source-validity checks, and interval-merging rules. Each
annotation records the recording and cue identifiers, onset,
offset, and source excerpt. We derive descriptors from these
records for later comparison. Wider context and within-
recording baselines support review. The original noncon-
secutive visual identifiers are retained for consistency. Text
passages are annotated separately by PHQ-9 domain, tem-
poral status, and semantic relation. These fields distinguish
current personal symptoms from history, denial, third-party
experience, and general discussion. They describe semantic
evidence and do not assign questionnaire scores.
Retained downward head posture (V05) appears in 92.86%
of depression-labeled and 81.97% of non-depression-
labeled D-Vlog recordings. The LMVD rates are 21.81%
and 21.97%. Limited head movement (V07) also shows
greater label-group separation on D-Vlog. These patterns
describe annotations retained by Evidence. They do not
measure exhaustive cue prevalence or clinical specificity.
Excluding an annotation does not establish that the cue is
absent. Cue-detection recall requires exhaustive human an-
notations.
Annotation tools and workflow.
We use automatic fea-
ture extraction, candidate interval localization, and multi-
agent source-evidence review. Visual features are extracted
using OpenFace, including facial action units AU15 and
AU14, face-tracking confidence, and head pitch and yaw
angles.
Audio is decoded into a 16 kHz mono signal.
FSMN-VAD identifies speech activity intervals, from which
we extract RMS loudness, fundamental-frequency trajec-
tories, speech duration, and pause information. Table S2
summarizes the annotation and measurement procedures for
each cue.

Distance aggregation and missing comparisons.
The
event-level discrepancy between a query and a class is the
median symmetric distance to reference samples of that
class. Measurements are averaged equally within each sta-

averaged equally within each cue, followed by an equal-

counting unit. Comparisons involving empty event sets or
missing references for either class are excluded from ag-

unavailability flag and receive the default class 0 in full-
denominator evaluation. Ties between class distances are
assigned to class 0.

of 256 tokens. Independent and direct source-evidence re-

initial generation budgets of 512 and 384 tokens and maxi-

Textual evidence organization.
We organize original
transcript passages into PHQ-9 symptom support, explicit
denial, personal-status statements, and contextual quali-

Unimodal text

denial, and alternative explanations; internal review inde-

temporal context, and symptom meaning. A valid review
changes the initial prediction only when it disagrees with
that prediction and its confidence in the selected class is at
least 0.8, yielding the final text label T.

S7.1
Human Annotation and Source-Grounding
Validation Protocol
The human evaluation covers annotated material from both
D-Vlog and LMVD. Humans and AI use the same pre-
segmented source units: consecutive 3-second windows for
audio and for video. Annotators select the applicable cues
from the literature-informed nine-cue codebook, using the
corresponding modality and observable criteria. Adjacent
windows with consistent cue assignments are merged into
annotation intervals.
The common segmentation avoids
matching independently chosen human and AI boundaries.
Three researchers in affective computing independently re-
view the source segments without seeing AI annotations.
For each segment, each cue receives a binary present/absent
judgment. A cue with unanimous initial judgments is re-
tained without further review.
Only cues with disagree-
ment are referred to a panel comprising the original three re-
searchers and two instructors in psychological counseling.
The instructors participate in reviewing the disputed cues
rather than creating a separate annotation set. All five panel
members then vote on each disputed cue, and a majority of
at least three determines its binary label. Agreement and
escalation are therefore assessed per cue, not by comparing
entire multi-label sets. The resulting human consensus is
the ground-truth basis for evaluating the AI annotations.
We calculate annotation accuracy as
Acccue = Kmatch
Keval
×100%.

racy is 91.02% across the two datasets. It is not a D-Vlog-
only result, an inter-rater reliability statistic, or a policy-
specific Support–Evidence comparison. No separate per-
dataset score is inferred from it.

Box T1: Textual Evidence Extraction Agent.
Role. You are a textual evidence extractor. Extract evidence related to PHQ-9 symptoms and personal status from the participant’s
transcript. Do not predict a binary label or estimate questionnaire scores.
Inputs. The original transcript with segment identifiers and the PHQ-9 symptom definitions.
Task rules.
• Read the source and extract symptoms, explicit denials, and personal-status statements expressed by the participant.
• Preserve the experiencer, time, negation, and contextual qualifications. Distinguish current or temporally unspecified personal
experiences from historical or resolved states, other people’s experiences, and general discussion.
• Quotes must come from their cited source segments. Do not join different segments or invent symptoms, frequency, or severity.
• Each symptom quote must express its assigned item. Ordinary task frustration, boredom with one activity, or general life stress
does not automatically establish a symptom.
• Physical illness, medication, and life events may qualify the evidence or provide alternative explanations, but do not automatically
erase an explicitly reported symptom.
• Record explicit personal depression status and treatment statements separately; they do not replace specific symptom evidence.
• Absence of mention is not denial. Do not fill unsupported items or count duplicate evidence repeatedly.
Output format. Return structured evidence containing symptom-support records (PHQ-9 item, source segment identifier, quotation,
and qualifications), explicit-denial or opposite-experience records (item, source identifier, and quotation), personal-status records
(depression or treatment quotation, experiencer, and time), and source-supported alternative explanations and uncertainties.
Box T2: Initial Textual Assessment Agent.
Role. You are a textual assessment agent. Produce a research binary prediction from structured textual evidence, not a clinical
diagnosis.
Inputs. Extracted symptom support, explicit denials, personal-status statements, alternative explanations, and uncertainties.
Decision rules.
• Integrate all evidence. Do not decide solely from a keyword, one symptom, or the number of symptoms.
• Persistent or explicit loss of interest or low mood, multiple independent current symptoms, functional impairment, and self-harm
expressions can strengthen a positive assessment.
• Reviewed personal current or temporally unspecified depression status and current antidepressant treatment may provide personal-
status evidence, but cannot substitute for specific symptoms.
• Other people’s experiences, general discussion, resolved history, and medication of unspecified purpose do not establish the
participant’s current status.
• A single mild, temporally unclear, or explicitly explained symptom does not automatically support a positive assessment.
• Unmentioned symptoms are not counter-evidence. Reflect uncertainty when support and denial coexist.
• Ground the rationale and key items in the supplied evidence. Do not infer the dataset label or questionnaire score.
Output format. Return a JSON object with the following fields:
• prediction_label2: initial label, 0 or 1.
• probability_label2_is_1: probability of class 1 in [0,1].
• rationale: principal evidence and reasoning for the assessment.
• key_item_numbers: the PHQ-9 items supporting the assessment.
• uncertainty: unresolved uncertainty.

Role. You are an independent textual evidence reviewer. Revisit the source to check symptom interpretations and their evidential
basis, and produce a review used to determine the final text label T.
Inputs. Original textual context with segment identifiers and speaker information, without audiovisual predictions or ground-truth
labels.

S9.4
Counter Challenge
Box A3: Counter Agent.
Role. You are a behavioral counter-evidence reviewer. Seek specific counter-evidence or confounding evidence that could overturn
the core behavioral claim. Do not replace Support with a supporting judgment.
Inputs. The behavioral claim, core-interval observations, surrounding-context observations, and relevant behavioral measurements
and quality information.
Task rules.
• Seek specific counter-evidence against the core behavior definition. Context provides interpretation but cannot replace the core
state or contribute to the core behavior proportion.
• Inaudibility does not establish stable loudness or slow pitch changes. A forward-facing head does not establish sustained downward
posture.
• Examine the necessary conditions of the behavior definition. Differences in task, sample, or statistical unit from the literature limit
generalization but do not automatically negate an observed behavior.
• For proportion-based cues, observing an action at least once does not establish a high occurrence proportion.
• Detection failure, low confidence, duplicate frames, tracking gaps, or camera motion do not establish low activity. Insufficient
quality means the claim cannot be verified, not that a behavioral counterexample exists.
• Use quality information only when it affects the current claim; it cannot replace core-media observation.
• Missing information is unknown, not normal or abnormal.
• Failure to find counter-evidence does not support the claim. Leave the validity of counter-evidence and conflicts between obser-
vations to the Evidence agent.
Judgment categories. Counter-evidence found: a specific observation may negate the core claim. No counter-evidence found: the
claim was checked without finding a specific counterexample; this provides no additional support. Unverifiable: observation or
quality is insufficient for reliable verification.
Output format. Return a JSON object containing only a judgment (counter-evidence found, no counter-evidence found, or unver-
ifiable) and a reason of at most 80 Chinese characters citing core observations and times and explaining the counter-evidence or
observation limit. Requests with audiovisual speaker verification enabled additionally return a speaker-verification result: partici-
pant, non-participant, or uncertain. Omit this field when that check is disabled.

pairment, and explicit self-harm expressions. Examine indirect self-harm expressions semantically rather than requiring a particular

S10
Scope and Further Evaluation
Behavioral cues are nonspecific, and annotations can con-
tain errors. Source links allow inspection but do not es-
tablish clinical validity. These cohorts were used during
cue and decision development. Using training references
alone does not make them untouched prospective evalua-
tions. Recording-level folds do not guarantee speaker sep-
aration. Single-run scores do not measure inference vari-
ability or isolate the effect of model size.
Further work
needs external cohorts, repeated inference runs, and policy-
specific evidence-quality evaluation. Source recordings and
quotations retain their original access restrictions. EDA has
not been validated for autonomous clinical use.
Completed validation and further paired diagnostics.
Section S7.1 reports the completed human protocol and
pooled 91.02% cue-annotation accuracy.
This source-
segment check is separate from policy-specific evidence
quality. A paired Support–Evidence evaluation would re-
quire the same source units, human labels, and downstream
settings. It could then measure retained-cue precision and
recall, erroneous admission, and missed supported cues.
These additional metrics are not reported here. Indepen-
dently annotated boundaries would also be needed for a
free-boundary localization metric.
Evidence quality and assessment utility.
Evidence qual-
ity asks whether a cue is supported by its cited source. As-
sessment utility asks whether the evidence workflow im-
proves prediction or revision. The completed human check
addresses cue agreement on common segments.
Admis-
sion counts describe selection behavior, not source preci-
sion. The final EDA Support model optimizes that selection
while retaining source verification. Its classification gains
over extended Evidence review do not establish superior an-
notation quality. Similarly, temporal events preserve local
source access without guaranteeing better classification.
Among accepted revisions, the beneficial proportions are
87.50% on D-Vlog Leave-Query-Out and 58.97% on
LMVD. Harmful changes account for 12.50% and 41.03%,
respectively. These are conditional on outputs that changed.
Their confidence intervals and net accuracy gains are
given in Table S14. Figure 4 shows completed source-to-
assessment examples, while these aggregate revision statis-
tics include successes and failures.
Backbone effects.
Table S7 reports the backbone com-
parison. On D-Vlog Leave-Query-Out, 30B-A3B EDA im-
proves accuracy and F1 over direct prediction by 8.81 and
6.39 points. A larger backbone does not always improve
EDA. With Evidence admission, 7B exceeds 30B-A3B by
3.12 F1 points. Both evidence organization and backbone
choice affect the result.
