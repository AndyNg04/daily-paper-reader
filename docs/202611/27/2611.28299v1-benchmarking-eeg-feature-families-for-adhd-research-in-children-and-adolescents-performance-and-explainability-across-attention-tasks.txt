Abstract 

Attention Deficit Hyperactivity Disorder varies in presentation. Diagnosis requires clinical and developmental assessment. Reproducible electroencephalography measures may comple-ment clinical and behavioural characterisation, but differing feature definitions and evaluation methods hinder comparison. We compared seven feature families and four combinations across attention tasks and assessed the stability of held-out explanations. Two serious games yielded four overlapping attention-task cohorts of 123-140 participants each. Repeated nested participant-held-out evaluation assessed recruitment-group discrimination. Exploratory paired participant-cluster bootstrap tests corrected for multiplicity in two separate families: 72 individual-task contrasts and 18 fusion contrasts. For eight configurations selected after inspecting predictive results, we explained held-out native probabilities before calibration. In the strongest task context, the highest configuration mean AUROC over three repetitions was 0.773, with a fixed-prediction 95% interval of 0.692-0.854 unadjusted for selecting the maximum. Precision, sensitivity and specificity were 0.740, 0.556 and 0.861. In that task, the paired AUROC difference for connectivity minus spectral features averaged +0.2083 across six classifiers and the common 8 s and 16 s durations, with pointwise 95% CI 0.1076-0.3072 and adjusted p = 0 .0036. F4-AF4 beta-band coherence led repetition-level attribution summaries in five selected procedures and was selected in every fit. Other contexts showed weaker discrimination and less consistent retention of leading scientific inputs. No general classifier winner emerged. Connectivity and its recurring coherence input warrant independent replica-tion, but do not justify a universal feature recommendation. These exploratory findings do not establish causal task effects, disease mechanisms or diagnostic validity. 

Keywords: EEG, ADHD, Feature selection, Benchmarking, Explainability, Connectivity 

# 1 Introduction 

Attention Deficit Hyperactivity Disorder (ADHD) is a heterogeneous neurodevelopmental con-dition [1]. Diagnosis relies on clinical and developmental assessment, including symptoms and functioning across everyday settings [2]. This heterogeneity motivates research into reproducible neurophysiological measurements that might complement clinical and behavioural characterisa-tion [3]. Electroencephalography (EEG) records neural activity non-invasively with high temporal resolution [4], but the information extracted depends on how the signal is represented. Comparing representations under common analytical conditions can therefore help identify measurements worth further investigation. Clinical application nevertheless requires independent replication and validation of individual-level performance. These candidate measurements do not replace clinical assessment [1]. Feature extraction turns these recordings into quantities that can be compared across participants, while preserving different aspects of the signal. Temporal/statistical features summarise amplitude and variability, whereas conventional spectral features describe band power and ratios [5]. Aperiodic spectral features distinguish broadband background structure from oscillatory peaks [6], and complexity/fractal measures characterise irregularity and scaling [5]. To describe interactions, functional connectivity quantifies statistical coupling and graph/network 1arXiv:2611.28299v1 [cs.LG] 20 Nov 2026 features summarise the organisation of those connections [7]. Time-frequency decompositions describe structure across temporal and frequency scales [4]. These seven families offer different measurement perspectives, but their definitions alone do not establish independent predictive information. Recent work combines multi-domain measurements with explainable, stability-aware selection [8]. However, favourable scores across studies remain difficult to interpret because cohorts, acquisition, preprocessing, feature definitions, durations, models and evaluation designs also differ [9]. A score reflects that whole analytical setting, rather than the feature family alone. What remains unclear is which feature families and combinations show favourable discrimination under a common participant-level evaluation protocol, and how consistently these patterns recur across task contexts. Those comparisons are informative only if evaluation limits optimistic bias. When correlated epochs from one participant occur in both training and test sets, apparent performance on unseen participants can instead reflect participant-specific information [10]. Keeping participants separate addresses this leakage, but feature selection and tuning must also be isolated from final evaluation through nested validation [11]. The safeguards serve different purposes: grouping separates people, while nesting separates model choice from performance estimation. Neither turns additional epochs into additional independent participants. Small samples can consequently leave substantial uncertainty [12], and internal validation does not establish performance in an external population. To address this gap, we draw on public BALLADEER EEG recordings collected during two serious games, Attention Robots and Attention Slackline [13, 14]. Participants selected robots that matched target features in Robots and pressed a button in response to on-screen flag patterns in Slackline [15, 16]. We treat four recording contexts as separate tasks in this benchmark, one for Robots and one for each of the three recorded Slackline levels (L1, L6 and L11). Building on the existing EEG preparation and feature tables, we compare representations, supported durations and classifiers through participant-grouped nested evaluation within each task. Exploratory paired comparisons complement descriptive performance patterns. Held-out explanations of models selected after predictive inspection then examine which individual measurements contribute to their predictions. The outcome is recruitment group, with participant-level AUROC as the primary criterion. Because cohorts, devices and partitions differ, comparisons between tasks remain descriptive. Four questions organise the existing exploratory study, rather than a preregistered protocol: RQ1. How do feature families and combinations compare in participant-level discrimination within each task? RQ2. How do observed performance and leading representation patterns vary across task contexts? RQ3. How do supported epoch durations and classifier choices relate to discrimination within each task? RQ4. Which inputs recur in the selected held-out explanations, and how consistent are their selection and attribution patterns? The study makes three contributions. First, it provides comparative evidence for seven families and four combinations across four attention-task contexts, supported durations and six classifiers under a common within-task participant-held-out protocol. Second, it identifies representation and duration choices for replication while showing why stronger Robots findings and weaker Slackline evidence do not support a universal task or classifier ranking. Third, it links broad representation findings to candidate individual measurements through held-out attribution and repeated selection in selected fitted models. Reporting both contribution and retention consistency helps distinguish recurring inputs from those prominent only in particular fits. 22 Related Work 

EEG feature studies in ADHD have extended conventional spectral measurements with broader engineered representations [5, 9]. In the study reported in [17], the authors compared conventional descriptors with a combined set that added amplitude-modulation descriptors, both extracted from wavelet-decomposed frequency bands. They reported improved classification across the tested classifiers and wavelet choices when modulation information was added. This result supports examining complementary descriptors within that pipeline, rather than ranking feature families across studies. The study in [7] examined a different representation: source-localised connectivity characterised through network modularity and communication measures. These source-space networks differ from scalp-channel coherence. The studies therefore address complementary questions about what information a representation captures and whether it improves prediction. Broader representations also raise the question of which inputs to retain. The MRS-SHAP framework presented in [8] combines attribution-based importance, temporal stability and performance-aware weighting to select feature subsets. Selection of individual inputs and comparison of feature families can coexist, but they answer different questions. This benchmark uses supervised selection within training folds for each specified feature view. Its held-out explanations then examine selected configurations after predictive inspection. Attribution describes contributions to those fitted predictions, alongside input retention across fits, rather than choosing the benchmark’s feature subsets. Interpreting such comparisons requires attention to both evaluation and temporal context. The analysis in [10] demonstrated segment-partitioning leakage in Alzheimer’s disease and epilepsy EEG. A separate study [18] examined partitioning strategies for cross-participant prediction. These findings support keeping participants separate when comparing representations. In [19], the authors compared preprocessing and chronological recording segments, reporting stronger classification in later portions. Recording position concerns when information is acquired, whereas epoch duration determines the window used to calculate features. A benefit in later recording portions therefore does not establish which feature window is preferable. Together, these precedents motivate comparisons that specify the representation, input selection, temporal unit and evaluation conditions. The present benchmark compares families and combinations across supported durations and classifiers under a common within-task protocol. It adds descriptive evidence on recurring patterns across task contexts and uses selected held-out explanations to identify measurements for replication. Differing cohorts, devices and partitions prevent those patterns from isolating causal task effects or establishing a universal feature winner.

We used our curated EEG and feature dataset [20], derived from public BALLADEER recordings [13, 14]. It contains preprocessed EEG, extracted features and documentation of data quality and processing. Across the full dataset, outcome-blind assessment retained 669 processed task recordings from 156 of 158 public participants aged 6-18 years across five source conditions. The benchmark evaluated Robots and each of the three Slackline levels as separate task contexts. Processed task recordings and eligible participants are distinguished in Table 1. Configurations combined cohort, view, duration and classifier: 36 supported view-duration combinations yielded 216 configurations per cohort and 1,080 overall. Outcome-stratified par-ticipant partitions comprised three repetitions of five outer folds, each with four inner folds for candidate selection. The 16,200 outer evaluations were not independent participant samples. Participant epochs stayed together, with shared within-cohort partitions and separate participant partitions for fusion. Nesting separated selection from evaluation [11]. Within inner-training partitions, preparation, selection and transformation preceded classifier fitting. Predefined classifier settings were evaluated with either no transformation or a Yeo-Johnson transformation. Epoch-level classifier training used inverse participant epoch-count weights before class weighting. Candidates required finite predictions and no convergence warnings in all four inner folds. Mean inner-fold participant AUROC, based on participant-mean raw scores, determined ranking, with ties resolved by average precision, model complexity and deterministic ordering. LSVM and RBF-SVM supplied decision scores, other models native positive-class probabilities. For each task, an unweighted logistic sigmoid with an intercept and inverse regularisation strength C = 10 6 was fitted to the selected candidate’s pooled inner-validation participant-mean scores. Preparation, transformation and classifier were refitted on outer-training data. The fitted calibration function transformed held-out epoch scores before participant averaging. Calibration thus fitted participant-mean scores but was applied to epoch scores. Fusion used separate task models sharing a candidate specification. Tuning pooled participant-task scores within inner folds before calibration and fusion. Late fusion equally averaged the three participant-task probabilities. Training-prevalence and stratified-dummy controls remained separate from the evaluated feature-model configurations. 

3.4 Metrics and exploratory statistical comparisons 

Participant-level AUROC was primary. Metrics were recalculated after pooling the five disjoint outer test folds within each repetition, and configuration summaries averaged three repetition metrics. Descriptive medians summarised configuration means across specified sets of evaluated configurations. Accuracy, balanced accuracy, precision, recall, specificity and F1 used a 0.5 probability threshold, with ADHD recruitment positive. F1 was the harmonic mean of participant-level precision and recall within repetitions, then averaged. With no positive predictions, precision and F1 were stored as zero and precision flagged undefined. Brier score measured unthresholded probability error, not complete calibration. For each task/classifier, the main-text tables selected the highest unrounded mean AUROC across 11 views at 8s after inspection. All metrics came from that row. Different views preclude a controlled classifier-only comparison. Appendix matrices retain every individual-task configuration at 8s. Per-configuration 95% intervals used 1,000 outcome-stratified participant-cluster bootstrap draws, retaining all three predictions per sampled participant. Paired comparisons used 20,000 6such draws with shared participant multiplicities across configurations. Factor-level means equally averaged configuration AUROCs by view, duration or classifier, distinct from descriptive medians. View/classifier contrasts used common 8s/16s support and duration contrasts seven views available at all four durations. References were SPEC, LSVM and 8s, respectively. Approximate two-sided null-centred paired bootstrap tests assessed zero factor-level AUROC differences. Holm adjustment covered 72 individual-task contrasts jointly, 18 per task, separately from 18 within-fusion contrasts. Adjusted values, padj , used a 0.05 threshold. The 95% intervals were pointwise, not multiplicity-adjusted. Epochs, folds, repetitions and configurations were not independent participants. References and testing families were selected after reviewing results. Fixed-prediction inference excluded uncertainty from refitting, selection and external-population generalisation. 

3.5 Held-out attribution and selection stability 

Feature-view performance does not identify individual contributors. We reconstructed eight 8s configurations selected after predictive inspection, covering 120 outer evaluations, without a new search, and verified that held-out predictions matched the original benchmark predictions. ALL/XGB supplied a common model, view and duration across four tasks without controlling cohort, device or partition differences. In Robots, CONN/XGB examined the observed maximum and NET/XGB a related representation. CONN/RF and CONN/HGB examined recurrence across procedures, not necessarily the next-best classifiers. All explained fits retained 64 scientific inputs. Tree ensembles permitted a common interventional TreeSHAP (Shapley Additive Explanations for trees) approach, without implying that omitted classes were inferior. TreeSHAP attributes fitted predictions to inputs relative to a background [22], here on the model’s positive-class probability before external sigmoid calibration. The background sampled one observed epoch per outer-training participant. Every held-out epoch was explained. Signed contributions were averaged within participant before conversion to magnitudes, weighting participants equally within repetition and repetitions equally. Leading scientific candidates used unconditional mean absolute participant contributions within each procedure, including zeros for available-unselected inputs and excluding unavailable inputs. A scientific input was considered available when present in the original feature view, regardless of whether it survived subsequent training-fold filtering. Conditional-selected means used participants from fits retaining the input and equally weighted available repetitions. Unlike attribution to fitted predictions, selection frequency records retention across fits. Frequency and repetition-level attribution ranks described stability, not additional predictive tests. Technical indicators were distinct from physiological measurements and available only where retained, but included in attribution ranks and top-ten counts.

The leading scientific Slackline inputs were less consistently retained and had variable repetition rankings. Petrosian fractal dimension at P3 was selected in 8/15 available fits for L1, and eigenvector centrality at Cz in the positive broadband Pearson-correlation graph in 12/15 for L6. Petrosian fractal dimension at Fp1 was selected in 10/15 available fits for L11. All 120 evaluations passed participant-prediction reproduction and native-probability additivity checks, establishing computational consistency. 

# 5 Discussion 

The within-task comparisons identify choices worth replicating. The weaker Slackline results limit the case for a single recommendation across task contexts. 

5.1 Representation choices across task contexts 

For RQ1, the clearest within-task differences were in Robots, where connectivity-containing views showed stronger discrimination than the conventional spectral view. Paired contrasts extend the descriptive pattern beyond one favourable configuration, but do not rank CONN, NET and ALL against each other, isolate family effects or establish benefit from adding graph features. Across task contexts (RQ2), discrimination was strongest in Robots. The leading view varied across the three Slackline levels, and the median AUROC across configurations was near chance at each level. Cohorts, hardware and partitions differed across tasks, so these descriptive comparisons cannot isolate an effect of task design. 

5.2 Epoch duration and classifier choices 

For RQ3, the matched-view comparison in Robots favoured 8s over 16s epochs on average, without establishing a universally optimal duration. The 8s Robots connectivity configuration with XGB had the highest observed mean AUROC, but the paired comparisons against LSVM did not establish a general classifier winner. Replication should therefore vary feature view and epoch duration explicitly to see whether the Robots pattern recurs. 

5.3 Attribution and selection stability 

In the exploratory held-out explanations (RQ4), F4-AF4 beta-band coherence recurred across the five selected Robots procedures. This narrows the replication priority to an individual measurement. Repeated selection and high attribution rank provide complementary information: an input can have a large attribution when selected but appear in few fits. Reporting availability, unconditional importance and selection frequency makes this distinction visible, particularly for the less consistent Slackline inputs. Attribution describes fitted-model use, not the direction of an ADHD-comparison physiological difference or an independent predictive effect. Correlated inputs can exchange attribution, and the background defines the comparison. Reproduction and additivity checks establish computational consistency, not biological validity. Other interpretation studies address different questions. Concept sensitivity in EEG abnormal-ity detection [23] differs from contributions of engineered inputs to recruitment-group predictions. Shapley-guided reduced-electrode motor-imagery evaluation [24] tests a downstream benefit not examined here. Our summaries identify measurements for replication, without demonstrating an electrode-reduction benefit. 12 This addresses the feature-comparison gap [5], but remains short of biomarker validation [1]. Independent studies should evaluate connectivity-containing Robots views and recurring coherence while checking whether weaker Slackline patterns persist. Predictive performance and attribution stability require separate assessment because agreement among selected-model explanations is not independent predictive confirmation. 

5.4 Limitations 

One source population supplied overlapping task cohorts. Recruitment group differs from assessment-derived status, precluding diagnostic-validity claims. Devices and separate fusion partitions prevent causal task comparisons and controlled fusion-benefit claims. Estimates are internally held out, without tested cross-task transfer or external validation. The maximum was selected from a large grid. Exploratory paired intervals condition on fixed predictions, excluding full refitting variability. Explanation configurations were selected after reviewing results. Native-probability attribution does not explain the externally calibrated score, and technical indicators may encode availability rather than physiology. Background sensitivity covered five Robots pilots, not every explained fit or Slackline. Their agreement cannot establish stability across all backgrounds or task contexts. 

# 6 Conclusions 

This benchmark provides comparative evidence for choosing EEG representations in paediatric ADHD research. Connectivity-containing Robots views merit independent replication relative to the spectral reference. Their recurring F4-AF4 beta-band coherence input supplies a more specific candidate measurement. Weaker Slackline discrimination and less consistent selection limit generalisation across task contexts. The duration contrast supports evaluating epoch length explicitly, while the classifier comparisons establish no general winner. Future work should test whether these predictive and explanatory patterns recur in independent cohorts under controlled acquisition and evaluation. The present findings guide research priorities without establishing disease mechanisms or clinical diagnostic validity.
