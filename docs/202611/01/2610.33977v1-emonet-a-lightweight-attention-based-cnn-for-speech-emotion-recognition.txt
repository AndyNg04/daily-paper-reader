Abstract —Speech Emotion Recognition (SER) is a fundamental task in affective computing that enables applications such as mental health assessment, human–computer interaction, and automated customer support systems. Accurate emotion classi-fication from speech remains challenging due to variations in speaker style, acoustic conditions, and the high computational cost of modern deep learning approaches. Many existing systems depend on large transformer-based architectures that require substantial computational resources, making them less suitable for real-time or resource-constrained environments. This research proposes EmoNet, a lightweight deep learning framework for speech emotion classification across seven emotion categories. The model is built on a four-block depthwise separable CNN encoder with squeeze-and-excitation attention and residual connections, combined with a Bidirectional LSTM for temporal feature learn-ing and a multi-head attention pooling mechanism for improved representation. A comprehensive 134-channel acoustic feature set alongside data augmentation techniques including audio-level perturbations and SpecAugment is employed to improve robustness under diverse acoustic conditions. Evaluated on a combined dataset of TESS, RAVDESS, and SAVEE, EmoNet achieves a test accuracy of 96.75% with only 4.11 million parameters. The proposed model outperforms its reproduced baseline and remains competitive with significantly larger state-of-the-art architectures. 

Index Terms —EmoNet, Speech Emotion Recognition, Depth-wise Separable Convolutions, Squeeze-and-Excitation, Bidirec-tional LSTM, Multi-Head Attention, Lightweight CNN, Multi-class Classification 

I. INTRODUCTION Speech Emotion Recognition (SER) is the task of au-tomatically identifying the emotional state expressed in a spoken utterance. Human speech contains rich paralinguistic information embedded in acoustic cues such as pitch, energy, tempo, and spectral characteristics. Emotional states influence nearly all aspects of the speech signal, making it challenging to learn representations that are both discriminative across emotion classes and robust to variations in speakers, recording conditions, and background noise [1]. This balance between discriminability and robustness makes SER a complex problem in speech processing. The importance of SER has increased considerably due to its wide range of real-world applications. In mental health monitoring, SER systems can assist in detecting early signs of stress, depression, or anxiety from conversational speech [1]. In call-centre analytics, emotion-aware systems can improve customer experience through intelligent call routing and es-calation handling. In automotive environments, SER can help detect driver fatigue, frustration, or panic, supporting safety-critical decisions. In social robotics and human–computer interaction, emotion recognition enables more adaptive and natural interactions. These applications have driven extensive research into deep learning-based SER systems. Despite significant progress, deploying SER models in real-world environments remains challenging. Most existing approaches rely on large pre-trained transformer-based archi-tectures such as Wav2Vec 2.0 [2], which contain hundreds of millions of parameters and require substantial computational resources, making them impractical for real-time or edge deployment. Another major challenge is limited generalization across datasets, as SER corpora are typically small and col-lected under different recording conditions. Models trained on a single dataset often fail to generalize to unseen domains, and combining multiple datasets introduces issues such as inconsistent labeling schemes, class imbalance, and domain shift. There is a strong need for lightweight yet accurate deep learning models that can classify speech emotion across multiple categories efficiently and in a way that can be applied in real-world systems. In this paper, we propose EmoNet, a lightweight deep learn-ing framework for speech emotion recognition that integrates a four-block depthwise separable CNN encoder with squeeze-and-excitation attention, a Bidirectional LSTM, and multi-head attention pooling to effectively capture both spatial and temporal dependencies in speech signals. In contrast to typical large transformer frameworks, EmoNet reduces computational arXiv:2610.33977v1 [cs.SD] 25 Oct 2026 complexity while maintaining competitive accuracy, enabling deployment in real-time and resource-constrained environ-ments. EmoNet is trained and evaluated on a combined corpus of TESS, RAVDESS, and SAVEE covering seven emotion classes. The main contributions of this paper are summarized as follows:

LITERATURE REVIEW The potential of deep learning for automatic speech emotion recognition has attracted considerable research interest in recent years. A number of architectures and training strategies have been proposed to improve emotion classification perfor-mance, ranging from lightweight convolutional models to large pre-trained transformer systems. Here, we highlight significant recent research on the topic. Chakrabarty et al. [3] propose a hybrid Transformer-CNN framework that fuses a fine-tuned Wav2Vec 2.0 backbone with a 1D-CNN branch that contributes spectral stability through noise-resistant features including MFCCs, ZCR, and RMSE. An Attentive Temporal Pooling mechanism combines both streams, and the model is further equipped with an Explainable AI suite using SHAP and Score-CAM. The framework is validated across RAVDESS, TESS, SAVEE, and CREMA-D, achieving a near-perfect accuracy of 99.78% on TESS. However, the Wav2Vec 2.0 backbone alone contributes ap-proximately 95 million parameters [2], making the full model computationally prohibitive for resource-constrained or real-time deployment. Furthermore, the system evaluates individual corpora in isolation rather than a unified multi-corpus seven-class setting, limiting its applicability to cross-corpus general-ization scenarios. Wang et al. [4] combine a fine-tuned Wav2Vec 2.0 encoder with a Neural Controlled Differential Equations (NCDE) clas-sifier, evaluated on the four-class IEMOCAP dataset, attaining a weighted accuracy of 73.37% and an unweighted accuracy of 74.18% while converging within a single training epoch. Despite this convergence efficiency, the large parameter burden of Wav2Vec 2.0 and the constrained four-class evaluation limit direct comparability to broader seven-class benchmarks. Kilimci et al. [5] evaluate raw waveform-based deep learning frameworks for SER across six datasets, EMO-DB, RAVDESS, TESS, CREMA-D, SAVEE, and a combined TESS+RAVDESS set, without any handcrafted feature extrac-tion stage. Multiple architectures are benchmarked, including SVMs, CNNs, LSTMs, and hybrid CNN-LSTM models. Their best result of 95.86% is achieved by a CNN model on the combined TESS+RAVDESS set. While the raw waveform strategy achieves strong per-corpus results, the paper does not report a unified parameter count for the proposed models, making efficiency comparisons difficult. Importantly, datasets are evaluated separately rather than in a unified multi-corpus multi-class framework. Li [6] explores a CNN-Transformer hybrid framework for SER on a combined RAVDESS+SAVEE+TESS corpus, sys-tematically investigating the impact of different loss functions on model accuracy and generalization. The system leverages Mel-Spectrogram features combined with multi-head self-attention to capture long-range temporal dependencies. While the combined dataset mirrors our experimental setup, the architecture does not employ lightweight depthwise separable convolutions, resulting in a heavier model. No explicit param-eter count is provided, and the study focuses primarily on the loss function comparison rather than architectural efficiency. Bhanbhro et al. [7] present a comparative analysis of CNN-LSTM and attention-enhanced CNN-LSTM models for SER, evaluated primarily on the RAVDESS corpus. Their results consistently demonstrate that incorporating attention mecha-nisms into the CNN-LSTM pipeline improves classification accuracy and temporal modeling quality compared to vanilla CNN-LSTM baselines, validating the importance of attention for SER. However, the evaluation is limited to fewer than seven emotion classes and does not consider a multi-corpus composite training regime, which restricts the generalizability of conclusions that can be drawn from the study. Barhoumi and BenAyed [1] propose a transformer en-coder combined with a comprehensive data augmentation pipeline, including noise injection, spectrogram shifting, and speed perturbation, to improve SER robustness. Both a Deep Neural Network and a Transformer Encoder are evaluated on RAVDESS, EMO-DB, and TESS, with the Transformer achieving 99.30% on EMO-DB, 92.00% on RAVDESS, and 100% on TESS. These are strong individual-corpus results; however, the system is not evaluated in a composite multi-corpus regime and the transformer encoder architecture is not optimized for parameter efficiency, contrasting with the lightweight focus of our proposed system. III. PROPOSED METHODOLOGY As demonstrated in Section II, numerous studies have put forth different architectures and training strategies for SER. While some approaches achieved greater accuracy at the expense of intricate and computationally demanding models, particularly those relying on large pre-trained transformers, others reported less than ideal outcomes when confronted with multi-corpus or multi-class settings. Consequently, our goal in this paper is to enhance classification performance while reducing the amount of memory and processing power needed for computation. 

A. Datasets Employed 

EmoNet is trained and evaluated on a composite corpus assembled from three publicly available SER datasets. Ta-ble I summarizes their properties. All audio is resampled to 16,000 Hz, padded or truncated to exactly 4 seconds (64,000 samples), and split in a stratified manner by class: 70% training (3,304 samples), 15% validation (708 samples), and 15% testing (708 samples), using a fixed random seed of 42. TESS (Toronto Emotional Speech Set) [8] contains 2,800 audio samples recorded by two female speakers from the Uni-versity of Toronto, covering seven emotions: angry, disgust, fear, happy, neutral, surprise, and sad, with perfectly balanced classes of 400 samples each. The studio-controlled recording conditions make TESS a high-quality anchor corpus. 

RAVDESS (Ryerson Audio-Visual Database of Emotional Speech and Song) [9] contributes 1,440 speech-only samples from 24 professional actors spanning both genders, covering all seven target emotions after mapping to the canonical taxonomy. RAVDESS adds substantial speaker diversity and varied vocal expression styles. 

SAVEE (Surrey Audio-Visual Expressed Emotion) [10] provides 480 samples from four male native British English speakers recorded at the University of Surrey. While the smallest corpus, SAVEE adds important acoustic diversity through a distinct accent and an exclusively male speaker pool, contrasting with the North American English of TESS and RAVDESS. 

B. Data Preprocessing & Augmentation 

A two-stage augmentation pipeline is applied during train-ing to improve generalization and robustness. At the audio level, each training sample is randomly subjected to one or two of: additive white Gaussian noise (SNR 10–25 dB), time stretching (rate 0.85–1.15), pitch shifting ( ±2 semitones), and random gain scaling ( −6 to +6 dB). At the feature level, SpecAugment [11] masks up to 25 time frames and 20 frequency bins per sample across two masks, forcing the model to learn robust representations from partial observations. D. Proposed Model Architecture 

The model follows a four-stage pipeline, as depicted in Figure 2: 

DS-CNN Encoder → BiLSTM → MHA Pooling → Classifier 

The total trainable parameter count is approximately 4.11 million, confirming that the system remains lightweight while capturing both spectral and temporal structure in speech. The hyperparameters used in training are shown in Table III. 

1) DS-CNN Encoder: The encoder comprises four sequen-tial blocks. Block 1 applies a standard convolution (134 →64 channels, kernel size 5, padding 2), Batch Normalization, ReLU, and MaxPooling (stride 2). Blocks 2 and 3 each apply a Depthwise Separable Convolution with an integrated Squeeze-and-Excitation (SE) module, expanding 64 →128 and 128 →256 channels respectively, followed by MaxPooling and Dropout (0.2). Block 4 is an additional DS-Conv block (256 →256, Dropout 0.2) without pooling, preserving the tem-poral resolution of the feature sequence for the subsequent BiLSTM. Each DS-Conv block factorizes a standard convolution into a depthwise spatial convolution followed by a pointwise 1×1

projection, substantially reducing the parameter count relative to full convolutions. The SE module uses global average pooling followed by two linear layers with a sigmoid gate to compute channel-wise attention weights, re-emphasizing informative spectral bands and suppressing less relevant ones:

RESULTS After training the model on the designated training set, its performance was assessed on a held-out test set consisting of 708 samples. Evaluation was conducted using several key metrics to measure the model’s effectiveness. To apply these metrics accurately, it is crucial to define the following terms: 1) True Positive (TP): A positive instance correctly pre-dicted as positive. 2) True Negative (TN): A negative instance correctly pre-dicted as negative. 3) False Positive (FP): A negative instance incorrectly predicted as positive. 4) False Negative (FN): A positive instance incorrectly predicted as negative. 5) Accuracy: The proportion of instances correctly pre-dicted by the model, reflecting its overall performance [14]. Accuracy = T P + T N T P + T N + F P + F N (4) 6) Confusion Matrix: A table that compares predicted la-bels with actual labels, showing the counts of true posi-tives, true negatives, false positives, and false negatives [15]. 7) Precision: The proportion of correctly predicted positive instances among all predicted positives. Precision = T P T P + F P (5) 8) Recall: The proportion of actual positive instances cor-rectly identified by the model [14]. Recall = T P T P + F N (6) 9) F1 Score: The harmonic mean of precision and recall, providing a balanced assessment of model performance by accounting for both false positives and false nega-tives. F1 Score = 2 × Precision × Recall Precision + Recall (7) These metrics collectively provide a nuanced understanding of the model’s performance, highlighting its strengths and areas for improvement. The final EmoNet model achieved a test accuracy of 96.75% with only 4.11 million parameters, outperforming the reproduced base-line while using approximately 34 × fewer parameters. V. CONCLUSION In this study, we introduced EmoNet, a lightweight speech emotion recognition framework built on depthwise separable convolutional layers augmented with squeeze-and-excitation channel attention, a two-layer Bidirectional LSTM, and 8-head multi-head attention pooling. The EmoNet architecture was designed to improve upon large transformer-based base-lines by drastically lowering the total parameter count while maintaining strong predictive capacity. Trained on a 4,720-sample composite of TESS, RAVDESS, and SAVEE using a two-phase training strategy incorporating MixUp, SpecAug-ment, and Cosine Annealing with Warm Restarts, EmoNet achieves a 96.75% test accuracy across seven emotion classes with only 4.11 million parameters, rivaling state-of-the-art models with significantly more parameters, as summarized in Table VIII and reported in Section II. Comparisons with recent approaches show that EmoNet strikes an effective balance between accuracy and computational efficiency, making it well-suited for real-time and resource-constrained deployment.
