## 摘要
人群模拟旨在生成丰富、自然且适应周围环境的智能体行为。机器学习领域的最新进展使研究者能够直接从轨迹数据中学习此类行为模式，从而让模拟模型捕捉日益复杂的人群动态。然而，这些系统的能力仍高度依赖训练数据的类型和多样性。在评估人群模拟器对真实世界数据集的泛化能力时，现有的大多数指标主要关注轨迹层面的差异，而非行为层面的相似性。本文提出 Trajeform 框架，该框架使用真实轨迹数据训练重构变分自编码器（Variational Autoencoder，VAE），以学习有意义的连续嵌入，并保留真实人群的行为特征。随后，可将训练好的编码器应用于模拟器生成的合成轨迹，并比较由此得到的嵌入分布。此外，所学习的嵌入还可用于分析补充数据，例如与模拟器主要行为对应的轨迹，或在受控环境（如骑行）中采集的数据，从而深入了解这些数据能否覆盖真实行为嵌入空间。该框架将三个模拟器（CCP、modCCP 和 RVO）的数据与真实标注数据集进行比较，并通过潜空间分析、分布度量以及从聚合后验分布中采样生成轨迹，对其进行定量和定性评估。结果表明，所学习的潜空间能够捕捉真实数据中有意义的轨迹依赖关系，因此，该框架既可作为评估合成数据可信度的补充指标，也可用于指导未来轨迹模型的训练。

## Abstract
Crowd simulation aims to generate agent behaviours that are rich, natural, and appropriate to their surrounding environment. Recent advances in Machine Learning have enabled such behavioural patterns to be learned directly from trajectory data, allowing simulation models to capture increasingly complex crowd dynamics. However, the capabilities of these systems remain strongly dependent on the type and diversity of the data used during training. When evaluating how well crowd simulators generalise to real-world datasets, most existing metrics focus primarily on trajectory-level differences rather than behaviour-level similarity. In this paper, we present Trajeform, a framework that trains a reconstruction VAE (Variational Autoencoder) on real trajectory data to learn a meaningful continuous embedding that preserves behavioural characteristics of real crowds. The trained encoder can then be applied to synthetic trajectories generated by simulators and used to compare the resulting embedding distributions. Additionally, the learned embedding can be used to analyse supplementary data, such as trajectories corresponding to a simulator's principal behaviours or captured data from controlled environments (e.g., cycling), providing insight into their ability to span the real behavioural embedding space. The framework compares data from three simulators (CCP, modCCP, and RVO) against ground-truth datasets and is evaluated quantitatively and qualitatively using latent-space analysis, distributional metrics, and trajectory generation through sampling from the aggregated posterior distribution. The results indicate that the learned latent space captures meaningful trajectory dependencies found in the real data, making the framework useful both as an additional measure of synthetic data believability and as a tool for informing the training of future trajectory models.
