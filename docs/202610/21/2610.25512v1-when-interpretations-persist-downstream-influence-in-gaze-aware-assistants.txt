> AI assistants may carry forward not only observations but also interpretations of what a person is doing or may do next across time. Once retained, these interpretations become context for later predictions and system-triggered outputs. We study whether this representation step changes later behavior in gaze-aware streaming assistance. Holding retrieved events, their order, and the target input fixed, we find that Free-form Interpretation amplifies historical influence in both directions: correct histories produce more helpful changes and wrong histories more harmful changes. Unsupported and broader interpretations about activity or future behavior are associated with greater harmful influence. Evidence-Bounded Interpretation preserves distinctions between observation, inference, uncertainty, and temporal scope, reducing unsupported and overcertain content and lowering wrong-source harmful influence while retaining most helpful influence. The same effect structure appears with a second model. These findings identify persistent interpretation as a consequential design choice for history-aware human-AI systems. 1 Introduction 

AI assistants that operate across an unfolding activity may need to use earlier information when deciding what to do next. A gaze-aware assistant, for example, may use earlier observations to recall scene history, predict a future action, or decide when to issue an alert. In such systems, what carries forward may be more than what was observed. The assistant may also retain its own interpretations of what a person was doing or might do next. Observing that a person looked at a tomato is different from retaining the interpretation that they were preparing to cut it. The latter turns a momentary inference about the person into context for later assistance. We call a model-generated description or inference derived from behavioral evidence a machine interpretation ,and a persistent interpretation when it is retained and later reused as context. Once persistent, an interpretation acquires a second function: it is no longer only an output about an earlier event, but also part of the information that shapes later system behavior. This makes persistent interpretation a design choice for history-aware AI systems. When the underlying behavioral evidence stays the same, does the way that evidence is turned into persistent interpretation change what the system later does? Prior work shows that model-generated history and retrieved experience can improve later performance, but can also propagate errors when retained information is incorrect or misaligned [33, 36]. Other work has identified failures in memory construction and updating, as well as representations that obscure the source or role of stored information [1, 5, 10, 37]. These findings establish that history matters, but leave a different step unresolved: whether transforming the same behavioral evidence into a fluent machine interpretation changes how strongly that history shapes subsequent decisions. Gaze-aware assistance makes this representational question concrete. Video and gaze provide relatively direct evidence about visible objects and visual attention, while interpretations of ongoing activity or possible future behavior require additional inference [2, 7–9, 21, 23]. The setting therefore lets us study what happens when statements that range from relatively observable evidence to broader interpretations about a person become persistent context for later assistance. Using STREAMGAZE [8], we hold the retrieved earlier events, their order, and the later target input fixed while varying the persistent representation constructed from those events. We find that Free-form Interpretation acts as an influence amplifier. Relative to Answer Only , correct source histories produce more helpful downstream changes, while wrong source histories produce more harmful changes. The same effect structure appears with GLM-4.6V-Flash [30]. The two-sided effect makes the form of persistent interpretation a design question: richer interpretation can make useful history more useful while also making wrong history more consequential. We therefore ask what form an interpretation should take when it persists. We evaluate Evidence-Bounded Interpretation (EB), a representation that keeps observation, inference, uncertainty, and temporal scope distinguishable. EB reduces unsupported and overcertain content and substantially lowers wrong-source harmful influence while retaining most of the helpful influence of Free-form Interpretation. Counterfactual correction further shows that the semantic content carried forward contributes to later failures. These results identify epistemic representation as part of the design of persistent machine interpretation. Our contributions are: 

• We identify persistent interpretation as a consequential representation step in the reuse of behavioral information. Transforming the same earlier evidence into Free-form Interpretation increases downstream influence in both directions, producing more helpful changes from correct histories and more harmful changes from wrong histories. This amplification pattern is reproduced with a second model. 

• We characterize what makes persistent interpretation more consequential. Even within wrong-source histories, unsupported interpretations are associated with substantially more harmful downstream changes, while a separate human audit shows higher harmful-flip rates as interpretations extend from relatively observable information toward activity and possible future behavior. 

• We derive and test a concrete design response for persistent machine interpretation. Evidence-Bounded Inter-pretation keeps observation, inference, uncertainty, and temporal scope distinguishable, reduces unsupported and overcertain content, and substantially lowers wrong-source harmful influence while retaining most correct-source helpful influence. Counterfactual correction further shows that the semantic content carried forward contributes to later failures. 

2 Related Work 2.1 Gaze-Aware and Streaming Video Understanding 

Eye gaze provides information about visual attention during ongoing behavior [2, 9]. Recent first-person and multimodal work uses gaze for collaborative video question answering [4], gaze anticipation [7], mistake detection [15], and gaze-conditioned visual-language reasoning [21, 23]. STREAMGAZE combines gaze with a causal streaming protocol spanning object, temporal, future-action, and proactive tasks [8]. Related benchmarks such as StreamingBench, OVO-Bench, and OVBench study online video understanding under temporally arriving evidence [3, 11, 17]. Together, these works show how gaze-conditioned reasoning is expanding from first-person scene understanding toward temporally evolving and online video settings. Our question concerns what happens after such evidence has already produced a model interpretation. In our study, we distinguish statements closely tied to visible objects or attended regions from statements that add activity or prospective inference, drawing on gaze and egocentric work that spans attention, action, and anticipation [2, 7, 9, 23]. This gives us a controlled setting for studying how increasingly interpretive statements behave after persistence. The works above primarily evaluate judgments produced from the current stream; we study what happens when a model-generated interpretation is retained and later reused as context for another decision. 4 Ding et al. 

2.2 Persistent Context, Memory Construction, and Experience Reuse 

Language agents increasingly externalize prior information into persistent state that can influence later reasoning. Generative Agents synthesizes higher-level reflections over accumulated observations [22], while Reflexion and ExpeL reuse textual experience from previous attempts [28, 41]. Long-term memory systems extend this idea through explicit storage and retrieval, including MemGPT, Agent Workflow Memory, A-MEM, ReasoningBank, and multimodal long-term memory [12, 18, 19, 32, 38]. These systems establish that model-generated information can persist beyond the interaction in which it was produced. Recent work also treats memory construction, retrieval, and management as distinct design problems. LongMemEval evaluates long-term interactive memory [34], SeCom studies memory construction and retrieval for conversational agents [20], and MemInsight, REMem, and Memory-R1 examine memory augmentation, episodic reasoning, and memory management [25, 29, 39]. A recent survey similarly describes a shift from simple storage toward systems that construct and use experience as reusable state [13]. This literature shows that memory construction and selection can affect later use. Persistent history can also introduce failure. StreamBench shows that self-generated experience can help or hurt subsequent performance depending on its correctness [33], while memory management affects experience-following behavior [36] and memory systems can contain hallucinated content [1]. Building on this literature, we isolate a different step before reuse: whether the same earlier behavioral evidence has different downstream consequences after it is transformed from a direct answer into a fluent machine interpretation. Holding retrieved source events, their order, and the later target input fixed lets us study this representation transformation separately from retrieval quality or history availability. 

2.3 Uncertainty, Provenance, and Memory Reliability 

How uncertainty is represented can affect how AI outputs are interpreted and relied upon. Work on communicating uncertainty examines how uncertainty expressions shape judgments [31], while studies of human-AI decision making investigate the relationship between uncertainty presentation, trust, reliance, and calibration [6, 14, 26, 27]. Linguistic calibration similarly addresses overconfident natural-language output [16]. These studies concern human interpretation of AI uncertainty, whereas our experiments measure downstream model behavior rather than user trust or reliance. We use this literature as design context for preserving information status, not as evidence of user-level effects in our setting. Related reliability problems arise when uncertain or model-generated information becomes persistent memory. Typed memory representations address failures to preserve the provenance and role of stored information [5], and memory provenance laundering examines amplification after the original status of persistent information becomes obscured [37]. HaluMem studies hallucinated content in agent memory [1], while Agentic Uncertainty Quantification considers uncertainty within agentic reasoning [40]. Belief Memory is closely related to our design motivation: it treats memory under partial observability and preserves uncertainty rather than collapsing ambiguous evidence into one deterministic belief [10]. Its representation uses multiple probabilistic candidate beliefs, whereas EB preserves evidence status within a structured textual interpretation. These works establish uncertainty preservation, provenance, and structured memory as important concerns in persistent AI context. 3 Study Setting 

We use gaze-aware streaming video as a controlled case in which an AI system can reuse earlier interpretations about a person when making later decisions. We define a machine interpretation as a model-generated description or inference derived from source-time behavioral evidence, and a persistent interpretation as such an interpretation when it is retained and later reused as context. As illustrated in Figure 2, we keep the retrieved source events, their order, and the target input fixed across conditions, while varying their persistent representation. Free-form Interpretation may add higher-level inference beyond the direct source answer, so the comparison evaluates the persistent representation as a whole. 

3.1 Persistence and Retrieval 

We use four STREAMGAZE tasks [8] as sources of earlier events: Object Identification Easy (OI-E), Object Identification Hard (OI-H), Object Attribute Recognition (OAR), and Non-Fixated Object Identification (NFI). The resulting source library contains 4,513 entries anchored to direct source predictions. We evaluate their downstream influence on six later target tasks: Object Transition Prediction (OTP), Scene Recall (SR), Gaze Sequence Matching (GSM), Future Action Prediction (FAP), Gaze-Triggered Alert (GTA), and Object Appearance Alert (OAA). For each target event, only source events from the same video that occur earlier in time are eligible for retrieval. Retrieval is performed once using fixed source-event and target-query representations defined independently of the generated persistent representations. Generated Free-form or EB text is never used for retrieval, and retrieval has no access to future frames, ground-truth labels, or downstream outcomes. Source and target retrieval texts are encoded with BAAI/bge-small-en-v1.5 [35], and eligible source events are ranked by cosine similarity. The top 𝑘 = 3 events are presented in chronological order and reused unchanged across all representation conditions. 

3.2 Representation Conditions 

We compare three representations of the same retrieved source events. Answer Only retains the model’s direct source answer. Free-form Interpretation expresses the source information as a fluent higher-level interpretation without explicitly separating observation, inference, or uncertainty. Evidence-Bounded Interpretation (EB) separates 6 Ding et al. retained content into Observed , for content the model treats as directly supported by the source-time evidence; Inferred ,for higher-level interpretation; Uncertain , for unresolved or weakly supported claims; and Temporal Scope , for when the statement applies. Free-form Interpretation and EB are generated by the same Qwen3.5-9B model [24] from identical source-time inputs: gaze/video evidence, the source question, and the model’s frozen direct source answer. Direct source answers are generated once before the representation comparison and are not regenerated across conditions. Source ground truth is not used for representation generation. No representation has access to future frames, target-time evidence, target labels, or downstream outcomes. The primary EB prompt was fixed before its reported evaluation outcomes were observed. At target time, all representation conditions receive the same target input and the same retrieved source events, so the persistent representation is the manipulated variable. We additionally evaluate a No History condition using the same target case without retrieved historical context.

4 Results 4.1 Free-form Interpretation Amplifies Downstream Influence 

We first examine whether changing the persistent representation constructed from the same retrieved source events changes their downstream influence. Figure 3 summarizes harmful flips in wrong-source (WS) histories and helpful flips in correct-source (CS) histories. Retrieval, source-event order, and target input are fixed across conditions. Free-form Interpretation increases downstream influence in both directions. In the WS population, harmful flips increase from 7.3% with Answer Only to 13.7% with Free-form Interpretation, a paired increase of 6.48 percentage points (95% CI [+4.17, +8.80]). In the CS population, helpful flips increase from 41.5% to 50.6%, a paired increase of 9.10 percentage points (95% CI [+6.72, +11.62]). Free-form Interpretation therefore does not behave as a uniformly beneficial or harmful form of persistent context. Instead, it increases how often historical information changes later decisions in both useful and harmful directions. Evidence-Bounded Interpretation changes this balance between harmful and helpful influence. Relative to Free-form Interpretation, EB reduces WS harmful flips from 13.7% to 6.0%, a decrease of 7.72 percentage points (95% CI [-10.03, -5.56]). CS helpful flips decrease more modestly, from 50.6% to 47.9%, a decrease of 2.66 percentage points (95% CI [-4.06, -1.26]). 4.2 When Are Persistent Interpretations Associated with More Harm? 

We examine two properties of Free-form interpretations associated with harmful changes: their support from the available earlier video and gaze evidence, and the inferential scope they add beyond relatively observable object and attention information. Figure 5 summarizes both analyses. 

Human annotation. We use two distinct annotation sets for complementary analyses. First, a detailed paired audit covers 500 unique cases from 146 unique videos, sampled without replacement from a frozen eligible pool using a fixed random seed and proportional stratification by target task. Sampling was defined independently of downstream harmful or helpful outcomes and did not condition on representation-quality or inferential-scope labels. For the inference-level analysis, annotators assign each Free-form persistent interpretation to one of four mutually exclusive levels, and we relate these labels to harmful flips under the Free-form condition. The same 500 cases are also used for a paired representation-quality comparison between Free-form Interpretation and EB, where unsupported content, contradictory content, and overcertainty are annotated as case-level binary properties for each representation. Two author annotators independently labeled all 500 cases in randomized order before discussion and without access to downstream harmful or helpful outcomes. Raw agreement was 85.0%, with Cohen’s 𝜅 = 0.70 computed from the pre-adjudication labels. Second, we use a separate binary evidence-support annotation covering the full frozen WS population ( 𝑛 = 648). Here, the annotation target is the Free-form persistent interpretation. When needed, the frozen direct source answer is also shown to clarify the generated statement. Annotators do not have access to source ground truth, the EB representation, target-time information, target predictions or labels, downstream harmful or helpful outcomes, or an explicit WS label. Evidence support within wrong-source histories. Source correctness and representation quality are related but distinct. Across the full library of 4,513 stored interpretations, 38.3% are anchored to an incorrect direct source answer, whereas 8.3% are flagged by the generation pipeline’s rule checks. We therefore examine evidence support within the fixed WS population rather than comparing support labels across a mixture of correct and incorrect source histories. Within the same wrong-source stratum, Free-form harmful flips occur in 22.41% of cases whose persistent inter-pretation is labeled Unsupported and 11.84% of cases labeled Supported. The difference is +10.57 percentage points (video-level clustered bootstrap 95% CI [+2.7, +19.3]), corresponding descriptively to a risk ratio of 1.89. The Wilson 95% confidence intervals for the two group rates are [15.78, 30.82] and [9.37, 14.87], respectively. This association is measured within a stratum in which source correctness is fixed to the wrong-source condition. 

Inferential scope. Evidence support does not capture how far a stored statement goes beyond relatively observable information. For this analysis, we use a study-specific operational taxonomy with four mutually exclusive categories: 

Observable object / attribute , Attention / gaze target , Activity / task state , and Prospective human inference . We compare harmful-flip rates across these categories under the Free-form condition. Free-form harmful-flip point estimates increase from 9.60% for observable object or attribute statements to 15.49% for attention or gaze-target statements, 21.14% for activity or task-state interpretations, and 24.32% for prospective human inference. The descriptive endpoint difference between prospective human inference and observable object or attribute statements is 14.73 percentage points. Their Wilson 95% confidence intervals are [13.36, 40.12] and [6.23, 14.50], respectively. We use this pattern as an associative characterization of inferential scope. The prospective category is small ( 𝑛 = 37) and has a correspondingly wide confidence interval. The pattern is consistent with a distinction between claims tied closely to visible objects or attention and claims that extend to activity or possible future states. These findings associate harmful downstream changes not only with source correctness, but also with evidence support and the inferential scope of the stored interpretation. This motivates testing a representation that keeps observation, inference, uncertainty, and temporal scope separate when history is reused.

4.4 Intervening on Persisted Content 

Correcting persisted content recovers harmful cases. We intervene on 117 cases from 58 unique videos that produced a harmful downstream flip under the frozen original Free-form condition, defined before any counterfactual outcome was observed: the No History prediction is correct, while the prediction with the original Free-form history is incorrect. For each case, we select the source entry with the highest similarity score under the fixed representation-independent retrieval ranking as the intervention target, before the retrieved top-3 entries are reordered chronologically for target inference. This selected entry is fixed across all interventions. Neutral replaces only this entry with semantically neutral wording of comparable length, Delete removes only this entry, and Oracle Historical Correction corrects only this entry using source-time evidence and, when needed, source ground truth. The other two retrieved entries and the target input remain unchanged. None of the interventions has access to future frames, target-time evidence, target labels, or downstream outcomes. Oracle historical correction recovers 41.03% of harmful cases, compared with 25.64% after removing the selected historical entry and 11.11% after a neutral length-matched edit. Oracle correction exceeds deletion by 15.38 percentage points (95% CI [+8.55, +22.22]) and the neutral edit by 29.91 percentage points (95% CI [+21.37, +39.32]). These interventions show that the semantic content of the selected persistent entry contributes to later failures. Because oracle correction may use source ground truth, we use it as a mechanism intervention. Appendix H provides one qualitative example tracing this process from source evidence to later outcome.

5 Discussion 5.1 Persistence Changes What Interpretation Does 

Persistence changes the role of a machine interpretation. At source time, an interpretation is a statement about an earlier event. Once retained, it becomes part of the system state used to reason about what happens next. Our two-sided amplification result shows why this distinction matters: Free-form Interpretation makes correct history more capable of changing later decisions in a helpful direction, but it also makes wrong history more capable of changing them in a harmful direction. Interpretation is therefore not only a way of describing prior evidence. When it persists, it becomes a mechanism through which the system assigns influence to its own earlier understanding. This matters for interactive assistants because later behavior is not limited to passive question answering. The evaluated target tasks include future-action prediction and system-triggered alerts based on gaze or object appearance. Our proactive results show that the representation of earlier interpretation changes these later system-triggered outputs as well. A statement that begins as an interpretation of one moment can therefore become part of the context behind what the assistant predicts, recalls, or decides to act on later. Gaze makes this transition especially visible because it creates an epistemic boundary between relatively direct behavioral evidence and broader interpretation. Visible objects and attended regions provide evidence about what a person saw or looked at, while activity and possible future behavior require additional inference. Within wrong-source histories, unsupported interpretations are associated with substantially more harmful changes, and the separate scope analysis shows higher harmful-flip rates for broader interpretive categories. When such distinctions are flattened into one fluent persistent statement, later reasoning receives the interpretation without an equally explicit account of how far it extends beyond the underlying evidence. 5.2 Design Principles for History-Aware Assistants 

Our findings point to three design choices that become important when AI systems retain interpretations about people across an unfolding interaction. Prior work on uncertainty and human-AI decision making has largely considered how the status of model output is communicated to people [6, 14, 26, 27]. Our results show a complementary issue: epistemic status also matters in the information an AI system carries forward for its own later reasoning. 

Preserve evidence status when interpretation persists. A persistent representation should retain the distinction between what the system observed, what it inferred, and what remains uncertain [5, 10, 37, 40]. For example, retaining that a person is “preparing to cut” is different from retaining that the person looked at a knife and tomato and that preparing to cut is an inference from those observations. Free-form Interpretation tends to collapse these roles into one fluent statement. EB provides one tested way to keep them available to later reasoning. 

Evaluate persistent representations at the point of reuse. The quality of persistent interpretation cannot be judged only by whether a stored statement appears plausible in isolation. Its consequence is realized when that statement is retrieved and changes a later decision. Our counterfactual intervention shows that correcting the semantic content of a harmful historical entry recovers more cases than deleting it or replacing it with neutral text. Persistent representations should therefore be evaluated by how their later reuse changes system behavior, including predictions and system-triggered outputs. 

Separate persistence from reuse. Retaining an interpretation and using it for a later decision are distinct design choices. Our full-task results show that history is useful for some target tasks but not for every target behavior. A system may therefore have reason to preserve an earlier interpretation without making it part of every subsequent decision. This separates three questions that are often collapsed in history-aware systems: what should persist, how it should be represented, and when it should be reused [20, 36, 39]. 

5.3 Limitations 

Our experiments evaluate persistent interpretation in a controlled gaze-aware benchmark. The measured outcomes are changes in model correctness, while user-facing outcomes such as trust, comfort, and perceived appropriateness remain outside the present evaluation. The primary analysis uses Qwen3.5-9B on STREAMGAZE, with GLM-4.6V-Flash providing a second-model replication of the core effect structure. WS and CS are homogeneous diagnostic strata: under the evaluated retrieval pipeline, all-wrong, mixed, and all-correct histories account for 10.4%, 61.0%, and 28.6% of eligible histories, respectively. Mixed histories remain included in ordinary full-task accuracy. The human analyses characterize associations between persistent content and downstream behavior. The evidence-support analysis fixes source correctness within the wrong-source population, while the inference-scope analysis includes a small prospective-inference group and correspondingly wide uncertainty. Both annotation studies use two author annotators who label cases independently before adjudication and without access to downstream outcomes; representation condition remains visually recognizable in the paired EB audit. Finally, the main manipulation evaluates persistent representation as a whole, including both semantic elaboration and textual organization. EB is likewise evaluated as a complete representation design. The neutral intervention isolates a role for semantic content, while oracle historical correction provides intervention evidence using source-time ground truth. 14 Ding et al. 

6 Conclusion 

We show that turning behavioral evidence into persistent machine interpretation is not a neutral way of carrying history forward. Free-form Interpretation amplifies the influence of earlier information in both directions: correct history becomes more capable of producing helpful downstream changes, while wrong history becomes more capable of producing harmful ones. Evidential support and inferential scope further characterize which persisted interpretations are associated with greater harmful influence, and the same core amplification structure appears across both evaluated models. These findings shift the design question from whether an assistant should interpret behavior to how its interpretations should persist. Evidence-Bounded Interpretation shows that keeping observation, inference, uncertainty, and temporal scope distinguishable can substantially reduce harmful influence from wrong histories while retaining most of the useful influence from correct ones. More broadly, when AI systems carry interpretations of people across an interaction, they also carry forward assumptions about what was observed, what was inferred, and what was uncertain. Those distinctions should remain part of the persistent context used to shape later assistance. 

Ethical Statement 

This study analyzes how model-generated interpretations of behavioral evidence influence later model decisions in a controlled gaze-aware benchmark. It does not measure whether a model understands a person’s intentions, activities, or future behavior, and the reported harmful and helpful changes refer only to model-output correctness rather than user harm or benefit. The findings should therefore not be used to justify profiling or making consequential judgments about real individuals based on gaze or model-generated interpretations. All experiments use the existing STREAMGAZE benchmark, and the human annotation reported in this paper was conducted by the authors on benchmark evidence and model outputs.

A Experimental Protocol and Reproducibility 

Source and target events. We use four STREAMGAZE tasks as sources of earlier gaze-conditioned events: Object Identification Easy (OI-E), Object Identification Hard (OI-H), Object Attribute Recognition (OAR), and Non-Fixated Object Identification (NFI). The resulting source library contains 4,513 entries anchored to direct source predictions. We evaluate downstream influence on six target tasks: Object Transition Prediction (OTP), Scene Recall (SR), Gaze Sequence Matching (GSM), Future Action Prediction (FAP), Gaze-Triggered Alert (GTA), and Object Appearance Alert (OAA). Source and target events come from the same video, and a source event is eligible only when it occurs before the target event. 

Fixed retrieval representation. Retrieval uses fixed textual event representations encoded with BAAI/bge-small-en-v1.5 [35] and ranked by cosine similarity. Each source event is represented by concatenating its source question with the model’s frozen direct source answer, while the target retrieval query contains only the target question. Source and target texts are encoded in the same embedding space. Generated Free-form or EB text is never used in either retrieval representation, so retrieval is fixed independently of the persistent representation being evaluated. 

Time-causal retrieval protocol. For each target event, retrieval is performed once over eligible earlier events from the same video. The retrieval stage has no access to future frames, source or target ground truth, target labels, downstream predictions, or downstream outcomes. We select the top 𝑘 = 3 source events according to cosine similarity and reorder the selected events chronologically before target inference. The same three events and the same chronological order are reused unchanged across Answer Only, Free-form Interpretation, and Evidence-Bounded Interpretation. 

Primary-model generation and freezing protocol. For the primary Qwen3.5-9B experiment, all representation condi-tions use the same checkpoint, decoding configuration, and fixed random-seed protocol. Direct source predictions are generated once and then frozen. Wrong-source and correct-source membership is defined once from these frozen direct source predictions and remains unchanged throughout the representation comparison. Free-form and EB representa-tions are also generated once from the frozen source-time inputs and are frozen before downstream evaluation. No representation condition regenerates source answers or changes WS/CS membership, and all representation conditions are evaluated on the same paired target cases. 

Representation generation. Free-form Interpretation and EB receive identical source-time inputs: the causal gaze/video evidence available at the source event, the source question, and the model’s frozen direct source answer. Source ground truth is not used for the primary representation generation. Representation generation has no access to future frames, target-time evidence, target labels, or downstream outcomes. The primary EB prompt was fixed before its reported evaluation outcomes were observed. 

Cross-model replication protocol. We repeat the complete representation pipeline with zai-org/GLM-4.6V-Flash [30]. GLM independently generates its own direct source predictions, which are generated once and frozen before defining GLM-specific wrong-source and correct-source populations. Free-form and EB representations are then generated by GLM from its own frozen source-time inputs and are frozen before downstream evaluation. Retrieval follows the same causal and representation-independent protocol as in the primary experiment: each source retrieval text concatenates the source question with GLM’s frozen direct source answer, the target retrieval text contains the target question, and both are encoded with BAAI/bge-small-en-v1.5 [35]. For each target, the top-𝑘 = 3 eligible earlier events are retrieved once and reused in the same chronological order across Answer Only, Free-form Interpretation, and EB. GLM 18 Ding et al. then performs target inference for all representation conditions. This replication tests whether the primary amplification and mitigation patterns remain visible when source prediction, representation generation, and target inference are all performed by a second model family. 

Target inference. At target time, all history-aware representation conditions receive the same causal target input and the same three retrieved earlier source events. Only the textual representation of those earlier events changes across Answer Only, Free-form Interpretation, and EB. No History uses the same target case without retrieved historical context. The target model has no access to frames occurring after the target time. 

Primary-model implementation. The same Qwen/Qwen3.5-9B checkpoint [24] is used for source prediction, repre-sentation generation, and target inference in the primary experiment. Generation uses temperature 0 .7 with the same decoding configuration and fixed random-seed protocol across conditions. All primary experiments are run on a single NVIDIA A100 80GB GPU. 

B Representation Generation Specification 

Free-form Interpretation and Evidence-Bounded Interpretation are generated from the same source-time inputs. In both conditions, the model receives the causal source-time gaze/video evidence, the source question, and the frozen direct source answer. Representation generation does not use source ground truth, future frames, target-time evidence, target labels, downstream predictions, or downstream outcomes. The generation rules are fixed across all evaluated cases. The following specifications summarize the information and output constraints that distinguish the two representation conditions. 

B.1 Free-form Interpretation 

The Free-form condition asks the model to convert the source-time information into a concise natural-language interpretation without explicitly separating observation, inference, uncertainty, or temporal scope. Its generation specification is: 

You are given an earlier event from an egocentric gaze-aware video stream. Use only the information available at this earlier event: - the source-time video and gaze evidence, - the source question, and - the model's source answer. Write one concise natural-language interpretation of what this evidence suggests about the person's current activity, situation, or plausible next behavior. You may connect the observed objects, gaze information, and surrounding context into a higher-level interpretation. Do not refer to any later event, future video evidence, When Interpretations Persist: Downstream Influence in Gaze-Aware Assistants 19 

target question, target answer, or ground-truth label. Output only the interpretation. 

This condition intentionally permits observation and higher-level inference to appear in one fluent persistent statement. It does not explicitly require the generated text to identify which parts are directly observed, inferred, or uncertain. 

B.2 Evidence-Bounded Interpretation 

The EB condition receives the same source-time inputs but requires the status of retained information to remain explicit. Its generation specification is: 

You are given an earlier event from an egocentric gaze-aware video stream. Use only the information available at this earlier event: - the source-time video and gaze evidence, - the source question, and - the model's source answer. Represent the event using exactly four fields: Observed: State only what is directly supported by the available source-time video and gaze evidence. Inferred: State the most reasonable interpretation that can be drawn from the observed evidence. Do not present an inference as an observation. Uncertain: State what remains ambiguous or is not supported well enough to treat as established, especially claims about the person's next action, intention, or future state. Temporal Scope: State whether the retained information describes the earlier observed event, an interpretation of that event, or a possible future state. Do not use later video evidence, a target question, a target answer, source or target ground truth, or downstream outcomes. Output exactly these four fields and no additional text. 20 Ding et al. EB does not prohibit interpretation. Its purpose is to keep directly supported evidence, model inference, uncertainty, and temporal scope distinguishable when the generated content becomes persistent context. The primary EB prompt was fixed before its reported downstream evaluation outcomes were observed. 

Generation-rule violations. The reported 8.3% rate refers to 373 of the 4,513 Qwen library entries flagged by the task-specific rule checks recorded in the generation pipeline. The pipeline uses JSON parsing, rule-based post-checks, and one corrective retry after a detected violation. This diagnostic flag is separate from direct source-answer correctness. 

C Human Annotation Protocols C.1 Detailed Paired Audit 

We conducted a detailed audit of 500 unique cases from 146 unique videos, sampled without replacement from a frozen eligible interpretation pool using a fixed random seed and proportional stratification by target task. The sampling procedure was defined before annotation and did not condition on downstream harmful or helpful outcomes, representation-quality labels, or inferential-scope categories. A case consists of the source-time evidence and the corresponding persistent representation to be evaluated. Two author annotators independently labeled all 500 cases in randomized order before any discussion, yielding 1,000 pre-adjudication judgments. The annotation interface did not expose downstream harmful or helpful outcomes. For this detailed 500-case audit, the reported pre-adjudication agreement statistics are 85.0% raw agreement and Cohen’s 𝜅 = 0.70. Disagreements were subsequently resolved through discussion, and all reported prevalence and characterization analyses use the adjudicated case-level labels. The detailed audit supports two separate analyses. First, the Free-form persistent interpretation is assigned to one of four mutually exclusive inferential-scope categories. These labels are later related to harmful flips under the Free-form condition. Second, the same 500 source cases support a paired representation-quality comparison between Free-form Interpretation and EB, where unsupported content, contradictory content, and overcertainty are coded as binary properties for each representation. Because EB has a recognizable four-field structure, complete blinding to representation condition cannot be guaranteed in the paired representation-quality audit. We therefore use these annotations as characterization evidence rather than as independent validation of the EB design. This limitation does not affect the primary downstream behavioral comparisons, which are computed directly from model outputs. 

C.2 Representation-Quality Labels 

For each representation, annotators evaluate three binary properties. 

Unsupported content is present when the persistent representation contains a substantive claim that is not supported by the available source-time video and gaze evidence. 

Contradictory content is present when the persistent representation contains a substantive claim that conflicts with the available source-time evidence. 

Overcertainty is present when uncertain or inferential content is expressed with stronger certainty than the source-time evidence supports. These properties characterize the generated persistent representation and are distinct from whether the frozen direct source answer itself is correct. C.3 Inferential-Scope Labels 

Each Free-form persistent interpretation is assigned to exactly one of four categories according to the broadest type of human-related claim expressed in the persistent text. 

Observable object / attribute covers statements restricted to visible objects or their observable properties. 

Attention / gaze target covers statements about what object or region the person is attending to. 

Activity / task state covers interpretations of what activity the person is performing or the state of the ongoing task. 

Prospective human inference covers claims about a possible future action, intention, or future state of the person. The final adjudicated distribution is 198 Observable object / attribute cases, 142 Attention / gaze target cases, 123 Activity / task state cases, and 37 Prospective human inference cases. These labels are used for associative characterization only. They are not treated as interventions on inferential scope. 

C.4 Wrong-Source Evidence-Support Annotation 

We use a separate binary evidence-support annotation covering the full frozen wrong-source population ( 𝑛 = 648). This annotation set is distinct from the detailed 500-case audit. The annotation target is the Free-form persistent interpretation. For each case, two author annotators independently judge whether the substantive content of the Free-form inter-pretation is supported by the source-time video and gaze evidence in the context of the source question. When needed, the frozen direct source answer is also shown to clarify the referent or meaning of the generated statement. Supported 

indicates that the substantive content of the persistent interpretation can be justified from the available source-time materials. Unsupported indicates that the interpretation adds substantive content that cannot be justified from those materials. Cases are presented in randomized order. Annotators do not have access to source ground truth, the EB representation, target-time information, target predictions or labels, downstream harmful or helpful outcomes, or an explicit indication that a case belongs to the WS population. Before adjudication, the two annotators agree on 592 of 648 cases (91.36%), with Cohen’s 𝜅 = 0.71. The remaining 56 disagreements are subsequently adjudicated, yielding 532 Supported and 116 Unsupported cases. Among these adjudicated groups, 63 Supported cases and 26 Unsupported cases produce harmful flips under Free-form Interpretation, corresponding to harmful-flip rates of 11.84% and 22.41%, respectively.

F Counterfactual Intervention Details 

We intervene on the persisted historical interpretation for the same 117 cases from 58 unique videos that produce harmful downstream changes under the frozen original Free-form reference condition. Each case has one fixed selected historical entry as the intervention target. Neutral editing, deletion, and oracle correction all operate on this same entry, while the other two retrieved entries and the target input remain unchanged. The neutral length-matched edit replaces the source-specific semantic content of the selected entry with semantically neutral wording of comparable length while keeping a textual entry present. The deletion condition removes the selected entry. The oracle historical correction 

replaces unsupported or incorrect content in the selected entry using source-time evidence and source ground truth when needed. The 117 intervention cases are defined before any counterfactual intervention outcome is observed, and target inference uses the same decoding configuration and fixed random-seed protocol as the original evaluation. Oracle correction has no access to future frames, target-time evidence, target labels, or downstream outcomes. A case is counted as recovered when the intervention changes the originally harmful target prediction back to the correct target answer.
