Abstract 

Current value alignment evaluations for large language models (LLMs) heavily rely on out-put compliance, overlooking whether LLMs maintain value consistency when alternating between generator and evaluator roles. Such consistency is increasingly important because modern LLM agents and alignment pipelines often rely on the same LLM to both generate actions and evaluate their acceptability. To mit-igate this limitation, we propose VALCON , a role-driven data construction framework that operationalizes value consistency into three benchmark suites (VAB-Consistency, VAB-Boundary, VAB-Challenge) via dual-role varia-tion. By systematically pairing an LLM’s gen-erative behavior with its evaluative judgments under identical principles, VALCON synthe-sizes target evaluation data that exposes hidden value discrepancies. Experiments on 28 LLMs reveal pervasive cross-role inconsistency : mod-els with highly compliant outputs frequently fail to align their judgments with their own generations. To understand and address this gap, we perform mechanistic probing that un-covers a role-dependent representational de-coupling associated with this behavioral asym-metry. Guided by these insights, we propose a representation-directed intervention that ef-fectively mitigates consistency failures. Our work establishes value consistency as a distinct alignment dimension and provides a complete evaluation-analysis-mitigation pipeline. Code and data are available at https://github. com/tjunlp-lab/VALCON .

2 Related Work 

Value Alignment Evaluation Value alignment has become a central concern due to the widespread deployment of LLMs (Shen et al., 2023). Early efforts have relied on human-curated datasets to as-sess conformity to ethical norms (Yao et al., 2024; Yu et al., 2024). Recent studies explore automated or semi-automated approaches for broader cover-age (Yao et al., 2025). Related benchmarks have also expanded value-oriented evaluation to multi-level safety risks and social biases (Shi and Xiong, 2025; Huang and Xiong, 2024). Another line of work examines whether value alignment transfers across languages and cultural settings (Xu et al., 2024, 2025), represents demographic pluralism (Zhu et al., 2026), or preserves higher-order value structures (Zheng et al., 2026). Benchmarks like Value FULCRA (Yao et al., 2024) and INVP (Liu et al., 2025) characterize value orientations under controlled settings. However, these studies treat alignment primarily as an output-level property. In contrast, we examine whether an LLM preserves an intended value target consistently across generator and context-cleared judge roles. 

Value Data Curation Constructing high-quality datasets is fundamental to value alignment evalua-tion. Early datasets are manually curated, offering high reliability but limited scalability and cover-age (Yao et al., 2024; Yu et al., 2024). To address these limitations, recent work explores automated or semi-automated data construction methods. For instance, Value FULCRA (Yao et al., 2024) em-ploys LLMs to generate value-related scenarios, while INVP (Liu et al., 2025) leverages adversar-ial templates to elicit value orientations. However, these methods typically construct data from a sin-gle role perspective (e.g., generator or judge), over-looking the possibility that the same LLM may apply values differently when acting in different roles. Our VALCON framework explicitly samples dual roles for each value principle, enabling the first systematic study of value consistency. 

Consistency and Intervention Consistency is widely studied to measure LLM robustness and coherence (Bonagiri et al., 2024; Jamshidi et al., 2025). However, existing evaluations typically ex-amine a fixed role and assume correctness. We decouple these concepts and extend consistency to dual-role dynamics. Evaluator Robustness and Self-Preference 

LLM judges can exhibit systematic reliability prob-lems, including position bias and sensitivity to evaluation protocols (Zheng et al., 2023; Wang et al., 2024). Evaluators may also recognize and favor their own generations (Panickssery et al., 2024). These studies focus on evaluator robust-ness or authorship-related preference, while SaGE examines moral and logical consistency within a fixed role (Bonagiri et al., 2024). VALCON is complementary: it removes the generation con-text, provides no explicit authorship cue, and tests whether an intended value label is preserved when the same model transitions from generation to context-cleared judgment under the same principle. It therefore targets dual-role value-label stability rather than evaluator preference alone. As shown in Table 1, existing benchmarks largely view alignment as a static, output-level property, whereas VALCON emphasizes reliability by testing value consistency across roles.

4.1 Value Taxonomy and Cultural Validation 

To build a rigorous normative foundation, we syn-thesize classical human value theories: specifically Maslow’s hierarchy (Maslow, 1943), Scheler’s phe-nomenology (Scheler, 1973), and Schwartz’s basic values (Schwartz, 1992). 4.2 Role-Driven Benchmark Tracks 

Using the 158 principles as seeds, the VAB fam-ily is partitioned into three complementary bench-marks to evaluate different dimensions of role con-sistency (Figure 2; Table 2): 

VAB-Consistency (158 principles). This bench-mark evaluates zero-shot end-to-end reliability across roles. For each principle, we use human-verified compliant and violating reference sam-ples as semantic anchors (Appendix C). Each LLM then independently generates 100 approxi-mately balanced query–response pairs per princi-ple. For dynamically generated cases, gold_label records the intended generation target y rather than an externally adjudicated normative label. VAB-Consistency therefore tests whether that target is preserved under context-cleared self-evaluation. 

VAB-Boundary (15,800 samples). Designed to test robust value boundaries in relatively clear-cut scenarios, VAB-Boundary is a high-confidence fil-tered corpus derived from recognized strong-model sources. We retain cases for which the source model’s generation target agrees with its context-cleared judgment and its self-reported confidence satisfies c ≥ 0.95 . Because this confidence is uncal-ibrated, it is used only as an auxiliary within-model filtering signal. Cross-model consensus is used only for VAB-Challenge, not for VAB-Boundary. See Appendix B for detailed source and label statis-tics. 

VAB-Challenge (710 samples). Existing bench-marks reveal persistent difficulties in complex multi-step and multimodal reasoning (Wu et al., 2025), while strategic-agent evaluations examine how LLMs balance competing interests and ethical principles in international events (Peng et al., 2025). Complementing these settings, VAB-Challenge fo-cuses specifically on value reasoning under norma-tive ambiguity. Its initial candidate pool consists of samples where LLMs exhibited internal dis-agreement during context-cleared self-evaluation. A rigorous cross-model verification protocol was then applied: only samples for which three state-of-the-art LLMs ( GPT-5.1 , Doubao-Seed , and 

DeepSeek-R1 ) reached consensus were retained. The resulting 710 class-balanced samples represent challenging edge cases (Appendix J) for evaluating robustness in boundary-blurring scenarios. 

4.3 Quality Control & Disagreement Audit 

We implement a multi-tiered quality-control pro-tocol. Track-Specific Filtering: VAB-Boundary uses same-model Generator–Judge agreement and self-reported confidence c ≥ 0.95 , whereas cross-model consensus is used only for VAB-Challenge. 

Human Validation: Blind review of 100 valida-tion samples yields 92.3% agreement and Cohen’s 

κ = 0.86 . Dataset Balancing: Stratified sam-pling maintains coverage across value principles and intended labels. Disagreement-Source Audit: 

We additionally conduct a blind audit of 200 strat-ified VAB-Consistency disagreements. Of these, 164 (82.0%) are attributed to Generator-side tar-get/content mismatch, 30 (15.0%) to Judge-side reversal, 4 (2.0%) to boundary ambiguity, 1 (0.5%) to refusal or evasion, and 1 (0.5%) remains unre-solved. These proportions are conditional on the audited disagreement subset and must not be inter-preted as unconditional benchmark accuracy. 

5 Experiments 

To ensure comprehensive evaluation, we assessed 28 representative LLMs spanning 0.6B to 671B parameters, including frontier reasoning LLMs (e.g., GPT-5.1, DeepSeek-R1), closed-source APIs, and open-weight LLMs (see Appendix A). All ex-periments followed the VALCON protocol using a unified pipeline ( temperature=0.7 for genera-tion, temperature=0.0 for verification). We fur-ther fine-tuned three models (Qwen3-0.6B, Qwen3-1.7B, and Llama-3-8B-Instruct) on VAB-Boundary via LoRA to analyze and mitigate dual-role incon-sistencies. Full implementation details are in Ap-pendix G. 

5.1 Evaluation Metrics 

We adopt four complementary metrics to evaluate dual-role alignment (see Appendix F for details). 

(1) Self-Review Consistency (SRC) mea-sures unfiltered agreement between the Generator-intended target and the context-cleared Judge la-bel. (2) Balanced SRC (B-SRC) equally weights the compliant and violating target classes. Nei-ther SRC nor B-SRC uses confidence. (3) τ -SRC 

reports agreement among judgments satisfying a within-model confidence threshold ( τ = 0 .95 ), while coverage Cτ = Nτ /N reports the retained fraction. (4) Effective-SRC combines τ -SRC with retained sample volume and is therefore treated as a complementary consistency–coverage metric rather than a pure consistency score or standalone ranking criterion. Self-reported confidence is un-calibrated and is not interpreted as a probability comparable across models.

6 Analysis & Intervention 

6.1 Behavioral Patterns of Inconsistency Reasoning-driven Consistency. As shown in Ta-ble 3, LLMs with explicit reasoning capabilities significantly outperform standard instruct versions. For instance, Qwen3-Next-80B-Thinking achieves an SRC of 98.91%, while its Qwen3-Next-80B-Instruct counterpart drops to 71.04%. This obser-vational gap suggests that explicit reasoning pro-cesses may support value consistency, although the comparison does not isolate chain-of-thought as the sole causal factor. 

Deceptive Consistency and Behavioral Collapse. 

Tables 3 and 6 reveal a deceptive consistency pat-tern: some LLMs, such as Qwen3-14B, achieve high raw SRC while generating very few violating cases (V-Ratio: 9.23%). This violation-generation avoidance produces a highly skewed case distribu-tion, inflating raw agreement without demonstrat-ing balanced consistency. The pattern motivates reporting V-Ratio and B-SRC alongside SRC to penalize distributional collapse rather than surface agreement. 

Value Heterogeneity. Figure 6 further shows that consistency is highly non-uniform across value di-mensions. Lower-capacity models exhibit system-atic degradation on abstract principles such as Lib-erty and Human Rights , whereas stronger models maintain more balanced value profiles, suggesting dimension-dependent robustness of internal value representations. Consistency as a Reliability Property. Our re-sults establish value consistency and normative cor-rectness as distinct dimensions: an LLM may excel in one yet fail in the other. For example, Qwen3-Next-80B-A3B-Instruct achieves 94.23% accuracy (Table 5) on VAB-Challenge, yet attains 71.04% SRC (Table 3) on its own generations. VALCON does not approximate moral truth; instead, it evalu-ates whether an intended value target is preserved across generator and context-cleared judge roles, a prerequisite for self-evaluation systems, where inconsistency constitutes a critical failure mode. 

Relation to Existing Consistency Phenomena. 

Evaluator robustness concerns whether judgments change under perturbations such as response or-der (Wang et al., 2024); self-preference concerns whether evaluators favor outputs recognized as their own (Panickssery et al., 2024); and SaGE evaluates moral or logical consistency within a fixed role (Bonagiri et al., 2024). VALCON in-stead clears the generation context, supplies no explicit authorship cue, and measures preserva-tion of a Generator-intended value label across a Generator-to-Judge transition. These phenomena may interact, but they capture distinct reliability properties. 

Illusions of Alignment under Explicit Principles. 

Several LLMs exhibit near-perfect self-consistency on well-specified principles but degrade sharply in contested scenarios, revealing an illusion of align-ment caused by evaluation regimes that overrep-resent unambiguous cases (Table 4). As a result, reliability may be overestimated, while brittleness emerges under ambiguity, especially when LLMs evaluate their own outputs. 

Sources of Cross-Role Disagreement. The blind audit shows that aggregate SRC combines multi-ple mechanisms: 82.0% of audited disagreements are Generator-side target/content mismatches and 15.0% are Judge-side reversals, with the remain-der due to boundary ambiguity, refusal, or unre-solved attribution. Implications for Alignment Training. 

Reasoning-capable models exhibit higher dual-role consistency, suggesting that explicit deliberation may help preserve value targets across roles. Controlled fine-tuning further shows that SOO-FT consistently improves violating-case accuracy over SFT-only. Its advantage beyond matched class weighting is substantial for Qwen3-0.6B and Llama-3-8B-Instruct but minimal for Qwen3-1.7B. These results suggest that representation-directed training is a promising yet model-dependent complement to token-level objectives while largely preserving general capabilities. 

8 Conclusion 

We introduced VALCON , an end-to-end evalua-tion–analysis–mitigation framework for studying dual-role value consistency in large language mod-els. Through three complementary VAB tracks, VALCON evaluates whether models preserve in-tended value targets across generation and context-cleared judgment. Experiments on 28 Chinese and English LLMs reveal substantial Generator–Judge disagreement. A blind audit disentangles its ma-jor sources, while RCM provides an associated representation-level perspective on the observed be-havioral asymmetry. Guided by this analysis, con-trolled SOO-FT consistently improves violating-case accuracy over SFT-only and yields additional gains beyond weighted CE for two of the three evaluated models. Overall, VALCON provides a unified framework for evaluating, analyzing, and mitigating dual-role value inconsistency, comple-menting existing work on normative accuracy, eval-uator robustness, self-preference, and fixed-role consistency. 

Ethical Considerations 

VALCON is designed for responsible AI evaluation. All benchmark cases are synthetically generated under explicit safety constraints, with automated screening and manual review to remove person-ally identifiable information and other unsafe con-tent. The value taxonomy and benchmark cases are curated and validated by domain experts who participated with informed consent. Because value judgments are culturally and contextually depen-dent, VALCON is intended as a research instrument for evaluating cross-role value consistency rather than as an autonomous arbiter of moral correctness or a basis for consequential decisions. Limitations 

VALCON has several limitations. First, its syn-thetic data may inherit alignment biases shared across current LLMs, which cross-model filtering cannot fully eliminate, particularly in culturally specific or contested cases. Second, VALCON measures cross-role value consistency rather than normative correctness and should not be treated as an arbiter of moral truth. Third, its linguistic and contextual coverage remains bounded by the source models’ prompt-following capabilities; natively au-thored multilingual principles, long-tail scenarios, and conflicts among multiple principles require further study. Finally, self-reported confidence is uncalibrated across models, making τ -SRC, cov-erage, and Effective-SRC auxiliary to confidence-independent SRC and B-SRC. The 200-case audit is conditional on sampled disagreements, RCM provides associative rather than causal evidence, and SOO’s incremental benefit over weighted CE is model-dependent.

G.1 Settings
