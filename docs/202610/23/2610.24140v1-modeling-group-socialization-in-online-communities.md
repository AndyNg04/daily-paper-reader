## 摘要
在线社区的新成员必须学习群体规范，才能顺利参与其中。这一过程称为社会化，一直是在线社区研究领域的重要议题。然而，社会心理学中关于群体社会化的奠基性研究表明，社会化实际上是一个双向过程：个体与社区会相互适应彼此的需求。本文构建了一个社会化的形式化定量模型，并利用 Reddit 数据首次对双向社会化假设进行了大规模实证验证。具体而言，我们聚焦于语言社会化，并将用户在在线社区中的评论建模为群体内部语言与其此前参与过的“背景”社区语言的混合。在一个包含 112 个 subreddit（共 780 万个词元）的数据集上，我们发现，与两个单向社会化模型相比，双向模型在跨用户和跨时间的预测中具有更好的泛化能力。此外，我们还进行了定性分析，表明该模型能够从数据中提取出可解释、且与现有文献相符的社会化模式。具体来说，模型能够学到：在主题更为小众、内容审核规则更严格的社区中，用户会更遵循群体规范；而长期用户比新成员更为遵循群体规范。我们强调，本文提出的主张是明确可证伪的——我们清晰阐述了对社会化过程的看法，并鼓励未来研究对我们的模型提出挑战。

## Abstract
Newcomers to online communities must learn a group's norms to participate successfully. This process is known as socialization, and has been the subject of considerable study in the online communities literature. Foundational work on group socialization in social psychology, however, suggests that socialization is actually a bidirectional process: individuals and communities adapt to each other's needs. In this work we develop a formal quantitative model of socialization, and present the first large scale empirical validation of the bidirectional socialization hypothesis using Reddit data. More specifically, we focus on linguistic socialization, and model users' comments in online communities as a mixture of in-group language and language from ``background'' communities they previously participated in. On a dataset of 112 subreddits (7.8 million tokens) find that our bidirectional model makes predictions that better generalize across users and time than two unidirectional socialization models. Furthermore, we conduct a qualitative analysis in which we demonstrate that our model is able to extract interpretable socialization patterns from the data that align with the existing literature. Specifically our model is able to learn that users conform more to group norms in topically niche communities with stricter content moderation rules, and that long time users conform more than newcomers. We emphasize that the claims made in this paper are explicitly falsifiable -- we are staking out a clear claim about what the socialization process looks like, and encourage future work to challenge our model.
