# TARSphere: A Robot-Oriented Full-Stack Framework for Long-Horizon Embodied Agents in Evolving Physical and Social Environments 

Yicheng Xu 1, Zhikai Xu 1, Ziyuan Chen 2, Yuhang Dong 1, Tao Zhang 1, Zhucun Xue 1, Yong Liu 1,

Jiangning Zhang 1,†

1Zhejiang University, 2Fudan University

Corresponding author 

Robots must act under physical and social constraints while adapting to changes in their environment and human needs. Existing embodied-agent systems often focus on specified goals or abstract execution into symbolic actions, limiting evaluation in an evolving world. We introduce TARSphere, a robot-oriented full-stack framework for physically and socially grounded long-horizon embodied agents. TARSphere-Infra runs robot policies in a physics simulator with embodied NPCs and constrained dialogue. An event-driven finite-state machine (FSM) uses completed actions and delivered information to update task states and evaluate outcomes. TARSphere-Harness, its embodied brain harness, lets a frozen multimodal large language model (MLLM) act through multi-level executable skills, using observations, hierarchical memory, and execution feedback to revise its decisions. On this foundation, agent-in-the-loop curation aligns task semantics, causal dependencies, and scene geometry to construct TARSphere-Bench: 360 episodes across 30 indoor scenes. Five paired conditions assess perceptual robustness, disruption adaptation, task update, human coordination, and intent understanding for navigate-and-report and fetch-and-deliver tasks. Our evaluation of 8 MLLMs shows that general-purpose models can outperform embodied models, while task updates and human interaction remain difficult . Frequent collisions and falls further motivate evaluating execution safety alongside task success, even with a shared action interface and decision budget.

1arXiv:2610.30043v1 [cs.RO] 21 Oct 2026 1 Introduction 

Robots operating in everyday environments must account for physical constraints [11], human safety, appro-priate interaction, and social norms [19, 23]. As the world evolves, sensory conditions, physical access, and people’s actions and needs can change during execution [8, 9, 23]. Long-horizon tasks therefore require tracking world states, interpreting human intentions, and adjusting plans using execution feedback [4, 8, 34] (Figure 1). Vision-language-action (VLA) models [3, 10] and world-action models (WAMs) [38] have advanced robot control, yet they often lack the ability to adapt to dynamic and uncertain environments. Large language models (LLMs) provide broad knowledge for reasoning yet lack the ability to perform complex, real-world tasks [1, 8, 9]. This complementarity motivates embodied agents that connect reasoning to action [1, 8, 14, 37]. However, Harness VLA [39], RoboClaw [13], and HumanCLAW [14] focus on tasks with specified goals, leaving an evolving world with human involvement largely unexamined. Other settings, including EmbodiedBench’s high-level tasks [37], LoTa-Bench [5], ALFWorld’s text environments [28], and Embodied Agent Interface [12], abstract physical execution into discrete symbolic actions. GRBench [35] combines robot execution with virtual NPC dialogue, while PARTNR [4] evaluates embodied human-robot collaboration with temporal task constraints. These gaps motivate a physically and socially grounded foundation for benchmarking robotic agents in an evolving world, with task outcomes tied to execution and human interaction. 

Insight. Studying embodied intelligence requires physically and socially grounded execution and tasks revealing capabilities across varied situations in a changing world (Sections 4.2, 4.3.2 and 4.3.4). 

Challenge 1: Embodied execution is not symbolic [Sections 4.2 and 4.3.4]. A valid action call does not guarantee successful execution: the robot must contend with contact, friction, balance, and human responses. The agent must therefore revise decisions using physical execution feedback. 

Challenge 2: The world changes during execution [Sections 4.3.2 and 4.3.3]. Physical conditions, human activity, and task requirements can change while a plan unfolds. The agent must interpret the current situation and reconsider what to do next. 

Challenge 3: Evaluation must assess diverse capabilities [Section 4.2]. Within this setting, evaluation must reveal how agents handle varied situations across multiple capability dimensions. Aggregate success alone can mask different capability gaps across task families (Section 4.2). We introduce TARSphere, a robot-oriented full-stack framework for embodied agents under physical and social constraints. TARSphere-Infra couples robot controllers with embodied NPCs in a physics simulator (Challenge 1). An event-driven FSM maintains world and task states, while TARSphere-Harness combines observations, memory, skills, and execution feedback for replanning (Challenge 2). We build TARSphere-Bench through agent-in-the-loop curation, pairing navigate-and-report and fetch-and-deliver tasks with five capability conditions: perceptual robustness, disruption adaptation, task update, human coordination, and intent understanding (Challenge 3). Paired variants retain their Base scene and task family. Across 8 MLLMs, general-purpose models can outperform embodied models, with capability gaps varying by task family (Section 4.2). Planning credit does not ensure successful execution, and collisions and falls remain common (Sections 4.2 and 4.3.4). These findings call for agents that combine general reasoning, adaptation to world changes, and safe execution (Sections 4.2 and 4.3.2). Our contributions are three-fold. • We formulate long-horizon embodied tasks under physical and social constraints, where execution outcomes and world changes continually shape the agent’s decisions. • We develop TARSphere Framework, where physical execution and interaction with embodied humans update task states and supply evaluation evidence; an embodied brain harness uses observations, memory, executable skills, and world feedback to guide model decisions. • We introduce agent-in-the-loop curation for 360 TARSphere-Bench episodes in 30 scenes and five paired capability conditions, aligning task semantics, geometry, and execution. 22 Related Work 

2.1 Embodied Interactive Simulation Environments 

VirtualHome [22] represents household activities as executable programs; Habitat [27] and RoboTHOR [6] support navigation and indoor interaction. Habitat 2.0 [29] adds rearrangement, and BEHAVIOR-1K [11] broadens household activities and object-state changes. RoboCasa [20] provides diverse kitchen scenes and composite tasks, while ManiSkill3 [30] enables GPU-parallel contact-rich manipulation. Human participation introduces additional demands: Habitat 3.0 [23] studies social navigation and collaborative rearrangement; PARTNR [4] evaluates household collaboration under spatial, temporal, and capability constraints. GR-Bench [35] combines robot execution with virtual NPC dialogue. TARSphere couples physical robot execution, embodied NPC behavior, and changing task requirements in one event-driven environment. Communication depends on proximity, visibility, hearing range, and human availability, while completed actions and delivered messages commit task-state changes. Paired conditions compare adaptation and interaction within matched scenes and task families, relating capability demands to decisions, physical execution, and completion. 

2.2 Embodied Foundation Models 

SayCan [1] grounds skill selection in executable behaviors, and Code as Policies [15] generates programs combining perception, control APIs, and feedback loops. Inner Monologue [8] uses scene descriptions, success feedback, and human interaction to revise decisions. SayPlan [26] uses hierarchical 3D scene graphs and simulator feedback for planning, while VoxPoser [9] composes observation-grounded 3D value maps for closed-loop manipulation. Complementary action models, including RT-2 [3], OpenVLA [10], and GR00T [2], connect multimodal inputs to robot control; HY-Embodied [33] targets spatial understanding and embodied action. TARSphere evaluates frozen multimodal models through a shared harness exposing bounded primitives, higher-level skills, hierarchical memory, and measured execution feedback. This setting examines how model decisions respond to evolving goals, access, and human needs, alongside ablations of the perception, memory, and skill interface supporting those decisions. 

2.3 Robotic Agent Systems and Interfaces 

ROS [24] and ROS 2 [18] provide modular infrastructure for robot perception, planning, and control. OK-Robot [16] integrates open-vocabulary perception, navigation, and manipulation for household pick-and-drop tasks. RoboClaw [13] links data collection, policy learning, and deployment through a VLM controller orchestrating learned primitives. ASPIRE [17] repairs control programs and accumulates reusable skills, while Zetta [7] develops runtime critics and recovery skills around a frozen policy. To separate decisions from execution, HumanCLAW [14] studies VLM decisions through a simulated human body excluding execution-side disturbances. TARSphere retains robot dynamics and records collisions, falls, motion residuals, and task outcomes. Its event-driven evaluator checks evidence against the requirements active when it was produced, including authorized task revisions and recipient acceptance, supporting analysis of how physical execution, adaptation, and human interaction jointly affect completion.

3.2 TARSphere Framework 

TARSphere Framework has two layers: TARSphere-Infra, the Execution Infrastructure (Figure 2), manages physical execution, embodied human interaction, task states, and evaluation. TARSphere-Harness, the Embodied Brain Harness (Figure 3), organizes observations, memory, executable skills, and feedback for a frozen MLLM. TARSphere-Infra returns synchronized RGB-D and LiDAR observations, measured feedback, and delivered public events to the harness; task requirements and scoring are private. We separately test execution fidelity and harness components (Sections 4.3.1 and 4.3.4). 

3.2.1 TARSphere-Infra Simulation and robot execution. In Figure 2, the harness sends robot actions to a shared execution environment containing G1, task objects, and NPCs in GRScenes-100 [35] scenes. Isaac Sim [21] and PhysX execute robot control under contact and friction: locomotion follows a learned policy, while grasping executes planned arm motion and requires physical object acquisition and retention (Section 4.3.4). Episode reset and synchronization keep sensing, robot control, and NPC motion consistent. Kinematic motion and skeletal animation keep NPCs’ visible poses aligned with their collision proxies, so visible human motion and robot contact refer to the same embodied person throughout execution and interaction. 

Embodied NPCs and constrained dialogue. The NPC runtime maintains each person’s pose, behavior, availability, and authorized knowledge. Its LLM backend routes natural questions to authorized topics and returns episode-defined replies. Robot-to-NPC queries require proximity and visibility; NPC speech reaches the robot only within hearing range, so available information depends on the current spatial configuration. 

Event-driven task state machine. The FSM (Figure 2) tracks event and NPC action status and active task bindings. It admits events when DAG prerequisites and live physical and interaction conditions hold; NPC actions follow Idle–Queued–Acting–Done . Confirmed action completion and actual message delivery 

commit state transitions, applying authorized updates and enabling dependent stages. The evaluator scores five dependency-checked outcomes against the applicable requirements; task-update events are unscored. A fixed semantic judge scores plans and reports; handover requires physical execution and recipient acceptance. We compare semantic scores with human judgments (Section 4.4) and trace responses to task updates through message delivery, goal revision, and physical completion (Sections 4.3.2 and 4.3.3). 

3.2.2 Embodied Brain Harness Closed-loop agent–world interaction. Execution outcomes and world changes can invalidate a plan (Sections 4.3.2 and 4.3.3). TARSphere-Harness executes actions serially and can return control at tool boundaries, canceling the remaining actions. Each decision receives current egocentric RGB, body-frame LiDAR, public events, and execution feedback through Figure 3(a, f). Motion residuals and collision bearings report movement error and contact direction for subsequent decisions. We ablate motion residuals and LiDAR observations to examine their contributions to replanning and task completion (Section 4.3.1). 

Multimodal context and hierarchical memory. As views and task requirements change, TARSphere-Harness retains context through current observations and the memory layers in Figure 3(a, b), whose contributions we examine through ablations (Section 4.3.1). 3.3 TARSphere-Bench 

Task Design and Benchmark Taxonomy. We construct 360 TARSphere-Bench episodes for navigate-and-report and fetch-and-deliver in 30 GRScenes-100 [35] scenes: 10 homes and 20 commercial spaces. Each Base episode has five paired conditions sharing its scene, task family, and five-stage evaluation. We test perceptual robustness (PR) through sensing changes and disruption adaptation (DA) through temporary blockage or person/tool unavailability while the goal remains fixed. For task update (TU), an authorized event revises the task; human coordination (HC) requires another person’s information or onsite work, and intent understanding (IU) requires clarification or contextual inference. HC seeks help toward a known goal; IU resolves what the person wants. Base comparisons quantify each condition’s effects (Section 4.2).

# 4 Experiments 

4.1 Experimental Setup 

We evaluate 8 frozen MLLMs: four proprietary models, GPT-5.6-Terra, Claude Sonnet-5, Gemini-3.8-Flash, and Qwen3.7-Plus; two general-purpose open VLMs, Qwen3.8-27B [25] and Gemma-4-31B [32]; and two open embodied models, Hy-Embodied-VLM-1.0 [36] and RoboBrain2.0-32B [31]. All use TARSphere-Harness on TARSphere-Bench’s 360 episodes, sharing prompts, observations, actions, a 100-decision cap, and termination rules. By default, decisions use the current image and 10 text-history steps to propose 1-5 actions. All model weights remain frozen, allowing comparisons through the same harness without benchmark-specific training, parameter updates, or adaptation to the evaluation tasks. 

Evaluation Metrics. We use quality scoring for the main results and ablations. The five stages are task understanding, onsite target observation, onsite task completion, recipient observation, and final task completion. The onsite outcome is an inspection conclusion, an answer received from a source, or a physical grasp, depending on the task. For episode e, let ge,i ∈ { 0, 1} indicate eligible evidence for stage i under its event dependencies and live conditions, and let qe,i ∈ [0 , 1] score that evidence. # 5 Conclusion 

TARSphere grounds long-horizon robot tasks in shared physical and social constraints (Sections 4.2 and 4.3.4). TARSphere Framework couples robot dynamics, embodied NPC interaction, and state-machine control and evaluation; TARSphere-Harness lets frozen MLLMs act through memory, executable skills, and measured feedback (Sections 4.3.1 and 4.4). TARSphere-Bench pairs two service task families with five capability demands, supporting trajectory-based comparisons of model decisions, completion, and execution quality as demands change during execution, including revised goals and dependencies on human participation. 

Limitations and Future Work. We evaluate one robot body and two indoor service task families in simulation. Real-robot transfer must address differences in sensing, contact, and human behavior. We plan Mobile-Franka transfer and task-adapted VLA, navigation, and manipulation backends.
