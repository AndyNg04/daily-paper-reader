# GelSlim 5.0: Unifying Vision and Touch with a Single Fingertip Camera 

Xiangdong Wang ∗, I-Hsuan Huang ∗, Xiaohao Xu ∗, Xili Yi, Nima Fazeli, and Xiaonan Huang 

Abstract — Vision and touch provide complementary cues for object pose and local contact geometry, but external cameras are often occluded at the contact site. We present GelSlim 5.0, a fingertip sensor that acquires visual and tactile observations with one fixed camera and a continuous UV-responsive contact layer. By switching illumination, the camera sequentially acquires through-membrane visual images and contact-sensitive tactile images from a shared viewpoint, avoiding inter-camera extrinsic calibration. To combine these observations, we introduce Vis-TacFusion, a multimodal transformer with separate prediction paths for planar object pose and contact geometry. Asymmetric bottleneck fusion incorporates visual context while preserving tactile spatial features for depth and surface-normal estimation. Modality dropout enables inference with paired inputs or either modality alone. On real validation data from 12 known objects, paired inputs reduce depth MSE, angular error, and translation 

L1 by 61.2%, 72.3%, and 61.3%, respectively, relative to tactile-only inference with the same model. Relative to visual-only inference, the corresponding reductions are 93.7%, 6.1%, and 8.6%. Project website 

I. INTRODUCTION Contact-rich manipulation requires estimating both object pose and the local geometry of contact. Vision supplies object context, while optical tactile sensors such as GelSight and GelSlim capture detailed contact structure [1], [2]. The modalities can resolve different ambiguities: visual context can distinguish object poses that produce similar contact patches, while tactile measurements reveal local surface geometry that visual appearance alone may not resolve. With external cameras, however, the hand and object can occlude the contact site, and viewpoint differences complicate spatial association with tactile measurements. A shared fingertip camera places visual sensing at the contact interface and provides a common frame for relating object appearance to contact geometry. Prior visuotactile sensors obtain scene access through semitransparent skins [3], controllable films [4], UV-visible markers [5], or spatial multiplexing [6]. Building on these designs, we couple a continuous contact interface with an estimator for object pose and spatially resolved contact geometry. We introduce GelSlim 5.0, a fingertip sensor that co-locates vision and touch within the GelSlim 4.0 body design [7]. A continuous UV-responsive contact layer allows one fixed camera to record through-membrane visual images with UV off and contact-sensitive tactile images with UV on (Fig. 1). GELSLIM 5.0: COMMON-VIEW SENSING GelSlim 5.0 is a monocular visual–tactile fingertip sensor that captures both observation modalities from a single camera by switching UV illumination (Fig. 2). The design has three elements: (i) a compliant contact membrane whose optical state changes under UV, (ii) an illumination ring that drives the switching, and (iii) a 3D-printed body housing one camera and a custom driver PCB. Below, we describe each element and the resulting acquisition protocol.

Visual and tactile acquisition With UV off, the camera records a through-membrane visual image Iv ; we refer to this state as proximity mode. With UV on, it records a contact-sensitive tactile image It. We use UV-responsive to describe this observed change in imaging state; transmission, reflection, and UV-induced emission have not been separately quantified. Each pair is captured sequentially under approximately stationary contact. Because the camera and lens remain fixed, the images share a viewpoint and image coordinates, supporting spatial association of appearance and contact features without inter-camera extrinsic calibration (Fig. 4). Refraction, membrane deformation, and residual motion can still affect pixelwise alignment. Camera intrinsics and object-pose calibration remain relevant to estimation. IV. LEARNING OBJECT POSE AND CONTACT GEOMETRY VisTacFusion combines visual context with tactile signa-tures through separate pose and geometry prediction paths (Fig. 3). The pose path aggregates features into a pose vector, while the geometry path preserves spatial detail for depth and normals. Cross-attention associates the modalities without requiring pixelwise correspondence.

Data and geometric references A KUKA LBR Med R820 arm collects approximately 400 contacts for each of 12 objects, recording both image states and end-effector pose at every contact. A reference-object calibration relates object pose to the sensor frame. Pose and object geometry generate the depth reference maps; real normal maps are obtained by finite differences. These are computed geometric targets, not independent measurements of the deformed membrane. For the 12 corresponding geometries, Blender renders visual images, depth maps, and normal maps. Simulated tactile images are synthesized from the rendered depth maps, with random ±15% depth scaling, by a depth-to-tactile generator (U-Net with a PatchGAN discriminator) trained only on real training pairs. Both image streams are center-cropped to 0.816 of their original dimensions and resized to 224 × 224 . Simulated visual images receive an additional 1.15 × center zoom to align their field of view with real images. Simulated poses are restricted to the per-object real rotation and translation ranges with a margin factor of 1.5. Simulation augmentation applies matched in-plane rotations within ±15 ◦, together with channel, illumination, and noise perturbations. Real images are not augmented. 

Validation and checkpoint selection Every tenth sample within each real session is held out for validation and checkpoint selection. Objects and acquisition sessions are shared between training and validation, and object identity is supplied to the pose queries. All reported scores therefore measure within-session generalization on known objects. Geometry and pose use separate checkpoints selected by depth MSE and mean angular error, respectively. Fusion models use both for selection; dedicated models use their native mode. Each selected fusion checkpoint is evaluated in all three modes without reselection, ensuring a common checkpoint for each within-task modality comparison. 

Geometry and pose metrics Depth and normal MSEs average over all pixels, including non-contact regions. Normal MSE also averages over its three components. Depth MSE is in mm 2. Rotation L1 sums the absolute cosine and sine errors per sample; angular error averages the inverse cosine of the clipped predicted–reference dot product, in degrees. Translation L1 averages both coordinate errors, normalized by half the object’s target size. 

Optimization The main fusion runs use real–simulation co-training at a real:sim ratio of 1:1.27. Optimization uses AdamW for 150 epochs with batch size 32, learning rate 

2 × 10 −4, weight decay 0.05, cosine decay, and 1,000 warmup steps. We use mixed precision and gradient clipping at 1.0. The input probabilities are (pVis , p Tac , p Both ) =(0 .05 , 0.25 , 0.70) , with pinject = 0 .75 .

Compared estimators All scores in Table II are local evalua-tions. TVL [28] and Sparsh-X [29] replace the tactile encoder while retaining MAE and our downstream architecture. The Sparsh-X image encoder is locally pretrained on the co-training images and then frozen. VITaL [35] and MViTac [30] use adapted ResNet-18 implementations; MViTac predicts pose only. Dedicated single-modality models attach geometry and pose heads to one frozen encoder. Symmetric MBT [31] replaces the fusion trunk while retaining the same encoders and heads.

0.618 ◦. We select SITR+MAE to prioritize contact geometry. This sweep compares both pretrained representations and architectures. 

Fusion configuration. Using the same encoders and heads, Table III shows that asymmetric fusion reduces paired-input angular error from 0.747 ◦ to 0.652 ◦ and translation L1 from 0.0054 to 0.0053. A. Discussion 

We presented GelSlim 5.0 and VisTacFusion as a common-view visual–tactile sensing and estimation framework for object pose and contact geometry. Our results suggest that the value of multimodal sensing lies not only in the distinct strengths of individual modalities, but also in their ability to resolve each other’s ambiguities. Proximity vision provides a global prior for object pose and contact interpretation, whereas tactile sensing supplies a direct local measurement of contact geometry. The further improvement obtained with paired observations indicates cross-modal synergy rather than a simple division of sensing roles, motivating task-specific asymmetric fusion and state-dependent mode selection for dexterous manipulation. Our real–simulation co-training study further reveals a trade-off between synthetic-data coverage and domain fi-delity. Adding simulated observations expands the range of object poses and contact geometries seen during training and improves real-world validation performance over real-only training. As simulated samples account for a larger fraction of training, these discrepancies may increasingly bias the learned representation away from the real-data distribution, resulting in negative transfer and an intermediate optimal mixing ratio rather than monotonic gains from additional simulation. In our setting, the selected real-to-simulation ratio of 1:1.27 provides the best overall trade-off for pose estimation while retaining competitive geometry accuracy. This optimum is specific to the current simulator, dataset, and training configuration; more broadly, the result suggests that effective sim–real co-training must balance synthetic coverage against domain fidelity. 

B. Future Work 

Miniaturization and thermal management The current prototype retains the GelSlim 4.0 body; future work will co-design the camera–lens module, illumination, electronics, and contact stack for a smaller, power-efficient fingertip while preserving field of view and contact sensitivity. We will quantify package size, energy per paired observation, and contact-surface temperature under matched acquisition conditions.
