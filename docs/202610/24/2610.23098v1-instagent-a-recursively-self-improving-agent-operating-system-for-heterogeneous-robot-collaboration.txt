Abstract — Heterogeneous robot teams enable complex, long-horizon tasks by combining complementary sensing, mobility, and manipulation capabilities across multiple embodiments. Yet existing systems struggle to retain execution-time corrections as reusable experience, maintain task-relevant context memory across space and time for reliable handoffs, and explicitly represent the inter-robot dependencies required for structured collaboration. In order to address these challenges, we present InstAgent, a recursively self-improving (RSI) Agent Operat-ing System for heterogeneous robot collaboration. InstAgent organizes team behaviors as agentic behavior trees(BTs) that explicitly model capability assignment, inter-robot dependen-cies and handoff checks. I. I NTRODUCTION 

Heterogeneous robot teams combine complementary ca-pabilities in perception, mobility and manipulation, enabling long-horizon tasks which are difficult for a single embodi-ment to complete. Such tasks, however, require much more than assigning individual skills to different robots. Robots operate across different locations and stages, and the outcome of one robot may determine whether another can proceed. Recent large-language-model-based agentic systems provide a promising abstraction for this setting by connecting high-arXiv:2610.23098v1 [cs.RO] 16 Oct 2026 level reasoning, heterogeneous skill invocation, multimodal perception, and execution feedback in a closed loop. This makes them particularly suitable for long-horizon heteroge-neous collaboration, where task procedures cannot always be fully specified in advance. Despite this promise, three challenges remain. First, com-plex physical tasks rarely succeed from an initial plan alone, yet execution corrections are often task-specific rather than accumulated as reusable executable experience. Existing work has explored reflection, skill reuse, and feedback-driven adaptation [1]–[4], but collaboration procedures corrected during execution are not always retained in a form that later tasks can inherit and further improve. Second, long-horizon collaboration requires persistent spatio-temporal context be-yond current local observations, which helps heterogeneous robots plan and act accordingly. Existing memory systems support spatial and temporal retrieval [5]–[8], but historical observations must still be connected to current physical checks before inter-robot handoffs. Third, treating hetero-geneous robots as a flat collection of callable skills under-specifies collaboration structure. Reliable execution requires explicit inter-robot dependencies, handoff conditions, and recovery logic, rather than isolated skill invocation alone [9], [10]. To address the aforementioned challenges, we present 

InstAgent , a recursively self-improving Agent Operating System designed to enable instant adaptation and deployment of heterogeneous robot teams across diverse and complex scenarios. As shown in Fig. 1, InstAgent represents collabo-ration as an executable behavior-tree (BT) program that can be grounded in shared environmental context, verified during physical execution, and improved through accumulated expe-rience. Its BT-based recursive self-improvement (RSI) revises failed collaboration structures from execution feedback and retains validated revisions for inheritance and further adap-tation. Shared Multimodal Spatio-Temporal Memory with Agent-Driven Retrieval (SMAR-T) provides task-relevant observations with spatial, temporal, and source information, while current observations verify physical handoff condi-tions. InstAgent further composes heterogeneous capabilities into BTs that explicitly encode inter-robot dependencies, handoff conditions, and recovery branches. Together, these components form a closed loop of composition, execution, verification, revision, and reuse. We evaluate InstAgent on real-world embodiments across 20 heterogeneous multi-robot tasks and three high-level models. Compared with BT execution without cross-task pro-cedural experience, feedback-updated experience improves task success by 15–25 percentage points across the three models, while increasing first-attempt success and reducing recoveries. In the meanwhile, RAVEN-QA independently evaluates task-relevant environmental observation retrieval. Our main contributions are:

A. Physical Agents and Embodied Execution 

Physical-agent systems connect language reasoning with physical execution through programs, robot interfaces, and environmental feedback. Code as Policies [11] generates robot programs that compose perception and control APIs. Inner Monologue [12] uses execution feedback for closed-loop planning. ROS-LLM [13] integrates language mod-els with ROS for multiple execution representations, skill extension, and feedback-driven reflection. RoboClaw [14] uses a VLM to orchestrate learned policy primitives. Re-cent AgentOS frameworks further combine spatial memory, heterogeneous capability scheduling, and runtime manage-ment: HoloAgent-0 [7] supports 3D spatial memory and cross-robot execution, ABot-Claw [8] provides shared cross-embodiment memory and feedback-driven correction, and ABot-AgentOS [15] stores environmental and task context in a source-grounded multimodal graph. These systems sup-port cross-robot coordination and feedback-driven recovery. Table I summarizes the capabilities of representative agentic robotic systems, including collaboration, spatial memory, and the two forms of RSI considered in this work. Despite these advances, mechanisms for turning collaboration failures into reusable handoff checks and recovery rules remain under-specified. InstAgent combines shared observations with col-laboration BTs to revise inter-robot dependencies and hand-off conditions using execution feedback and retain effective revisions for subsequent tasks. 

B. Heterogeneous Multi-Robot Collaboration 

LLM-based multi-robot systems organize joint actions through dialogue negotiation or hierarchical task allocation. RoCo [16] negotiates task plans through inter-robot dialogue and refines execution using motion planning and environ-mental feedback. Chen et al. [17] further compare central-ized, decentralized, and hybrid communication architectures in terms of task success and communication efficiency. These approaches support flexible and interpretable coordination, although communication and context requirements increase with team size and task horizon. Hierarchical methods sepa-rate global coordination from local execution [18]. COHER-ENT [9], for example, uses a centralized task assigner to match heterogeneous capabilities and adjusts plans through feedback from robot executors. Executing the allocated tasks also requires organizing action dependencies and recovery procedures. In robotics, behavior trees (BTs) provide a control structure for this purpose [19], [20], combining reactivity and modularity with ease of modification and interpretability [21], [22]. LLM-HBT [10] integrates this structure with task allocation, using condition checks, local subtree extension, and inter-robot task reassignment to recover from failures. These methods im-prove coordination and runtime adaptation, but leave under-specified how corrected handoff conditions and recovery de-pendencies are validated, persistently retained, and inherited for further revision across tasks. InstAgent retains validated handoff checks and recovery logic as reusable collaboration subtrees, enabling subsequent tasks to inherit and further improve effective revisions made during execution. 

C. RSI and Executable Experience 

Existing embodied-agent research has explored how exe-cution feedback can be converted into reusable experience. Reflexion [1] stores feedback as verbal reflections and rep-resents Experience-Context RSI, whereas Voyager [2] and LRLL [3] convert experience into retrievable, composable, and executable skills, representing Structured-Program RSI. However, these methods primarily target a single agent or single-robot skills and do not represent role bindings, hand-off conditions, or recovery dependencies in heterogeneous teams. In the context of physical AgentOS systems, PhyAgen-tOS [4] organizes execution, verification, diagnosis, revi-sion, re-verification, and experience consolidation into aself-evolution loop. ABot-AgentOS [15] uses failure-driven self-evolution to improve memory writing, retrieval, and evidence-selection policies. These systems primarily repre-sent Experience-Context RSI, rather than directly revising heterogeneous multi-robot collaboration programs. Experience-Context RSI is flexible, but translating experi-ence into concrete execution still depends on the Agent’s current reasoning and does not inherently preserve sta-ble execution orders and explicit dependencies. Structured-Program RSI retains conditions, execution orders, and re-covery branches in the program structure, making it better suited to repeated or structurally similar collaboration tasks. InstAgent combines both forms of RSI: Experience-Context RSI uses experience documents, failure feedback, and shared observations to guide revisions, while Structured-Program RSI stores validated revisions as collaboration BT subtrees for subsequent inheritance, rebinding, and improvement. System Architecture Overview. InstAgent is a ROS 2-based agentic OS that coordinates heterogeneous robots through BTs. At the lowest level, capabilities are exposed as atomic skills, each wrapped as a minimal Skill BT (see Sec. III-A). Guided by task goals, observations, and retrieved experience, the Agent uses HeteroComposer (see Sec. III-B) to combine these Skill BTs into a collaboration BT that specifies cross-robot dependencies, handoff conditions, and recovery branches. The Coordinator binds its action nodes to available skills, while the Executive executes the tree and returns feedback. Collab-RSI (see Sec. III-C) is the program-level recursive self-improvement mechanism for collaboration BTs. The Agent uses execution feedback to revise the tree, which resumes execution after passing the required checks. After successful execution and goal verification, the BT is saved as a reusable subtree in the experience library, with apaired document recording its applicability and invocation conditions. Later tasks inherit and adapt these subtrees, writing further validated revisions back for reuse. SMAR-T (see Sec. III-D) supports planning and failure diagnosis by retrieving shared robot observations according to semantic content, location, and time. 

A. Skill BTs: From Atomic Skills to Executable Trees 

Atomic skills in InstAgent are provided by shared modules and robot modules. Shared modules provide navigation and perception capabilities, while robot modules expose motion, speech interaction, and manipulation capabilities specific to each robot. For executable atomic skills, we represent a con-crete skill invocation as a minimal behavior tree containing a single Action node, termed a Skill BT , which serves as a basic unit for subsequent task composition. The single-

Action tree is the minimal atomic instance. The Skill BTs category in Fig. 2(a) also includes reusable capability procedures composed from these atomic instances. To support skill understanding and selection, the mul-timodal large language model (MLLM) reads functional descriptions, interface definitions, and documented invoca-tion contexts, calling procedures, and usage conditions, and converts them into the common skill representation used by the RSI library. Each experience records the skill’s purpose, parameter meanings, and usage constraints, maintaining con-sistency with the registered interface. The system constructs the minimal Skill BT through a deterministic wrapper: the 

skill field identifies the interface as module/skill , and 

args carries the invocation arguments. The resulting tree is stored and linked to its corresponding experience. Before execution, the system checks the BT format, interface exis-tence, and argument consistency with the interface schema. Arguments requiring runtime resolution are checked against the schema once resolved. During task planning, the Agent selects Skill BTs based on task requirements and skill experiences, and supplies the arguments required by the current task. The selected Skill BTs serve as basic subtrees for constructing the complete task BT; subtree composition and dependencies across robots are detailed in the next subsection.

C. Collab-RSI: Experience-Guided BT Evolution for Hetero-geneous Collaboration 

Collab-RSI enables program-level recursive self-improvement in InstAgent by revising inherited collaboration procedures and their composition using execution feedback, and retaining validated changes for subsequent tasks. When a collaborative BT constructed by HeteroComposer encounters a failed handoff, an unmet execution dependency, or an insufficient recovery procedure, the Agent identifies the affected coordination steps using the current program, task goal, observations, and related experience. Revisions may modify the internal structure of a reused subtree, such as by adding an outcome check, or modify the relationships among subtrees by adjusting action assignments, handoff conditions, and recovery paths across robots. These revisions address whether the outcome produced by one robot satisfies the conditions required by a subsequent robot. After structural and skill-interface checks, the candidate program is re-executed to verify the relevant handoff condi-tions and task outcome. When cross-task experience updates are enabled, validated programs are written to the experience library as linked BT structures and experience documents. Each call may search the full mem-ory or refine a returned candidate pool, including returning to the full memory on a later call. Tool order depends on the task; the illustrated request establishes the living-room and recent-time scope before retrieving the red-sofa scene. After each call, the Agent jointly evaluates images, cap-tions, and metadata against the original constraints and updates its task context. Ranking alone does not establish constraint satisfaction. The loop succeeds when evidence supports all explicit constraints and supplies the required response fields; conflicts, missing fields, or unresolved refer-ences prompt further retrieval. Each real-robot query allows at most 10 calls across the three tools. If evidence remains insufficient and the pool is empty or the budget is exhausted, the Agent reports insufficient evidence and may request a fresh observation. Sufficient evidence supports a grounded response and, when requested, BT construction or revision. Success criteria and recovery budgets. Perturbations in-clude excessive arrival error, navigation and loading failures, and user-requested destination changes. Relative movement and turning must be within 0.10 m and 10 ◦; workstation navigation requires a successful module return and human confirmation of arrival within 0.5 m. Success requires all physical and interaction postconditions to be verified. Each task permits at most five executed failure-triggered recoveries or retries; a goal change alone consumes no retry. For BT conditions, each failure episode additionally permits three candidate revisions, with the counter reset per episode. Re-jected candidates consume candidate attempts, not executed-recovery budget. An incomplete run fails when the recovery budget is exhausted or, if BT revision is required, all three candidates are rejected or safe stopping cannot be confirmed (Algorithm 1). 

Configurations and experience control. Simple CoT in-vokes Skills from a textual plan without a BT, under the same tasks, success criteria, and recovery budget. BT Recovery (E0) builds BTs from registered atomic Skills without cross-task subtree retrieval. Fixed-Experience BT (E1) additionally retrieves existing subtrees while preserving their executable versions. RSI-Updated Experience BT ( E2,full InstAgent) starts from the same base subtrees as E1,but uses matched historical feedback and case availability to revise, validate, and retain them before evaluation. All BT conditions allow the same failure recovery, task-local structural revision, and retries under identical budgets. Each model has its own procedural library; the E1 and E2

snapshots are prepared before evaluation and frozen during measured runs. Task-local repairs are not written back, so one run cannot update another’s library. Evaluation tasks are excluded from library construction. Tasks, models, prompts, perturbations, and recovery budgets are shared across BT conditions; E1 and E2 also use the same retrieval procedure. Thus, E0–E1 tests experience reuse, whereas E1–E2 tests retention of validated revisions. Model parameters, atomic Skills, low-level controllers, VLA/VLM components, and task specifications remain fixed. 

Metrics. Task success rate (SR) measures complete task success. First-attempt success rate (FSR) requires success without failure-triggered recovery or retry; a user-requested goal change alone does not count as a retry. Initial-BT logical coherence (LC) follows the precondition-respecting execution-order criterion of [26] for action, stage, and hand-off dependencies. Alternative valid orders are accepted, re-paired trees are excluded, and LC does not apply to Simple CoT. Mean recovery count ( ¯ R) averages failure-triggered recoveries and retries over all runs, including ultimately unsuccessful ones.

C. RAVEN-QA Memory Evaluation 

The methods differ in how they represent and select visual experience. RAVEN directly indexes original observations with multimodal visual embeddings, preserving image details but using no language description as a second ranking signal [6]. ReMEmbR retrieves caption-based memories [5], making semantics explicit but losing details omitted by captioning. Our method combines their complementary sig-nals: visual similarity first forms a high-recall candidate set, captions refine its order, and the Agent verifies the selected captions against their original images. Language thus assists selection without replacing visual evidence. Our method reaches 93.14% overall, exceeding RAVEN by 8.57 points and ReMEmbR by 28.65 points. Its largest gains over RAVEN occur on RealWorld (6.19 points) and Habitat-Sim (13.32 points), consistent with caption reranking helping distinguish visually similar scenes by object identity, attributes, or role. IRS leaves only a 1.85-point margin because direct visual retrieval is already strong. The near tie on IRS-hard (82.93% versus 82.90%) exposes the method’s boundary: reranking cannot recover targets that are absent, occluded, or missed by the visual candidate set. Returning Top-1 without the Agent reduces overall SR from 93.14% to 69.31%, with the largest drops on Habitat-Sim and IRS-hard. The Agent is a unified retrieval controller that performs candidate comparison, position and time tool calls, evidence interpretation, and caption verification. Thus, this 23.83-percentage-point gap measures the overall con-tribution of the integrated Agent-driven retrieval workflow rather than the contribution of any individual component. The smaller IRS drop is consistent with its more direct queries, for which Top-1 is more often sufficient. V. C ONCLUSION 

InstAgent connects capability binding, shared context, and physical execution through an agent operating system with BT-based RSI. Linked documents and executable subtrees retain feedback-driven changes to collaboration programs, allowing inherited procedures to be composed and revised again. Real-robot evaluation across three models shows higher task and first-attempt success with feedback-updated experience than with cold starts on the evaluated tasks. The service case illustrates how a retained BT condition changes the handoff between loading and transport. Future work will examine transfer to new task compositions and robot teams, and the reliability of experience updates over longer deployment periods. ACKNOWLEDGMENTS
