Introduction
Human-Object Interaction (HOI) synthesis refers to generating plau-
sible and dynamic movements of both humans and manipulable
objects, which plays a crucial role of human behavior analysis deeply
embedded in daily activities. HOI presents increasing attention in
various human-centric applications such as Virtual/Augmented Re-
ality (VR/AR) [Guzov et al. 2024], Embodied AI (EAI), robotics [Chen
et al. 2025; Pan et al. 2022], and avatar animation [Cao et al. 2024;
Kim et al. 2016]. In recent years, numerous works have been con-
ducted to address this challenging task.
Pioneering works in HOI usually focus on generating human
interactions with individual objects, conditioned on object trajecto-
ries [Li et al. 2023; Zhang et al. 2025] or textual descriptions [Li et al.
2024a; Xue et al. 2025; Zeng et al. 2025]. Meanwhile, to enhance
physical plausibility, a body of studies actively incorporates contact
information to impose explicit constraints on interaction [Cong
et al. 2025; Peng et al. 2025; Song et al. 2024]. Only few researchers
attempt to explore multi-object scenarios by imposing geometric dis-
tance constraints [Lv et al. 2024] or trajectory synchronization [He
et al. 2025], which treat the entity relations as implicit kinematic
byproducts primarily. However, these approaches ignore explicit
contact constraints implied by interaction semantics. This limitation
is critical in multi-object scenarios, where objects exposes distinct
affordance regions corresponding to different interacting partners.
For instance, given the instruction “pick up a kettle and pour
the water into the cup": the motion requires maintaining phys-
ical contact with the kettle’s handle (human-object) while simulta-
neously ensuring precise spatial alignment of the spout with the
cup (object-object), as illustrated in Figure 1. Individual prediction
of human-object or object-object affordances may generate local
plausibility, but cannot ensure global alignment between multiple
entities. In addition, the spatial inconsistency draws an undesirable
impact on temporally synchronized and physically aligned motion.
Thus, the unified and aligned interaction relations display indis-
pensable roles in HOI generation. This highlights two main chal-
lenges: 1) The difficulty of uniformly modeling diverse interactions,
as human-object and object-object relations involve different con-
tact types. 2) The challenge of generating synchronized motions
that keep all entities physically aligned over time.
To address these challenges, we propose UniffordInteractor, a
Reasoning-then-Interacting framework that leverages unified af-
fordance priors to enable realistic and long-horizon human and
multi-object interaction generation. Inspired by the recent success
of 3D Multimodal Large Language Models (3D-MLLMs) in under-
standing and grounding spatial geometries [Chu et al. 2025; Qi et al.
2024; Yu et al. 2025], at the Reasoning stage, we introduce the Unified
Affordance Reasoner (UAR) to perform explicit affordance reasoning
via a pre-trained 3D-MLLMs. Different from previous works [Chu
et al. 2025; Li et al. 2024b] that just focus on isolated affordance for
a single object, we extend the affordance grounding task to human
and multi-object interaction scenarios. Specifically, our framework
could simultaneously predict the identity of the interaction target
and a matched pair of affordance regions on the interacting objects.
To support this, we construct a dataset of over 13k interaction pairs.
Crucially, this dataset unifies Human-Object and Object-Object in-
teractions into a standard format, annotating the interaction target,
functional roles, and their corresponding affordance.
To synthesize coherent long-horizon interactions, we propose a
Hierarchical Interaction Generator (HIG) in the Interacting stage,
building upon an autoregressive transformer framework. Here, to
address the disparity between global dynamics and fine-grained
manipulation, we employ hierarchical decoupled quantization to
disentangle motion into root, body, and hand levels. This strategy
effectively mitigates the interference between distinct motion scales,
enabling the model to capture precise hand-object contacts with-
out sacrificing whole-body coordination. Specifically, we introduce
an Object-Centric Interaction Adapter (OCIA), which fuses object
geometry with unified affordance priors into geometric tokens as
a prefix condition for the autoregressive generator. Driven by this
prefix and leveraging the decoupled representation, we implement
a hierarchical autoregressive strategy that generates tokens in a
sequence of root→body→hand→objects. This structured genera-
tion flow ensures that the interaction is spatially anchored by the
affordance priors, while intrinsically modeling the causal chain of
interaction through a coarse-to-fine paradigm.
Overall, our contributions are summarized as follows:
• We propose UniffordInteractor, a Reasoning-then-Interacting frame-
work that leverages unified affordance priors to enable realistic,
kinematically coherent, and long-horizon human and multi-object
interaction generation.
• We introduce the Unified Affordance Reasoner (UAR), which real-
izes a novel unified affordance reasoning strategy with the help
of fine-tuned 3D-MLLMs.
• We design the Hierarchical Interaction Generator (HIG), which
formulates the human and multi-object generation as a hierarchi-
cal autoregressive prediction, conditioned on the unified affor-
dance.
• Extensive experiments show that our method outperforms state-
of-the-art counterparts, displaying high-fidelity and geometrically
consistent interactive motions in complex multi-object scenarios.

Related Work
Human-Object Interaction Generation: Early works [Hassan
et al. 2021; Starke et al. 2019; Zhang et al. 2022] focus on generating
interactions with static objects through a limited set of interaction
modes, like sitting and lying. Others [Li et al. 2023; Zhang et al. 2025]
conditioned human motion on given object trajectories, failing to
jointly synthesize both motions. InterDiff [Xu et al. 2023] firstly pro-
poses a diffusion-based framework to predict whole-body interac-
tions with dynamic objects conditioned on a historical sequence. To
generate more controllable interaction, a series of works [Cong et al.
2025; Diller and Dai 2024; Li et al. 2024a; Peng et al. 2025; Song et al.
2024; Xue et al. 2025; Zeng et al. 2025] leverage textual descriptions
to guide human–object interaction. Among them, HOI-Diff [Peng
et al. 2025] enforces contact at predicted keypoints via a guidance

Affordance Learning: Affordance learning initially focuses on
2D images [Chuang et al. 2018; Do et al. 2018], aiming to detect
object-level affordance regions. Subsequent research [Fang et al.
2018; Luo et al. 2022; Nagarajan et al. 2019] leaps to model affor-
dances at the part-level, allowing more accurate identification of
functional regions. Driven by embodied AI, research shift to 3D
environments. 3D AffordanceNet [Deng et al. 2021] introduced the
first point cloud benchmark based on PartNet [Mo et al. 2019]. Build-
ing on this, some researchers [Yang et al. 2023] attempt to transfer
interaction priors from 2D HOI priors to 3D segmentation. Neverthe-
less, these approaches typically infer affordances purely from visual
information, overlooking the semantic dimension. LASO [Li et al.
2024b] proposed language-conditioned segmentation to ground tex-
tual intent, while 3D AffordanceLLM [Chu et al. 2025] leveraged
LLMs for open-vocabulary reasoning. Yet, these approaches are
largely confined to isolated objects. While SeqAfford [Yu et al. 2025]
attempts to reason multi-object affordance, it independently pre-
dicts every object affordance in a sequential manner. Our framework
jointly identifies the interaction counterpart and predicts a matched
pair of affordance regions on the interacting objects.

Data Representation. We leverage the SMPL-X [Pavlakos et al.
2019] parametric model to represent the human motion, where the
human state in each frame is defined over 52 joints, consists of
3D joint positions, continuous 6D joint rotations, and global root
translation. Meanwhile, for each object, the state is defined by global
6D rotation, 3D translation, and 2D object articulation parameters.
And for rigid objects, the articulation term is omitted. Specifically,
we instantiate each object geometry 𝐺𝑘∈G as a sampled point
cloud 𝑃∈R2048×3, which serves as one input for both affordance
reasoning and interaction generation.
3.2
Unified Affordance Reasoning
A critical limitation in human and multi-object interaction lies in
the isolated treatment of human manipulation, which overlooks
the mutual constraints between interacting objects. To address this,
we introduce Unified Affordance (Unifford), defined as the set of
spatial interaction regions on a source object, corresponding to the
interacting entities within a specific interaction task. For Human-
Object interactions, the object is always designated as the source
by definition, as the affordance characterizes a functional region
on the object surface. For Object-Object interactions, the source is
the object that initiates the interaction and the target is the inter-
acting counterpart. For example, in contain, the apple initiates the
containment interaction with the bowl, so the apple is the source
and the bowl is the target. Crucially, this Unifford representation
unifies diverse interaction types, which seamlessly integrates the
human manipulation region and the aligned object-object geometric
regions into a consistent spatial format.
Specifically, to disentangle specific spatial contact constraints
from complex task instructions, we propose the Unified Affor-
dance Reasoner (UAR), which leverages a fine-tuned 3D-MLLM
to perform explicit affordance reasoning. We introduce a newly
designed token <AFF> into the original LLM vocabulary, that endow
the affordance prediction capability into the LLM. Formally, given a
set of point clouds and a text instruction consisting of motion instruc-
tion and task instruction, the model is trained to predict an inter-
leaved identity-affordance sequence Y = ⟨𝐼1, <AFF>1, 𝐼2, <AFF>2, . . .⟩,
where each <AFF>𝑘decodes the affordance region 𝐴𝑘correspond-

ShapeLLM [Qi et al. 2024] as our backbone, comprising a 3D vision
encoder ReCon++ [Qi et al. 2024] to capture fine-grained object

3.3
InterAfford Dataset Construction
To support the relational reasoning and dynamic multi-object manip-
ulation scenarios mentioned above, we constructed an instruction-
tuning dataset derived from high-fidelity interaction sequences [Kim
et al. 2025; Lv et al. 2024], dubbed InterAfford. This dataset provides
the necessary semantic-geometric grounding by unifying Human-
Object and Object-Object interactions into a consistent format. The
construction pipeline consists of the following key stages:
Hybrid Affordance Annotation. To capture diverse interac-
tion types, we employ a hybrid strategy for generating dense 3D
affordance maps. For contact-based interactions, we calculate the
vertex-level distances between the human and the object. A vertex
is marked as contacting when the distance is below 2 cm. We accu-
mulate the binary contact indicators over the interaction interval
and normalize by the number of valid frames to obtain a per-vertex
contact-frequency map. For non-contact interactions such as pour-
ing, we use the pre-trained LASO model [Li et al. 2024b] to generate
pseudo-affordance maps. Annotators inspect the predictions with a
point-cloud annotation tool, remove incorrect regions, and refine
missing or inaccurate functional regions.
Semantic Parsing and Augmentation. For semantic process-
ing, we leveraged a Large Language Model (LLM) to parse raw

3.4
Hierarchical Interaction Generator
Given the structured interaction cues from UAR, the subsequent
goal is to synthesize kinematically coherent and temporally synchro-
nized interactions. As shown in Fig. 5, we introduce the Hierarchical
Interaction Generator (HIG), which consists of two stages: Hierar-
chical Decoupled Quantization (Sec. 3.4.1) discretizes continuous
motion into multi-scale tokens, and Hierarchical Autoregressive
Modeling (Sec. 3.4.2) synthesizes interactions via a coarse-to-fine
autoregressive process.
3.4.1
Hierarchical Decoupled Quantization. Modeling human-object
interactions via a holistic representation is often suboptimal due to
the fundamental structural disparities: human motion follows a com-
plex skeletal topology, whereas objects exhibit diverse kinematic
structures. To address this, we propose Hierarchical Decoupled
Quantization, a dual-stream framework illustrated in Figure 5 (top).
For the human branch, to prevent large-scale global dynamics from
overshadowing fine-grained hand details, we explicitly decompose
motion into Root, Body, and Hand levels, encoding each into a dedi-
cated codebook. In parallel, the object branch adopts an instance-
level encoding strategy that augments temporal object states with
explicit geometric information. Specifically, a Geometry Encoder
utilizing PointNet++ [Qi et al. 2017] extracts geometry-aware key-
point representations from object point clouds. To enhance spatial
expressiveness, we apply a high-frequency spatial encoding to the
3D keypoint coordinates, allowing the representation to capture
fine-grained geometric variations before fusing it with rigid motion
features.

et al. 2023] to discretize these decoupled streams. Instead of nearest-
neighbor lookup in an explicit codebook, FSQ constructs an implicit
combinatorial codebook by bounding the continuous latent vector

Besides, ParaHome dataset captures human activities in a realistic
household environment, which features sequential multi-object ma-
nipulations covering both rigid and articulated objects. We segment
these long-term, multi-stage activities into atomic interaction clips
based on temporal annotations. Subsequently, for each interaction
clip, we retain the relevant objects involved in the interaction and
standardize the scene by transforming all spatial coordinates to be
relative to the human’s root pose at the first frame. In total, the
processed dataset consists of 5,476 clips where the training set com-
prises 31 subjects and the test set includes the remaining 7 subjects.
Implementation Details: We use K=3 object slots in all exper-
iments based on our dataset’s scenarios. Longer scenes involving
more than three objects are decomposed into temporally ordered
subsequences, which are generated autoregressively. In the rea-
soning stage, we adopt ShapeLLM-7B as the 3D MLLM backbone,
freezing the pre-trained vision encoder. Here, LoRA [Hu et al. 2022]
is employed for efficient fine-tuning with the rank set to 8 and the
alpha set to 16. For the hierarchical quantization, we adopt FSQ and
crop all motion sequences to 𝑁= 64 frames during training. The
model encodes the motion into four distinct codebooks correspond-
ing to the root, body, hand, and objects, with hidden dimensions
configured as 128, 512, 512, and 128, respectively. The codebook
sizes were set to 𝑁𝑟𝑜𝑜𝑡= 512 and 𝑁𝑏𝑜𝑑𝑦/ℎ𝑎𝑛𝑑/𝑜𝑏𝑗𝑒𝑐𝑡𝑠= 4096. The
initial learning rate is set to 1e-4 with a batch size of 128. We set
{𝜆𝑠𝑎, 𝜆𝑑𝑖𝑠𝑡, and 𝜆𝑝𝑒𝑛} = {1.0, 1.0, 0.5}. The autoregressive transformer
is built upon LLaMA-based architecture with approximately 775M
parameters. We utilize a frozen T5-XL model as the text encoder
to process the textual descriptions. To mitigate exposure bias, we
employ token-level scheduled sampling, where the probability 𝜖
of substituting ground-truth tokens with model predictions is an-
nealed from 0.0 (teacher forcing) to 1.0 (pure autoregressive) via a
cosine schedule. During inference, our full pipeline, including uni-
fied affordance reasoning and hierarchical interaction generation,
takes approximately 8.27 seconds per sample. All the experiments
are conducted on 4 NVIDIA A100 GPUs.

Evaluation Metrics. To comprehensively assess the capabili-

distinct perspectives: Motion Quality, Interaction Quality, and Di-

• Motion Quality: Fréchet Inception Distance (FID) measures

simultaneously.
We selected 30 text prompts, and for each prompt, all five methods
generated one motion sequence. Each trial compared the result of
our method against one baseline under the same prompt. Each
participant evaluated 20 randomly sampled trials, with balanced
coverage across the four baselines, resulting in 360 comparison trials
in total and approximately 90 comparisons per baseline.
Specifically, participants evaluated the motion pairs along three
dimensions: (1) Motion Quality, assessing the naturalness and
smoothness of the motion itself; (2) Interaction Quality, focus-
ing on the geometric consistency of human–object interactions,
including contact consistency and penetration artifacts; and (3)
Text Alignment, measuring how well the motion matches the se-
mantic content of the text description. The order of motion pairs
and the left/right positions of different methods were randomized
to avoid presentation bias. Participants were required to make a
forced choice for each evaluation dimension. As shown in Figure 11,
participants consistently preferred UniffordInteractor over all base-
line methods across motion quality, interaction quality, and text
alignment. The preference trends indicate that our method produces
more natural and smoother motions, more kinematically coherent
human–object interactions, and motions that better align with the
input text descriptions, demonstrating overall improvements in per-
ceptual quality.
5
Conclusion
In this paper, we propose UniffordInteractor, a novel framework
designed to generate realistic long-horizon human and multi-object
interaction sequences. To overcome the limitations of isolated inter-
action modeling, we design the Unified Affordance to explicitly unify
Human-Object and Object-Object relations. To this end, we establish
a Reasoning-then-Interacting paradigm in which the Unified Affor-
dance Reasoner infers global spatial priors to guide the Hierarchical
Interaction Generator. In this fashion, the generated interactions
are semantically consistent and kinematically coherent. Extensive
experiments demonstrate that our framework outperforms SOTA
counterparts with superior fidelity and geometric consistency. In
the future, we will pay more attention to long-term human and
multi-object interaction, and complex human-scene interaction.
