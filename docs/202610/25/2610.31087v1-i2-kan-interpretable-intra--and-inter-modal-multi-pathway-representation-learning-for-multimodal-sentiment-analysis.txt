# I2-KAN: Interpretable Intra- and Inter-Modal Multi-Pathway Representation Learning for Multimodal Sentiment Analysis 

Xiaoting Xie, Feng Qiao, Xianwei Zhang, Hailong Chen, Kaiwen Zheng, Joemon M. Jose, Xuri Ge †, Yujun Li †

Abstract —Multimodal sentiment analysis (MSA) aims to infer sentiment by integrating linguistic, acoustic, and visual informa-tion. However, many fusion methods integrate modality-specific representations and cross-modal interactions into joint represen-tations, making it difficult to determine whether predictive utility arises from a modality itself or from its interactions with others. More fundamentally, a modality’s standalone predictive utility may not faithfully reflect its interaction-conditioned predictive utility. To address this issue, we propose I 2-KAN, an interpretable multi-pathway framework that explicitly maintains intra-modal and pairwise inter-modal information in separate structural path-ways. Each pathway is refined by a Task-Decoupled Linear-Gated Denoising Bottleneck (TDL-GDB) to learn compact and task-relevant representations, followed by structured Kolmogorov– Arnold Network fusion that retains explicit pathway associations and supports multi-level interpretation. Extensive experiments on CMU-MOSI, CMU-MOSEI, and CH-SIMS v2 demonstrate strong overall performance. Ablation and interpretability anal-yses consistently show across the three benchmarks that low standalone predictive utility does not necessarily imply low interaction-conditioned predictive utility: a modality that appears weak in isolation can remain informative through pairwise interactions. These findings highlight the interaction-dependent nature of multimodal utility and suggest that modality utility should be characterized from both standalone and interaction-conditioned perspectives. 

Index Terms —Multimodal sentiment analysis, multimodal rep-resentation learning, cross-modal interaction, interpretable ma-chine learning, Kolmogorov–Arnold networks.

Standalone predictive utility does not necessarily reflect interaction-conditioned predictive utility: a modality that exhibits limited utility in isolation can still provide substantial predictive utility through pairwise interactions. By explicitly maintaining intra-modal and pairwise inter-modal information within a multi-pathway structure, I 2-KAN supports three complementary levels of interpretation: where predictive sensitivity is concentrated across pathways, what compact features exhibit higher sensitivity within each pathway, and how those features are nonlinearly transformed toward the final decision. 

These modalities provide complementary affective cues, mak-ing their joint modeling important for understanding mul-timodal sentiment [1]. Owing to its relevance to human– computer interaction [2], social media understanding [3], conversational agents [4], and human-centered artificial in-telligence [5], MSA has become an important problem in multimodal learning. A substantial body of research has investigated how to represent and fuse heterogeneous modalities. Early tensor-based methods explicitly modeled high-order multimodal in-teractions [6], [7], while Transformer-based approaches cap-tured long-range cross-modal dependencies through attention mechanisms [8]. Later studies further explored modality-specific representation learning, auxiliary supervision, mutual-information maximization, and interaction-aware optimization arXiv:2610.31087v1 [cs.CV] 22 Oct 2026 2

[9]–[13]. Despite this progress, cross-modal relations are often used as intermediate representations during prediction, making it difficult to separately trace the roles of standalone modalities and pairwise interactions within a unified decision structure. A key issue is that a modality’s standalone predictive utility does not necessarily reflect its utility when interacting with other modalities. As illustrated in Fig. 1, the acoustic modality may exhibit limited standalone predictive utility, whereas the Text–Audio and Visual–Audio pathways can exhibit substantially greater predictive utility in multimodal contexts. Thus, a modality that appears weak in isolation may still provide useful information through its interactions with other modalities. Evaluating only the original modalities may therefore provide an incomplete characterization of multi-modal utility. Meanwhile, existing interpretability approaches mainly provide modality-level importance or analyze nonlinear fusion behavior [14], [15], making it difficult to jointly trace standalone and pairwise information sources throughout the decision process. More importantly, this discrepancy raises an interpretability requirement: a multimodal model should not only characterize the utility of different information sources, but also reveal where predictive sensitivity is concentrated, what compact features the prediction is sensitive to within each pathway, and how those features are transformed toward the final decision. To address these issues, we propose I 2-KAN, an inter-pretable intra- and inter-modal multi-pathway framework that explicitly maintains standalone and pairwise inter-modal in-formation as separate structural pathways. I 2-KAN consists of four stages. Modality-specific encoders first extract textual, acoustic, and visual representations, which are organized into a structured multi-pathway representation. A Task-Decoupled Linear-Gated Denoising Bottleneck (TDL-GDB) then inde-pendently refines each pathway through denoising compres-sion, information-preserving shortcuts, and task-specific gated projections. Finally, the compact pathway representations are fused through a structured KAN [16]. By retaining explicit pathway associations throughout fusion, the framework sup-ports pathway-level analysis of where predictive sensitivity is concentrated, compressed-feature analysis of what compact features the prediction is most sensitive to, and functional-edge analysis of how those features are nonlinearly transformed toward the final prediction. Extensive experiments on CMU-MOSI, CMU-MOSEI, and CH-SIMS v2 demonstrate competitive performance across different sentiment evaluation settings. Interaction-pathway and component-wise ablations validate the effectiveness of the multi-pathway representation, TDL-GDB, and KAN-based fusion. Multi-level analyses consistently show that low stan-dalone predictive utility does not necessarily imply low interaction-conditioned predictive utility, supporting a more comprehensive characterization of modality utility beyond isolated modalities, while the structured multi-pathway design provides a traceable view of where, what, and how multimodal information participates in the learned decision process. The main contributions can be summarized as follows:  

> •

We show across three benchmarks that standalone predic-tive utility and interaction-conditioned predictive utility can differ substantially. Motivated by this observation, we propose a multi-pathway framework (I 2-KAN) that sep-arately maintains intra-modal and pairwise inter-modal information, enabling both forms of multimodal utility to be modeled and analyzed within a unified structure.  

> •

We design a Task-Decoupled Linear-Gated Denoising Bottleneck (TDL-GDB) to learn compact and task-relevant representations for heterogeneous pathways. By combining denoising compression, an information-preserving shortcut, and task-specific gated projections, TDL-GDB allows different sentiment objectives to selec-tively preserve complementary information before subse-quent global fusion.  

> •

We construct a structured KAN fusion mechanism that retains explicit pathway associations throughout nonlinear fusion. Together with pathway-level, compressed-feature, and functional-edge analyses, this design provides a trace-able view of how standalone and interaction-conditioned information participates in the final sentiment decision. II. R ELATED WORK 

A. Multimodal Fusion and Interaction Modeling 

Multimodal fusion is central to multimodal sentiment anal-ysis because affective information is distributed across linguis-tic, acoustic, and visual signals. Early studies mainly focused on constructing expressive joint representations. Tensor Fusion Network (TFN) explicitly modeled unimodal, bimodal, and trimodal interactions through tensor products [6], Low-rank Multimodal Fusion (LMF) reduced the computational cost of tensor-based fusion through low-rank decomposition [7], and Recurrent Multistage Fusion Network (RMFN) decom-posed cross-modal fusion into multiple recurrent stages [17]. MulT further employed directional cross-modal attention to effectively model long-range dependencies between unaligned multimodal sequences [8]. Subsequent studies increasingly emphasized modality-specific information and structured cross-modal relationships. MISA separated modality-invariant and modality-specific rep-resentations [9], Self-MM introduced unimodal auxiliary su-pervision [10], and MMIM retained task-relevant information through mutual-information maximization [11]. MTAG mod-eled temporal and cross-modal relations using a heterogeneous attention graph [18]. Related multimodal tasks have similarly explored explicit relational structures: Li et al. introduced dual-gated fusion for task-oriented intra- and inter-modal associations [19], developed an inductive framework for text-video representation alignment [20], and further modeled high-order intra- and inter-modal correlations through a variational multimodal hypergraph [21]. More recent studies have explicitly investigated intra- and inter-modal relational learning in MSA. HGraph-CL mod-els intra-modal structures using unimodal graphs and fur-ther captures inter-modal relations through hierarchical graph contrastive learning [22]. Semi-IIN independently captures intra- and inter-modal interactions through masked attention and dynamically regulates their importance using a gating mechanism under a semi-supervised learning framework [23]. 3

TEMSA employs text-guided pairwise cross-modal mapping and reconstruction to jointly model inter-modal consistency and modality-specific information [24]. More recently, I2RL progressively learns intra- and inter-modality relations through multi-scale intra-modal modeling, cross-modality shuffling, and reconstruction [25]. These studies demonstrate the impor-tance of explicitly considering different forms of multimodal relations rather than relying solely on holistic fusion. Interaction-oriented analyses have also shown that strong multimodal performance does not necessarily imply effec-tive cross-modal reasoning [26]. MRO decomposes predic-tions into unimodal, bimodal, and trimodal effects [27], while MPID [13], TriagedMSA [12], and CACR [28] fur-ther explore modality-preserving, disagreement-aware, and interaction-aware representation learning. However, these ap-proaches primarily focus on learning, selecting, or decompos-ing intra- and inter-modal relations during representation con-struction or prediction. In contrast, I 2-KAN explicitly main-tains intra-modal and pairwise inter-modal representations as separate structural pathways throughout pathway-specific refinement and nonlinear fusion. This design preserves the source association of each compact representation and enables standalone modality behavior to be examined separately from pairwise interaction behavior at the pathway, compressed-feature, and functional-edge levels. 

B. Modality Imbalance and Robust Representation Learning 

Different modalities often exhibit unequal information den-sity, noise levels, and optimization difficulty, causing mul-timodal learning to be dominated by stronger modalities. Existing approaches address this issue through auxiliary su-pervision or optimization coordination. Self-MM constructs unimodal supervision signals from multimodal labels [10], while MMPareto coordinates unimodal and multimodal gradi-ent directions through Pareto optimization [29]. More recent methods improve weak or under-optimized modalities through representation enhancement and reliability-aware optimization, including MFON [30], PaSE [31], and EBMC [32]. Robust representation learning further seeks to suppress redundant or noisy multimodal information. The text-guided hierarchical noise eliminator t-HNE reduces intra- and inter-modal noise [33], MISR learns stable sentiment representa-tions under distribution shifts and cross-modal conflicts [34], and KAN-MCP combines a denoising information bottleneck with multimodal optimization to obtain compact unimodal representations for KAN fusion [15]. However, these methods primarily regulate modalities or their joint optimization and do not explicitly consider that standalone and interaction path-ways may require different information preservation strategies. Moreover, different sentiment objectives may rely on different low-dimensional evidence. Our TDL-GDB therefore inde-pendently refines each intra-modal and interaction pathway while using task-decoupled gated projections to preserve task-relevant information before global fusion. 

C. Interpretability in Multimodal Sentiment Analysis 

As multimodal fusion networks become increasingly com-plex, understanding how different information sources affect prediction has become important. Multimodal Routing pro-vides global and instance-level modality explanations through routing weights [14], MTAG visualizes salient temporal and cross-modal graph connections [18], and MRO quantitatively decomposes unimodal and multimodal interaction effects [27]. However, such importance scores or interaction decomposi-tions do not directly reveal how individual compact features are nonlinearly transformed during fusion. Kolmogorov–Arnold Networks (KANs) offer an intrinsi-cally interpretable alternative by replacing scalar connection weights with learnable univariate functions on network edges [16]. KAN-MCP introduced KAN into MSA to visualize non-linear mappings from compressed unimodal representations to sentiment predictions [15], but its inputs remain limited to the three original modalities. In contrast, I 2-KAN preserves asso-ciations for both intra-modal and pairwise inter-modal path-ways throughout KAN fusion, enabling interpretation at the pathway, compressed-feature, and functional-edge levels and allowing the roles of standalone and interaction-conditioned information to be traced toward the final prediction. III. P ROPOSED METHOD 

In this section, we present I 2-KAN, an interpretable intra-and inter-modal multi-pathway framework for multimodal sentiment analysis, as illustrated in Fig. 2. For notational simplicity, the Text, Audio, and Visual pathways are hereafter denoted as T , A, and V , respectively, while the Text–Visual, Text–Audio, and Visual–Audio interaction pathways are de-noted as T V , T A , and V A .

A. Overall Framework 

Given a multimodal utterance, I 2-KAN organizes multi-modal affective evidence into source-traceable intra-modal and pairwise inter-modal pathways and processes them through four stages: Feature Extraction, Semantic Pathway Construc-tion, Task-Decoupled Linear-Gated Denoising Bottleneck, and Interaction-Aware KAN Fusion. Modality-specific encoders first produce textual, acoustic, and visual representations, which are organized into three intra-modal and three pairwise inter-modal pathways. Each pathway is then independently refined by TDL-GDB into a compact task-adaptive represen-tation. Finally, the six compact pathway representations are integrated by KAN in a fixed pathway order to produce the sentiment prediction while retaining explicit source-pathway associations for subsequent interpretation. The following sub-sections provide detailed formulations of these components. 

B. Feature Extraction and Semantic Pathway Construction 

Multimodal sentiment evidence can arise from both stan-dalone modalities and their pairwise relationships. To preserve these two information sources explicitly, I 2-KAN organizes modality-specific representations into separate intra-modal and pairwise inter-modal pathways rather than immediately mixing them within a joint latent representation. zt = Et(Xt), za = Ea(Xa), zv = Ev (Xv ), (1) where Et(·), Ea(·), and Ev (·) denote the textual, acoustic, and visual feature extraction functions, respectively. To explicitly preserve both standalone modality information and pairwise cross-modal information, the extracted represen-tations are organized into six architecturally designated se-mantic pathways. The three intra-modal pathways are directly defined as 

xT = zt, xA = za, xV = zv . (2) The three pairwise inter-modal pathways are constructed by concatenating the corresponding modality representations: 

xT V = [ zt; zv ], xT A = [ zt; za], xV A = [ zv ; za], (3) where [· ; ·] denotes feature concatenation. These pairwise pathways do not assume that concatenation itself constitutes cross-modal interaction; rather, they preserve paired modality sources, from which pathway-specific nonlinear interactions are subsequently learned by TDL-GDB. No additional learn-able interaction operator is introduced at this stage. Instead, each concatenated pairwise representation is subsequently pro-cessed by its own TDL-GDB, allowing modality-pair-specific information to be refined independently before global fusion. The resulting six-pathway representation is therefore com-pactly written as 

X = {xT , x A, x V , x T V , x T A , x V A }. (4) This construction preserves the structural source of each pathway throughout subsequent refinement and fusion, allow-ing standalone modality pathways and pairwise inter-modal pathways to remain distinguishable for task-specific modeling and subsequent interpretability analysis.

D. Interaction-Aware KAN Fusion 

After obtaining the refined representations of the six seman-tic pathways from TDL-GDB, I 2-KAN performs interaction-aware fusion through a structured Kolmogorov–Arnold Net-work (KAN). In conventional multimodal fusion methods, het-erogeneous modality representations are commonly projected into a shared latent space through fusion operations, where modality-specific and cross-modal interaction information is implicitly mixed [6]–[8]. Although these methods effectively capture multimodal dependencies, the structural origin of each modality and interaction pathway becomes difficult to trace during the fusion process. Therefore, I 2-KAN maintains explicit pathway assignments throughout nonlinear fusion, en-abling a more transparent analysis of how different information sources participate in the final sentiment decision. The six refined pathway representations generated by TDL-GDB are denoted as hT , hA, hV , hT V , hT A , and hV A ,where each hm ∈ Rdc denotes the compact representation of the corresponding pathway. They are concatenated in a fixed pathway order to form 

H = [ hT , h A, h V , h T V , h T A , h V A ], (20) where H ∈ R6dc . This structured formulation retains the source association of each compact representation, allowing intra-modal and pairwise inter-modal pathways to remain distinguishable during nonlinear fusion. IV. E XPERIMENTS 

A. Datasets and Evaluation Metrics 

CMU-MOSI [53]. CMU-MOSI is a widely used English multimodal sentiment analysis benchmark containing 2,199 opinion utterances extracted from 93 videos. Each utterance is annotated with a continuous sentiment score ranging from 

−3 to +3 . Following the standard split, 1,284 utterances are used for training, 229 for validation, and 686 for testing. 

CMU-MOSEI [35]. CMU-MOSEI is a large-scale En-glish multimodal sentiment dataset with sentiment annotations ranging from −3 to +3 . Although the dataset also provides emotion labels, only sentiment annotations are considered in this work. We follow the standard partition containing 16,326 training, 1,871 validation, and 4,659 test utterances. 

CH-SIMS v2 [50]. CH-SIMS v2 is a Chinese multimodal sentiment dataset collected from diverse scenarios, including films, television programs, interviews, and talk shows. Each sample is annotated with a continuous sentiment score ranging from −1 to 1. We use the official split containing 2,722 training, 647 validation, and 1,034 test samples. For CMU-MOSI and CMU-MOSEI, we report seven-class accuracy (Acc7), binary accuracy (Acc2), weighted F1 score, mean absolute error (MAE), and Pearson correlation coef-ficient (Corr). Acc7 maps continuous sentiment predictions to seven levels from −3 to +3 . Following the commonly adopted positive/negative evaluation protocol, samples with zero ground-truth sentiment are excluded when computing Acc2 and F1. For CH-SIMS v2, we report three-class accuracy (Acc3), Acc2, weighted F1, MAE, and Corr. Acc3 divides the sentiment space into negative, neutral, and positive classes using −0.2 and 0.2 as thresholds. Higher Acc7/Acc3, Acc2, F1, and Corr and lower MAE indicate better performance. 

B. Implementation Details 

All experiments are implemented in PyTorch. For CMU-MOSI and CMU-MOSEI, DeBERTa-v3-base, COVAREP, and FaceNet512 are adopted to extract textual, acoustic, and vi-sual representations, respectively. For CH-SIMS v2, Chinese BERT, OpenSMILE, and OpenFace are used for the three modalities. Temporal mean pooling is applied when necessary to obtain utterance-level representations. The compact dimen-sion of each semantic pathway is set to 3 for all datasets. The intermediate dimension of TDL-GDB is set to 1024, 518, and 1024 for CMU-MOSI, CMU-MOSEI, and CH-SIMS v2, respectively, while the number of hidden nodes in KAN is set to 4, 4, and 3. The internal functional parameterization of KAN follows the default configuration of the adopted implementation [16]. 7

C. Performance Comparison 

Baselines. We compare I 2-KAN with representative and recent methods covering multimodal fusion, representation learning, modality optimization, and interaction modeling. The compared methods and their corresponding references are summarized in Tables I and II. The best and second-best results are highlighted in bold and underlined, respectively. As shown in Table I, I 2-KAN achieves the best results across all five metrics on CMU-MOSI. In particular, it improves both classification-oriented metrics (Acc7, Acc2, and F1) and regression-oriented metrics (MAE and Corr), demonstrating consistent performance across discrete and continuous senti-ment evaluation. On CMU-MOSEI, I 2-KAN achieves the best Acc2, F1, MAE, and Corr while remaining competitive in Acc7. These results indicate that the proposed framework maintains strong performance on the substantially larger CMU-MOSEI bench-mark across both classification and regression metrics. As shown in Table II, I 2-KAN achieves the best results in Acc2, F1, MAE, and Corr on CH-SIMS v2. In particular, the improvements in Acc2 and F1 indicate a clear advantage in sentiment polarity discrimination. Overall, the results across the three benchmarks confirm the effectiveness of I 2-KAN under different languages and sentiment annotation settings.

E. Interpretability Analysis 

The structured six-pathway representation of I2-KAN enables the inference process to be examined at three complementary levels: pathway-level predictive sensitiv-ity, compressed-feature predictive sensitivity, and KAN functional-edge behavior. Since each of the six semantic pathways contributes three compressed dimensions to the KAN input, the resulting decision process remains traceable from pathway-level information sources to individual compact features and their nonlinear propagation through KAN. 

1) KAN Functional-Edge Analysis: Each KAN connection is associated with a learned univariate transformation rather than a conventional scalar weight. To characterize its data-conditioned behavior, we evaluate the variation of each edge response over the test set and normalize the resulting strength within each KAN layer. Accordingly, thicker and more opaque edges in Fig. 3 indicate stronger data-dependent functional responses. Edge colors are used only for visual distinction, and the edge strength should not be interpreted as sentiment polarity or causal contribution. As shown in Fig. 3, the learned functional responses are concentrated in a subset of nonlinear routes rather than being uniformly distributed across all connections. The input nodes correspond to the compressed dimensions of T , A, V , T V ,

T A , and V A , while the hidden nodes represent learned aggre-gation routes without predefined semantic meanings. CMU-MOSI mainly exhibits stronger hidden-to-output responses through H3 and H4, CMU-MOSEI through H2 and H4, and CH-SIMS v2 through H1 and H2. Since the models are trained independently, these hidden-node indices should not be interpreted as semantically aligned across datasets. The distinct routing patterns are consistent with the different distributions of affective evidence across the three benchmarks. CMU-MOSEI shows a stronger reliance on standalone textual information, whereas CMU-MOSI and CH-SIMS v2 exhibit more interaction-oriented structures, as further confirmed by the pathway-level analysis below. This suggests that KAN does not impose a fixed fusion topology but adapts its nonlinear aggregation routes to dataset-specific multimodal characteris-tics. The following analysis further quantifies which semantic pathways the final prediction is most sensitive to. 

2) Pathway-Level Predictive Sensitivity Analysis: To quan-tify the predictive dependence on each semantic pathway, we mask all three compressed dimensions of one pathway at a time and measure the resulting change in model output. The obtained scores are normalized across the six pathways for comparison, as shown in Fig. 4. As shown in Fig. 4, pairwise interaction pathways constitute a substantial part of the learned decision structure across all three datasets. A particularly consistent pattern appears for the acoustic modality: although the standalone A pathway exhibits low predictive sensitivity, the audio-related interaction path-ways T A and V A can exhibit considerably higher predictive-sensitivity scores. This observation across different datasets supports our central argument that low standalone predictive utility does not necessarily imply low interaction-conditioned predictive utility. The dominant pathways also differ across datasets. CMU-MOSI is strongly interaction-oriented, with T A and T V 

exhibiting the highest predictive-sensitivity scores, whereas CMU-MOSEI shows markedly higher sensitivity to the stan-dalone textual pathway, with T reaching a normalized score of 0.398 . This is consistent with the large-scale opinion-video setting of CMU-MOSEI, where explicit linguistic sentiment often provides stable direct evidence [35]. F. Ablation Study 

We conduct a leave-one-component-out study on CMU-MOSEI, the largest benchmark considered in this work, to examine the roles of pairwise interaction pathways, TDL-GDB, and KAN fusion. Each variant modifies only one major component while keeping the remaining settings unchanged. The w/o Pairwise Interactions variant removes T V , T A , and 

V A while retaining the three intra-modal pathways with TDL-10 

GDB and KAN. The w/o TDL-GDB variant retains all six pathways and KAN but replaces TDL-GDB with a standard low-dimensional bottleneck of the same output dimensionality. The w/o KAN variant retains the six pathways and TDL-GDB but replaces KAN with an MLP of comparable hidden width. The corresponding ablation results are reported in Table V. As shown in Table V, removing the pairwise interaction pathways leads to a clear performance degradation across all metrics, indicating that incorporating T V , T A , and V A is beneficial beyond the intra-modal-only configuration under the current architecture. This result is consistent with the interaction-combination study and the pathway-level interpre-tation presented above. Replacing TDL-GDB with a conventional bottleneck also degrades performance, showing that simple dimensionality reduction is insufficient for learning compact representations from heterogeneous intra-modal and interaction pathways. This comparison supports the effectiveness of task-decoupled denoising and gated information selection in TDL-GDB. Finally, replacing KAN with a comparable-width MLP results in lower performance while retaining the same six pathways and TDL-GDB representations. This suggests that the gains of I 2-KAN do not arise solely from introducing additional interaction pathways, and that KAN provides an ef-fective nonlinear fusion mechanism for the structured pathway representation while retaining the functional interpretability analyzed in Section IV-E. V. C ONCLUSION 

In this paper, we proposed I 2-KAN, an interpretable mul-timodal sentiment analysis framework that explicitly orga-nizes intra-modal and pairwise inter-modal information within a structured multi-pathway representation. TDL-GDB learns compact and task-adaptive representations for heterogeneous pathways, while structured KAN fusion retains explicit path-way associations during nonlinear sentiment prediction. Ex-periments on CMU-MOSI, CMU-MOSEI, and CH-SIMS v2 demonstrate the effectiveness of the proposed frame-work, with interaction-pathway and component-wise abla-tions further supporting the proposed architectural design. More importantly, the multi-level analyses consistently show that standalone predictive utility does not necessarily reflect interaction-conditioned predictive utility. These findings high-light the interaction-dependent nature of multimodal informa-tion and suggest that modality utility should be considered from both standalone and interaction-conditioned perspectives.
