## 摘要
本文面向远程专家应用场景，提出了一套用于多尺度非对称协作的集成系统，重点关注专家端。在该系统中，远程专家以混合现实（MR）中的外视视角，通过有形用户界面（TUI）在缩放后的桌面微缩世界（WIM）中精确定位并旋转自己的化身。在 WIM 内，专家化身与操作者处于相同尺度，并共享第一人称视角；同时，专家仍可从全局桌面视角俯瞰工厂，以支持协作。参与式设计阶段对行业专家的访谈表明，远程专家端需要能够从第一人称视角查看工厂内部，以实现充分协作。我们设计并研究了三种用于整合和控制专家化身注视方向的交互方式与可视化形式：VR（完全沉浸式，直接通过头部控制）、Head（浮动屏幕，直接通过头部控制）和 Touch（化身屏幕，通过触控板控制）。我们招募了 29 名参与者，在两项故障检测任务中评估这些方法：Same Machine，即两位协作者检查同一台机器；Twin Machine，即两位协作者分别检查位于不同地点的相同机器。VR 条件结合了沉浸感、直接头部控制和全尺寸视图，在整体表现和用户体验方面效果最佳；与此同时，桌面化身设置也因其协作支持能力而受到认可。研究结果为非对称 MR–VR 协作中的注视控制技术设计提供了参考，并揭示了未来研究面临的挑战与机遇。

## Abstract
This paper proposes an integrated setup for a multi-scale asymmetric collaboration scenario in the context of a remote-expert use case, focusing on the expert side. In this system, the remote expert (with an exocentric view in Mixed Reality (MR)) uses a Tangible User Interface (TUI) to precisely position and rotate their avatar within a scaled tabletop World-in-Miniature (WIM). Inside the WIM, the expert's avatar and the operator share the same scale and egocentric view, while the expert retains a global tabletop overview of the plant to support collaboration.   Interviews with industry experts during the participatory design phase revealed the need for an egocentric view inside the plant on the remote-expert side to enable full collaboration. We designed and studied three interaction-visualisation combinations to integrate and control the expert avatar's gaze: VR (fully immersive with direct head control), Head (floating screen with direct head control), and Touch (on-avatar screen with touchpad control).   We evaluated these methods with 29 participants across two fault-detection tasks: Same Machine, where both collaborators inspect the same machine, and Twin Machine, where they inspect identical machines in separate locations. The VR condition, which combines immersion, direct head control, and a full-scale view, yielded the strongest overall performance and user experience, while the tabletop avatar setup was well received for collaboration. Our findings inform the design of gaze-control techniques for asymmetric MR-VR collaboration and reveal challenges and opportunities for future work.
