## 摘要
社会智能 AI 的开发仍高度依赖人工标注数据，限制了社会理解模型所能获得的规模与广度。从未标注数据中提取训练信号的方法为摆脱这种依赖提供了一条路径，但社会预测缺乏数学和编程领域中可用的验证预言机。此外，情感、意图、偏好和语用意义等核心社会目标往往具有歧义。同一种行为可能支持多种合理解释，因此难以验证哪种解释得到的证据支持最充分。为应对这一挑战，我们提出基于比较性证据的强化学习（Reinforcement Learning with Comparative Evidence，RLCE），这是一种无需依据真实标注构造奖励、从未标注训练数据中学习社会理解的强化学习方法。给定一组 rollout 中的不同答案，RLCE 会构造证据测试，以识别支持某个答案而非另一个答案的可观察证据；随后根据输入样本验证这些测试，并汇总测试结果，以确定证据支持最充分的解释。随着策略生成新的答案，测试会重新生成，从而能够随策略不断演进。在涵盖情感、语用、交际意图和偏好的四个基准测试中，与七种不使用真实训练标签作为奖励的方法相比，RLCE 的表现最佳；这些方法包括共识、策略模型 LLM 评审验证、多模态协同演化和基于评分标准的奖励。相较于最强基线，性能提升最高达 18.93 个百分点。进一步分析表明，与所比较的评分标准方法相比，RLCE 的奖励变化在正确预测与错误预测之间分化得更明显；它能够推翻由策略自身导出的错误偏好；并且能够从成对测试构造、组合式测试聚合和在策略测试演进中获益。

## Abstract
Developing socially intelligent AI remains heavily dependent on human-annotated data, limiting the scale and breadth of social understanding models can acquire. Methods that derive training signals from unlabeled data offer a path beyond this dependence, but social predictions lack the verification oracles available in mathematics and coding. Moreover, core social targets such as affect, intent, preference, and pragmatic meaning are often ambiguous. The same behavior can support multiple plausible interpretations, making it difficult to verify which is best supported. To address this challenge, we introduce Reinforcement Learning with Comparative Evidence (RLCE), a reinforcement learning method that learns social understanding from unlabeled training data without constructing rewards from ground-truth annotations. Given distinct answers in a rollout group, RLCE constructs evidence tests that identify observable evidence favoring an answer over another, validates these tests against the input sample, and aggregates test outcomes to determine the best-supported interpretation. Tests are regenerated as the policy produces new answers, enabling them to evolve with the policy. Across four benchmarks spanning affect, pragmatics, communicative intent, and preference, RLCE attains the strongest performance among seven methods that use no ground-truth training labels for rewards, including consensus, policy LLM-judge verification, multimodal co-evolution, and rubric-based rewards. Gains over the strongest baseline reach up to +18.93 points. Analyses further show that RLCE exhibits a larger share of reward variation between correct and incorrect predictions than compared rubric methods, can overturn erroneous policy-derived preferences, and benefits from pairwise test construction, compositional test aggregation, and on-policy test evolution.
