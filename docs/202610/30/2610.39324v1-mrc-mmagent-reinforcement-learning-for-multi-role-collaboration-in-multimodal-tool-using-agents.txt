# MRC-MMAgent: Reinforcement Learning for Multi-Role Collaboration in Multimodal Tool-Using Agents 

# Shengqin Wang ∗, Jie Jin ∗, Xiaofan Li, Wentao Yan, Yu Cheng, Qingsong Hu Yihang Chen, Weilin Luo, Jingyu Gong, Xin Tan, Yuan Xie, Zhizhong Zhang 

East China Normal University, Shanghai Innovation Institute, University College London, Huawei Noah’s Ark Lab 

Abstract 

Multimodal tool-using agents augment vision-language mod-els with access to external information and capabilities, en-abling them to solve knowledge-intensive and visually com-plex tasks that cannot be addressed using visual context alone. However, existing reinforcement learning pipelines largely train single-role agents to search and answer using terminal rewards, overlooking how humans inspect retrieved evidence, reformulate queries, and revise conclusions during search. Consequently, they fail to optimize this self-corrective pro-cess over complete interaction trajectories. We propose MRC-MMAgent, a multi-role collaborative reinforcement learning framework that incorporates trajectory assessment and con-tinuation directly into the executable agent loop. First, the framework establishes a multi-role collaborative loop that in-tegrates solving, inspection, and continuation, enabling the agent to acquire evidence through tools, evaluate the preced-ing trajectory, and resume problem solving when the cur-rent answer is insufficiently supported. Second, we intro-duce a role-coupled learning objective that combines task success, interaction validity, and successful correction with full-trajectory policy optimization across the decisions gen-erated by all roles. Extensive experiments show that, using only the original baseline training set without constructing additional datasets, our method consistently outperforms the corresponding base models and achieves leading or highly competitive performance across benchmarks. 

# Introduction 

Vision-Language Models (VLMs) (Liu et al. 2024; Chen et al. 2024a; Liu et al. 2023; Zhu et al. 2025; Zhang et al. 2024a) have rapidly improved on perception, recognition, and visual question answering, yet many user questions can-not be answered from images alone. Multimodal agents ad-dress this limitation by coupling a foundation model with tools such as reverse image search, text search, region zoom-ing, and retrieval (Chen et al. 2022, 2024b; Yu et al. 2024; Hu et al. 2023; Jiang et al. 2024; Zhang et al. 2024b). Systems such as MMSearch-R1, WebWatcher, DeepEyes2 optimize tool-augmented policies for multimodal search and visual question answering (Wu et al. 2025; Geng et al. 2025; Hong et al. 2025). SenseNova-MARS (Chng et al. 2025) further constructs high-resolution multimodal agent data and pro-poses BN-GSPO for training autonomous tool-using VLMs.

These methods establish Reinforcement Learning (RL) as a foundation for developing agents’ core tool-use capabilities. However, prevailing RL formulations train multimodal tool agents as single-role solvers under terminal-answer su-pervision, leaving a critical control process weakly super-vised. This formulation has two limitations: its single-role design lacks a dedicated mechanism for evidence assessment and continuation control, while terminal-only rewards fail to jointly optimize assessment, control, and revision across the full trajectory. First, as illustrated in Figure 3, a single agent loop may retrieve relevant snippets and still answer based on the wrong comparison basis, they lack an explicit, trainable mechanism that connects evidence assessment with trajec-tory control, making it difficult to identify under-supported answers and initiate targeted continuation. Second, as shown in the "reflection" part of Figure 3, where we insert the in-spection stage into the solver’s loop, the reflection merely repeats or rationalizes the initial reasoning. Mirroring hu-man information seeking, the agent assesses the preceding solution trajectory, finalizes the answer when it is sufficiently supported, and otherwise resumes tool use to refine its rea-soning and revise the answer. This conditional assessment-and-continuation process closes the loop over the entire in-teraction, highlighting the importance of full-trajectory op-timization for reliable reasoning. To address these limitations, we propose a multi-role col-laborative reinforcement learning framework for multimodal tool agents. First, we organize solving, inspection, and contin-uation as role-specific behaviors within a unified executable arXiv:2610.39324v1 [cs.CV] 29 Oct 2026 loop. The solver gathers external evidence and proposes a candidate answer, while the inspector assesses whether the accumulated visual–textual observations support it. If the evidence is sufficient, the rollout terminates; otherwise, the Inspector returns control to the solver for targeted tool use and revision. This design turns evidence assessment into a trainable control decision and enables all roles to co-adapt within a shared policy. Second, we formulate a role-coupled learning objective. We define behavioral rewards tailored to solving, inspection, and continuation, and aggregate them into a trajectory-level reward. Full-trajectory GRPO applies the aggregated reward signal to all role decisions rather than confining each role-specific signal to its corresponding role, thereby coupling optimization across the complete trajec-tory. Theoretical analysis and controlled ablations validate both the reward and optimization designs. Using only the MMSearch-R1 training set, with no additional supervised fine-tuning (SFT) trajectories or RL data, our method im-proves upon the base model by 11.1 points. Extensive exper-iments show that, without constructing any extra datasets, our method consistently outperforms the corresponding base models and achieves leading or highly competitive perfor-mance across search-intensive and visually complex bench-marks. Our contributions are: 

• We propose a multi-role collaborative reinforcement learning framework that organizes solving, trajectory in-spection, and continuation within a unified executable loop, enabling evidence-conditioned finalization and tar-geted recovery in multimodal tool use. 

• We formulate a role-coupled learning objective that com-bines a trajectory reward for task success, interaction va-lidity, calibrated finalization, and effective correction with full-trajectory over all generated role decisions. 

• Across diverse multimodal benchmarks, our method de-livers consistent gains over its base models and leading or competitive results among same-scale systems, without auxiliary SFT trajectories or additional training data. 

# Related Work 

Multimodal Tool-Using Agents 

Recent multimodal search and visual question answering agents (Wu et al. 2025; Chng et al. 2025; Hong et al. 2025; Wang et al. 2025a) continuously acquire problem-solving capabilities by training VLMs to use tools. However, most current agents rely on expensive SFT trajectories to regular-ize model behavior, while improving performance through terminal rewards alone. This fixed behavioral regime ne-glects effective evaluation of the model’s reasoning process and further limits the ability to perform meaningful revision. 

Multi-Agent and Multi-Module Tool-Use RL 

Multi-agent frameworks optimize role decomposition among planners, workers, and specialized agents (Li et al. 2025; Mo et al. 2025; Park et al. 2025; Chen et al. 2026a), while other works improve process supervision and multimodal tool use (Tan et al. 2025; Zhang et al. 2026; Dong et al. 2025; Zhao et al. 2026; Hong et al. 2025). Reflection meth-ods such as Look-Back and ReGRPO revisit evidence or use teacher feedback to revise solutions (Yang et al. 2026; Klis-sarov et al. 2026; Zhang and Shou 2026), but do not jointly learn reference-free inspection and recovery within a multi-modal tool loop (see Supp. A for detailed comparisons). In contrast, MRC-MMAgent trains solver, inspector, and con-tinuation within one shared policy, without auxiliary SFT or teacher critiques, using a role-coupled reward and full-trajectory updates to jointly optimize calibrated finalization and successful correction. 

# Method 

Overview 

Figure 2 presents our multi-role collaborative reinforce-ment learning framework, which couples an executable solve–inspect–continue loop with trajectory-level learning. A shared policy πθ is conditioned by frozen prompts S1, S3,and S2 to act as the initial solver, inspector, and continuation solver, respectively. The solver gathers evidence and proposes an answer; without access to the reference answer, correct-ness labels, or future outcomes, the inspector determines whether the trajectory sufficiently supports it and either fi-nalizes or requests targeted continuation. We train all roles with a role-coupled reward for task success, interaction valid-ity, and inspection-guided correction. Full-trajectory GRPO then propagates the group-relative signal to every model-generated decision, allowing the shared policy to co-adapt across the complete interaction.

Main Results 

Table 1 and Figure 1 reports the main multimodal search-intensive results. We compare our method with trained multimodal tool agents at the 7B and 8B scales. Robustness and Effectiveness Analysis. We conduct ex-tensive ablation studies, with all results reported over the first 100 training steps to mitigate the influence of conver-gence fluctuations. Supplementary Material D investigates the effect of the number of inspection-refinement loops. The accuracy curves for the single-loop ( Nm = 1 ) and two-loop (Nm = 2 ) settings almost completely overlap, yielding accu-racies of 50.9 % and 50.3 %, respectively—indicating highly comparable performance. However, the two-loop setting in-creases the average output length from 1,717.5 to 2,751.7 tokens and the average number of tool calls from 2.5 to 2.9, incurring substantial computational overhead. This suggests that additional loops introduce contextual redundancy rather than performance gains; therefore, we adopt Nm = 1 as the default configuration to maintain efficiency without sacrific-ing performance. Furthermore, compared with the baseline, our method achieves notable accuracy improvements with only a marginal increase in token and tool-call costs. This indicates that our approach does not introduce appreciable latency while delivering superior performance, and that it ef-fectively helps the model avoid redundant attempts. Failure Modes of Role-Selective Masking 

Correct initial answers may still need solver-side credit. 

If the first solver answers correctly but the inspector incor-rectly rejects, masking may update only the inspector. Yet the solver may have produced weak or ambiguous evidence, making the answer hard to verify. Full-trajectory GRPO can reinforce both better inspection and more verifiable solver trajectories. 

Correct rejection does not imply useful feedback. If the first solver is wrong, the inspector correctly rejects, but con-tinuation fails, masking the inspector assumes the inspector is not responsible. However, the rejection reason may be unin-formative. Full-trajectory optimization encourages feedback that helps downstream correction, not merely a locally cor-rect label. 

Relative advantages can reinforce the wrong local com-ponent. GRPO advantages are group-relative. A trajectory with an imperfect local decision can still receive positive ad-vantage if it outperforms other samples. Masking may pre-serve only selected role tokens and assign them this advan-tage, even when the selected local action was not the source of improvement.
