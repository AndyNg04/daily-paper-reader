Abstract. Current Large Language Models (LLMs)-based mental health chatbots often lack sufficient empathy, contextual awareness, and safety mechanisms, potentially leading to ineffective or harmful interactions. This paper proposes a novel framework that integrates sentiment and depression analysis with a rule-based filtering mechanism prior to model inference. By dynamically constructing prompts based on emotional and risk-related insights, the framework aims to significantly improve the con-textual relevance, empathy, and risk-aware of model-generated responses. We evaluated four model strategies and two prompt engineered variants across qualitative metrics such as empathy, helpfulness, clarity, relevance, safety, as well as toxicity assessments using the Perspective API. Exper-imental results indicate that the prompt engineering approaches outper-forms DSFT and RLAIF by 1.8% and 1.1% respectively. 

Keywords: Sentiment Analysis · Mental Health Chatbot · Prompt En-gineering. 

1 Introduction 

A mental disorder is a clinically significant disturbance in the intellect, emotional control, or behaviour of an individual. While mental health struggles can occur without a formal diagnosis, factors like employment, living conditions, and ser-vice access influence well-being, affecting individuals and families. In Australia, 20% experience mental illness annually, with young people (16–24) most affected (38.8%). Global health systems fall short in meeting the needs of those with men-tal disorders, leading to a gap in treatment availability and quality. Barriers like stigma, financial constraints, and limited access prevent many from seeking help. For instance, 31% of Americans fear being judged when seeking mental health support. This situation has led to exploring technology, such as AI applications, to assist individuals coping with mental health issues. The rapid development of Large Language Models (LLMs) have the poten-tial to disrupt and transform the way people work and live in modern society. Technology has significantly advanced in accurately identifying and labelling emotions by analysing motion, facial expressions, online activities, phrasing, and

– Integrating sentiment analysis to dynamically adapt chatbot interactions based on emotional and psychological states of the user. 

– Experimenting the prompt engineering through two distinct strategies: qual-itative and quantitative prompting. 

2 Related Work

The 2017 emergence of transformers revolutionised NLP with self-attention mechanisms, leading to early LLMs like GPT-1 in 2018. Subsequent models, in-cluding GPT-3 in 2020 and ChatGPT in 2022, further enhanced human-like dialogue through reinforcement learning. Mental health chatbots have gained popularity, especially after the COVID-19 pandemic, leading to increased research in the field. 3 Methodology 

3.1 Proposed model architecture 

This paper proposes a novel framework, named PromptSense , for empathy, contextual relevance, and a risk-aware chatbot (see Fig. 1). The framework con-sists of four modules: sentiment analysis model, risk assessment module, model inference module, and song recommendation module. User inputs are processed by a RoBERTa transformer with three heads: the Mental Head detects critical mental health risks like suicidal thoughts for prompt action; the Emotion Head identifies various emotional states; and the VA (Valence-Arousal) Head measures mood and emotional intensity. These outputs inform the chatbot’s responses, en-suring they’re empathetic and relevant. Additionally, the music recommendation module offers personalised song suggestions based on emotional insights for extra support. Further details on each module will be provided in later sections. 

3.2 Sentiment Analysis Model (SAM) 

The proposed multi-output sentiment analysis model is built on MentalRoBERTa, a specialised variant of the RoBERTa encoder optimised for mental health appli-cations [10]. Two residual blocks are stacked atop the encoder to support multi-head outputs. 3.3 Mental Health LLM Model 

The "DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Llama-8B" model, pre-trained by Unsloth [6], serves as the base model for this research. We used a DSFT approach with the Counsel Chat dataset [1] to adapt the model to mental health conversations while main-taining its generalisation capabilities. This led to the development of the "DSFT" model, which retains general knowledge and integrates specialised understand-ing. Further refinement was achieved through RLAIF. The model’s outputs were iteratively improved based on evaluations from the "Mistral-7B-Instruct-v0.3" judge model [12], using metrics such as Empathy, Helpfulness, Relevance, Clar-ity, and Safety. The final model is subsequently referred to as the "RLAIF" model. See Fig. 2 for more details. 3.4 Prompt Engineering 

Crafting an effective prompt can be a complex task. Several factors influence its effectiveness, including the model selected, its training data, hyperparameter settings, word choice, style and tone, structure, and context. As a result, prompt engineering is an iterative process. Poorly constructed prompts can lead to am-biguous or inaccurate responses, limiting the model’s ability to deliver meaning-ful output [2]. In this study, we will explore two distinct prompting strategies: qualitative and quantitative (probability-based). For the quantitative approach, prompt construction will directly incorporate the numerical probability values generated by the Sentiment Analysis Model. 

Example 1. Example of Prompt using quantitative (probability) scale as follow. 

> You are a compassionate psychologist who responds with warmth, empathy, and validation. Context: 1. Currently user’s mental state is 78.45% self.offmychest 2. User’s current emotion is unclear. Respond gently and explore their feelings carefully. 3. Right now user’s valence is 0.49 and user’s arousal is 0.67 Offer emotional support and thoughtful guidance tailored to the user’s feelings and concerns. ONLY OUTPUT UP TO FIVE SENTENCES. DO NOT provide any reasoning.

On the other hand, a more intuitive depiction of emotional states will be attainable through mapping the original probability values to the qualitative description: 1) Probabilities 0.8 ∼ 1.0: Very High; 2) Probabilities 0.6 ∼ 0.8: High; 3) Probabilities 0.4 ∼ 0.6: Moderate; 4) Probabilities 0.2 ∼ 0.4: Low; and 4) Probabilities 0.0 ∼ 0.2: Very Low.

3.5 Datasets 

This study analyses emotional states in conversations using five datasets (see Table 2 for more details). The first dataset, from Ji et al. on suicidal ideation [11], includes 54,412 entries categorized into five mental health classifications sourced from mental health-related subreddits. The second dataset, GoEmotions [7], consists of Reddit comments annotated with 27 emotion categories plus a Neutral class, enhancing the model’s sentiment detection capabilities. The third dataset, the NRC VAS Lexicon [14], offers valence and arousal scores for over 55,000 English words, enriching the GoEmotions dataset with quantified emotional dimensions through semantic analysis. Finally, the fourth dataset, Counsel Chat [1], contains 2,612 cleaned conversational scenarios with therapists, ensuring objectivity by removing personal information. For evalua-tion, the EmpatheticDialogues dataset [15] includes 418 randomly selected test conversations to assess the model’s contextual and ethical appropriateness.

Mental Health LLM This study uses a two-stage evaluation approach to comprehensively assess model-generated outputs. In the initial evaluation stage, three distinct LLM judge models are used. The first judge, Mistral AI’s "Mistral-7B-Instruct-v0.3" (MIST), is the same as the model applied during the Re-inforcement Learning with AI Feedback (RLAIF) phase, assuring consistency in evaluation standards. Mistral AI’s "Mixtral-8x7B-v0.1" (MIXT), the second judge, provides an independent perspective as a general evaluator. Lastly, the third judge model, "MentaLLaMA-chat-13B" (MELL) [16], specialises in men-tal health scenarios, bringing domain-specific expertise to the evaluation process. Across all judges, evaluation criteria remain uniform, covering empathy, helpful-ness, clarity, relevance, and safety. In addition, the outputs are analysed using the Perspective API (https: //www.perspectiveapi.com) to quantify levels of toxicity, identity attacks, in-sults, and threats, serving as metrics for detecting bias and ensuring ethical com-pliance. Moreover, supplementary metrics such as compassion, respect, nuance, and incoherence will also be measured to provide a comprehensive evaluation framework. 4 Experiments and Results 

4.1 Sentiment Analysis Model 

To evaluate the proposed sentiment analysis model, four experiments were con-ducted. The original model uses the standard RoBERTa-base architecture, while the baseline employs MentalRoBERTa, a variant pre-fine-tuned on mental health datasets [10]. The proposed model enhances MentalRoBERTa by adding two residual blocks with layer normalisation and dropout layers. Initially, the weights and bias of the proposed model were frozen to retain the pre-trained values, al-lowing only the new residual blocks to adapt. The model was then unfrozen for end-to-end fine-tuning to optimise all parameters. The results in Table 4 indicate that the proposed model significantly im-proves upon the baseline across all metrics: mental health accuracy, emotion classification (F1), and affective regression (valence and arousal RMSE). The unfrozen model achieves a mental accuracy of 0.6803 (17.5% increase) and an F1 score of 0.4840 (504.2% improvement) compared to the frozen model. Al-though the F1 score shows notable enhancement, there is still room for better minority-class classification. Additionally, the reduction in valence and arousal RMSE (0.2025 and 0.1835, respectively) suggests the unfrozen model effectively captures nuanced affective states.

5 Conclusion and Future Work 

This study indicates that prompt engineering, particularly qualitative prompt-ing, significantly enhances empathy and overall response quality in mental health chatbot applications. In contrast, advanced fine-tuning techniques such as DSFT 12 S. Lee, J. Zhou & J. Sun 

and RLAIF offer only marginal improvements, suggesting that current fine-tuning strategies may not effectively capture the nuanced demands of emotion-ally sensitive dialogue. Although AI-based evaluation provides scalability and consistency, it remains limited in capturing clinically meaningful aspects such as emotional appropriateness, and long-term psychological impact. Future work will incorporate human evaluations from mental health professionals, and refine prompt generation using reinforcement learning from expert feedback.
