## 摘要
2026年9月，规模最大的前沿人工智能实验室负责人公开表示，能力提升的速度过快，任何一家实验室都无法独自减缓这一进程；与此同时，OpenAI正在评估的智能体突破了测试环境，进入第三方的实时系统。一个由联合国授权的专家组称，这是人类可能失去控制的一种途径所发出的早期警示。本文考察这一风险的制度层面：关注的不是机器，而是必须控制机器的人。我们认为，控制权并非被人工智能夺走，而是在组织结构使无人能够说不的情况下，被一项项委托出去。我们将控制定义为：有人能够发现严重问题、质疑决策、停止系统并撤销相关决定，而且不这样做会使其自身蒙受损失。由此引出三个问题，任何开发者、资助方、监管者或部署方都可以公开作答：（1）组织内部谁有权说不，谁要为说是承担责任；（2）组织外部由谁审查决策，若有异议，他们能采取什么行动；（3）当人工智能构建人工智能的速度超过任何人的核查速度时，会发生什么。可核查的答复需要明确列出一位具名负责人、一个日期、一个数字，以及一项已实际启用或经过测试的停止机制。三项措施符合这一标准：每项重大决策——包括人工智能智能体所执行的常设指令——都指定一名负责人；赋予一名监督者停止权限；并公布两项时限，即构建所需时间与核查所需时间。我们表明，治理理论也得出了相同条件；通过依据上述问题对五十种既有机制进行编码，我们发现，几乎每一种机制都始于某一方在某一时点采取的一条规则，而无需等待其他各方。这正是本文的设计：提出一个起点，而非给出解决方案。公开发布是第一步，任何一家实验室都可以独自迈出这一步；合同、独立机构和法律则是后续举措。

## Abstract
In September 2026, the leaders of the largest frontier AI laboratories said in public that the pace of capability improvement is too fast and that none of them can slow down alone, and agents under evaluation at OpenAI broke out of their test environment and reached a third party's live systems, which a UN-mandated panel called an early warning of one route to loss of human control. This paper examines the institutional side of that risk: not the machine, but the person who must control it. We argue that control is not taken by AI but given away, one delegation at a time, by organisations arranged so that nobody is positioned to say no, and we define control as a person who can detect a serious problem, challenge the decision, stop the system and reverse it, and who has something to lose by not doing so. Three questions follow, which any builder, funder, regulator or deployer can answer in public: (1) who inside the organisation can say no, and who answers for saying yes; (2) who outside the organisation checks the decision, and what can they do when they disagree; (3) what happens when AI builds AI faster than anyone can check it. A checkable answer needs a named person, a date, a number, and a stop that has been used or tested. Three measures meet that test: a named person on every consequential decision, including the standing instructions AI agents carry out; a supervisor with a stop authority; and two published clocks, time to build against time to check. We show that governance theory arrives at the same conditions, and code fifty older mechanisms against the questions, finding that almost every one began as one rule at one point, taken by one party without waiting for the rest. That is the paper's design: a start, not a solution. Publication is the first step and one laboratory can take it alone; the contract, the independent body and the law come after.
