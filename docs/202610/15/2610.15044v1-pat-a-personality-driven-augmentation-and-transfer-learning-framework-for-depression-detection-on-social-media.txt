Abstract 

Depression is a prevalent mental disorder that affects millions worldwide, with early detection crucial for effective intervention. While exist-ing methods have achieved promising results in automated depression detection, they face two key limitations: (1) noise in post screening: symptom-based semantic screening is easily misled by superficial lexical patterns, thereby introducing false-positive posts; (2) weak op-timization of the post encoder: relying solely on coarse user-level supervision signals pre-vents models from capturing depressive cues within individual posts. To address these limita-tions, we propose PAT, a Personality-driven 

Augmentation and Transfer learning frame-work. In the first phase, PAT optimizes a post-level encoder via a fine-grained contrastive learning objective, utilizing LLM-based data augmentation driven by classic personality the-ories to alleviate data scarcity. In the second phase, the trained post-level scorer is deployed for high-quality post filtering, while its en-coder parameters are seamlessly transferred for user-level tasks. Extensive experiments demon-strate that PAT significantly outperforms exist-ing methods. Furthermore, PAT delivers multi-granular interpretability by tracing post-level mental-state trajectories and highlighting key symptoms, thereby offering reliable diagnostic evidence for clinical practice.

Besides, these methods only output user-level predictions with limited explanations, lack-ing the evidence needed for clinical interpretation. Recent efforts have attempted to address this gap using Large Language Models (LLMs) for post-level information extraction and explanation gener-ation (Lan et al., 2025; Zheng et al., 2024). Al-though promising, LLM-based approaches face their own constraints: the risk of hallucination and high computational costs that hinder real-world deployment (Zhang et al., 2025; Ravenda et al., 2025). To address these limitations, we propose a two-phase Personality-driven Augmentation and 

Transfer learning framework (PAT), whose over-all structure is shown in Figure 2. In the first phase, PAT trains a post-level scorer to evaluate depressive severity, which is subsequently lever-aged to perform score-based filtering on user-level inputs while its encoder component is transferred for user-level training. To alleviate the inher-ent scarcity of post-level data, we draw inspira-tion from psycholinguistic findings that personal-ity drives distinct linguistic manifestations (Kout-soumpis et al., 2022). Specifically, we employ LLMs for personality-driven data augmentation based on personality theories, including the Big Five (Costa and McCrae, 2008) and MBTI (Jung and Beebe, 2016), thus expanding the diversity of data in various expression styles. Furthermore, a fine-grained contrastive learning objective is de-signed to effectively utilize both the augmented and labeled samples for robust representation learn-ing. Beyond performance enhancements, PAT dis-tinguishes itself by providing multi-granular inter-pretability: it delivers user-level predictions, traces post-level mental-state trajectories, and highlights key symptoms, thus offering comprehensive diag-nostic evidence. In summary, the contributions of this paper are threefold: • We propose a novel transfer learning frame-work, PAT. Extensive experiments on multi-ple benchmarks demonstrate that PAT signifi-cantly outperforms existing strong baselines and can serve as a plug-and-play component to enhance other methods. • We design a personality-driven data augmenta-tion method with a corresponding fine-grained contrastive learning algorithm. Experiments demonstrate that these methods effectively op-timize the embedding space of the post en-coder, while revealing the limitations of con-ventional supervised contrastive learning. • We build a comprehensive, interpretable anal-ysis system. Beyond user-level depression detection, PAT traces post-level mental-state trajectories and highlights key symptoms, of-fering insights that improve practical utility and trustworthiness. 

2 Related Work 

2.1 User-Level Depression Detection 

User-Level Depression Prediction aims to assess depression risk using a user’s social media posts. Early researchers explored diverse machine learn-ing techniques (Ding et al., 2020; Vasha et al., 2023; Hossain et al., 2021). With the rapid advancement of deep learning, various neural networks have be-come the dominant approach for this task, such as Convolutional Neural Networks (Yates et al., 2017; Lin et al., 2020; Narayanan et al., 2022) and Re-current Neural Networks (Kour and Gupta, 2022; Gamaarachchige and Inkpen, 2019). Zhang et al. (2022) proposed a post screening method based on psychological scales and adopted a hierarchi-cal model that sequentially conducts post encoding and user classifying, which has since become a mainstream approach in depression detection. Fol-lowing this paradigm, Liu et al. (2024) introduced capsule networks to model features of different symptoms. Wang et al. (2025) further improved the screening method by making it end-to-end train-able. However, these methods typically rely on user-level binary labels for the whole training. 2.2 Transfer Learning 

The core of transfer learning is to leverage knowl-edge from a data-rich source domain to allevi-ate data scarcity in a target domain (Pan and Yang, 2009). In mental-health research, Ji et al. (2022) and Zhai et al. (2024) pre-trained a domain-specialized Mental-BERT in the psychological cor-pora. Wu et al. (2023) transferred knowledge from sentiment classification to depression detection via distillation. These methods perform pre-training or transfer on domain-related corpora or auxiliary tasks, whose alignment with the target task remains suboptimal. In this work, we propose using post-level depression detection as the source task and transfer the post encoder to the user-level depres-sion detection model. This design is expected to achieve a tighter knowledge transfer by directly bridging the gap between the two closely related levels of depression detection. 

3 Proposed Methodology 

The architecture of the proposed PAT framework is shown in Figure 2. The framework follows a two-stage design, comprising a post-level task and a user-level task of depression detection. The depression-level scorer trained in the post-level task is deployed for user-level post screening, while its encoder is transferred to the user-level task. 

3.1 Post-Level Depression Detection Problem Definition: The input is a single social media post denoted as p. The objective is to formu-late a regression task that estimates a depression-3intensity score r ∈ [0 , 3] , where a higher value indicates increased severity at four progressive lev-els: None, Slight, Moderate, and Serious. 

3.1.1 Personality-Driven Augmentation 

We employ psychological personality theories to guide a large language model (LLM) in generating augmented samples that reflect distinct personality tendencies, thereby mitigating the scarcity of post-level annotated data. Specifically, we adopt two mainstream personality theories: (1) the Big Five: taking the high and low tendencies of each of the five traits (Extraversion, Agreeableness, Conscien-tiousness, Neuroticism, Openness), resulting in ten categories; (2) the MBTI (Jungian eight-function): directly using its eight cognitive-function dimen-sions including Extroverted, Introverted, Sensing, Intuitive, Thinking, Feeling, Judging, and Perceiv-ing. We implement the augmentation through a two-step procedure. First, the LLM analyzes the original post with reference to the 21 symptom dimensions of BDI-II, identifies the depression-related symptoms supported by the text, and esti-mates the severity of each identified symptom. The analysis is returned as a list of symptom–severity pairs. Second, the original post and the symptom– severity pairs are jointly provided to the LLM to generate augmented sentences. We constrain the LLM to preserve the original depressive-symptom semantics while accentuating the linguistic style associated with the specified personality trait. This approach achieves style-diverse and semantically controlled text augmentation. The prompts for both steps are shown in Appendix A.1. After data augmentation, we perform post-level model training. First, a pretrained encoder Mpost 

encodes the post pi into a text embedding. Then, we utilize a two-layer linear regression head to pro-duce an unnormalized score ˜ri. The Mean Squared Error (MSE) loss is computed as:

3.2 User-Level Depression Detection Problem Definition: The input is a set of so-cial media posts from a user, denoted as P =

{p1, ..., p n} where pi is the i-th post. The objective is to predict a binary depression label l ∈ { 0, 1},with 0 indicating non-depressed and 1 indicating depressed. 

3.2.1 Post Screening 

Given a set of user posts P = {p1, . . . , p n}, pre-vious methods filter relevant posts based on se-mantic similarity to symptom descriptions S =

{s1, ..., sm} from the Beck Depression Inventory-II (BDI-II) (Beck, 1996). In contrast to rely-ing on semantic matching, we directly adopt the trained scorer on the post-level task to assign a depression-level score ˆri to each post. We select the top-K posts with the highest scores as the input 

X = {p1, ..., p K } for the user-level model.

3.2.3 Comprehensive Interpretability 

In addition to the user-level depression prediction result ˆyuser , the PAT framework can also output the following two types of explanatory information: • Post-Level Mental-State Trajectory: 

Since we have already trained an effective depression-level scorer in the post-level task, we leverage it to analyze each of the user’s posts, thereby constructing a mental-state trajectory. • Key Symptoms: From the symptom attention module, we extract A ∈ Rm×K where each element Aij reflects the relevance between the j-th post and the i-th BDI-II symptom. We select the top-3 symptoms with the high-est attention weights as the user’s symptom explanation. 

4 Experimental Settings 

4.1 Datasets 

For the post-level dataset, we employ the depres-sion severity four-class dataset constructed by Yang et al. (2021). The dataset contains 6,115 Chinese posts from Sina Weibo (a Chinese social media plat-form). The dataset is split into training, validation, and test sets in a 8:1:1 ratio. To adapt post-level training to the Chinese and English downstream user-level tasks, we translate each Chinese post into an English version. The post-level model is trained on both the original Chinese posts and their English translations. For personality-driven data augmentation, we use Qwen3.5-Plus to generate the augmented samples. Due to the differing aug-mentation scales of the two methods (10 × for Big Five vs. 8 × for MBTI), we randomly retain eight augmented posts per instance for the Big Five to ensure a balanced comparison. For the user-level dataset, three datasets are cho-sen: SWDD (Cai et al., 2023), eRisk2017 (Losada and Crestani, 2016), and eRisk2018 (Losada et al., 2018). All three datasets are annotated with bi-nary depression labels for users based on their self-reports. The SWDD dataset is sourced from the Chinese social media platform Sina Weibo, while eRisk2017 and eRisk2018 are collected from the English Reddit forum. For each dataset, we split the data into training, validation, and test sets in a 3:1:1 ratio. 4.2 Implementation Details 

Since our task comprises both Chinese and En-glish datasets, we employ ernie-3.0-base-zh 

(ERNIE) (Sun et al., 2021) as the post encoder for PAT and all the competing methods. While its architecture aligns with that of BERT (Devlin et al., 2019), ERNIE supports both Chinese and En-glish tasks. In the post-level task, we set τ = 0 .5,

wlow = 0 .3, α = 0 .5, and γ = 4 . For the user-level task, we set K = 16 and d′ = 768 . The learning rate is fixed at 1e-5 for all experiments. Models are optimized with AdamW and trained for 10 epochs with a batch size of 16. The checkpoint achieving the best performance on the validation set is selected for final evaluation. To ensure ro-bustness, each model is trained with five different random seeds. All experiments are conducted on an NVIDIA RTX 3090 GPU (24GB). The code implementing the proposed method is available in an anonymous repository at https://anonymous. 4open.science/r/PAT-demo-AB67 .

4.3 Competing Methods 

We compare our proposed method in the user-level depression detection task with the following ones: • HAN (Zhang et al., 2022): This method em-ploys post screening followed by a hierarchi-cal attention network for classification. • DeCapsNet (Liu et al., 2024): Similar to 

HAN , this approach replaces the user-level hierarchical module with a capsule network. • Mental-BERT (Ji et al., 2022): This method pre-trains BERT on a psychology-specific cor-pus to improve domain adaptation. We replace our post encoder with its parameters. We use 

Chinese Mental-BERT (Zhai et al., 2024) for the Chinese dataset, SWDD. • E2LPS (Wang et al., 2025): This approach achieves end-to-end trainability by using a straight-through technique. • LLM : We perform depression detection via prompting a large language model with the Chain-of-Thought (COT) (Wei et al., 2022) and 2-shot examples from the train set. In practice, we adopt GPT-5.4 and 

Qwen3.5-Plus for testing.

The PAT framework can be seamlessly integrated into other baseline methods as a plug-and-play module. 5.5 Why We Need Fine-grained Contrastive Learning? 

During post-level model training, we find that simply adding augmented data yields marginal gains (+0.76% Acc), while further incorporating supervised contrastive learning (SCL) stagnates or even degrades performance. This originates from the overly restrictive assumption in standard SCL, which treats all same-label samples as absolute pos-itive pairs. However, instances sharing the same label are not necessarily identical in the semantic space. An intuitive example is that posts within the same depression severity level can exhibit com-pletely distinct types of symptoms. Consequently, we propose fine-grained con-trastive learning (FGCL). FGCL treats direct aug-mented counterparts as strong positives like unsu-pervised contrastive learning, while treating other same-label instances as weaker positives weighted by their relative spatial distances. As shown in Table 3, FGCL triggers a performance increase (+2.62% Acc). We further visualize the embedding space of contrastive learning on the post-level dataset, as shown in Figure 5. 6 Conclusion 

In this paper, we present PAT, a personality-driven augmentation and transfer learning framework that effectively addresses existing bottlenecks in user-level depression detection. To alleviate the scarcity of post-level data, we propose personality-driven augmentation and fine-grained contrastive learn-ing techniques specifically tailored for optimizing post-level tasks. This enables the core encoder component to be robustly optimized before being transferred to the user-level model. Empirical results demonstrate PAT’s superior performance and its unique capability to serve as a plug-and-play module for other methods. More-over, PAT provides comprehensive interpretabil-ity by tracing post-level mental-state trajectories and highlighting key symptoms, which greatly en-hances the trustworthiness of its predictions and 9offers actionable insights for clinical diagnosis and intervention.

A.4 Parameter Sensitivity Analysis of K

We investigate the relationship between the number of retained posts K in the post screening phase and the model performance, with the results illustrated in Figure A.3. It can be observed that the model performance generally peaks at K = 16 , whereas both larger and smaller values of K lead to perfor-mance degradation. A.5 More Cases A Case of a User Labeled as Non-Depressed 

Figure A.4 presents the interpretability analysis re-sults of PAT for a user labeled as non-depressed. It can be observed that none of the user’s posts reach the "Serious" level, with the majority falling below "Slight," indicating a relatively low risk of depression. Cases of Model Misidentification In this sec-tion, we present several error cases for analysis. As shown in Figure A.5, a depressed user was incor-rectly classified as non-depressed. The depression trajectory of this user’s posts indicates that their depression level remained at "Moderate" for an ex-tended period but never reached the "Serious" level, which likely contributed to the missed detection. The results of the symptom attribution analysis are largely consistent with the user’s expressions, ex-cept for the absence of any mention of "suicide." Another case in which a user labeled as non-depressed was misclassified as depressed is pre-sented in Figure A.6. Although the user’s depres-sion trajectory shows prolonged "Moderate" levels and occasional spikes to "Serious", further analysis reveals that many of the posts, especially those reaching "Serious", actually contain quotes ex-pressing negative emotions from comics or movies, which likely led to the model’s misjudgment. How-ever, it should be noted that some of the user’s posts also reflect experiences of domestic violence and prolonged low mood, indicating a state of emo-tional distress that carries a risk of further deteri-oration. Therefore, while the classification may be technically inaccurate in the diagnostic sense, the identification of this case by the model could still be considered meaningful from a risk-aware perspective.
