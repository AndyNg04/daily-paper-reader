## 摘要
情感计算已从类别化情绪识别发展到借助大型多模态模型开展开放式情感分析。然而，情感科学将情绪描述为一种不断展开的过程，受评估、调节和社会性解读共同塑造，而这一过程在计算层面仍未得到充分探索。我们提出认知导向框架 TRACE，通过三个相互关联的阶段——情境（Condition）、情感（Affect）和效应（Effect）——对情绪事件进行形式化建模，并将可观察线索与内部立场、情绪表达调节等认知因素相结合。基于这一表述，我们构建了 TRACE-Bench，用于评估多模态模型在真实社会场景中的表现。该基准涵盖五项任务：有依据的情感识别、情绪调节解码、原因推理、效应推理和完整链条重构；数据集包含646段视频中的3,746组结构化问答。人类与模型的匹配对比显示，两者表现存在显著差距；与此同时，专门针对情感任务的模型总体上也落后于通用大型多模态模型（MLLM）。模型输出呈现出反复出现的失误，包括将外显行为视为真实感受，以及在长链生成过程中编造缺乏依据的事件。我们进一步提出 TRACER，一种以认知为基础的结构化推理方法，将每项推断与来自事实观察、认知评估及已有上游结论的明确前提相结合，从而构建可追溯的中间结论与目标结论图。TRACER 在五项任务中的表现均优于所有受评估的模型基线。项目主页：https://cogaffc.github.io/TRACE

## Abstract
Affective computing has progressed from categorical emotion recognition to open-ended affective analysis with large multimodal models. Yet affective science describes emotion as an unfolding process shaped by appraisal, regulation, and social interpretation, which remains underexplored computationally. We propose TRACE, a cognition-oriented framework that formalizes an affective episode through three interrelated stages: Condition, Affect, and Effect, integrating observable cues with cognitive factors such as internal stance and regulation of emotional display. Based on this formulation, TRACE-Bench evaluates multimodal models in real-world social scenes through five tasks spanning grounded affect recognition, regulation decoding, cause reasoning, effect reasoning, and full-chain reconstruction, with 3,746 structured question-answer pairs over 646 videos. A matched human-model comparison reveals a substantial performance gap, while affect-specialized models also generally lag behind general-purpose MLLMs. Model outputs show recurring failures, including treating displayed behavior as genuine feeling and fabricating unsupported events during long-chain generation. We further propose TRACER, a cognition-grounded structured reasoning method that couples each inference with explicit premises from factual observations, cognitive appraisals, and established upstream conclusions, forming a traceable graph of intermediate and target conclusions. TRACER outperforms all evaluated model baselines on each of the five tasks. Project page: https://cogaffc.github.io/TRACE
