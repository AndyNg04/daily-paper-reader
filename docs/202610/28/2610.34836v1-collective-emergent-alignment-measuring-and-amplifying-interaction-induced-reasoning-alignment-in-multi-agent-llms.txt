## Abstract 

Multi-agent large language model (LLM) systems can exhibit emer-gent behaviors through interaction, yet whether a similar phenom-enon exists for reasoning alignment remains largely unexplored. We study whether agent interaction produces additional reference-grounded reasoning alignment beyond matched individual self-refinement, and introduce REMAP (Reference– Entailment– Matrix for Agentic Process), a framework that quantifies this effect through Semantic Coverage and interaction-specific alignment gains. Across diverse model families and two multi-hop reasoning benchmarks, we observe broadly positive interaction gains, particularly on rea-soning content that depends on integrating evidence distributed across agents. These gains are consistently associated with indepen-dently measured peer-information recovery, while further analysis reveals composition-dependent heterogeneity effects and asymmet-ric information transfer across agent capability levels. Motivated by these findings, we further introduce a fact-level multi-agent communication framework that replaces full reasoning-trace ex-change with compact, gap-aware, and selectively routed informa-tion. Our experiments show that compact fact exchange improves reasoning alignment and task performance while reducing commu-nication overhead, whereas more selective communication exposes a trade-off between acquiring distributed information, retaining local content, and communication efficiency. Overall, our results show that collective emergent alignment can be quantified, linked to information-transfer patterns, and used to guide more effective multi-agent communication.

## 1 Introduction 

Large language model (LLM) agents increasingly work in multi-agent systems (MAS) [9, 40], where they exchange information, discuss candidate solutions, and revise their reasoning [15, 25]. A central motivation is emergent collective behavior : interaction can help a group combine partial information into outcomes that isolated agents may not reach [3]. Previous work has observed emergent coordination in generative-agent simulations [23] and improved reasoning and factual accuracy through multi-agent de-bate [5, 21]. These findings motivate a closer look at how the interac-tion changes reasoning and whether the agentic process contributes to emergent behavior in reasoning alignment. ## 2 Related Work 

Evaluating Reasoning Alignment. The Structured Alignment Score [33] compares generated and reference reasoning through semantic-relation matrices. ROSCOE [7] includes reference-based and reference-free diagnostics. ReCEval [24] evaluates step correct-ness and informativeness using claims, prior steps, and context. REVEAL [11] benchmarks relevance, evidence attribution, and logi-cal correctness. The NLI-hypergraph audit of Kohli et al. [14] checks how support relations compose within a trace. REMAP builds on reference-based evaluation and applies it separately to each agent’s states along matched self-refinement and MAS trajectories. 

Evaluating Multi-Agent Reasoning. MultiAgentBench [45] eval-uates milestone progress and coordination. Who&When [43] lo-cates failure-responsible agents and steps, while MAST [2] clas-sifies system-design, inter-agent, and verification failures. CARA assesses inter-agent consistency. Its CARA-GT variant measures expert-reference alignment and coverage across debate rounds [37]. Together, these studies support process-level analysis [44], includ-ing reference-based MAS evaluation. Table 1 compares six capabilities: reference-based evaluation (Ref.), multi-agent evaluation (MAS), matched self-refinement (Self), evidence ownership (Own.), quality evaluation (Qual.), and emer-gent alignment (Emerg.). REMAP combines these capabilities to quantify interaction gains and distinguish acquired peer informa-tion from retained local content. Its matched trajectory comparison isolates interaction-associated alignment gains beyond individual refinement. The ownership-aware decomposition further reveals how agents acquire missing evidence and preserve existing reason-ing, providing actionable insights for MAS communication design.

## 3.5 Fact-Level Communication 

REMAP reveals that peer interaction helps agents acquire missing information but can also weaken their existing reasoning. This motivates fact-level communication to better control information acquisition and local retention. We compare three communication policies using the shared fact interface in Figure 3. In the following, we will introduce the details of the proposed MAS architecture. 

Fact cards from the initial generation. At state zero, one LLM call produces a local-evidence fact 𝑓 𝑖 , its source identifier, gaps 𝑄 𝑖, 0,three reasoning steps, a conclusion, and an answer. A deterministic parser reads the fact field directly. All architectures reuse these initial records, with source cards fixed across updates. A gap is a self-identified information need. 

Complete compact broadcast. Receiver 𝑖 has two initial peer cards, F−𝑖 = {𝑓 𝑗 : 𝑗 ≠ 𝑖 }. Compact-All inserts both into its update prompt at every communication round. The receiver then makes its normal reasoning-update call. Delivery uses deterministic field selection. Random-K uses the same cards and integration prompt, but samples a subset matching the number delivered by Jev-GAP-Fallback for the same receiver and update as a control group. 

Gap-aware selection. GAP-Transfer selects the highest-MiniLM-cosine peer fact for each current gap. An NLI gate compares can-didates with receiver memory: contradictions are quarantined, en-tailments are treated as duplicates, and other selected facts are accepted. ## 4 Experiments and Results 

We first introduce the experimental setup, followed by results that address each research question (RQ). 

## 4.1 Experimental Setup 

Datasets and models. We evaluate seven model families on held-out three-hop MuSiQue and corrected 2Wiki questions [8, 28]. Com-munication experiments span diverse model families and capability levels, including 3 closed-source models and 4 open-weight models. 

References and protocol. References come from annotated sub-answers or evidence triples for offline scoring. Three agents receive distributed evidence, generate initial responses, and perform two synchronous updates under self refinement or MAS, using seeds 7/42/123. Architecture conditions share the initial outputs and the fact cards produced alongside them. Execution details and prompt templates are provided in Appendices B and N. 

Baselines. We compare Full-MAS, AgentPrune, AgentDropout, and PEAR with Compact-All, Random-K, GAP-Transfer, and Jev variants. Full adaptations are provided in Appendix J.

Δ𝑇 gold subtracts the self-refinement initial-to-final recovery change from the corresponding MAS change, aver-aged over agents. We report three-seed means and paired question-bootstrap intervals using 10,000 resamples. Appendices B and D detail the evaluation protocol and scoring procedures.

## 5 Conclusion 

We study whether interaction induces reasoning alignment beyond matched self-refinement. REMAP measures alignment changes across agent trajectories and separates local retention from peer-information gains. Across model families, interaction yields broadly positive gains on non-owned content, supported by independent gold recovery. The 2Wiki saturation highlights controlled evidence integration, while alignment gains describe changes within rea-soning. We then introduce fact-level communication with compact broadcast and selective routing. Compact exchange improves align-ment and pooled answer quality with fewer peer tokens, while se-lection reveals acquisition, retention, and cost trade-offs. Together, these findings show that collective emergent alignment can be quan-tified and used to guide more effective multi-agent communication.

## A.3 Ranges and Invariances 

Boundedness. Each local NLI match lies in [0, 1]. A row maxi-mum and any arithmetic average of row maxima therefore lie in 

[0, 1]. It follows that 𝐺 𝑋 ∈ [− 1, 1] and 𝐸 Δ ∈ [− 2, 2], with the same ranges for the non-owned versions whenever their reference sets are nonempty. No normalization of the change contrast is applied in the primary results.

## A.5 Computation and Caching 

For a trace with 𝐽 generated units and 𝐾 reference units, the lo-cal matrix needs at most 2 𝐾 𝐽 directional NLI evaluations before caching. With two conditions, 𝑁 agents, and 𝑇 + 1 states, the un-cached upper count is 4 𝑁 (𝑇 + 1)𝐾 𝐽 per question. The three-agent, three-state, four-unit setting therefore needs at most 576 directional pair evaluations per question. Repeated identical pairs are retrieved from the local cache. Matrix construction dominates scoring. Row maxima and averaging are linear in the matrix size. Evidence owner-ship changes only the row aggregation and reuses the same scores. For the initial three-hop semantic analysis, the local cache con-tained 27,554 directional pairs across the frozen traces. This re-ported count reflects deduplication and the specific run. It is not an asymptotic complexity claim. The scoring stage performs no language-model generation requests. 

## B Data, Protocol, and Execution Details B.1 MuSiQue Reference Construction 

MuSiQue-Ans development examples provide the question, an-swer, aliases, paragraph context, and question decomposition [28]. The primary filter keeps exactly three decomposition steps with valid distinct supporting paragraphs, resolvable references, and nonempty answers. The 100-question held-out set excludes the 30 pilot IDs. Dataset selection uses seed 42 and a frozen ID manifest. For each decomposition step, a placeholder such as #1 is replaced with the corresponding earlier gold subanswer. The deterministic reference unit is: 

> The answer to ‘ resolved subquestion ’ is ‘ gold subanswer ’.

A final conclusion states the question’s gold answer. These reference strings are constructed deterministically. Each agent receives the original question and its assigned source paragraph, with decom-position metadata and reference text excluded from the generation prompt. The paragraph-to-agent mapping is deterministically shuffled per question, stored, and reused across self refinement and MAS. Later model-family experiments reuse the original 100 question IDs and evidence allocations. 

## B.2 Corrected 2Wiki Construction and Audit 

The source 2Wiki development data supplies contextual support and explicit evidence triples [8]. Filtering for exactly three distinct gold triples yields 88 valid examples. The original implementation mixed supporting sentences and rendered triples. An audit found seven incorrect or insufficient mappings among 60 inspected evidence assignments. All 88 exact-gold answer-evaluation invariants passed. 20 inspected reference chains were internally consistent. The initial zero-answer result was therefore retained as a pipeline diagnostic, outside the scientific generalization results. The corrected protocol uniformly renders the official triples as simple factual text. Each agent owns one rendered fact, explicitly reports its local fact in its response, and may use peer-reported local facts during integration. Multiple legitimate values for a rela-tion are retained. No gold final answer, reference-chain ordering, or ownership labels are shown to the agents. ## B.3 Condition Semantics 

Self refinement retains the original question, the agent’s local source, and its own last response at each update. MAS adds the other agents’ last responses. The synchronous barrier is placed after every generated state. An update consumes only messages from the immediately preceding state. The standard output contains steps ,

conclusion , and answer . Exactly three steps are required in the main three-unit runs. The prompt descriptions here specify the protocol. The full ver-batim prompts and parameter manifests remain in the experimental archive. They should be attached with the final code release. In-dependent refinement can change answer content and semantic coverage in either direction, which is why both conditions are measured at their own initial and final states.

## B.5 Answer Aggregation and Uncertainty 

The final agent answers are normalized with the frozen evaluation code and combined by the same deterministic plurality rule in self refinement and MAS. Seed means and their standard deviation describe run variation separately. Matched-model com-parisons resample shared question IDs together. Independent depth sets use independent bootstrap differences in means. Intervals are pointwise and describe their specified comparison. ## F GAP Architecture: Protocol and Comparisons F.1 Architecture State and Runtime Inputs 

The GAP study uses three agents, two synchronous updates after state zero, and seeds 7, 42, and 123. Each initial response emits a local fact, information-gap queries, three reasoning steps, a conclu-sion, and an answer. The state-zero record is physically generated once per model, question, seed, and agent, then reused by every architecture condition. The source evidence remains assigned to its original agent. The runtime directory exposes the agents’ generated local facts. Gold references, aliases, labels, and evaluation scores enter only offline scoring. The local-fact field is emitted alongside reasoning in the same initial model response. The program extracts and stores that field di-rectly. It does not invoke a separate LLM to summarize a completed trace. This initial-generation cost is shared across architecture con-ditions. Later receiver calls are ordinary reasoning updates using the selected input fields. The fact directory stores each sender’s initial local fact. A re-ceiver’s current gaps are embedded with the cached MiniLM model and compared with peer facts by cosine similarity. The highest-scoring fact for each gap is selected, followed by deduplication and a cap of two facts. An empty gap set selects no facts. The candidates stay fixed across states. The receiver’s queries and memory can change. The design does not recursively forward acquired claims as new source facts. The local NLI gate assigns the most probable class to each com-parison between a candidate and a memory item. A contradiction with any memory item takes priority and quarantines the candi-date. Otherwise, entailment with an item marks a duplicate. All remaining candidates are accepted. GAP-Transfer and GAP-SOFT check the current reasoning-derived memory, while hard GAP-MAS checks the persistent anchor and accepted peer facts. This memory distinction is part of the hard treatment. The source reports log routing decisions and gate actions separately.

## H.2 Typed Outputs and Confidence Semantics 

Each candidate is evaluated with three binary Noul questions— relevance, novelty, and conflict—and one Choice over SEND, SKIP, and QUARANTINE. Choice returns the selected action, option prob-abilities, and the official action-confidence statistic. Noul returns a probability without a separate confidence field [30, 31]. The bi-nary diagnostic certainty |2𝑝 − 1| is stored separately. The fallback threshold uses only the official Choice confidence. JEV-GAP sends up to two SEND candidates, ordered by SEND probability, and withholds SKIP and QUARANTINE candidates in its selective path. JEV-GAP-FALLBACK tests whether a gap has at least one SEND candidate with confidence at least 0.70. When this condition holds, it uses selective delivery. Otherwise it supplies the compact peer fact cards. The fallback is triggered by the absence of a confident SEND, which includes states where other actions have high confidence. The protocol broadens delivery within the compact-card pool. The supplementary source report and execution task retain the specified fallback behavior. Exact delivered-card logs are part of the experiment archive. The binary diagnostics are parallel judgments of the supplied state. The controller study does not introduce a learned numerical utility function or a second NLI screening layer after the Choice decision. The evaluated change is the substitution of typed decision control for the heuristic relevance/NLI routing policy.

## J.1 Compact and Random Controls 

Compact-All broadcasts the two model-generated initial peer fact cards to each receiver at every update. These cards were produced within the shared state-zero calls. Broadcast only reads and seri-alizes the stored peer fields into the update prompt. It adds no summarizer, selector, or controller model call. Each receiver still makes its scheduled reasoning-update call. The initial cards remain unchanged across rounds. It uses the factual integration interface without relevance selection or NLI gating. Random-K samples from the same initial peer-card pool, matching the number delivered by Jev-GAP-Fallback for each question, seed, receiver, and update. The schedule is inherited from the frozen target run. This controls selection content conditional on the target’s message-count sched-ule. It does not randomize when the target requests messages. New Random-K trajectories are generated from the shared initial states. Message-count matching leaves small token differences because selected cards have different lengths. Random-K uses 97.685% of fallback’s MuSiQue payload and 97.759% of its 2Wiki payload. The common-state study retains two candidates per receiver. Compact-All transmits both cards. A count-matched random decision is iden-tical to all-card delivery whenever both candidates are selected. These are properties of the evaluated candidate pool.

For corrected 2Wiki, the recorded protocol additionally instructs agents to answer unknown when their available evidence is insuffi-cient. MuSiQue supplies the assigned source paragraph. ## N.2 Fact-Level Communication 

Architecture conditions share one initial response containing rea-soning, a local fact, and gap queries. Source-identified fact cards remain fixed across updates. The field names below normalize this extended interface. 

P4. Shared initial reasoning and fact card. 

> Question: {question} Your assigned evidence: {local_evidence} Produce a compact local fact supported by your evidence and identify information gaps relevant to the question. Also produce exactly three reasoning steps, a conclusion, and a short answer in the same response. Return these fields: {"local_fact": "...", "gaps": ["..."], "steps": ["...", "...", "..."], "conclusion": "...", "answer": "..."}

The runtime attaches the sender/source identifier to the emit-ted fact. It extracts the fact directly from this response. Fact-card generation and initial reasoning therefore share one model call. 

P5. Fact-level integration update. 

> Question: {question} Your current reasoning state: {current_reasoning_and_answer} Your current information gaps: {current_gaps} Delivered peer facts and source IDs: {delivered_fact_cards} Use the delivered facts to address your information gaps and update your reasoning.
