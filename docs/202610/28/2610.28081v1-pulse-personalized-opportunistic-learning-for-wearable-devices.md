## 摘要
可穿戴设备日益支持个性化健康与福祉应用，但部署后获取的数据可能与训练数据存在显著差异；与此同时，纯本地自适应可能受限于单台设备在真实运行过程中可获取的数据量。传统的联邦学习和去中心化学习依赖周期性通信与稳定连接，而现有的机会式方法往往利用可用的设备相遇机会，却未充分考虑其效用、兼容性或通信成本。我们提出 PULSE，这是一种事件驱动的个性化机会式学习框架，将每次设备相遇视为在线协作决策，在不进行交互与选择有潜力的邻近设备之间作出选择。仅当同伴梯度与本地学习方向兼容时，才将其纳入更新；同时，利用过往交互指导后续的同伴选择。在采用合成与真实接触轨迹的动作识别、跌倒检测和压力识别任务中，与机会式模型平均相比，PULSE 在保持或提升个性化分类性能的同时，将同伴辅助通信次数减少了 80%–94%。设备端实验进一步证明了该方法在可穿戴硬件上的可行性：同伴辅助操作的能耗较低，完整实现仅占用 149.8 KiB 的 RAM 和 561.8 KiB 的闪存。结果表明，选择性机会式协作能够在有效实现个性化的同时，降低资源受限可穿戴设备的通信与资源开销。

## Abstract
Wearable devices increasingly support personalized health and well-being applications, but post-deployment data can differ substantially from those used for training, while purely local adaptation may be limited by the data available on a single device during real-world operation. Conventional federated and decentralized learning rely on recurrent communication and stable connectivity, whereas existing opportunistic approaches often exploit available encounters without adequately considering their utility, compatibility, or communication cost.   We propose PULSE, an event-driven framework for personalized opportunistic learning that treats each encounter as an online collaboration decision, selecting either no interaction or a promising nearby peer. Peer gradients are incorporated only when compatible with the local learning direction, while past interactions guide future peer selection. Across human activity recognition, fall detection, and stress recognition under synthetic and real contact traces, PULSE preserves or improves personalized classification performance, while reducing peer-assisted exchanges by 80-94% compared with opportunistic model averaging. On-device experiments further demonstrate its feasibility on wearable hardware: peer-assisted operations require limited energy, while the complete implementation occupies 149.8 KiB of RAM and 561.8 KiB of flash. The obtained results show that selective opportunistic collaboration can provide effective personalization while limiting communication and resource demands on constrained wearable devices.
