# Co 2Gen: Collaboratively Generating Task Plans for Decentralized Multi-Robot and Human Collaboration 

Yi Loo 1, Siddhant Shrivastava 1, Minh Long Pham 1 and Malika Meghjani 1

Abstract — Visual Language Action (VLA) models are de-signed to be generalist robot models that can be implemented across embodiments, tasks and environments. However, their ability to collaborate with other robots and humans remains underexplored. In this work, we propose a framework to automate the generation of multi-robot collaborative data for decentralized VLA training without requiring human teleoper-ation. Our framework first utilizes a decentralized collaborative planning approach to generate multi-robot collaborative robot plans in the form of an executable function. We then utilize the collaborative plan to produce collaborative demonstration data to finetune VLA models in a decentralized manner. We show that our approach produces demonstration data that when used to finetune VLA models on decentralized collaboration tasks matches the task performance of VLA models finetuned on human-teleoperated collaborative data. 

I. I NTRODUCTION 

Visual Language Action (VLA) models [2, 3, 6, 11] unify visual perception, language understanding and low-level robot actions into a single model, enabling them to generalize across embodiments, tasks and environments. Modern VLA models pre-train on large scale human demonstration datasets [10, 18] and utilize Supervised Finetuning (SFT) on smaller task specific datasets for task adaptation [11, 21]. If these VLA models are to be deployed on robots in real world environments, they invariably have to operate in the vicinity or even directly collaborate and coordinate with other robots and humans. However, the modren VLA models’ ability to perform collaborative tasks with other robots or even humans in zero-shot remains underexplored. In this paper, we show that VLA models, even when fine-tuned on task specific single robot demonstrations, fail to co-ordinate with each other on collaborative manipulation tasks as most of the pretrained data of VLA models come from non-collaborative single robot demonstration data. Hence, the VLA models fail to learn to coordinate their policy execution with one another. We then show that finetuning on demonstration data that is collected collaboratively in a multi-robot manner does improve VLA models performance on decentralized collaborative tasks. Furthermore, we extend these results to zero-shot human collaboration as well. However, the data collection process for simultaneous multi-robot collaborative data can prove challenging due to either additional human cost for each additional robot or the lack of teleoperation interfaces to collect said demonstration

To bridge this gap, we propose Co 2Gen: Collabora-tive Plan Generation for Decentralized VLA Training for Collaborative Tasks, a framework to autonomously generate collaborative data for multi-robot systems without multi-robot collaborative demonstrations. Our key insight is that collaborative robot demonstrations require the coordination and sequencing of each individual robot’s sub-tasks to be conditioned on the successful sub-task execution and state of another robot. For example in Figure 1, the arm on the right should only perform its sub-task of placing its cube when the cabinet is in an open position. Hence, any collaborative plan would need to sequence robot sub-tasks conditioned on both environment state and sub-tasks of other robots. Given a collaborative task prompt and environment state, our proposed approach, Co 2Gen, first leverages Visual Lan-guage Models (VLM) to generate a collaborative execution plan in the form of executable Pythonic function [14, 22]. We structure the plan generation process to be decentralized, where each robot utilizes a VLM to collaboratively propose and refine the generated execution plan using a collaborative Monte Carlo Tree Search (MCTS) process [19, 25]. The resultant execution plans composes and calls on individual skills that are available for each robot and utilizes VLMs as a semantic verifier to check for sub-task preconditions for each robot skill execution. We then utilize the generated collaborative execution plan arXiv:2610.30671v1 [cs.RO] 22 Oct 2026 to generate collaborative robot demonstration data together with a human verifier to check for task success. Finally, we finetune the VLAs on resultant demonstration data. Each robot’s VLA model is finetuned separately in a decentralized manner from its local data stream. We show that it enables VLAs to match the task execution success rate of VLAs fine-tuned on human teleoperated collaborative demonstrations. We further show that they also retain the match the for zero-shot human-robot collaboration across manipulation tasks. To summarize, our contributions are: 1) We show that VLA models fail to generalize to simple collaborative tasks even when finetuned on task spe-cific single-robot demonstrations. 2) We show that collaborative robot demonstrations fine-tuning massively improves VLA models’ performance on collaborative tasks. 3) We propose Co 2Gen, an autonomous way to produce collaborative robot data through an executable Python function that composes single robot skills into collab-orative plans. The collaborative plan is also generated in a decentralized manner by utilizing a collaborative MCTS process. II. R ELATED WORK 

Foundation Models for Single and Multi-Robot Sys-tems. Since their introduction, many works leverage the sematic reasoning capabilities of FMs for robot planning and task execution. Most notably, SayCan [1] matches lan-guage instructions with low-level robot skills through a pre-trained value function for skill selection in open ended tasks. ProgPrompt [22] and CaP [14] leverage the code writing capabilities of LLMs to generate robot policies in the form of code given access to low-level skills, code libraries and environment specifications for single robot task execution. In our work, we extend this capability to generate robot plans for collaborative tasks. In the multi-robot context, SMART-LLM [9] utilizes a central LLM planner to perform task decomposition and allocation for a heterogeneous multi-robot system whereas LaMMA-P [24] utilizes the central LLM planner to generate PDDL problem descriptions for each robot and a PDDL planner at the task execution level. RoCo [16] utilizes multi-agent dialog to generate sub-task plain which then fed to a centralized motion planner. For our approach, we utilized multi-agent dialog to perform collaborative tree search to generate a programmatic collaborative plan that decomposes and allocates sub tasks while also coordinating sub-task sequencing by checking for sub-task pre-conditions. Each robot then executes their sub-task via a decentralized planner. 

VLA models for Multi-Robot Tasks. Though initial VLA models are primarily applied to single robot tasks [3, 4, 11], recent works have explored applying VLA models to multi-robot systems, primarily on the long standing problem of bi-manual manipulation [12, 15]. These approaches utilize information sharing between arms to coordinate each of their policies. InterAct [13] uses Hierarchical Attention En-coder to facilitate information sharing between robot arm policies. TwinVLA [7] utilizes cross attention between the VLA models of both robot arms for information exchange and coordination. MolmoAct2 [6] couples an embodied-reasoning VLM backbone with a flow-matching continuous action expert to enable coordinated dual-arm manipulators. SkillVLA [23] proposes a hierarchical reasoning approach to bi-manual manipulation, where a high level VLM generates sub-tasks for each individual arm which are then passed on to low-level VLM linked to each arm’s action experts. In our work, we extend bi-manual manipulation to a true multi-robot system where each arm has a separate policy via a VLA model. To our knowledge, we are the first to explore VLA models’ ability to collaboratively execute tasks in a decentralized manner and to collaborate with humans in a zero shot manner. III. A. Collaborative Plan Generation Module. 

An effective multi-robot execution plan would need to reason about the constraints and preconditions to schedule and allocate sub-tasks to the appropriate robot. For example, an allocated sub-task or skill of one robot should only be Fig. 2: Our bi-manual arm setup for all of collaborative tasks. scheduled to be executed after the successful execution sub-task execution from another robot. To that end, we identify three crucial features of such a plan: (i) Identify sub-task pre-conditions, (ii) Perform sub-task allocation and (iii) Schedule sub-tasks for overall task coordination. We propose a collaborative plan generation module that leverages VLMs to generate the collaborative plan in form of a executable Pythonic function. Our motivation for utilizing a code based execution plan is two-fold. First, LLMs and VLMs have demonstrated their ability to generate functions [14, 22] in robotic plan generation that connects environmen-tal inputs with relevant robot skills. In the context of multi-robot collaboration, this allows the function to condition low-level robot skills on environment feedback. Second, a code-based robot execution plan enables decentralized parallel execution for multi-robot systems. We also design the plan generation module itself to be fully collaborative and decentralized. We adopt the approach by [25] to generate and iteratively refine the collaborative executable function via collaborative MCTS. Each of the two robot’s plan generator is represented as a VLM agent, one assigned as an actor and the other a critic. Both actor and critic are given the overhead camera observation of the task environment together with a collaborative task prompt and the set of available single robot skills S. The generation process starts with the actor agent proposing an initial execution function. The critic then evaluates and scores the generated function and provides targeted feedback to the actor. This initial proposal and critique serve as the root node

Expansion Once a leaf node is reached, the module then expands the tree with n new child nodes. Each child node’s plan is a refinement of the parent node’s plan based on the critique in the parent node. 

Evaluation The critic agent gives a score r for each generated plan based on three different criteria, the score of each criteria range from 1 to 5 for a maximum score of 15 .The three scoring criteria for each plan are: (i) Progress Score evaluates how much the generated plan makes meaningful progress towards the task. (ii) Distribution Score evaluates how much the generated plan allocates the appropriate sub-task to each robot. (iii) Condition Check Score evaluates whether the plan utilizes environment feedback to check for sub-task pre-conditions. 

Backpropagation The total critic score r is back-propagated from the evaluated node to the root. The visitation count and value at each node along the traversal path are updated as follows:

B. Task Execution Module 

We then utilize the generated collaborative execution plan to produce collaborative trajectories by connecting the semantic understanding of the environment with low-level robot skills. On the semantic understanding side, the collaborative plan uses VLMs to query pre-conditions in the environment via the overhead camera and individual wrist cameras. The low level robot skills consist of a perception Fig. 3: The system diagram for our proposed collaborative plan generation approach. module and trajectory planner. During task execution, the perception module utilizes hybrid blob detection and Yolov8 [8] via the overhead depth camera to obtain rough object coordinate estimates. The trajectory planner then utilizes an Inverse Kinematic (IK) solver [5] to generate robot trajectories towards the detected object coordinates. Finally, the robot arms use their wrist cameras to get precise object coordinates for grasping. 

C. Collaborative Data Generation 

Here we describe our proposed framework for collabora-tive data collection. We incorporate a human into the data collection loop as a task verifier. After each task execution, the human verifier checks if the task is successfully executed. Successfully executed robot episodes are then collected into a demonstration dataset Dtask . We chose a human verifier over direct VLM verification to mitigate any false positive trajectories from being included into the Dtask due to VLM errors/hallucinations. We show our data collection framework diagrammatically in Figure 5. 

D. Decentralized VLA Finetuning 

Finally we perform Supervised Finetuning (SFT) on VLAs using the collected collaborative task dataset Dtask . We split 

Dtask into robot specific data streams Dntask . Each data-stream consists of the robot’s joint state, wrist camera stream and shares the overhead stereo camera stream. We then finetune each robot’s VLA in a decentralized manner on their respective data streams. We propose two distinct approaches to utilize Dtask for VLA model training. The first approach trains a VLA model with Dtask as is and second approach combines Dtask with single robot human demonstration data. Single robot human demonstrations can produce diverse and varied trajectories but lack the coordination signal that is inherent to collaborative tasks, whereas the trajectories in 

Dtask may lack the diversity of human demonstrations. Combining Dtask , with single human data, results in a hybrid dataset that covers both trajectory diversity and collaborative data without the need for multi-robot human teleoperation. V. E XPERIMENTS

B. Implementation Details 

We use GPT 5.6 [17], specifically gpt-5.6-sol as the VLMs for Collaborative Plan Generation and for Semantic Precondition Checking during collaborative task execution. For the collaborative plan generation MCTS we set the max tree depth dmax = 5 . For the VLA model, we use the lightweight SmolVLA [21], specifically smolvla base 

across all of our experiments. For each collaborative task we collect 50 episodes of demonstration data and finetune for 20000 steps. We also use DAgger [20] to collect further correction data to improve the reliability of each model. The robot episodes are split evenly by collaborative roles for each arm.

In this work, we introduce Co 2Gen, a framework to gen-erate data for VLAs to collaborate in a decentralized manner without the need for human teleoperated collaborative data. We show that a collaborative Python function generated via a collaborative MCTS process can act as a collaborative multi-robot execution plan by verifying sub-task preconditions through VLMs. We then show that the generated collabo-rative plan can substitute human teleoperation to produce collaborative multi-robot data for VLA finetuning. Finally, we show the VLA models finetuned on Co 2Gen data match the task performance of VLA models finetuned on human teleoperated collaborative data. For future work, we hope to extend this work across dif-ferent embodiments such as humanoids and mobile robots on longer horizon collaborative tasks in complex environments and in greater team sizes.
