Abstract 

Adapting a forecasting system to a changing world classically means retraining. We ask whether the task of continually learning regimes for time series forecasting can be represented by a piece of text: a guidance block, evolved by outcome-driven reflection and used in a frozen LLM forecaster’s prompt. On a refreshed multimodal benchmark (weekly search indices, daily currencies, daily commodities, each window paired with web-retrieved news), we run GEPA-style prompt evolution under strict contamination control to prevent the data leakage common in time series benchmarks: the forecaster’s knowledge cutoff predates every evaluated window. A ∼900-word evolved guidance carried by Llama-3.3-70B beats Time-Series Foundation Models (TSFMs) on held-out data (GM MASE 0.776 vs. 0.898 for TabPFN-TS-3), transfers to more recent models without measurable loss, and costs dollars rather than GPU-days to update. We characterize the loop: out-of-loop gains concentrate in the first reflection, in-loop improvements overstate out-of-loop gains 2–3× unless an untouched monitor arbitrates, and error-only objectives silently suppress the text modality. We provide two extensions. First, compiling the guidance into executable code with a TSFM as a callable tool recovers most of the gain deterministically. Second, ablations show the advantage widens at short horizons and that removing the news text does not hurt the guided forecaster, consistent with the text’s value being distilled into policy during learning. We argue that continual prompt evolution is a practical, training-free alternative to retraining for multimodal forecasting. 

1 Introduction 

Time series forecasting supports decision-making in numerous high-stakes domains, and the advent of Time Series Foundation Models (TSFMs) [1, 3, 4] has established a unified, zero-shot forecasting paradigm for numerical sequences. However, relying solely on structured numerical inputs isolates these models from the broader real-world narratives that drive structural breaks and regime shifts; conversely, large language models (LLMs) parse unstructured context easily but are uneven numerical

forecasters [5, 6]. Benchmarks that pair numeric histories with contemporaneous news context [7, 8] report that carefully engineered LLM pipelines can exploit text, but the engineering is manual, static, and brittle under distribution shift. This paper treats the problem as one of continual learning with the learned state living in prompt rather than weights. When a forecasting system must track a changing world, the standard approach is retraining: fine-tuning the model on recent windows, or re-pretraining the TSFM. We instead freeze every model weight for all the models in use and continually learn a guidance text : a block of up to 

∼1,000 words inputs into the forecaster’s prompt, telling it how to approach a window. Learning proceeds through outcome-driven evolutionary loop in the spirit of GEPA [10], a prompt-optimization method in which an LLM reflects in natural language on execution traces to propose prompt mutations, and candidates are kept on an instance-level Pareto front rather than winner-takes-all. Concretely: (1) a LLM forecasts training windows with the current guidance, (2) a reflector LLM reads the full execution traces (what the news said, what the numbers did, what the model predicted, what actually happened) and rewrites the guidance, (3) an acceptance gate discards rewrites that do not beat their parent on fresh windows, and (4) survivors are scored on a fixed selection set under instance-level Pareto selection. No gradient exists anywhere. Every version of the learned artifact is readable, diffable, and auditable: one can point at any rule and ask which windows taught you that? 

Our study runs on a refreshed copy of the TimesX multimodal benchmark [7] (data through March 2026; §3), under contamination control by construction: the default forecaster (Llama-3.3-70B, Dec-2023 cutoff) predates every evaluated window. The stronger executor used for transfer and code-compilation experiments (Claude Opus 4.6, Aug-2025 cutoff) is evaluated strictly on windows whose origins postdate its cutoff. The reflector writes only general ex-ante rules outcome-gated on windows it never saw, and final test tiers are spent exactly once. 

Our contributions are the following: 

1. A continually learned prompt beats TSFMs on their own terms (§5): the evolved guidance Gnum makes Llama-3.3-70B outperform TimesFM-2.5, TabPFN-TS-3, and Chronos-2 on two held-out tiers, updating for dollars with no weight changes. 2. The artifact is portable (§5.4): guidance evolved for one forecaster transfers to a stronger one (Claude Opus 4.6) without measurable loss (the transferred arm overall results are statistically indistinguishable from the originally evolved model). 3. A measured anatomy of the loop (§5.3): the depth of prompt evolutions tracks the artifact’s capacity, with one reflection capturing most of what a relatively small fixed-size text prompt can gain; in-loop improvements overstate held-out gains 2–3×; and accuracy-only objectives teach the forecaster to ignore the news text, on two different LLMs. 4. Guidance compiles to code (§6): a coding LLM compiles each guidance into a deterministic function with TabPFN as a callable tool and an LLM arbiter; the learned composition beats every naive one and posts the best SearchTrend score. 5. Ablations that locate the value (§7): the advantage widens at short horizons (re-evolving for h=3 

does not beat truncating); removing the unstructured news text entirely does not hurt the guided forecaster, consistent with its decision-relevant content being distilled into the (predominantly numeric) policy during learning, so inference can skip context retrieval. 

2 Related work 

LLMs and context for time series. Encoding observations as strings enables zero-shot LLM extrapolation [5]. Reprogramming approaches adapt frozen LLM backbones [14, 15], though ablations question how much the backbone itself contributes [6]. Context-aided benchmarks, CiK [8] and TimesX [7], pair numeric histories with textual context. Agentic decompositions such as Nexus [9] calibrate guidelines from backtests. Our loop instead makes the guideline itself the sole, continually evolved learning object under explicit selection pressure and overfitting monitors. FLAIRR-TS [2] refines a forecaster’s prompt per task at test time, whereas we learn one guidance 2across all series at training time, monitor it out-of-loop, and deploy it frozen. PostTime [16] post-trains an LLM (SFT then RLVR) on TimesX to revise, preserve, or ignore a TimesFM-2.5 prior; we target the same LLM-over-TSFM division of labor (§6) without any weight update, learning it as an auditable, transferable text artifact. 

TSFMs. TimesFM-2.5 [1] operates on univariate numeric histories, whereas Chronos-2 [3] and TabPFN-TS-3 [4] also accept numerical covariates. We evaluate all three in their standard numeric-only configurations, as baselines and, in the compiled extension, as tools the learned guidance decides when to trust, blend, or override. 

Prompt optimization and continual learning. Our loop instantiates reflective prompt evolution [10]: natural-language reflection over execution traces as the mutation operator, an instance-level Pareto front as the selection operator. Related work includes textual-gradient optimization [11] and self-refinement [12]. We contribute a forecasting-specific instantiation with strict temporal integrity, a study of its behaviour, and the compile-to-code extension. Classical continual learning updates weights under stability–plasticity constraints [13]; here the entire learned state is a short text, so updates are cheap, reversible, versioned, and “forgetting” is a visible diff. 

3 Benchmark and evaluation protocol 

Data. We use a refreshed copy of the TimesX benchmark [7] (through 2026-03-31), a leakage-free, automated data generation pipeline of high-quality real-world multimodal time series with textual context: SearchTrend (weekly Google search-interest indices, 0–100, ∼120 topics), Currency (daily USD rates, 10 currencies), and CommodityPrice (daily prices, ∼60 commodities). Each window provides a 96-step history, a 12-step target, and text available at the forecast origin : a background description, ∼8–10 dated news events as prose, sibling-series statistics, and holidays in the prediction window. Forecasts are scored by MASE (error scaled by the series’ typical seasonal-step change, so SeasonalNaive ≈1), aggregated as geometric means (GM); contrasts are paired per-window deltas with standard errors. 

Contamination control and tiers. Evaluating LLMs on windows preceding their cutoff measures capability plus memory: LLM forecast error rises markedly once windows postdate the model’s cutoff [7]. Our default forecaster is Llama-3.3-70B (Dec-2023 cutoff, clean on every evaluated window); the reflector (Claude Opus 5) is not required to have a cutoff preceding the data, but only writes only general, ex-ante rules (no variable names, no memorized outcomes) that are outcome-gated on windows it never saw. Reflector leakage is enforced against three ways: (1) binding prompt constraints (“ex-ante only, no variable names from the evidence, no memorized post-origin outcomes”), (2) structurally, guidance is plain text applied to windows disjoint from those reflected on, so a memorized specific has no key to fire on without naming a series or date, and (3) auditability, every artifact is human-readable, was read, and contains no post-origin dates, values, or events. Cross-model experiments with Claude Opus 4.6 (Aug-2025 cutoff) use only origins after September 2025. Optimization consumes data at three rates: a selection tier consulted every iteration (optimistic by construction); an untouched monitor never used for decisions, whose gap to selection measures overfitting; and final tiers set postdating every model’s cutoff touched exactly once for evaluation.

5 Main results 

5.1 The evolved prompt beats TSFMs on held-out data 

Table 1 is the paper’s central result: on the most recent, provably uncontaminated data, all four evolved cells beat all three zero-shot TSFMs overall (nominally best: Llama with the ∼900-word 4Gnum guidance, GM 0.776 vs. TabPFN-TS-3’s 0.898). On Currency, the best TSFM is nominally ahead (0.853 vs. 0.864), a statistically indistinguishable gap (paired ∆ 0 .045 ± 0.039 , evolved arm winning 44% of windows). Both unguided frontier LLMs sit at SeasonalNaive : raw LLM capability contributes nothing typical-case, and the entire gap between useless and state of the art is carried by a piece of text. Where does the value come from? It is domain-concentrated : event-heavy daily commodities improve 

∼ 2× over card-only baselines, while weekly search barely improves, and on post-refresh 2025– 26 SearchTrend windows every method, TSFMs included, is worse than SeasonalNaive. Reading the evolved text (Appendix A) explains the mechanism: G num is a regime-first numeric policy 

(damped random-walk anchors for daily series, calendar-analog seasonality for weekly ones, explicit spike-decay and off-season floors), with news demoted to a tightly scoped role: “dated, quantified, in-horizon shocks only, applied at half the believed size.” TimesX’s own benchmark-level insight, that an explicit no-change option is the single most valuable ingredient, is rediscovered by evolution and sharpened into quantitative, situation-scoped caution rules. 

Outperforming TimesX benchmark’s strongest naive composition. TimesX’s best composed method, the simple TSFM+LLM average, scores 0.979 on our tier, worse than TimesFM alone 

(0.939): post-cutoff, the LLM half contributes SeasonalNaive-grade noise. Averaging a TSFM into the evolved arm also hurts (TabPFN+G num : 0.887 vs. 0.776); only averaging the two evolved artifacts edges the best single (0.770, within one SE). Composition pays only when every ingredient carries learned guidance (Appendix B; cf. §6). 

5.2 Cost: prompt updates versus retraining 

An accepted iteration costs ≈$2 of API calls; a full campaign with its final evaluation is ≈$50–100 on no dedicated hardware. The artifact updates by diff : adapting to a new regime means one more reflection over recent traces, not a retraining run, and every update is a readable, reversible text change. The price is inference-time length ( ∼1k extra prompt tokens) and the pathologies quantified next. 

5.3 Prompt evolution dynamics insights 

Three findings replicated across independent experiment designs: 

(i) Generation-1 dominance. Essentially all out-of-loop gains arrive in the first reflection; 8–13 further operator applications per campaign at best tied the champion on the untouched monitor, and merges went 0-for-3 against best-parent baselines. One pass over ∼50 windows of traces captures most of what ∼900 words can encode here (see §9 for why this does not obviate the loop). 

(ii) Selection optimism dominates. Gains measured on data the loop consults ran 2–3× the untouched-monitor deltas ( ∼40% optimism in the champion experiment). An out-of-loop monitor is a design necessity for reflective self-improvement. 

(iii) Error-only objectives suppress the text modality. Under a MASE-only objective, evolution converges to text-gating guidance and directional recall collapses, rediscovered across experiment designs and frontier executor’s own traces. On this corpus, where news is largely redundant with the numeric history, the selection is accurate: text slightly hurts guided inference (§7.3) and contributes only calibration during learning (§7.4). Text usage in the guidance survived only under an explicit direction objective.

6 Extended experiment: compiling guidance to code, with a TSFM in the loop 

Reading the evolved champions (Appendix A) reveals that they are heavy on time-series features 

(anchors, damped drifts, seasonal analogs, spike rules) i.e. largely executable content. This motivates the question: how much of the learned guidance survives being compiled into deterministic code, and can the loop then learn to use a TSFM as a tool rather than compete with it? 

Setup. The genome is still a guidance text (word cap 6,000; it may contain formulas and literal Python) but its phenotype is a two-stage runtime. (1) A coding LLM (Claude Opus 4.6; all data postdates its cutoff) compiles each candidate once into a deterministic forecast(window) function in an offline sandbox against a 12-window dev set; the session is engineering-only by contract, capped at 6 tool turns, and the harness independently re-runs the scorer on the final code, so implementation quality can neither block nor fake a candidate. (2) Per window, the code emits a proposal ; a final LLM pass sees the window, guidance, code, proposal, and default TabPFN forecast, and makes the final call. The code receives only ex-ante inputs including a precomputed TabPFN-TS-3 forecast and may re-invoke TabPFN live with engineered inputs (transformed targets, truncated context, event flags parsed from the text); TabPFN is the only learned model permitted, and the code stage evaluates at zero LLM cost. Internal baselines: V0 , raw TabPFN passthrough; V0L , that proposal plus an 

unguided final LLM. 

What the guidance learned to do with the TSFM. The champion’s text (Appendix C) partitions windows by route and assigns TabPFN a different role in each: lead on smooth daily commodities (weight up to 0.65, rule drift shrunk), ignore on staircase series (hold the plateau), blend into a strict year-over-year calendar analog on weekly search, and feed with engineered inputs where events matter, while the final LLM is held to tight pass-through discipline: an auditable division of labor mirroring the text-gating that pure prompt evolution discovered. 

Results. Twelve iterations produced eight accepted candidates. We attribute the longer runway to the richer phenotype: the reflector now debugs mechanisms (“the year-over-year ratio was computed against a window centred on the analog, double-counting the seasonal phase; construct it strictly YoY”) rather than only rewriting advice. G code scores GM 0.807 (Commodity 0.608 / Currency 0.845 / SearchTrend 1.042), ahead of every TSFM, every unguided arm, and every naive TSFM-involving ensemble, and statistically tied with the best evolved arms overall (within one SE of Llama+G num ,GM ratio 1.039) (Table 2). Three sub-results stand out: (1) its SearchTrend 1.042 is the best of any experiment (next: 1.110; Llama+G num : 1.422), the reflector’s YoY mechanism being the first real progress; G code beats every naive TSFM+LLM average (best cell 0.854), so the learned division of labor is worth ∼0.05 GM over per-window averaging; and the code stage runs in milliseconds per window, deployable as an auditable program with one optional LLM arbitration.

7.4 Ablating text from the reflection loop 

We re-run the campaign in two arms sharing seeded minibatches: arm A’s reflector sees full execution traces, arm B’s sees the same traces with all text withheld. The forecaster’s prompt is identical in both arms, and each frozen champion is spent once on the 249-window tier. Arm A, a fresh-seed replica of the original campaign, lands at 0.741 versus G num ’s 0.776 (GM ratio 0.954 ± 0.019 ), so seed variance is of the same order as the effect and only the paired A–B contrast carries causal weight: text-blind B reaches 0.768, worse than A by +0 .036 ± 0.015 log-MASE (GM ratio 1.037, same sign in all domains), yet recovering nearly all of the guided gain (unguided 1.043). The artifacts explain the gap: B writes news rules from priors alone (“Never push to a new record extreme without hard, dated, quantified news”) but cannot calibrate them, while A fits amplitudes (“a major dated event for that exact topic justifies +30–60% above baseline in the one or two weeks containing it”). Text evidence teaches the calibration of event rules. The rest of the policy comes from numeric errors and world-knowledge priors. 

8 Continual deployment: a protocol and one executed cycle 

A continual deployment protocol. We propose a pre-registered rolling loop over the deployed guidance gt, run whenever a new batch of outcomes resolves (period t). When period t closes: (1) record gt’s performance on a fresh monitor sample from t; (2) re-adapt : at most four gated reflection iterations on t’s resolved windows; (3) promote conservatively : ship gt+1 only if it beats gt

on held-back validation; (4) deploy on period t+1 . The full multi-period run reports deployed MASE against frozen-g1 and re-evolved-from-scratch baselines, cost, and forgetting, which is uniquely inspectable here as a text diff of deleted rules. Gnum , evolved on 2024 data and deployed blind on 2025–26, kept its commodity and FX gains but broke at the SearchTrend regime shift (1.42, Table 1). We ran steps (2)–(4) on that break: four reflection iterations over resolved post-refresh search windows promoted the first child, which repaired the domain-specific performance. Since the update helps the broken domain but slightly harms the others, the natural deployment is per-domain: keep the original G num for commodities and FX and use the updated guidance only for SearchTrend. As a single global artifact the update also exhibits the stability–plasticity trade-off concretely: small, text-diff-inspectable forgetting on the daily domains. Routing the update to the broken domain only sets a new best on the final tier ( 0.760 ). Continual re-adaptation is thus cheap, auditable, and effective, with forgetting managed by routing. 

9 Discussion and limitations 

What continual prompt evolution buys: a weight-free loop with an auditable, portable, versioned artifact updateable for dollars, turning a frozen open-weights LLM from SeasonalNaive-equivalent into the strongest single method here. Does generation-1 dominance invalidate the loop? The plateau tracks artifact capacity , not the loop: three protocols sharing the ≤1k-word text phenotype all plateaued, while the wider phenotype of §6 kept improving into generation 2, its mutations changing in kind from rephrasing advice to debugging mechanisms. And under generation-1 dominance the 8loop’s role shifts from search to verification : selection ran 2–3× optimistic and champions were rarely the first child, so the gate and untouched monitor are what make a generation-1 artifact shippable. For continual deployment this is an asset: each quarter is a fresh generation-1 problem, hence K≤4.Section 8’s executed cycle grounds the continual claim: a measured regime break was repaired, with forgetting made inspectable and managed by routing; the multi-period rolling run is future work. 

Limitations and future work. All current findings rest on one benchmark family. The caution-first character of every evolved policy, and the result that guided inference can discard the news text, may be joint properties of the MASE objective and of web-retrieved context whose events are often already priced in [8]. Generation-1 sufficiency may likewise reflect the benchmark’s homogeneity (three domains, one window geometry); more heterogeneous corpora may extend the useful depth of evolution, as the richer code phenotype already hints. The LLM arms also receive calendar, holiday, and sibling-series information that pure-sequence TSFMs do not; a controlled parity study would locate how much of the remaining gap is information rather than policy. Finally, the rolling deployment of §8 carries one executed cycle; the multi-period run is future work.

9A The G num and G text evolved prompts 

Gnum is the numeric-policy champion and G text the text-using one. G num was evolved in a single reflection over a 16-window minibatch of traces, never on the untouched monitor beaten in 10 subsequent iterations. Verbatim original G num :

# FORECAST GUIDANCE Your dominant historical failure mode is **inventing trends**: taking the last few points’ direction ( or a news narrative’s "bullish/bearish" tone) and extrapolating a smooth 12-step ramp. This lost badly again and again. MASE rewards the **conditional median path**, which for most of these series is close to flat, or close to last year’s same-calendar shape. Follow the procedure below. ## 0. Core priors (apply before any reasoning about news) 1. **News text almost never tells you the level path.** Use it only for: (a) scheduled, dated events falling *inside* the horizon; (b) confirming which calendar season you are in. Reports dated weeks /months before the origin are already in the price/index -- do not re-apply them as a directional push. 2. **No monotone 12-step ramps** unless justified by a repeated calendar pattern in the series’ own history. 3. Total movement from the last value across the whole horizon should be *small* relative to the series’ historical 12-step swings, unless seasonality says otherwise. When in doubt, flatter is better. 4. Never exceed the historical range; respect hard bounds (search indices 0-100; off-season floors such as 1). ## 1. Decide which regime the series is in **A. Daily series (FX, commodity prices)** -> random-walk regime. **B. Weekly search indices** -> annual-seasonality / event regime. ## 2. Regime A: daily FX & commodity prices These are near-random walks with mild mean reversion at 1-3 week horizons. Momentum in the last 5-10 days carries almost no information. Procedure: 1. Compute ‘L5 = mean(last 5 obs)‘, ‘L20 = mean(last 20 obs)‘, and ‘last‘. 2. **Anchor** = ‘0.6*last + 0.4*L5‘. If ‘last‘ deviates from ‘L20‘ by more than ~1.5x a typical daily move xsqrt5 (i.e. the last 1-3 points are a sharp jump/drop away from the recent plateau), pull the anchor 30-50% of the way back toward ‘L20‘ -- a fresh sharp move is as likely to retrace as to continue. 3. **Drift**: fit a slope over the last ~20 observations, then use **at most 25% of it**, and let it decay to zero by step 6-8. Cap the cumulative 12-step drift at roughly the size of one typical * weekly* move in that series. If the recent window is flat/low-volatility, use zero drift. 4. Output a nearly flat path around the anchor. Repeated identical values in history (stale quotes, discrete price steps, weekends, holidays) mean the series *sticks* -- for such series just repeat the anchor, rounded to the observed price grid, for all 12 steps. Do not step it down or up "to be safe." 5. Only override this with a directional path if the context contains a **dated, quantified, in-horizon ** supply/policy shock (e.g. an announced tariff, an official price change effective on a date inside the horizon). Even then, apply half the move you believe in. Sanity check before submitting: *Would a flat line at the last value beat my path if the series just wobbled?* If your path drifts more than a couple of percent away from the anchor, delete the drift. ## 3. Regime B: weekly search interest The history is ~96 weeks ~ 1.85 years, so the horizon weeks have an **analog one year earlier at indices (t-52)**. That analog is your single most valuable signal -- far more valuable than the last 3 weeks’ direction. Procedure: 1. Extract the 12 values at positions ‘t-52 ... t-41‘ (the same calendar weeks last year) -> ‘S[1..12]‘. 2. Compute a level ratio ‘r = mean(last 8 obs) / mean(the 8 obs ending at t-52)‘. Clip ‘r‘ to [0.7, 1.4], then shrink it toward 1: ‘r’ = 1 + 0.6*(r-1)‘. 3. Base path = ‘r’ * S[i]‘, then **shrink extremes toward the recent local level**: replace each point with ‘0.7*(r’*S[i]) + 0.3*(recent 4-week mean)‘. This keeps the seasonal shape but hedges timing error. 4. Handle spikes explicitly: - If ‘S‘ contains an isolated one/two-week spike (annual event, championship, sale day) and you can tie it to a **calendar date visible in the context or an obvious annual recurrence**, keep it but at ~60-70% of its scaled amplitude, and put a partial bump (~30%) in the adjacent week. - If the spike’s timing is uncertain, flatten it to ~35-45% amplitude spread over 2-3 weeks. A misplaced spike costs double under MAE. - If last year shows an idiosyncratic one-off spike unlikely to repeat, drop it and use the surrounding baseline. 

10 5. **Post-spike decay rule** (critical): if the recent 5-15 weeks contain an unusually large news-driven spike that is already declining, forecast continued decay toward the **pre-spike baseline** (the median of the 20+ weeks before the spike), reaching it within ~3-5 steps, then flat. Never extrapolate the *rise* of a spike or restart one. 6. **Off-season rule**: if the series is currently at its floor (e.g. near-constant minimum) and the same weeks last year were also at the floor, output the floor value repeated. Do not add wiggle. 7. **Deseasonalized flat series**: if the series has no clear annual pattern and is currently mid-range, output the recent 6-8 week median, essentially flat, with at most a small tilt (<=10% of level) in the direction of the last-year analog’s slope. 8. U.S. public holidays inside the horizon: ignore them for weekly search indices and commodity levels unless the topic *is* the holiday. They are not a reason to add fluctuation. ## 4. Final checklist (all domains) - Is my path a straight ramp? -> Almost certainly wrong; flatten or re-derive from the year-earlier analog. - Did any part of the news text change my numbers? -> Justify it with a date inside the horizon; otherwise revert. - Does my first forecast value sit within a typical one-step move of the last observation? -> It must. - Is the spread of my 12 values plausible versus how much this series has actually moved over 12 steps in its own history? -> If larger, compress toward the anchor. - Rounding: match the granularity of the history (integers for search indices, the observed price grid for commodities, 4 decimals for FX). Output exactly the 12 numbers requested; keep reasoning brief and grounded in the numeric history, not the narrative. 

Note what the text is made of: anchors, damping factors, capped drifts, seasonal analogs, spike handling, time-series features and policies , not text comprehension. This observation motivated the compiled extension of §6. Verbatim original G text :

**UNITS.** u = one seasonal unit = the MASE denominator’s typical step change (estimate: mean absolute change at the seasonal lag over the 96-step history). Every judgement below is stated in u. The call is horizon mean - last value, bucketized at +/-1.0u. **STEP 0 -- GATES: what to throw away before reasoning.** 1. **Expired subject.** Items whose reported period closed before the origin (last month’s print, last season’s report, a concluded protest/tour/conference) -> discard for direction. 2. **Wrong-year / scheduled-calendar items.** Notices of future meetings, "committee meets eight times a year", dates outside the horizon -> discard. 3. **Already-absorbed news.** If the series has already made a >=2u move consistent with the item and then flattened, the item is absorbed -> discard. Old news cannot be re-spent as new drift. 4. **Non-mechanical items.** Advocacy, corporate PR, litigation procedure, satisfaction surveys, historical retrospectives, generic macro recaps (CPI/payrolls/PMI/GDP already published) -> discard: these have no reliable sign for the next 12 steps. 5. **Level quotes.** Any absolute price/target/index in text is a different construct than the series; may never set the forecast level. 6. **Mutual cancellation.** Equally fresh and specific items with opposing signs cancel. **STEP 1 -- REGIME CLASSIFICATION (choose exactly one).** - **R1 Daily, flat/low-volatility:** last 10 steps within ~1u, no run. Baseline: hold last value. - **R2 Daily, trending:** >=5 of last 7 steps one direction and last-10 move >=1.5u. Baseline: hold last value plus 50-70% of the 10-step slope, decaying, capped 2.5u total. - **R3 Weekly index, seasonal:** baseline = mean of last 3, plus 60% of the prior-year analog path ( mean of weeks t-51...t-40 minus x_{t-52}) when |analog| >= 1.5u. - **R4 Post-spike / boundary:** last value in the top or bottom decile of the 96-step range, reached in <=6 steps, or a text-named cause has passed. Baseline: revert 1.0-2.0u toward the trailing 12-step mean. - **R5 Exhausted move:** last value at the 96-step extreme after a long slide/rally that has since flattened for >=6 steps. Baseline: hold last value; **never** extend the move. **STEP 1b -- EVENT TAXONOMY (how surviving text modifies the regime baseline).** - **T1 Physical supply/demand change with a date inside or just before the horizon** (new harvest hitting the market, mine/plant outage, export ban, weather-damaged crop, mandated coverage/tariff effective in-horizon): +/-1.0-1.5u, in the direction of scarcity/glut; timing = start within the first 3 steps. - **T2 Two or more independent, quantified, <=6-week-old items agreeing in direction:** +/-1.0u. A single item, however dramatic, is +/-0.0-0.5u. - **T3 Official forecast revisions** (agency/analyst estimates for a season): direction only, +/-0.5u, and only if not contradicted by a fresher revision; never a level anchor. - **T4 Scheduled in-horizon events for search indices** (holidays, launches, campaigns, sports/seasonal peaks): apply the previous cycle’s response at the matching horizon steps, up to +/-2u locally; the whole-horizon mean shift is whatever that shape implies. - **T5 Monetary/policy divergence text for FX:** by itself +/-0u. It counts only as confirmation of R2, adding +0.5u in the direction the price is already moving. - **T6 Event-driven attention spikes for search indices:** the news that caused the spike implies decay, not continuation -> supports R4 downward reversion. 

11 **STEP 2 -- DECISION.** - **D1** Take the regime baseline; add the taxonomy adjustments; cap total deviation of the horizon mean at 2.5u (daily) / 3.0u (weekly). - **D2 Direction lock:** text may not push against the live numeric run. If T-adjustments oppose R2’s slope, keep the numeric side and halve the text side. - **D3 All-or-nothing:** if the resulting horizon-mean deviation is < 1.3u, publish the flat baseline instead (deviations below the bucket edge only cost error). If >=1.3u, shape the path so the deviation is real: reach ~2/3 of it by step 4 and the full amount by step 8, then hold. - **D4 Move-call warrants.** A non-flat call is justified when **any** of: R2 fires cleanly; R3’s analog is >=1.5u; R4/T6 gap to the trailing mean >=1.5u; T1 fires with the numeric trend not opposing. In these cases you must call the move -- declining to call is a scored error, not caution. - **D5 Flat-call warrants.** Stay flat when: R1 or R5 holds and no T1/T4 fires; all text was gated; text conflicts (gate 6); or the only argument for a move is a slope smaller than 1.3u over 12 steps. - **D6 Path hygiene:** no undamped slope extrapolation (worst-performing policy at population level, worst on weekly); no long monotone ramp on a weekly index without an analog behind it; no fine-grained fake volatility. **FAILURE MODES TO AVOID.** - **F1 Recall collapse:** treating flat as the safe answer everywhere. D4 exists to force calls; a run with almost no up/down calls is failing half the objective. - **F2 Stale-narrative drift:** small trends imported from months-old reports, against a flat or opposite recent tape (gates 1-3, D2). This is the dominant way persistence beats us. - **F3 Level import:** steering toward a figure printed in a report (gate 5). - **F4 Momentum on the wrong horizon:** applying daily-style trend continuation to weekly indices, where the annual analog dominates (R3, D6). - **F5 Continuing an exhausted decline into a floor** instead of holding or reverting (R5, R4). - **F6 Under-committed moves:** deviations of 0.3-1.0u that lose MASE against persistence and earn no directional credit (D3). - **F7 Macro-report guessing on FX:** deriving direction from generic economic releases with no live price confirmation (gate 4, T5).

D Compute resources 

No GPUs were self-managed. All LLM inference (forecasting, reflection, compilation, arbitration) used a commercial serverless cloud API, interactively for small batches and via batch inference for evaluations of ≥100 prompts. All local computation ran on a single consumer laptop (Apple-silicon CPU, 32 GB RAM): the TSFM baselines (TabPFN-TS-3 at ∼1 s per window on CPU; TimesFM-2.5; Chronos-2) and the offline Docker sandbox for compiled candidates (4 CPUs, 8 GB memory per container; a 444-window evaluation of a compiled candidate takes 10–15 minutes). Individual runs: one evolution iteration costs minutes to ∼1 hour wall-clock including batch queueing; a full campaign with its final evaluation is several hours; the executed re-adaptation cycle of §8 took under two hours. Every LLM reply is cached, so reproducing any reported number from the released caches requires no API calls.
