## ABSTRACT 

Recent advances in multimodal large language models (MLLMs) have substan-tially improved spoken interaction and video understanding. Existing online and streaming benchmarks primarily assess causal multimodal perception through timestamped question answering, whereas real-world interaction requires mod-els to continuously identify task-relevant multimodal cues and decide whether, when, and how to respond. This leaves open how well current models can sus-tain real-time interaction throughout an evolving multimodal stream. To address this gap, we introduce DIVE , a benchmark for evaluating full-duplex interaction with visual engagement under continuous and realistic multimodal streams. Our benchmark organizes six tasks into three complementary interaction scenarios. 

(1) Proactive Intervention evaluates whether models can identify emerging vi-sual or semantic cues and intervene at the appropriate moment. (2) Continuous Engagement evaluates sustained participation through continuous visual tracking and visually grounded query interaction. (3) Interaction Coordination evaluates how models coordinate listening and speaking under overlapping and multi-party interactions. DIVE contains 525 audio/video samples with fine-grained human annotations and precise temporal metadata, covering six task-oriented interaction settings grounded in realistic scenarios. Its evaluation protocol separates response quality, participation decisions, and response timing. We evaluate representative real-time models using response quality, window response rates, and reaction la-tency. Experiments show that leading real-time models achieve sub-second av-erage response latency, yet their average window response rates remain at or be-low 53.77%. Response quality varies substantially across models and tasks, with adapting ongoing responses to newly arriving information remaining particularly challenging. We hope DIVE facilitates progress toward more natural and capable real-time multimodal interactive systems.

## 2 RELATED WORKS 

2.1 DUPLEX MULTIMODAL MODELS 

Duplex modeling has been explored most extensively in real-time speech interaction. The GPT-Realtime series OpenAI (2025) and Gemini 3.1 Flash Live Google DeepMind (2026b) provide low-latency end-to-end speech interaction. Moshi D´ efossez et al. (2024) and MoshiRAG Chien et al. (2026) support simultaneous listening and speaking, and OmniFlatten Zhang et al. (2025) enables seamless full-duplex spoken dialogue within a unified autoregressive framework. Related efforts extend real-time speech modeling in complementary directions: MoshiVis Royer et al. (2025) augments duplex speech interaction with visual context, while Voxtral Realtime Liu et al. (2026a) develops stream-synchronous modeling for low-latency speech recognition. More recent systems move toward multimodal duplex interaction. Proprietary systems such as TML Interaction Mod-els Lab (2026) and SeedRealtime Seeddance (2026) advance real-time interaction to full-duplex multimodal settings, continuously processing audio, video, and text inputs while producing con-current outputs over ongoing interaction streams. In parallel, openly released models such as Du-plexOmni Huang et al. (2026b) and Aero Realtime Zhang et al. (2026b) pursue the same paradigm. Together, these systems represent a shift from turn-based multimodal interaction toward continuous full-duplex modeling, where incoming multimodal observations and model responses are jointly coordinated throughout the interaction. 2.2 STREAMING AND DUPLEX BENCHMARKS 

Recent benchmarks have increasingly evaluated real-time and full-duplex interaction beyond con-ventional offline understanding. Full-Duplex-Bench Lin et al. (2025; 2026a), FLEXI Ge et al. (2025), HumDial-FDBench Wang et al. (2026a), EchoChain Modi et al. (2026), DuplexSLA Zhang et al. (2026a), and DuplexWorld Bhosale et al. (2026) primarily focus on spoken interaction, cov-ering turn-taking, interruption, conversational state tracking, and response timing. More recently, evaluation has expanded toward multimodal settings. VideoFDB Mazumdar et al. (2026) evaluates audio-visual conversational capabilities, while Omni-DuplexEval He et al. (2026) and OmniInter-act Lu et al. (2026b) further broaden the scope of evaluation to include proactive response, contin-uous monitoring, and interruption-aware interaction over streaming multimodal inputs. In contrast, DIVE complements these settings with explicit task-level requirements for risk intervention, re-sponse revision, repeated action counting, and addressee-dependent participation. 

## 3 DIVE 

3.1 TAXONOMY 

Real-time full-duplex interaction requires a model to maintain an evolving understanding of the audio inputs and visual cues, identify task-relevant evidence as it emerges, and integrate it with ongoing speech to decide how to behave in real time. DIVE therefore organizes interaction into three complementary scenarios. Proactive Intervention evaluates whether models can recognize emerging visual or semantic evidence that calls for timely intervention. Continuous Engagement 

evaluates whether models can maintain visual context over time to track recurring actions and answer successive, visually grounded queries. Interaction Coordination evaluates whether models can use ongoing audio-visual cues to adapt responses under overlap and determine when an utterance is directed toward the model. Together, these scenarios cover six tasks that examine how models perceive, reason over, and act upon continually evolving multimodal context. DIVE comprises 525 evaluation instances across six tasks, constructed from 413 base scenarios (Table 2). Proactive Intervention evaluates whether a model can identify emerging mistakes or risks from an ongoing interaction and intervene at an appropriate moment. We consider two complementary set-tings with visual- and speech-driven triggers. 

Video Timely Intervention. We construct this task from egocentric procedural datasets that provide temporally localized annotations of execution and procedural errors across diverse activities, includ-ing PIE-V Loginova & Keller (2026), MATT Li et al. (2026), Ego-EXTRA Ragusa et al. (2026), and CaptainCook4D Peddi et al. (2024). Specifically, the model receives a persistent instruction before the activity begins and is expected to intervene once sufficient visual evidence of the mistake becomes available. We further annotate the earliest moment at which the mistake becomes visually identifiable, providing a reference point for evaluating intervention timing. 

Speech Proactive Intervention. We construct speech-driven proactive intervention scenarios from LLaVAShield Huang et al. (2026a) and LlamaPIE Chen et al. (2025), supplemented with manually authored cases. We convert unsafe situations into natural spoken narratives and generate correspond-ing videos with Wan 3.0 Wan et al. Continuous Engagement evaluates whether a model can maintain active participation in an evolv-ing interaction and produce repeated or successive responses over time. We consider two settings involving ongoing action tracking and interleaved query answering. 

Continuous Action Tracking. We construct this task from repetitive-action datasets including Rep-Count Hu et al. (2022), Multi-RepCount Zhao et al. (2025), and CPR-rac Liu et al. (2026b). Their repetition-level annotations are converted into temporally aligned counting interactions, where the model receives a persistent instruction: it must generate a response upon completion of each target action, and stay silent between repetitions. This setup evaluates both continuous visual tracking and precise response timing. 

Continuous Query Interaction. We construct sequential query sequences from Live-Aid Lei et al. (2026), supplemented with manually authored scenarios. Each sequence alternates between two cat-egories of questions: visually grounded questions, whose answers depend on the current video con-tent, and video-independent questions that can be answered without visual evidence. For manually designed cases, we generate the corresponding videos with Wan 3.0 Wan et al. (2025). The model must dynamically switch between visual grounding and general reasoning as successive queries arrive. 3.4 INTERACTION COORDINATION 

Interaction Coordination evaluates a model’s ability to produce well-timed responses that coordinate with incoming user behavior in overlapping and multi-party interactions. We consider two distinct settings: adapting its responses when interruptions occur, and determining appropriate moments for the model itself to engage in the conversation. 

Overlapping Response Adaptation. We adapt interruptions and state-update scenarios from EchoChain Modi et al. (2026) and HumDial-FDBench Wang et al. (2026a). We rewrite interrup-tions to introduce meaningful corrections, new constraints, or contextual updates; we also augment the original dialogues with visually relevant information. Corresponding videos are then generated to form audio-visual interactions, requiring the model to continue perceiving incoming context while speaking and adapt its ongoing response accordingly. 

Audio-Visual Addressee Tracking. We construct participation patterns from multi-party dialogue resources such as Speak or Stay Silent Bhagtani et al. (2026) and When2Speak Nama et al. Response Quality Remains Uneven Across Models and Tasks. As shown in Table 4, Gemini-3.1-Flash-Live achieves the highest average response quality score of 56.38, followed by GPT-Realtime-2.1 and Qwen3.5-Omni-Plus-Realtime with 51.93 and 47.07, respectively. Among the evaluated open models, Mage-VL obtains the highest average score of 24.23, indicating a pro-nounced performance gap between the leading proprietary and open models. The leading models also exhibit different strengths: Gemini performs best on SPI, AVAT, and CAT, while GPT-Realtime-2.1 leads on VTI, CQI, and ORA. Nevertheless, the three highest-scoring models all score lower on ORA than on VTI and SPI, highlighting the difficulty of adapting response content when new infor-mation arrives during an ongoing response. These results suggest that current models still struggle to produce consistently high-quality responses across diverse and evolving interaction contexts. 

Low Average Latency Does Not Ensure Consistent Window Compliance. Table 3 shows that low average latency does not necessarily translate into consistently responding within the valid inter-action windows. GPT-Realtime-2.1 achieves the highest average window response rate of 53.77%, followed by Qwen3.5-Omni-Plus-Realtime at 41.43% and Gemini-3.1-Flash-Live at 40.58%. De-spite their sub-second average response latencies, these models still leave considerable room for im-provement in meeting the annotated response windows. Performance also varies considerably across tasks. GPT-Realtime-2.1 and Qwen3.5-Omni-Plus-Realtime both reach 100.0% on VTI, but achieve only 20.0% and 19.8% on SPI, respectively. Even the best-performing model on SPI, MiniCPM-o 4.5, reaches only 24.8%. This disparity highlights speech-driven proactive intervention as a particu-larly demanding setting, and underscores the need to evaluate window compliance alongside average response latency. 

Substantial Response Delays Persist in Open Models. Proprietary models markedly outper-form open models in terms of response latency (Table 5). Among all evaluated open models, the best-performing Aero-Realtime-4B still has an average response latency of 2.567 s; several other models take more than 5 s to respond after the earliest appropriate interaction moment. In contrast, recent proprietary real-time interaction systems substantially reduce this delay. Qwen3.5-Omni-Plus-Realtime, GPT-Realtime-2.1, and Gemini 3.1 Flash Live all achieve an average latency below 1 second. Beyond Event Recognition: The Challenge of Timely Proactive Intervention DIVE further evaluates whether models can respond promptly once sufficient visual or auditory evidence for in-tervention becomes available. In the Video Timely Intervention (VTI) task, we measure the temporal offset from the earliest moment at which an error or risk becomes visually identifiable. These results highlight a challenge beyond conventional streaming under-standing: models must not only recognize relevant events, but also determine when the accumulated evidence is sufficient to trigger a proactive response. Timely interaction decisions therefore remain a key bottleneck for current real-time multimodal models. 

## 5 LIMITATIONS 

Although DIVE provides a comprehensive evaluation of continuous audio-visual interaction, sev-eral important capabilities fall outside its current scope. First, DIVE does not explicitly evaluate agentic interaction beyond verbal responses. Recent real-time interaction systems have begun to support concurrent tool use and proactive actions during ongoing conversations. In such settings, even without an explicit user request, a model may need to autonomously determine when to retrieve external information, invoke tools, or draw on previously observed context, and then appropriately incorporate the resulting information into the ongoing interaction. Second, despite DIVE’s emphasis on continuous interaction, the current samples remain limited in duration. Therefore, they do not fully capture challenges that arise over longer time horizons, such as persistent multimodal memory, long-range dependencies, and context accumulation across extended interactions. In addition, controlled within-model comparisons isolating the effects of visual input and concurrent perception are left for future work. 

## 6 CONCLUSION 

We introduce DIVE, a benchmark for evaluating full-duplex multimodal interaction with visual en-gagement under continuous audio-visual streams. With 525 samples covering six tasks across three complementary interaction scenarios, DIVE assesses interaction decisions, temporal alignment, and response quality. Our experiments show that low response latency alone is insufficient for reli-able interaction: substantial room for improvement remains in responding within valid windows and consistently producing high-quality responses across tasks. Speech-driven proactive interven-tion and response adaptation during overlapping interactions continue to pose particular challenges. DIVE provides a testbed for investigating these limitations and advancing multimodal models to-ward sustained, timely, and contextually appropriate interaction. 9Preprint

Speech Proactive Intervention (SPI): For scenarios involving auditory or visual cues, the response window is the gap between two relevant consecutive utterances by the speaker in the video. It begins when the first utterance ends and closes when the next utterance begins. 

Overlapping Response Adaptation (ORA): The response window begins when the information requiring a response first becomes fully available in the video and ends when the person in the video initiates their own reaction. 

Audio-Visual Addressee Tracking (AVAT): For slots requiring a model response, the response window is the gap between utterances by the other participants, from the end of one utterance to the onset of the next. Conversational context and visual addressee cues determine whether the model should speak within that gap.
