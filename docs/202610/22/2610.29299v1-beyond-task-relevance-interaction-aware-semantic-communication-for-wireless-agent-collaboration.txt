# Beyond Task Relevance: Interaction-Aware Semantic Communication for Wireless Agent Collaboration 

Yiru Wang, Pengxin Guan, Xiumei Deng, Zehui Xiong, Senior Member, IEEE 

Abstract —Semantic communication in multi-agent systems of-ten assumes that task-relevant information benefits the team once delivered. Yet semantic sharing can alter the receiver’s decision and increase inter-agent dependence, potentially reducing team utility. Motivated by this phenomenon, we develop an interaction-aware framework for wireless collaboration between heteroge-neous agents. We analytically relate communication-induced de-pendence to expected best-of-team utility and define a team-level semantic value for candidate messages. We consider semantic units at different abstraction levels and formulate a deadline-and power-constrained problem that maximizes predicted team-level net surplus. We derive an interaction-aware policy that selects at most one unit and its transmit power, or chooses not to transmit. Experiments on two verifier-scored optimization tasks show that full-solution exchange induces stronger structural convergence and more adverse team-level effects, whereas method hints exhibit pronounced directionality. Across the evaluated power budgets, our policy achieves the highest net team-level surplus while consuming less energy than semantic-aware and full-solution baselines. 

Index Terms —Semantic communication, multi-agent systems, semantic interaction tax, interaction-aware transmission 

I. I NTRODUCTION 

# SEMANTIC communication (SemCom) has emerged as a promising paradigm for next-generation wireless networks by shifting the communication objective from accurate bit recovery toward the delivery of task-relevant meaning. Early learning-based SemCom systems demonstrated that semantic representations can improve transmission efficiency and ro-bustness over constrained wireless channels [1]. This paradigm has subsequently evolved toward task- and goal-oriented com-munication, where information is selected and transmitted according to its contribution to downstream tasks rather than source-level fidelity [2], [3]. Meanwhile, advances in large artificial intelligence (AI) models are transforming wireless terminals from passive data consumers into autonomous agents capable of perception, reasoning, planning, and decision-making. Recent studies have explored large-model-powered agents for intelligent commu-nications and AI-native edge systems, where agents interact with wireless infrastructures and other agents to accomplish

Instead, the received information can directly influence an agent’s subsequent reasoning and action. The effect of inter-agent information exchange, however, is not always beneficial. Studies of multi-agent reasoning have shown that the value of interaction depends strongly on how agents communicate: multi-agent debate does not consistently outperform independent or ensemble reasoning, while communication topology can affect both performance and efficiency [6], [7]. More recently, interaction tax was iden-tified in large language model-based multi-agent reasoning, showing that exposing heterogeneous agents to one another’s complete solutions can rapidly increase solution similarity and erode the diversity advantage of independent reasoning [8]. This observation raises a question for wireless SemCom: 

does information useful to a receiver necessarily improve the team’s best available solution? Task relevance indicates whether a message pertains to the receiver’s task, while receiver-local gain measures its effect on that agent. Neither alone determines its value to a team. After reception, the receiver’s updated candidate may change both its quality and its dependence on the sender’s candidate, thereby changing expected best-of-team utility. Existing SemCom designs often consider task-related quality alongside bandwidth, latency, and energy [9], [10], but these criteria alone do not quantify this interaction-induced effect. Meanwhile, the interaction tax in [8] identifies diversity loss at the reasoning level without addressing semantic abstraction or wireless delivery. We there-fore ask how to value semantic units by their downstream team-level effects and use that value to decide whether to transmit, what to transmit, and at what power. To address this question, we develop an interaction-aware semantic communication framework for wireless heterogeneous-agent systems. Our contributions are as follows:

We establish an analytical framework that relates interaction-induced dependence to the expected best-of-team utility, and define an interaction-aware semantic value to quantify the downstream team-level effect of semantic exchange beyond receiver-local improvement.  

> •

A. Collaborative Multi-Agent System 

We consider a wireless multi-agent system consisting of a set of heterogeneous agents N = {1, . . . , N } that col-laboratively solve a common task. The agents may employ different foundation models, reasoning strategies, or local observations, and therefore explore different regions of the solution space. At the start of interaction round t, each agent 

i ∈ N holds a pre-transmission candidate solution si(t).Due to agent heterogeneity and stochastic generation, the candidate solutions produced by different agents can exhibit complementary characteristics, which provides the potential benefit of multi-agent collaboration. During task execution, agents can exchange task-related semantic information over wireless links. Instead of treat-ing the complete candidate solution as the only transferable message, we represent the information available at agent i

A. Communication-Induced Correlation 

A successfully received semantic message can affect the subsequent solution generated by the receiving agent in two distinct ways. First, useful semantic content, such as a failure cause or a verified hint, may improve the quality of the receiver’s next candidate. Second, exposing the receiver to another agent’s reasoning or solution may steer its subsequent search toward that of the transmitter, thereby altering the statistical dependence between their future solution qualities. We model these two effects separately. Consider a semantic unit mi,k (t) generated by agent i and successfully decoded by agent j at interaction round t. We focus on the one-step effect of this interaction on the receiving agent, while the transmitter’s current candidate is treated as the reference during this communication event. This paper develops an interaction-aware semantic com-munication framework by analytically characterizing how communication-induced dependence affects expected best-of-team utility. Building on this relationship, we formulate a deadline- and power-constrained problem to maximize pre-dicted team-level utility gain net of weighted transmission energy. The resulting policy either remains silent or selects a semantic unit from different abstraction levels for transmission at an appropriate power. Experiments show that full-solution exchange induces the strongest structural convergence and more adverse team-level effects, whereas method hints exhibit direction-dependent effects while better preserving solution diversity. Across the evaluated power budgets, our policy achieves higher net team-level surplus with lower energy consumption than semantic-aware and full-solution baselines. REFERENCES [1] H. Xie, Z. Qin, G.
