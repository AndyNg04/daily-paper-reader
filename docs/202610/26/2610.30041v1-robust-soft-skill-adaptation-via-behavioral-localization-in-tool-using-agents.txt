# Robust Soft Skill Adaptation via Behavioral Localization in Tool-Using Agents 

Zijian Jiang 

School of Computer Science University of Surrey Guildford, Surrey GU2 7XH, UK z.jiang@surrey.ac.uk

School of Computer Science University of Surrey Guildford, Surrey GU2 7XH, UK wangxilu@surrey.ac.uk 

## Abstract 

Text skills can extend the capabilities of tool-using agents, but long skill descrip-tions increase computational costs. Soft prompts provide a compact alternative, yet learning from entire successful trajectories may also introduce unnecessary behav-ioral changes. We find that the useful effect of a text skill is highly concentrated in a small subset of trajectory positions. Based on this observation, we propose Behavioral Localization, which compares the frozen agent with and without the text skill to identify positions where the skill both increases the likelihood of suc-cessful tokens and meaningfully changes the next token distribution. We use these localized positions to supervise a short soft prompt. Experiments on Spreadsheet-Bench with a frozen Qwen3.6-35B-A3B agent show that the top 10% of trajectory positions capture 96.12% of the total positive skill gain. In the shared prompt setting, selective supervision improves success from 30.36% with full trajectory to 36.79%. Under task-specific conditions, using the top 10% localized positions achieves 57.38%, outperforming both the full text skill and the on-policy OPCD baseline. These results show that a small set of behaviorally relevant trajectory positions can provide effective supervision of soft skills. 

## 1 Introduction 

As agents are increasingly deployed across diverse domains, tasks often differ substantially in their knowledge requirements, operational procedures, and behavioral patterns. These task-specific differences are difficult to address efficiently through repeated retraining or modification of the underlying agent Liu et al. [2024]. A flexible solution is to provide agents with task-specific guidance that helps them develop more effective task behaviors Li et al. [2026]. A direct approach to task adaptation is known as an agent skill, where natural language prompts are used to specify task goals, operational rules, and expected behaviors, allowing the model to perform new tasks without updating its parameters Anthropic [2025]. However, this approach often relies on lengthy and carefully designed prompts, and its effectiveness can be sensitive to wording and example selection. As tasks become more complex, the prompts typically grow longer, increasing both context usage and inference cost Mu et al. [2023]. To obtain more compact and learnable skill representations, prior work has explored directly opti-mizing prompts in continuous representation spaces. Compared with natural language instructions, soft prompts typically require less storage and can support task adaptation by updating a small set of trainable prompt embeddings Tao et al. [2026], Lester et al. [2021]. However, these continuous vectors lack clear counterparts in natural language Deng et al. [2022]. As a result, while learning a target skill, a soft prompt can also introduce unexpected behavioral changes Ma et al. [2022].

For agents, because generation is sequential, even small shifts in early predictions can propagate and amplify over subsequent steps, leading to incorrect tool selection, malformed outputs, or task execution failures Bengio et al. [2015], Li et al. [2023]. Thus, acquiring new capabilities may come at the cost of degrading abilities that the base model already possesses Zhu et al. [2022]. We find that the behavioral effect of a skill is highly concentrated in a small number of critical decision points, while much of the remaining trajectory is already correctly handled by the base agent. This suggests that robust soft skill adaptation should focus on which behaviors truly need to be learned. Based on this observation, we propose Behavioral Localization, which identifies the trajectory positions where a text skill meaningfully changes the predictions of base agent and selectively uses these positions to train a compact soft skill. At the same time, we constrain selected preservation positions toward the base distribution to reduce unnecessary behavioral drift. Our contributions are shown below. First, through token-level distributional analysis, we find that skill induced behavioral changes are highly sparse. Second, we show that training a soft prompt using only selected positions by Behavioral Localization in the trajectory as supervision outperforms training on the full trajectory. 

## 2 Related Work 

Prompt learning provides a foundation for representing task-specific capabilities in continuous spaces. Prompt Tuning, Prefix-Tuning, and P-Tuning show that a frozen base model can be adapted by optimizing only a small set of continuous prompt embeddings Lester et al. [2021], Li and Liang [2021], Liu et al. [2022]. Later work explores how these representations can be reused. For example, SPoT transfers learned soft prompts from source tasks to similar target tasks Vu et al. [2022]. XPrompt finds that some prompt tokens can hurt downstream performance and removes these negative parts Ma et al. [2022]. These studies mainly focus on how soft prompts are parameterized and reused. Another line of work compresses long natural language contexts into compact latent representations. Gisting compresses long prompts into a small number of reusable gist tokens Mu et al. [2023]. ICAE encodes long contexts into a few memory slots that can directly condition a language model Ge et al. [2024]. These methods mainly aim to preserve the semantics of the original text after compression. Agent skills differ from general text compression because their main purpose is to guide an agent’s behavior. Therefore, a compressed representation should preserve the behavioral effect introduced by the skill. Recent work has begun to reuse successful agent trajectories to compress textual skills offline. SoftSkill compresses a natural-language skill into a short continuous prefix while keeping the base model frozen Tao et al. [2026]. It trains the prefix by predicting the next tokens in successful trajectories. LatentSkill instead converts textual skills into task-specific LoRA adapters, moving reusable skill knowledge from the context space into the model’s weight space Yu et al. [2026]. These studies show that textual agent skills can be encoded as compact latent representations. However, they mainly focus on how a skill should be represented or stored. In contrast, we study which behavioral changes introduced by a skill should be used to learn such a representation.

## 4 Experiment 

4.1 Setup 

We conduct our experiments on SpreadsheetBenchMa et al. [2024]. We use GPT-5.5 to generate the successful teacher trajectories. All experiments use Qwen3.6-35B-A3B as the frozen student model, with all parameters fixed during training. Each soft prompt has a length of 8 and is initialized using the embeddings of the first same tokens of the full text skill. During evaluation, we use greedy decoding with a maximum of 4,096 token cost and set the execution timeout for each task to 600 seconds. 4.2 Main Result 

We use the Behavioral Locator to select key positions and optimize both a shared soft prompt across tasks and task-specific soft prompts for individual tasks. As shown in Figure 1, the positive gain is highly concentrated in a small subset of trajectory positions. Selecting only 10% of the tokens captures 96.12% of the total positive Skill effect. This motivates us to use a sparse set of relevant tokens as supervision for soft skill learning. No Skill measures the capability of the base agent, while Text Skill represents the up bound by providing the full text skill. SoftSkill is the offline full-trajectory compression baseline. OPCD is an important on-policy distillation baseline that trains online rather than relying on fixed teacher trajectories. Our method instead relies on lower cost offline teacher trajectories. Under the shared prompt setting, our method improves over full-trajectory SoftSkill, although it remains below the on-policy OPCD baseline. More importantly, under task-specific condition, selecting the top 10% localized positions achieves 57.38%, outperforming both Text Skill and OPCD. These results support a small subset of token can provide sufficient supervision for learning effective soft prompts.
