# Can LLM User-Proxies Stand In for Humans in Reliance Measurement? Replicating Rel-A.I. with Simulated Users 

Shuai Wang Rui Hu 

Zhejiang University 

Abstract 

Can language-model user proxies reproduce hu-man reliance on an AI answer across contexts? Published simulated-user studies do not estab-lish validity for this partial-answer decision, and Rel-A.I.’s released human record omits trial-level choices. We audit its 90 printed ques-tions and 15 domain-by-marker reliance rates using sampled decisions and counterbalanced A/B probabilities from open models through 72B parameters. A matched crossover varies the printed agent specialty within each ques-tion, separating question content, label aver-ages, and their interaction. Mistral Small’s strong-marker math-minus-law label marginal is −0.4 percentage points, yet matching the agent label to the question domain raises re-liance by 13.3 points on average across the five domains. The best A/B proxy has 22.5-point absolute-rate error and 8.3-point domain-effect error against the human table. A two-sample independent-binomial human replica-tion has 3.5-point expected domain-effect error under the stated working model. The interac-tion is model dependent, and inference layout changes identical-prompt probabilities. This single-study audit does not establish proxy va-lidity for human effect sizes or general direc-tional screening.

People often decide whether to use an AI answer before they can verify it. The wording of a partial answer and the domain of the question can change that decision. Rel-A.I. measures reliance by show-ing people the beginning of an agent’s answer and asking whether they will use the completed answer or look it up themselves (Zhou et al., 2025). Its domain study reports rates across five subjects and three classes of certainty language. Repeating the study for every interface or model revision would require new participants. A language-model proxy could be useful if it preserves the human changes across conditions. Validation requires more than a plausible single choice. A screening instrument must preserve the ordering of conditions, while an effect-size substi-tute must also reproduce the distance between them and the baseline reliance level. Simulated users can misestimate human task success (Seshadri et al., 2026), and survey simulations can distort between-group differences (Chen et al., 2026). Neither find-ing establishes how a simulated respondent behaves 1arXiv:2610.25788v1 [cs.AI] 19 Oct 2026 in Rel-A.I.’s partial-answer task. Its printed ques-tions and aggregate human table provide a direct behavioral reference, although the original marker assignments and participant-level decisions are un-available. Those missing records limit uncertainty analysis and exact replay. We ask whether a proxy preserves reliance lev-els and changes across contexts well enough to prioritize conditions for a human study. A constant decision can appear accurate on small domain ef-fects, while a preference for mathematical subjects can match many correlated pairwise signs. The missing experimental distinction is whether a do-main shift follows the agent’s stated specialty or the questions assigned to that specialty. Figure 1 shows our matched test: the same question appears with all five agent-domain labels, each marker class, and both option orders. We retain the original sampled replay and a separate counterbalanced probability protocol to place this test against the published human table. Our evaluation compares every published domain-by-marker rate, all within-marker domain pairs, and strong-minus-weak marker effects. Ab-solute rates describe how often someone would accept an answer in a condition. Domain contrasts indicate which subject might merit further testing; marker contrasts measure sensitivity to expressed certainty. A model can rank conditions while plac-ing nearly every decision at the floor or ceiling. We therefore compare effect errors with a zero-domain-effect rule and a same-size human resample un-der a stated working model. Human-table-fitted marker and STEM rules are descriptive checks on the score, not deployable competitors. The human reference lacks participant records, so the resam-pling reference cannot reproduce its within-person covariance. The lowest-error A/B proxy, Mistral Small, has 8.3 percentage points of domain-effect error. A two-sample independent-binomial human replication has 3.5 points of expected error under the stated working model. In the matched crossover, Mis-tral Small’s strong-marker mathematics-minus-law label marginal is near zero, but a label matching the question domain raises reliance by 13.3 points on average. Thus a marginal label comparison can hide a substantial question-by-label interaction. Few human domain pairs resolve under the discrete-binomial working approximation, and the resolved set changes with assumptions about within-person correlation. This table does not establish a general directional screening ability. This paper contributes a reconstruction that keeps published and inferred materials separate, an audit of the complete released domain table, and a within-question label crossover across three prox-ies. The crossover identifies a question-dependent effect of the printed agent-domain label on proxy decisions while leaving the model’s own question accuracy unmeasured. The aggregate human record cannot determine within-person covariance or a matched greeting effect. The resulting conclusion is limited to this task and these protocols: the tested proxies do not support replacing human reliance measurements. 

2 Related Work 

Behavioral reliance. Rel-A.I. records a decision to defer to an incomplete agent answer and varies greetings, prior confidence, and question domain (Zhou et al., 2025). Earlier human experiments by its authors examined how certainty markers and model reluctance to express uncertainty affect re-liance (Zhou et al., 2024). The reported Rel-A.I. domain rates provide the external target here. Self-reported trust instruments, including the Trust-In-LLMs Index, measure a related construct (De Duro et al., 2025). We evaluate the observed rely-or-look-up choice and make no claim about a proxy’s internal trust. 

Simulated respondents. Demographically con-ditioned “silicon samples” can approximate re-sponse distributions for survey subgroups (Argyle et al., 2022), and interview-grounded language-model agents can reproduce some survey responses and experimental outcomes (Park et al., 2026). Sim-ulated users in agentic evaluations can misestimate human task success and vary across simulator mod-els (Seshadri et al., 2026). Interactive literature-search agents are likewise evaluated against a fixed LLM user simulator that sees the hidden gold pa-pers, with leak checks and a small human study because simulated feedback cannot fully replace users (Li et al., 2026). A cross-domain survey au-dit also finds inflated differences between demo-graphic segments (Chen et al., 2026). These studies make the target estimand central to validation. We test a behavioral rely-or-look-up decision against published human condition effects, including abso-lute rates and response-interface sensitivity. 2Response interfaces. Multiple-choice selection can shift when answer positions or option identi-fiers change (Zheng et al., 2024). Our A/B protocol balances option order and measures the residual dif-ference between orders. The resulting rates remain conditional on the two labels, which is why we compare them with the sampled-response protocol as a separate measurement rather than attributing a difference to model psychology. 

3 Method 

3.1 Reference and estimands 

Rel-A.I. asks a person whether to use an agent’s hidden completed answer or seek the answer in-dependently (Zhou et al., 2025). Its domain study reports a reliance rate for each combination of five domains and three epistemic-marker classes. The article prints 90 questions but does not release par-ticipant choices or the marker assigned to each question. We preserve the printed questions and the reported class counts, then reconstruct assign-ments for proxy trials. This is a comparison with the published aggregate estimand, rather than an exact replay of the original trial sequence. Let d index the five domains, e the three marker classes, m a proxy model, p a prompt protocol, and 

i a trial. The response Ympei is one for reliance and zero for independent lookup. Within a domain and marker class, Rmpde is the mean of within-replicate reliance rates. The corresponding published human rate is Hde . We compare all 15 pairs (Rmpde , H de )

and, for every marker class, all ten unordered do-main pairs:

(1) The college-mathematics-minus-law effect is one of these pairs. We also score the strong-minus-weak marker effect within each domain. All do-main pairs share cells, so their sign counts are descriptive rather than independent hypothesis tests. Absolute-rate and effect errors are arithmetic means of absolute proxy-minus-human differences, in percentage points. A zero proxy effect has no direction. In the thresholded all-pair count, both hu-man and proxy effects must reach one percentage point; the human-resolved count instead requires a human working interval that excludes zero and a proxy effect of at least one point. Spearman cor-relation summarizes the ordering of the 15 rates; a constant proxy rate has no defined correlation. The zero-domain-effect rule sets each domain to the published mean within its marker class. We also display a constant at the overall human mean and a one-point mathematics-and-algebra increment on the marker means. All three rules are fitted to the scored human table. The STEM increment is a posthoc descriptive check on the resolved-pair structure, never a prospective predictor. 

3.2 Sampled binary replay 

Each proxy sees a domain-labeled agent, a printed question, and an answer prefix containing a marker; the complete answer is withheld. The system in-struction asks for a single RELY or LOOK UP de-cision. Each trial starts a fresh conversation. A seeded permutation assigns four questions per do-main to strengtheners, ten to weakened strength-eners, and four to weakeners for each prompt replicate. The marker phrase is sampled within its class. The default protocol has no persona descriptor. The diverse protocol cycles evenly through eight descriptions crossing trivia familiar-ity, answer-checking habit, and chat-assistant use. We generate 48 prompt replicates under each proto-col. A replicate is a repeated prompt construction and sample, not a heterogeneous human participant. The parser retains malformed text as missing and never coerces it to a decision. 

3.3 Counterbalanced probability control 

The control shows each printed question once un-der a representative phrase from each marker class and each of two option orders. In one order, A means rely; in the other, B means rely. The sys-tem instruction asks for A or B. We normalize the model’s next-token logits over the A and B tokens and record the probability assigned to the rely option. The balanced rate is the arithmetic mean of the two order-specific probabilities, first within question and then within domain and marker class. This protocol has 540 prompt evaluations per model. Its order shift is the mean absolute dif-ference between order-specific domain-by-marker rates. It measures a two-label conditional choice probability, not the probability of all possible free-form responses. Crossing marker classes within questions and using a fixed phrase also differ from the sampled replay, so the two protocols are sepa-rate measurements. To separate a domain label from the question it accompanies, we cross every printed question with all five agent-domain labels while preserving its marker and both option orders. The matched 3crossover has 2,700 evaluations per model and is run for Qwen 7B, Qwen 72B AWQ, and Mis-tral Small. For each marker, a question-content marginal averages every question over the five la-bels before comparing its source domain with an-other source domain. An agent-label marginal av-erages each label over all 90 questions before com-paring labels. The original question-label pairing is the diagonal of this design. These marginals are separate counterfactual contrasts, not additive com-ponents of the diagonal. For each source domain, we define congruence as reliance with the match-ing agent label minus mean reliance with the four other labels, averaging over its 18 questions and both option orders. Averaging congruence over all five source domains cancels additive label effects in this balanced design, leaving a question-domain-by-label interaction. Batching keeps all ten option-order and label variants of one question and marker together, so the within-question comparison shares a padding layout. 

3.4 Uncertainty and resource measures 

For the sampled replay we resample prompt repli-cates and printed questions independently within domain, recompute participant-first cell rates, and repeat this procedure 2,000 times. For the proba-bility control we resample the 18 printed questions within each domain, preserving each question’s two option orders and three marker classes. We use per-centile intervals from these replicates for contrasts. Their coverage concerns these sampled proxy pro-cedures; it does not absorb uncertainty from recon-structing the human marker assignments. Rel-A.I. recruited 50 people for its domain set-ting and assigned four, ten, and four questions per class within each domain (Zhou et al., 2025). Its aggregate table gives no participant records or covariance. For a transparent working compari-son, we simulate independent binomial choices with the nominal per-cell decision counts and the published rates. One resample scored against the published table measures one-sided sampling er-ror. Scoring two independent resamples against each other approximates a same-size human repli-cation compared with a noisy reference. Both are conditional on the published rates as population probabilities and are not empirical human replica-tions. Positive within-person correlation can widen cell intervals yet narrow within-person contrast in-tervals; its net effect cannot be identified from the table. We call a direction jointly resolved only when both the proxy interval and this human work-ing interval strictly exclude zero. We also examine exchangeable-response correlations in Section 5. No human-proxy difference is claimed to be statis-tically established from the published table alone. Generation batches record input and output to-kens and accelerator inference time. These quan-tities measure proxy computation; they exclude model loading and human-study administration. Rel-A.I. reports an hourly compensation minimum, but not total domain-study time or expenditure, so a dollar-per-effect comparison cannot be recovered from the published record.

4.3 Does a larger, counterbalanced proxy improve agreement? 

Table 3 makes the probability control’s 15 cells inspectable for all four models. Mistral Small has the lowest absolute-rate error among these controls, 22.5 percentage points; Qwen 72B has 30.9 (Ta-ble 4). Their domain-effect errors are 8.3 and 19.6 points, respectively, against 3.5 for a two-sample plug-in human comparison and 4.6 for the zero-domain-effect rule. Qwen 72B has rate rank cor-relation 0.87 yet large level and effect errors. The table-fitted marker and STEM rules are descrip-tive oracles, not prospective competitors. These comparisons are conditional on this prompt and the batch layout; they do not identify model-intrinsic validity. Only eight human pairs have discrete-binomial working intervals that strictly exclude zero. The plug-in human resample averages 22.6 thresholded sign matches and 7.9 hits on these pairs (Table 4). All eight compare mathematics or algebra with a humanities domain. Qwen 7B matches seven af-ter a one-point proxy-effect threshold, while the posthoc STEM rule matches eight by construction. A common permutation of the five domain labels matches or exceeds Qwen 7B’s seven hits in 12 of 120 assignments. This test has little resolution beyond the STEM-versus-humanities partition. A normal working model resolves ten pairs at zero within-person correlation and fourteen at correla-tion 0.3; the STEM rule then matches ten and thir-teen, respectively (Section 5). The discrete-versus-normal difference concerns boundary pairs whose rounded effects sit near zero. The 30 pairs share only 15 cells and are not independent confirma-tions. The question-resampling analysis preserves both option orders within each item. With the human table fixed, each control’s domain-effect-error in-terval stays above 4.6 points; Section 5 prints those endpoints. Counterbalancing averages the two or-ders but does not remove wording sensitivity. Ap-pendix D reports the observed shift, and the experi-ment artifact retains each order-specific cell rate.

6 Conclusion 

Across the released Rel-A.I. domain table, the tested sampled and counterbalanced proxies have large reliance-level and diagonal effect errors. The matched crossover shows that agent-domain labels have question-dependent effects: Mistral Small’s small global math-minus-law label marginal coex-ists with a substantial matching-label lift. Question-set differences remain after averaging over labels, but the models’ own answer accuracy is unknown. Identical-prompt probabilities also vary with infer-ence layout. The human aggregate resolves few domain contrasts and leaves within-person covari-ance unavailable. This single-study audit does not establish proxy-based directional screening or re-placement of human reliance measurements under the tested protocols. 

Limitations 

The human reference contains 15 rates from one study with 50 people, rather than released trial-level decisions. The binomial working intervals use nominal decision counts and ignore dependence among decisions by one person. The bias can point either way for within-person contrasts; these inter-vals cannot substitute for a participant-level boot-strap or the original study’s covariance. Only eight of the 30 domain pairs resolve under this work-ing model, limiting any directional validity claim against this table. A plug-in independent human resample scored against the published table mea-sures one-sided noise; the two-sample comparison is the closer approximation to a new human repli-cation. Both omit within-person dependence. The 30 domain-pair effects share the same 15 cells and are not independent confirmations. The printed questions are preserved, but their marker assignments are reconstructed. Different assignments, interface timing, or participant in-8structions could alter the comparison. The five domains contain distinct question sets, so domain differences can reflect question content, difficulty, or model knowledge alongside perceived domain competence. The sampled replay’s prompt repli-cates differ by permutations and random genera-tion; without persona descriptors, their spread is sampling and item-assignment variation, not hu-man heterogeneity. One generator seed was used, so the bootstrap does not estimate between-seed generation variation. The diverse descriptors are a prompt intervention and are not sampled from a human demographic distribution. An affine calibra-tion fitted and scored on the same 15 human cells would be in-sample; no separate human domain table is available for a prospective calibration test. The probability control uses one marker phrase per class and crosses all classes with every ques-tion. It therefore answers a related sensitivity ques-tion under a cleaner within-item design, not an exact reanalysis of the original human trials. A/B-normalized next-token probabilities depend on la-bel wording and inference-batch layout. The iden-tical diagonal prompts in the four-item control and ten-item crossover have cellwise mean absolute dif-ferences of 0.36, 0.08, and 0.07 points for Qwen 7B, Qwen 72B, and Mistral Small; their largest cell differences are 1.88, 0.33, and 0.37 points. At the individual prompt level the largest differ-ences are 21.51, 2.15, and 2.60 points. This is an unresolved implementation sensitivity, so neither cross-batch difference is interpreted as a behavioral effect. Within-crossover label comparisons share a batch layout, although that does not establish batch-size-one invariance. Label format, marker phrase, question assignment, and sampling change together between the sampled and probability pro-tocols, so their difference has no identified cause. The largest evaluated proxy has 72B parameters in four-bit AWQ form, but no frontier API model was tested. Quantization may affect its responses. The single English, closed-form reliance task cannot establish validity in longer conversations, other lan-guages, or consequential decisions. No additional human reliance dataset with released compatible trials was used. The crossover isolates the printed domain cue while holding the question fixed; no answer key was released, so it does not measure the proxy’s own question accuracy. The greeting probe reuses domain questions be-cause the human greeting-study item assignments were not released. Its result cannot be scored against the human greeting effect. The paper’s evidence therefore concerns domain-conditioned reliance; it does not validate greeting or prior-confidence effect sizes. Rel-A.I. reports an hourly participant compensation minimum but no total domain-study time or expenditure, preventing a like-for-like dollar cost comparison.
