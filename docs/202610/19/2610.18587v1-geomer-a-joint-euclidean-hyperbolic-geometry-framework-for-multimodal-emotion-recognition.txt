Abstract. Multimodal Emotion Recognition (MER) aims to under-stand human emotions by integrating information from multiple modal-ities such as speech and text. Psychological studies suggest that emo-tions exhibit latent hierarchical structured relationships in their seman-tic representations [1]. However, most MER approaches rely on Eu-clidean representations, which capture fine-grained cross-modal inter-actions but do not explicitly provide a geometric prior for organizing hierarchical emotional structures. To mitigate this gap, we propose Ge-oMER, a joint Euclidean-Hyperbolic modeling framework that intro-duces hyperbolic emotion-core routing to capture higher-level, abstract emotional representations for MER. The proposed framework jointly models token-level cross-modal semantic interactions and latent emo-tion representations in two complementary geometric spaces. Specifically, GeoMER consists of a Euclidean Multimodal Fusion Module (EuclFu-sion) for multimodal representation fusion and a Hyperbolic Geomet-ric Core Routing (HypGeoRoute) module that performs geometry-aware core assignment and aggregation in a hyperbolic manifold to capture more abstract feature representations. The HypGeoRoute module com-plements the Euclidean token-level modeling. We experiment on the IEMOCAP multimodal MER benchmark and demonstrate that the pro-posed method achieves the best performance, with 83.54% weighted ac-curacy and 83.35% unweighted accuracy, outperforming Euclidean base-lines, including MemoCMT. 

Keywords: Multimodal emotion recognition · Hyperbolic geometry · Speech emotion recognition · Multimodal feature fusion · Affective com-puting. 

# 1 Introduction 

Multimodal Emotion Recognition (MER) aims to enable machines to interpret human emotions more accurately by integrating multiple modalities, such as arXiv:2610.18587v1 [cs.SD] 13 Oct 2026 2 T. Zhu et al. 

speech and text [18]. Recent advances in deep neural architectures and cross-modal attention mechanisms [7, 11] have significantly improved MER perfor-mance. However, emotional expressions possess hierarchical structured relation-ships that go beyond simple categories. Psychological emotion theories [14, 15] suggest that emotions have multi-component hierarchical structures and con-tinuous relationships, supporting emotion mixtures (e.g., joy + anticipation → optimism) and intensity variations (e.g., annoyance → anger → rage). While modern Transformer-based models can learn expressive representations in Eu-clidean space, standard Euclidean latent spaces do not explicitly provide a ge-ometric structure for organizing hierarchical emotional structures. In contrast, hyperbolic geometry naturally excels at representing tree-like or multi-level rela-tionships [12], making it a suitable complementary representation space for mod-eling structured underlying emotional cues. These limitations motivate exploring non-Euclidean representation spaces to model structured emotion in MER [3]. Hyperbolic geometry has recently attracted attention as a powerful represen-tation space for hierarchical data due to its exponential expansion [5]. Hyperbolic representation learning has been successfully applied in various domains, includ-ing natural language processing and affective computing [3, 5]. Fine-grained emo-tion classification [20] suggests that emotion categories are not isolated labels, instead exhibiting multi-level and intensity-dependent relationships. HypEmo [3] introduces label-aware hyperbolic embeddings to model hierarchical structures among fine-grained emotion categories. Earlier works applied hyperbolic layers to multimodal sentiment and emotion recognition [1], implementing both se-quential and parallel mixing schemes between Euclidean and hyperbolic dense layers. Although these studies demonstrate the potential of hyperbolic geometry for affective computing, they focus on the sample’s label hierarchy or on general Euclidean and hyperbolic layer composition. On the contrary, our GeoMER is a task-specific dual-geometry design framework. This design stems from the fact that the MER requires both abstract struc-ture modeling and cross-modal semantic alignment between heterogeneous modal-ities such as speech and text [5]. At the same time, mapping all representations into hyperbolic space may improve structural modeling, but weaken the ad-vantages of Euclidean representations in cross-modal alignment and introduce an optimization stability problem. Therefore, developing a unified, geometry-aware hybrid model that preserves the strengths of Euclidean representations and hyperbolic geometry is more suitable for MER. Thus, unlike previous works, our work is not a simple replacement of Euclidean embeddings with hyperbolic ones or a direct reuse of these components for MER. Instead, GeoMER is a joint Euclidean-Hyperbolic framework for multimodal emotion recognition. It consists of two branches: a) the Euclidean branch learns discriminative cross-modal representations with cross-attention transformers, and b) the hyperbolic branch models the latent structure of emotions in a hyperbolic manifold. The two branches are jointly optimized and integrated via a unified fusion mechanism, enabling the model to simultaneously preserve both Euclidean discriminative GeoMER 3

representations and more abstract hyperbolic features. The main contributions are as follows: 1. We propose GeoMER, a joint Euclidean-Hyperbolic MER framework that specializes both the discriminative semantic modeling (Euclidean space) and abstract hierarchical structural modeling (hyperbolic space). 2. We propose a HypGeoRoute module that softly assigns token representations to learnable geometric cores in the Poincaré ball via distance-aware routing. 3. We propose a core-wise hyperbolic self-attention mechanism that captures relationships among geometric cores in the Poincaré ball, preserving geomet-ric consistency while enhancing contextual modeling. 

# 2 Related Work 

Early MER studies [18] relied on handcrafted features and employed simple fu-sion methods, which were sensitive to noise. Recent works have used deep neural networks with attention mechanisms [11] and Transformer-based architectures such as TDFNet [21] and MemoCMT [7] to capture dynamic interactions across modalities. To better capture structured emotional relationships, researchers have in-troduced hyperbolic representation learning as it can model hierarchical data with exponential capacity [5]. The hyperbolic Transformer extends the self-attention mechanism to non-Euclidean spaces for hierarchical sequence mod-eling [19]. Several studies have explored the application of hyperbolic mod-eling in MER, including conversational emotion recognition with hyperbolic hypergraph fusion [22], hyperbolic fusion of heterogeneous speech representa-tions [13], and label-aware hyperbolic embeddings for fine-grained emotion clas-sification [3]. Meanwhile, hybrid geometric learning has been preliminarily stud-ied, in which Euclidean encoders are combined with hyperbolic components for multimodal representation learning [1]. Emotion Collider [5], HypEmo [3], and HyperPoint [17] further explore hyperbolic modeling for emotion mixture, label-aware classification, and multimodal representation learning. However, there are two key limitations. First, most existing MER methods operate in Euclidean space, which struggles to capture the more structured and abstract emotional representations. Although hyperbolic approaches have been explored, they remain limited in scope and often focus on specific components, such as embeddings or labels. Second, hybrid geometric methods remain under-developed, typically combining Euclidean and hyperbolic representations in a loosely coupled manner without a unified framework for joint optimization.

3.1 Feature Embedding 

We use the HuBERT encoder to encode the raw audio into a series of acoustic embeddings. All audio signals are 16 kHz. The RoBERTa model is employed to obtain contextual tokens from the transcriptions. 

3.2 Euclidean Multimodal Fusion Module 

The EuclFusion is designed to model cross-modal interactions in Euclidean space. The architecture follows MemoCMT [7] while improving on simple concatena-tion by introducing a bidirectional Long Short-Term Memory (BiLSTM) network for sequential aggregation. Two multi-head cross-attention mechanisms are first employed to effectively fuse speech and text cues. This step enhances token-level alignment across modalities. While cross-attention focuses on aligning and exchanging information between speech and text tokens, the subsequent BiL-STM serves a different role by modeling the temporal evolution of the fused GeoMER 5

multimodal sequence at the utterance level. Specifically, the fused multimodal token sequence is processed by a BiLSTM. This step dynamically integrates cross-modal cues over time, which is crucial for capturing the evolving nature of emotional expression in speech [8, 10]. Let the audio and text token sequences

Unlike tradi-tional Euclidean representation modeling, HypGeoRoute operates in the Poincaré ball to organize multimodal token embeddings around learnable geometric cores. # 4 Experiments 

MER Benchmark We use the IEMOCAP benchmark [2] that consists of 12 hours of audio-visual emotional conversations performed by 10 professional ac-tors (5 male, 5 female). Each session includes both scripted and improvised di-alogues designed to elicit natural emotional expressions. It provides multimodal recordings, including audio, text, facial motion capture, and videos. Only speech and text modalities are used in this paper. Following prior studies, four emotion classes, including angry , happy , sad , and neutral , are chosen. IEMOCAP con-tains 5,531 utterances in total, 4479 training set, 498 validation samples, and 554 test samples. The distribution of the training set, test set, and validation set is shown in Table 1. Evaluation Metrics. Given the class imbalance in IEMOCAP, we evaluate model performance using both overall and class-balanced metrics. Weighted ac-curacy (WA), unweighted accuracy (UA), and Macro-F1 are reported. WA mea-sures the overall classification accuracy, while UA averages recall across all classes to better reflect performance under class imbalance. In addition, Macro-F1 is ob-tained by averaging the F1-scores of all classes. 

Experiment Setup. All experiments are implemented in PyTorch and con-ducted on one NVIDIA RTX 3090 GPU. HuBERT-base and RoBERTa-base are used as frozen acoustic and text encoders, producing 768-dimensional features. The model is optimized using AdamW with β1 = 0 .9, β2 = 0 .999 , ϵ = 10 −8, and weight decay 5 × 10 −3, and the cross-entropy loss. The initial learning rate is 

1 × 10 −4 and decayed by a factor of 0.1 every 30 epochs. The model is trained for up to 60 epochs with early stopping based on validation UA. The hyperbolic curvature is c = 1 .0, the hyperbolic routing core is Nc = 4 , and the scaling coefficient α is learnable.

# 5 Conclusion 

In this paper, we propose GeoMER, a dual Euclidean–hyperbolic framework for multimodal emotion recognition. The proposed method combines a Euclidean Multimodal Fusion Module (EuclFusion) for modeling cross-modal interactions with a Hyperbolic Geometric Core Routing (HypGeoRoute) module to cap-ture latent hierarchical structures in emotional representations. Unlike exist-ing hyperbolic emotion embedding methods that mainly focus on label-level or representation-level modeling, distance-aware assignment, manifold-consistent aggregation, core-wise hyperbolic self-attention, and cross-modal gated fusion on the Poincaré ball, our HypGeoRoute module improves performance by capturing hierarchical structure across modalities. By integrating Euclidean multimodal fusion with hyperbolic geometric modeling, GeoMER provides a more expres-sive representation for complex emotional structure. Experimental results on the IEMOCAP benchmark show that the proposed method performs well, achieving 83.54% WA and 83.35% UA, outperforming the MemoCMT baseline by 2.49% WA and 2.02% UA. The ablation study further validates the effectiveness of the proposed components.
