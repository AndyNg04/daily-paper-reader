ABSTRACT 

Stress detection based on heart rate variability (HRV) has re-ceived increasing attention. However, existing personalized approaches integrate personal attributes with HRV through latent representations, limiting the interpretability of their in-teractions. In this work, we propose an LLM-enhanced per-sonalized stress detection framework that integrates HRV fea-tures and personal attributes through LLM-based semantic in-terpretation. The proposed framework achieves a 29.9% rela-tive MCC improvement over the strongest baseline on TILES-2018 while providing human-readable descriptions of indi-vidual physiological responses. The results highlight the po-tential of LLM-based semantic interpretation for achieving both effective and interpretable personalized stress detection. 

Index Terms — Personalized Stress Detection, Heart Rate Variability (HRV), large language models (LLMs), semantic interpretation 

1. INTRODUCTION 

With increasing awareness of mental health, physiological signal-based stress detection has become an important re-search topic. Among various physiological indicators, heart rate variability (HRV) has received considerable attention, as numerous studies have demonstrated its association with psy-chological stress [1, 2]. However, HRV responses vary across individuals due to personal attributes, such as age and gender [3, 4], as well as work-related factors, such as shift schedules [5], suggesting that personal context should be considered when interpreting HRV patterns for stress detection. Recent studies have incorporated personal attributes into HRV-based stress detection by aligning HRV with survey in-formation through contrastive learning [6], regularizing HRV embeddings using pairwise attribute similarities [7], and modeling higher-order HRV–attribute interactions through hypergraph learning [8]. Despite their effectiveness, these feature-based approaches implicitly encode HRV–attribute in-teractions in latent representations, providing limited insight into how relatively static personal attributes contextualize dy-namically changing HRV responses for stress detection [9]. Large language models (LLMs) offer a promising alterna-tive, having been applied across medical domains to integrate clinically informative text into downstream tasks [10]. Re-cent efforts have translated and aligned non-linguistic, high-dimensional physiological signals with LLMs for various downstream tasks [11, 12, 13]. Their ability to express con-textual information provides a natural means of describing how personal attributes relate to physiological responses. Motivated by this capability and the limitations of con-ventional feature-based integration, we propose the Beyond-Latent Personalization (BLP) framework, an LLM-enhanced stress classification framework that takes HRV features and LLM-generated domain-specific descriptions as inputs, with each description integrating personal attributes with HRV information from a specific physiological domain. As each HRV domain reflects distinct physiological characteristics, these descriptions provide complementary perspectives on an individual’s stress-related physiological responses. To reduce redundancy across their semantic representations, we impose an orthogonality constraint and adaptively ag-gregate them through learnable source weights, while an intra-subject contrastive objective is employed to enhance stress-discriminative representation learning. This design provides an interpretable alternative to conventional black-box integration: the generated descriptions explicitly reveal how the LLM integrates personal context with physiological information, while the learned source weights quantify the relative contribution of each HRV domain to stress detection. On TILES-2018, BLP achieves a 29.9% relative MCC im-provement over the strongest baseline, while its performance on WESAD supports its generalizability across datasets. These results demonstrate improved stress classification and transparency into how personal attributes complement physi-ological information, revealing higher-level relationships less accessible through conventional feature-based integration.

2.1. HRV Representation Learning 

For each subject, HRV feature vectors xh are temporally or-dered to form a sequence that captures the evolution of physi-ological responses over time. Positional encodings are added to xh, which are then processed by a Transformer encoder to model temporal dependencies, yielding encoded HRV em-beddings hh. An HRV projector then maps hh to ph for bidi-rectional cross-attention. 

2.2. LLM-based Semantic Interpretation 

The semantic interpretation module transforms structured physiological and personal information into a textual repre-sentation. Following prior studies that contextualize phys-iological measurements with domain knowledge and user information for LLM-based health analysis [11, 12], we con-vert each sample’s HRV features and personal attributes into textual components and incorporate them into predefined 

Physiological Interpretation Prompts . Specifically, the HRV features are organized into four physiological domains: time, frequency, multi-scale RR, and multi-scale dRR, with a separate prompt constructed for each domain. As shown in Table 1, the subject’s personal attributes are converted into the [Neutral Baseline Profile], which is shared across the four domain-specific prompts, while the HRV features and their interpretation context for each domain are organized into the [Data Interpretation Context] and [Current Physiological Markers]. Together with the predefined [Task] and [Output Constraints], these sections form the complete prompt. Dur-ing LLM Interpretation , the prompt is processed by an LLM to generate a Domain-Specific Description that describes the physiological state in the context of the individual’s baseline characteristics without directly predicting the stress label. 

2.3. Semantic Representation Learning 

The Domain-Specific Descriptions generated by Qwen3.5-9B [14] are encoded using Qwen3-Embedding-0.6B [15], yielding four 1024-dimensional semantic embeddings ht

for each sample. Each embedding is then transformed by a domain-specific Source Projector, producing four learn-able representations on which the orthogonal loss is ap-plied. These representations are adaptively aggregated using softmax-normalized learnable weights to capture the relative contribution of each HRV domain. Finally, the aggregated representation is projected into a 100-dimensional shared embedding space, yielding the semantic representation pt for subsequent cross-modal fusion.

3. EXPERIMENT SETUP 3.1. Dataset and Feature Extraction 

TILES-2018 [16] is a relatively large-scale longitudinal dataset collected from 212 hospital workers over 10 weeks under naturalistic conditions, whereas WESAD [17] is a smaller controlled dataset of 15 participants under Baseline, Stress, and Amusement conditions. Together, they provide complementary settings for evaluating personalized stress detection and framework generalizability. Both datasets use the same 61 HRV features across four domains (Table 2). For TILES-2018, following the data quality screening and label construction in [7], 185 participants are retained. HRV features are extracted from 5-min RR segments derived from heart rate measurements, with a 4-min overlap, and aggre-gated daily using 13 statistical functionals: mean, standard deviation (SD), coefficient of variation (CV), median, min-imum, maximum, 1st and 3rd quartiles, skewness, kurtosis, weighted mean, weighted SD, and weighted CV, resulting in 793 features that are reduced to 100 by Recursive Feature Elimination (RFE). WESAD uses 700-Hz ECG signals to extract HRV features from RR intervals using the recom-mended 60-s windows with a 0.25-s shift; windows with less than 80% RR coverage are excluded, and each valid window retains its 61-dimensional HRV vector without daily aggre-gation or RFE. Following [17], Baseline and Amusement are labeled non-stress and Stress as stress. The 17 TILES-2018 attributes from self-reported sur-veys are gender, age group, student status, current position, professional experience, organizational tenure, work shift, weekly working hours, monthly overtime, number of su-pervisees, race, English-language experience, relationship status, annual household income, number of children under 18, housing status, and daily commute time. The 11 WESAD attributes include age, height, weight, gender, and dominant hand; coffee consumption, sports participation, smoking sta-tus, and illness status on the day of data collection; and coffee consumption and smoking within the last hour. The HRV encoder uses one Transformer layer with two attention heads and a hidden dimension of 100, while bidirec-tional cross-attention uses four heads. Training uses a batch size of 16 subjects for up to 400 epochs, with λintra and λortho

w/o Embedding Extractor retains the LLM interpretation but removes the subsequent embedding extractor, directly us-ing the normalized last-token representation from the LLM as the semantic embedding. w/o LLM Interpretation removes LLM-based semantic interpretation and directly encodes the original prompt with the embedding extractor.

Ablation Study : Table 3 shows that, on TILES-2018, removing HRV or personal attributes from the LLM prompt reduces MCC by 54.4% and 27.6%, respectively. The former shows that HRV provides day-to-day physiological variations and distinct domain information, whose complementarity is preserved by the orthogonal constraint; the latter shows that personal attributes provide subject-specific baselines to contextualize such dynamic information. Together, they enable more complete personalized interpretations. LLM-Only substantially underperforms HRV-Only, indicating that semantics complement rather than replace physiological rep-resentations. Removing LLM interpretation reduces MCC by 8.0%/3.1% (TILES-2018/WESAD), showing that, although the structured prompt preserves HRV and attribute informa-tion, LLM interpretation further establishes semantic rela-tionships between them. Removing the semantic embedding extractor causes larger reductions of 17.6%/2.3%, demon-strating its role in extracting task-relevant representations from rich semantics. Its greater impact on TILES-2018 may reflect the more complex daily physiological and personal information in its longitudinal naturalistic setting.

5. CONCLUSION AND FUTURE WORK 

In this work, we proposed BLP, which integrates HRV and personal attributes through LLM-based semantic interpre-tation to generate personalized, human-readable descrip-tions fused with temporal HRV representations for stress classification. BLP outperforms all compared baselines on TILES-2018, while its performance on WESAD supports its generalizability across datasets. Ablation results verify the complementary contributions of HRV features, personal attributes, semantic interpretation, and dedicated embedding extraction. However, the framework remains reliant on HRV representations, as LLM-generated semantic representations alone are insufficiently discriminative. Future work will enhance the task-relevant information in LLM-generated de-scriptions while preserving semantic interpretability, aiming to better balance interpretability and predictive performance. 6.
