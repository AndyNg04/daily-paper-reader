A B S T R A C T 

Social media users interact with their own social networks but also with platform-wide content, where a small number of highly popular users may receive a disproportionate share of attention. In this paper, we study how those interactions outside the social networks and user popularity regulate the formation of opinion clusters. We design a bounded-confidence opinion dynamics model on a multilayer network consisting of a social network coupled to a fully connected explore layer of potential interaction contacts. We show that uniform sampling on the explore layer increases mixing across social neighbourhoods and reduces opinion diversity while also weakening the influence of the underlying social network. We then introduce a popularity-driven interaction mechanism in which contact selection is biased by a time-evolving popularity score, so that frequently selected users become increasingly likely to be re-selected. The resulting concentration of explore-layer interactions on a small number of highly popular users reduces the homogenising effect of the explore layer, increasing opinion diversity. The resulting changes relative to a single-layer system depend on the underlying social network structure. As the popularity inequality increases, a small number of highly popular users can act as opinion attractors and sustain polarisation in regimes where the corresponding model without popularity heterogeneity converges to consensus. 

## 1. Introduction 

On social media platforms, users are exposed to a variety of opinions on different topics and in different ways. On those platforms, users switch between different information feeds, where they can interact in different ways with friends, influencers, other like-minded users, or advertisements. In-teractions have traditionally been constrained to a user-selected friendship network, but in more modern platforms, mechanisms of exploration have been introduced to allow users to interact with users, not necessarily like-minded, beyond their direct social network through recommenda-tion, discovery, and advertising mechanisms 1. Furthermore, users have been increasingly exposed to posts chosen and ranked via algorithmic personalisation based on user simi-larity, past activity, popularity scores, and platform rules 2,3 .Opinion dynamics models have been used to study the effects of these interactions and activity patterns on the evolution of the user opinions and on the emergence of opinion consensus and polarisation 4–6 .Bounded-confidence models form an important class of opinion dynamics models in which interactions depend on the opinion-distance between individuals 5. One of the most well-known continuous opinion dynamics models is the Deffuant-Weisbuch model 7–9 . Different extensions of bounded-confidence models including heterogeneous be-haviours have been studied. It has been shown, for exam-ple, that heterogeneity in stubbornness 10–12 can pull other users’ opinions towards them, affecting opinion diversity or creating extremes. Heterogeneity in the number of friends and the structure of the social network can affect opinion formation through features such as social clustering, highly connected users, and their interplay with personalisation, which may reinforce the formation of echo chambers 16–18 . These mod-els assume interactions via a single social layer. However, in social media, information can be fed via different layers and this can be studied by modelling the system as a mul-tilayer social network influencing opinion formation 19,20 .Each layer may correspond to one mechanism of interaction, e.g., direct friendship interactions or platform-wide content recommendations. Previous research has shown that multi-plexity may hinder consensus formation 19,21 . These temporal evo-lutions can be included in opinion dynamics models by giving the users the possibility of making and breaking so-cial ties. These rewiring mechanisms can be random 23 , ho-mophilic 24–26 , or heterophilic 24 . Rewiring can affect opin-ion diversity and consensus formation 23,26 , and lead to the formation of echo chambers 27 . Temporal evolution can also be included by considering time-evolving attributes of the users 28,29 , such as stubbornness 12,30 or susceptibility. These agents are, under some circumstances, able to stir the opinions of other agents towards them via social pres-sure or nudging, thereby affecting the group opinion land-scape 35–37 . In situations where individuals are more subjec-tive to these highly influential agents, the whole population becomes subjective to the external influence 38 . We represent these interactions using a multiplex social network, with a complex social network layer and a fully connected explore layer. In the baseline model, interactions on the explore layer allow the mixing of users opinions across the population independently of the social network structure. We then introduce a time-evolving popularity mechanism that biases partner selection on this explore layer, such that frequently selected users become increasingly likely to be re-selected due to changes in their popularity level. The two mechanisms therefore have com-peting effects. On one hand, the explore layer broadens the set of potential contacts, whereas popularity reinforcement concentrates these interactions on a small number of users. We study how this competition affects the formation of opinion clusters and whether sufficiently concentrated popu-larity can counteract the homogenising effect of interactions outside the social network.

3.2. Emergence of Popularity Heterogeneity 

In the individual model, there are no viral events and the popularity of the users depends entirely on being chosen by other users in the explore layer. This process does not affect all agents equally because popularity reinforces the chance of future selection. Users who are chosen early on and increase their popularity in the first time steps have a higher chance of being chosen in subsequent time steps, leading to a further increase in popularity due to reinforcement. Figure 2a shows that independently of the time step 𝑡 , the emergent distribution is not skewed and most of the users have low to intermediate popularity levels. In contrast, the quasi-stationary popularity distribution for viral models is right-skewed if the viral impact 𝑖 𝑣 is large enough (Fig. 2b-e). Viral models produce some users with a significantly higher popularity than the rest. If 𝑖 𝑣 is small, there are no high-popularity users and the popularity distribution resembles that of the individual model. A larger viral impact, together with the reinforcing process, results in a flatter popularity distribution compared with smaller values of the viral impact, for which users experiencing a viral event receive less reinforcement. Figure 2f shows that the fitted slope increases when the probability of interacting in the explore layer is increased, due to the larger contri-bution of the individual popularity mechanism in the viral model. Furthermore, increasing 𝑓 𝑣 results in a decrease in popularity heterogeneity due to the reduced contribution of viral events to popularity (Figs. 2d,e).

3.4. Network Structure and Opinion Dynamics 

We now analyse how the popularity-driven mechanism depends on the underlying social network structure. The viral dynamics on a CM structure (Fig. 6a-c) is similar to the ER case (Fig. 3). For 𝑃 𝑟 = 0.1 the viral model can increase the number of major opinion clusters for all values of 𝜖 . For larger values of 𝑃 𝑟 , the results also resemble what was observed for other network structures, indicating that frequent interactions in the explore layer weaken the influence of the underlying social network topology. A different behaviour appears for the highly clustered WS networks. For 𝑃 𝑟 = 0 .1, small 𝜖 , and large 𝑖 𝑣 , the WS01 network produces more major clusters than for larger values of 𝑃 𝑟 (Fig. 6d-f). In contrast, for the ER network, the number of major opinion clusters increases with 𝑃 𝑟 at 𝜖 = 0 .1 and large 𝑖 𝑣 . This difference results from the interplay between the two network layers. When 𝑖 𝑣 is large, a small number of users accumulate most of the popularity and dominate interactions in the explore layer. At small confidence bounds, however, many of these interactions do not lead to opinion updates because the opinion difference exceeds 𝜖 . Since 𝑃 𝑟 is small, the opinion dynamics therefore remains largely gov-erned by interactions on the social network layer. The high clustering coefficient of the WS01 network promotes greater opinion diversity. Despite this increase, the viral model still produces fewer major clusters than the corresponding single-layer model up to 𝜖 ≈ 0 .3. For larger 𝜖 , however, the viral model can sustain a polarised state while the single-layer and baseline models converge to consensus. The increase in the number of clusters for small 𝑃 𝑟 , small 𝜖 , and large 𝑖 𝑣 is stronger for the WS001 network, consistent with the larger clustering coefficient (Fig. 6g-i). For 𝑃 𝑟 ∈ {0 .5, 0.9} , the viral dynamics show much weaker dependence on the un-derlying structure. Because the corresponding single-layer dynamics remain strongly dependent on network structure, the viral model can produce either more or fewer opinion clusters than the single-layer model depending on the struc-ture and the value of 𝜖 . Figure 6j-l shows that the empirical network follows the same qualitative behaviour. Increasing the viral impact 𝑖 𝑣 increases opinion diversity for all values of 𝑃 𝑟 . At small 𝑃 𝑟 , the outcome remains more strongly influ-enced by the underlying network structure, whereas at larger 

𝑃 𝑟 , the dynamics is increasingly dominated by interactions with highly popular users. The results show that the relative importance of the social network structure and popularity-driven interactions depends on both 𝑃 𝑟 and 𝑖 𝑣 . At small 𝑃 𝑟 , the network struc-ture remains important because most interactions still occur through the social layer, while increasing 𝑖 𝑣 leads to an increase in the popularity heterogeneity generated by viral events. At large 𝑃 𝑟 , interactions in the explore layer become more frequent, so the effect of increasing 𝑖 𝑣 becomes more pronounced, i.e. a small number of highly popular users increasingly dominate the dynamics and can sustain polar-isation over a wider range of 𝜖 .

## 4. Conclusions 

Individuals change their opinion through repeated social interactions, which are constrained by social networks. On social media, users are also exposed to content outside their immediate social neighbourhood, and a small number of highly visible users may be encountered much more often than the majority of others. In this paper, we studied how the coupling of a social network layer to an explorative interaction layer, and the introduction of popularity-driven interactions on this second layer, affect the formation of opinion clusters in a bounded-confidence opinion dynamics model. Adding an explore layer to an underlying social network increases the diversity of interaction partners and reduces opinion diversity. In the single-layer model, highly clustered networks produce more opinion clusters at small confi-dence bounds. Explore-layer interactions strongly suppress this additional fragmentation, reducing the differences be-tween network structures. Consequently, opinion diversity becomes less dependent on the underlying social network structure as users interact more frequently through the ex-plore layer. The popularity heterogeneity generated by the individual popularity mechanism is not large enough to substantially affect the final number of major opinion clusters relative to the baseline model. In contrast, viral events create popularity heterogeneity, which can increase opinion diversity com-pared to the baseline model.
