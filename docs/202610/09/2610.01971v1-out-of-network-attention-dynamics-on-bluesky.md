## 摘要
个性化社交媒体通常依赖显式的关注关系图来决定用户会看到哪些内容；然而，用户有多少注意力会跨越其未建立的关系边界，目前仍缺乏大规模研究。我们在 Bluesky 上研究这一问题。Bluesky 是一个大型去中心化微博平台，其默认信息流采用简单的按时间倒序排列的内容推荐机制。我们分析了 2023 年 2 月至 9 月期间从近乎完整的平台数据快照中收集的 1.73 亿次用户—作者互动（点赞、转发、回复和引用）。我们按照注意力路径长度对每次互动进行分解（来自已关注账号、由已关注账号转传、在两跳关注范围内可达，或超出该范围），发现 74.5% 的互动是通过用户已关注的账号触达用户的。若按关注关系图中的距离而非传播路径衡量，80.6% 的互动发生在两跳关注范围内，远高于基于期望度数零模型预测的 22.4%。随后，我们刻画了探索行为在不同用户之间及用户使用时长变化过程中的差异。少数覆盖范围广的用户贡献了四分之三的探索活动总量；而总体上探索行为随使用时长增加而下降这一现象，掩盖了个体层面的三种不同变化轨迹。最后，来自两跳范围之外的注意力转化为新关注关系的比率，不到两跳范围内探索性注意力转化率的三分之一。总体而言，这些结果表明，该平台上的网络外探索规模可观，但受到网络邻近性的强烈制约，且不太可能转化为新的社交关系。

## Abstract
Personalized social media commonly relies on explicit follow graphs to shape what content users encounter; yet how much attention crosses ties they have not formed remains largely undocumented at scale. We study this question on Bluesky, a large decentralized microblogging platform whose default feed relies on a simple, reverse-chronological content recommender. We analyze 173 million user-author interactions (likes, reposts, replies, and quotes) collected from a near-complete platform dump between February and September 2023. We decompose each interaction by attention-path length (already followed, relayed by a followed account, reachable within two follow-hops, or beyond) and find that 74.5% of interactions reach the user through an account they already follow. Measured by distance in the follow graph rather than by route, 80.6% of interaction lands within two follow hops, far beyond the 22.4% an expected-degree null predicts. We then characterize how exploration varies across users and over tenure. A broad-reaching minority generates three quarters of all exploratory activity, while aggregate declines in exploration with tenure mask three distinct individual trajectories. Finally, attention reaching beyond two hops converts into new follow ties at less than one third the rate of two-hop-local exploratory attention. Together, these results depict a platform where out-of-network exploration is substantial in volume but strongly constrained by network proximity and unlikely to translate into new social ties.
