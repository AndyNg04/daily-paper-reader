Abstract 

We introduce AgentPersonaBench (APB) 1, a benchmark evaluating whether persona conditioning faithfully steers downstream agent behavior. While language models are increasingly deployed for persona-driven user simulation , existing benchmarks primarily evaluate conversational styling or self-reports rather than authentic behavioral fidelity. APB evaluates latent persona adherence one trait at a time, embedding each target trait within a complete synthetic profile without explicitly naming the trait or disclosing the test. Ground-truth adherence is verified strictly from observable actions across four interaction surfaces of increasing realism: survey, chat, web (interactive web environments), and app (desktop software environments). APB comprises 

2,460 tasks spanning 867 traits, verified through automated audits and expert review. Our evaluation of 20 frontier model arms demonstrates that high-fidelity user simulation is already attainable: leading models achieve up to 84.7% full-pass adherence under unprompted conditions. At the same time, APB identifies clear behavioral boundaries: adherence drops across interaction modalities (only 37.9–64.3% pass all four surfaces), multi-attribute demands degrade retention, and competing model families exhibit pronounced behavioral divergence. 1 Introduction 

Deployed language-model systems increasingly embody personal identities. Recommender agents reflect consumer tastes, social science experiments deploy synthetic cohorts to simulate human respondents, autonomous agents stand in for users during software testing, and personal assistants operate under persistent user profiles. In each setting, a system prompt establishes an identity, and the model is entrusted to sustain it across diverse tasks without explicit reminders. We define this paradigm as persona-driven user simulation : a persona specifies an individual’s latent attributes, an agent acts them out, and consequential downstream decisions are made based on the agent’s observable behavior. Yet existing evaluations focus almost exclusively on conversational role-play: maintaining stylistic tone, avoiding breaking frame, and recalling biographical trivia. These criteria evaluate only the conversational surface. When an agent acts on a user’s behalf, the decisive question is behavioral: does a latent trait observably govern what the agent decides in an everyday situation where that trait is never mentioned? As illustrated in Figure 1, a rich persona may fully describe who an agent is without consistently steering what it does. A vegan persona ordering a meal must not place meat in the shopping cart, and a risk-averse persona rebalancing a portfolio must avoid high-leverage assets. If the trait genuinely guides the agent, it directly dictates the physical choice; if the model merely generates persona-flavored prose, the behavioral constraint is lost. To evaluate authentic behavioral steerability, we introduce AgentPersonaBench (APB) , a benchmark evaluating whether latent persona conditioning faithfully guides downstream agent behavior across everyday digital tasks. APB comprises 2,460 tasks spanning 867 traits. Rather than testing traits in isolation with minimal stub prompts, APB embeds each target trait into a complete individual profile sampled from a one-million persona corpus. Because the profile spans hundreds of realistic background attributes, the tested trait must actively compete for attention, faithfully reproducing the distracting conditions of production deployments. To evaluate steerability under varied interaction modalities, tasks are constructed across four interaction surfaces of increasing environmental realism (§3.1.1): survey (questionnaires with randomized options), chat (dialogues with scripted off-persona pressure), web (interactive web environments evaluated from browser state and interface choices), and app (desktop applications evaluated through visual interaction and application state). Across all surfaces, tasks are constructed under strict latent conditioning: the target trait is never named, the evaluation is never announced, and ground truth is strictly isolated from the agent’s view. Every task is audited to eliminate guessing shortcuts, position bias, and superficial prompt leaks before admission (§3.3). APB evaluates model performance strictly from observable environment artifacts rather than conversational self-reports or post-hoc justifications. Approximately four-fifths (78.5%) of all checks are deterministic code predicates over structured artifacts (selected option IDs, DOM state, and application files), reserving standardized rubric evaluation solely for open-ended dialogue turns. Across 20 frontier model arms from eight providers, our evaluation shows that leading models can achieve up to 84.7% full-pass adherence under unprompted conditions, while identifying cross-surface fragility and multi-attribute co-exposure as primary open challenges. This paper provides three primary contributions: 

• A Latent Behavioral Measurement Protocol : We formalize an evaluation methodology that assesses persona adherence exclusively through unprompted, verified actions rather than conversational self-reports or explicit instructions, enforcing strict leak prevention and audit verification. 

• A 2,460-Task Multi-Surface Benchmark : We construct and release the first comprehensive benchmark evaluating latent persona adherence across four distinct interaction surfaces (including interactive web environments and desktop graphical interfaces) spanning 867 traits. 

• Empirical Findings on Frontier Models : We evaluate 20 model arms across eight providers. Leading models (including gemini-3-8-flash at 84.7%, opus-5-5 at 82.9%, and gpt-6-astra at 82.1%) show that reliable persona simulation is achievable, while our diagnostics identify cross-surface fragility and multi-attribute co-exposure as key challenges. 

2 Related Work 

User simulation and persona agents. Language models are widely evaluated as persona agents, either through conversational role-play benchmarks assessing voice consistency, frame retention, and biographical recall (Zhang et al., 2018; Wang et al., 2023b; Tu et al., 2024; Samuel et al., 2024; Zhou et al., 2024b; Tseng et al., 2024), or through synthetic cohorts simulating demographic populations and human subjects in social science (Argyle et al., 2023; Santurkar et al., 2023; Horton et al., 2023; Park et al., 2023; 2024; Ge et al., 2024; Yao et al., 2024). However, conversational benchmarks evaluate whether generated text “sounds like” a character; an agent writing eloquent prose while selecting meat for a vegan passes text-based role-play rubrics but fails in practice. Meanwhile, demographic simulations evaluate aggregate statistical alignment, where individual errors can cancel out across AgentPersonaBench 3

cohorts. In contrast, APB measures individual behavioral fidelity under deterministic ground truth : does an unmentioned trait decisively govern an agent’s concrete, observable action? 

Instruction following, steerability, and system-prompt adherence. Prior work evaluates instruction following (Ouyang et al., 2022; Zhou et al., 2023; Jiang et al., 2024b), alignment through preference optimization (Christiano et al., 2017; Rafailov et al., 2023; Bai et al., 2022b), system-prompt robustness (Qin et al., 2024a; Mu et al., 2025), and steerability toward specific tones or viewpoints (Dong et al., 2023; Chen et al., 2025). However, these benchmarks explicitly declare the target behavior in the prompt. Under explicit prompting, models readily display sycophancy (Sharma et al., 2023) and decouple professed principles from concrete actions (Gu et al., 2025). A persona is not an operational command; APB evaluates whether a persona implicitly guides decisions when the trait itself is never named or requested. 

Agentic and computer-use benchmarks. Recent benchmarks evaluate agents operating across interactive digital environments: web browsers (Yao et al., 2022; Zhou et al., 2024a; Koh et al., 2024; De Chezelles et al., 2024), operating systems and native GUIs (Xie et al., 2024; Rawles et al., 2024; Trivedi et al., 2024), tool use (Patil et al., 2025), and general assistance (Mialon et al., 2023; Jimenez et al., 2024). These suites evaluate functional competence , determining whether an agent can successfully execute an end-to-end task. APB uses similar interactive environments (web browser tasks and desktop applications) but inverts the objective: functional execution is treated as a baseline control, and the target metric is whether the agent acts in accordance with its latent persona. We provide an extended survey, taxonomy, and benchmark comparison in Appendix B. 

3 Benchmark Design 

3.1 Overview 

APB measures whether a specific personal trait, carried implicitly by an assigned persona profile, systematically governs what an agent decides and executes in a situation that never mentions that trait. Rather than treating personas as monolithic textual roles, a task in APB isolates an individual trait, embeds it within an agent initialized with a complete persona possessing it, and places the agent in an everyday operational errand where that trait dictates observable consequences. The benchmark’s architecture systematically operationalizes this behavioral objective across three core design commitments: 

3.1.1 Design Principles The benchmark evaluates individual traits rather than holistic characters. Target traits are sampled from a comprehensive persona schema. A single-attribute task isolates one trait to yield a binary verdict (held or violated), whereas a multi-attribute task scores multiple traits co-occurring in a single artifact out of 𝑁 . This distinguishes whether individual traits survive on their own from whether multiple traits can be sustained jointly. 

Adherence is verified through unprompted downstream actions rather than self-reports. Tasks never name the target trait or announce the evaluation, preventing models from relying on simple instruction-following or superficial self-affirmation. Instead, behavior is recorded from concrete artifacts across four interaction surfaces: option selections (survey), dialogue turns (chat), web interaction traces (web), and application state files (app). Ground truth is strictly isolated from the agent’s view across all surfaces under a standardized execution protocol. 

Target traits must compete within complete and distracting persona profiles. Each persona is drawn from a 1M-profile synthetic corpus with hundreds of background attributes, reproducing the distracting conditions of real deployments. Every task is audited for leaks, position bias, and guessing shortcuts before admission (§3.3). Approximately four fifths of all checks are deterministic predicates over structured artifacts, reserving a standardized LLM judge solely for free-text dialogue.

3.2 Task Construction 

Constructing an APB task requires addressing three coupled design requirements: embedding the target trait within a complete, distracting persona profile; engineering scenarios that force meaningful behavioral divergence across diverse interaction media; and verifying adherence from observable execution artifacts rather than self-reports. To achieve rigorous experimental control, each candidate task must systematically operationalize an abstract personal dimension into an unprompted, observable behavioral test without leaking the underlying trait to the agent. Below, we detail our three-stage construction pipeline (§3.2.1) illustrated in Figure 3, followed by two concrete worked examples (§3.2.2) spanning all four interaction surfaces. AgentPersonaBench 5

3.2.1 The Construction Pipeline 

Tasks are systematically generated from the claim under test in a dependency-preserving sequence (Figure 3): the target trait is fixed first, the environment is constructed to expose behavioral choices governed by that trait, and the checker is defined over the resulting artifacts. Rather than relying on unstructured generation, this progression operationalizes three formal components in sequence: ❶ sampling a whole persona carrying the target trait, 

❷ constructing the environment across four interaction surfaces, and ❸ defining the deterministic checker that evaluates the resulting execution artifact. 

❶ Sample the persona. We first draw a target trait from the schema as the primary claim under test, paired with a contrast value that implies an opposing observable choice. We then sample a complete persona from the persona corpus, conditioned on holding that target value. The trait takes precedence over the persona profile: the persona serves as the carrier of the claim rather than the initial anchor, enabling structured coverage across the schema. We preserve the persona whole, forcing the tested trait to compete for attention with hundreds of realistic background attributes rather than standing isolated in the prompt. At inference time, the full profile is rendered into the agent’s system prompt. This ensures measurement remains latent rather than instructed, since the trait appears as one descriptive attribute among hundreds, never as an operational directive. The identical persona prompt is maintained across all four interaction surfaces for each model. 

❷ Build the environment. The evaluation situation is formulated as an everyday errand rather than an explicit trait query. Scenarios are engineered so that holding the pinned trait leads to a distinct behavioral decision from holding the contrast value, while completely omitting the trait itself from the task presentation. The concrete interaction is tailored to each surface: survey presents multiple-choice questionnaires with randomized, unlabelled options evaluated against an independent answer key; chat engages the agent with an interlocutor that applies progressive conversational pressure to adopt off-persona choices, testing multi-turn stability; web deploys interactive web pages, recording user interface selections and submitted decisions; and app operates interactive desktop software via visual observations and interface actions. Across all four surfaces, the underlying persona, instruction, and decision contract remain identical; only the medium of interaction changes. 

❸ Design the checker. Every task pairs the scenario with an automated verification procedure and a declared 

adherence signal defining the criterion for success. For approximately four fifths of the checks, adherence is evaluated by deterministic predicates over structured artifacts: selected choices, web transaction outcomes, or files written by the application process. For the remaining checks where evidence exists solely in open-ended text, a standardized LLM judge evaluates conversational turns against the declared criteria. Candidate tasks are then submitted to our verification pipeline (§3.3): automated contract audits verify schema validity, leak absence, and discriminative power, followed by expert human review. Tasks that fail verification are excluded, ensuring that the benchmark strictly satisfies latent measurement and empirical validity.

Figure 5: Task admission workflow. A candidate task produced by the construction pipeline (Figure 3) passes through two verification stages (an automated audit and human review) before entering the released suite. A failure at either stage returns the task to the contributor. Stage 1 is automated and runs on every submission; the Stage 2 rows are questions no checker could answer. 

Syntactic validity alone is insufficient; an admissible task must resist trivial shortcuts, label leaks, and guessing heuristics. 

Stage 1: automated audit. Automated verification enforces five programmatic contract checks against a standardized audit specification: 

• Contract integrity : Validates task specification compliance, ensuring the persona profile strictly contains the targeted trait, the instructions contain no evaluative hints or trait names, and reference solutions execute correctly without template placeholders. 

• Leak detection : Exhaustively scans all agent-accessible materials (task context, environment resources, and application data) for trait identifiers or evaluative verdict markers. In particular, checks distinguish objective properties (e.g., dish ingredient metadata) from evaluative shortcuts, which are strictly rejected. 

• Discrimination & bias audit : Enumerates the complete decision space under content-blind policies to ensure an agent cannot pass via positional bias (e.g., consistently selecting the first option) or loose pass rules. 

• Cross-surface parity : Asserts structural parity across all four surface realizations of a trait, verifying that declared metrics, answer counts, and evaluation thresholds are mathematically equivalent. 

• Verifier execution suite : Stress-tests the verifier code against synthetic positive, negative, partial, and malformed submissions, confirming that positive credit is awarded only for all-adherent outcomes and that unexpected formats fail gracefully rather than crashing. All findings are categorized by agent exposure: internal diagnostic notes are permitted, but any leakage accessible to the evaluated model triggers an immediate blocking rejection.

4.1 Benchmark Leaderboard 

Table 1 reports the comprehensive APB leaderboard across all 20 evaluation arms, presenting full-pass rates for single-attribute tasks, multi-attribute tasks (co-exposing three traits simultaneously), and performance across each of the four interaction surfaces, alongside overall pass rates and average cost per task. Surface differences depend heavily on the model architecture and interaction modality. Across the leaderboard, chat represents the lowest-scoring surface for 15 of the 20 arms. In multi-turn chat dialogues, models face active conversational pressure from an interlocutor nudging them toward off-persona choices, causing adherence to degrade substantially relative to single-turn surveys. The desktop app surface presents another steep hurdle: interacting via coordinate clicks and visual perception introduces grounding errors that compound persona drift, and app is the lowest-scoring surface for four of the six OpenAI arms. Multi-attribute tasks expose an even sharper vulnerability across all model families. While top-tier reasoning models like gpt-6-astra maintain 80.6% adherence under multi-trait co-exposure (compared to 83.6% on single-attribute tasks), smaller or lighter models suffer steep drops: gpt-6-luna falls from 74.4% to 45.7%, 

gpt-5-6-luna from 72.1% to 40.8%, and deepseek-v4-flash from 64.8% to 36.4%. Holding multiple latent traits in context without explicit prompting rapidly strains the model’s attentional capacity. In the following subsections, we examine domain-level adherence (§4.2) before turning to three matched-cohort questions: whether adherence transfers across surfaces (§4.3), which checks remain satisfied when a task fails (§4.4), and whether models fail on the same tasks (§4.5).

4.4 Joint Trait Adherence 

We next compare the six arms on 1,172 shared multi-attribute tasks. Figure 6b shows the fraction of tasks meeting each normalized-credit threshold. Its right endpoints give full-pass rates; the drop as the threshold increases shows how much adherence is partial. Astra passes every check on 81.1% of tasks, retains some checks on 16.6%, and receives zero credit on 2.3%. 4.6 Run-to-Run Stability 

Every leaderboard cell comes from a single run per task. To measure how much a rerun changes the result, we evaluate the GPT-6 pair ( gpt-6-sol and gpt-6-luna ) three times on all four surfaces of the current task set (502 survey, 565 chat, 707 web, and 686 app tasks), the same tasks as Table 1. Persona, task, prompt, reasoning effort, and judge are identical across repeats, so the repeats differ only through sampling in the evaluated model, the simulated conversational partner, and the judge. Because survey and chat were re-run on the current harness, their absolute rates differ from Table 1, whose survey and chat cells come from the frozen-release runs. 

Aggregate rates are stable. Table 3 shows that the standard deviation of a model’s surface-level pass rate across the three repeats is at most 2.0 points. Two repeats of the same model on the same tasks differ by 1.1–2.3 points on average and by at most 4.7. The sol–luna gap ranges from 8.9 to 20.8 points, its 95% interval excludes zero on every surface, and sol leads luna in all twelve surface–repeat comparisons. Model differences of the size reported in Table 1 are therefore not artifacts of run-to-run variation. 

Task-level agreement and dialogue dynamics. Across tasks scored in all three repeats, pairwise repeat agreement remains substantial, ranging from 74.3% to 92.0% (Cohen’s 𝜅 0.46–0.75). Consistent with the stochasticity of an unscripted multi-turn conversational partner, chat exhibits lower agreement ( 𝜅 0.46 and 0.47) than deterministic interface surfaces like web and app ( 𝜅 0.63–0.75). A single run thus supports robust model-level comparisons at the resolution of a few points, though fine-grained task-level statements, such as which model passes another’s failures (Section 4.5), reflect empirical rates under natural dialogue variance rather than invariant properties of a task. 5 Discussion 

5.1 Decoupling Competence from Persona Fidelity 

Results across APB show that latent persona adherence operates largely orthogonally to general task competence. Standard post-training optimizes foundation models for helpfulness, technical accuracy, and unconstrained problem solving. Human simulation, by contrast, frequently requires operating under bounded rationality: respecting localized knowledge ceilings, subjective tastes, risk-averse heuristics, or declining mathematically optimal shortcuts. When a model exhibits capability intrusion (such as an elementary student persona spontaneously solving formal algebra), the error stems not from weak reasoning, but from an inability to suppress pre-trained capability in favor of assigned profile constraints. Conversely, successful environmental execution does not guarantee persona compliance. In interactive browser and desktop environments, an agent may navigate multi-step interface operations (such as finding navigation paths, filling forms, and executing checkout flows) while selecting items that directly violate latent lifestyle AgentPersonaBench 14 

or dietary constraints. For example, in social-media-skeptic-web , top-tier models like gpt-6-astra and 

opus-5-5 complete the interface sequence to populate home-screen applications on a newly initialized device, yet select photo-sharing and public feed apps that violate the persona’s latent skepticism toward social media. In contrast, arms such as gpt-6-sol pass by selecting local-only encrypted notes and a weather utility with minimal permissions. Here the failure is governed entirely by the selected content rather than interface execution competence. Benchmark competence metrics on suites such as SWE-bench or OSWorld capture whether an agent can achieve an errand, but offer no guarantee regarding how that errand is resolved relative to user profile specifications. Evaluating agentic systems therefore demands verifying behavioral boundaries alongside raw functional success. 

5.2 Cross-Surface Fragility and Attentional Load 

Our matched cohort evaluations reveal that conversational adherence provides an incomplete indicator of real-world steerability. Models that maintain high compliance in structured surveys or text-only dialogue frequently experience steep performance drops when deployed across interactive web and desktop software environments. An agent that readily declares vegetarian preferences in an interview may nonetheless select animal-derived ingredients when navigating a dense grocery delivery website. These cross-surface discrepancies indicate that persona conditioning is fragile, and that conversational role-play benchmarks overestimate an agent’s true fidelity in practical computer-use environments. Multi-attribute tasks introduce substantial attentional competition. While top-tier reasoning models like 

gpt-6-astra partially sustain multi-trait combinations, lighter and non-reasoning architectures suffer severe performance degradation under joint exposure, dropping by upwards of 25 percentage points. Holding multiple latent attributes in context without explicit per-turn reminders competes directly with the attentional capacity required for multi-step tool use and environmental navigation. Finally, our failure-overlap analyses (§4.5) demonstrate that no single model family uniformly subsumes another across all personas and surfaces. Different architectures display idiosyncratic behavioral blind spots, suggesting that robust user simulation in production workflows will benefit from heterogeneous model routing and surface-specialized ensembles rather than defaulting to any single monolithic model. 

6 Conclusion 

AgentPersonaBench evaluates latent persona adherence in frontier AI agents through unprompted, verified downstream behavior. Across 2,460 tasks, four distinct interaction surfaces, and 867 discrete traits, our empirical evaluation of 20 model arms demonstrates that persona fidelity cannot be inferred from conversational dialogue or aggregate benchmark competence. High adherence on one surface frequently coexists with failure on another, multi-attribute conditioning strains context allocation across all model families, and competing models exhibit pronounced behavioral complementarity. By evaluating concrete actions rather than conversational self-reports, APB establishes an empirical standard for testing whether autonomous agents can reliably simulate diverse human users in interactive digital environments.

B Extended Related Work 

This section expands our discussion of prior literature across persona agents, instruction following, steerability, and computer-use benchmarks. Table 4 summarizes the structural differences between APB and representative benchmark paradigms. 

B.1 Persona Agents, Role-Play, and User Simulation 

Prior research on simulating humans with language models generally divides into two lines of inquiry: character consistency in conversational role-play, and synthetic population sampling in empirical social science. While both directions offer valuable measurement tools, neither evaluates whether background identity guides unprompted behavioral decisions. 

Character consistency and conversational role-play. The first tradition investigates whether a language model can faithfully sustain an assigned character persona. Foundational work centered on open-domain dialogue conditioned on brief persona sentences (Zhang et al., 2018). Recent advances extend this to literary and historical personas, evaluating whether models sustain conversational voice, avoid breaking character, and accurately retrieve biographical facts (Shao et al., 2023; Wang et al., 2023b; Tu et al., 2024; Chen et al., 2024b;a). Subsequent frameworks have assessed persona agents along multi-dimensional behavioral and social axes (Samuel et al., 2024), administered standard psychometric inventories (e.g., Big Five, MBTI) to role-played personas (Wang et al., 2024; Jiang et al., 2024a), placed agents into dynamic social episodes to evaluate multi-party interactions and negotiation (Zhou et al., 2024b), and systematically measured how persona consistency degrades over extended multi-turn conversations (Li et al., 2024). Comprehensive surveys chart the growth and taxonomies of these role-playing systems (Tseng et al., 2024). 

Synthetic cohorts and human substitution in social science. A complementary tradition deploys persona-conditioned models to stand in for human participants in empirical social science. Researchers have examined whether models can emulate representative survey respondents across demographic slices (Argyle et al., 2023; Santurkar et al., 2023; Durmus et al., 2024), explored the replication of classic behavioral economics and psychology experiments using synthetic participant pools (Aher et al., 2023; Horton et al., 2023), and actively debated the epistemic validity and representational fidelity of such substitutions (Dillion et al., 2023). Large-scale interactive simulacra of human communities (Park et al., 2023; 2024; Li et al., 2026) and extensive synthetic persona registries (Ge et al., 2024) provide rich demographic profiles for these experiments. Concurrently, simulated users have increasingly been adopted as automated, interactive evaluators for task-oriented agents in customer support and e-commerce domains (Yao et al., 2024; Barres et al., 2025; Zhang et al., 2024b). AgentPersonaBench 23 

The gap addressed by APB. Despite their breadth, both traditions leave a fundamental question unanswered. Role-play benchmarks predominantly evaluate the surface of a persona: whether generated text “sounds like” the assigned character, typically evaluated by LLM judges or surface perplexity. An agent composing eloquent Victorian prose while placing meat in a vegetarian’s grocery basket passes text-based role-play rubrics but fails the core requirements of an acting proxy. Conversely, human-substitution studies evaluate aggregate population fidelity, measuring whether marginal distribution statistics match demographic benchmarks. However, aggregate calibration can be achieved by an unfaithful model whose individual errors cancel out across cohorts. APB departs decisively from both paradigms: it evaluates individual decisions under known, deterministic ground truth. The target trait is never mentioned in the task prompt, requiring the agent to translate background identity into concrete, observable action. 

B.2 Instruction Following, Steerability, and System-Prompt Adherence Instruction tuning and alignment. Instruction-tuned language models are optimized to follow explicit commands and satisfy declarative constraints (Wei et al., 2022; Wang et al., 2022; 2023a; Ouyang et al., 2022). Reinforcement learning from human feedback (RLHF) and direct preference optimization provide normative steering signals (Christiano et al., 2017; Stiennon et al., 2020; Bai et al., 2022a; Rafailov et al., 2023; Askell et al., 2021), while constitutional frameworks embed explicit principles directly into self-refinement loops (Bai et al., 2022b; Kundu et al., 2023). 

Constraint satisfaction and steerability benchmarks. A wide range of benchmarks evaluate how reliably models adhere to explicit instructions. Verifiable constraint benchmarks assess whether models follow negative constraints, formatting specifications, and length limitations (Zhou et al., 2023; Jiang et al., 2024b; Qin et al., 2024b; Zeng et al., 2024), while others evaluate multi-turn or multilingual execution (He et al., 2024b; Kwan et al., 2024) or rank responses using pairwise LLM judges (Zheng et al., 2023; Chiang et al., 2024; Dubois et al., 2024). Parallel investigations probe whether models preserve system prompts under adversarial user jailbreaks (Qin et al., 2024a; Mu et al., 2025; Wallace et al., 2024), and steerability suites measure how effectively a model can be directed toward a specified persona, tone, or opinion (Dong et al., 2023; Chen et al., 2025). 

Explicit prompting vs. latent persona guidance. Instruction following differs fundamentally from latent persona adherence. Instruction and steerability benchmarks explicitly prompt the expected behavior (e.g., “Respond concisely in pirate speech” or “Adopt the viewpoint of an environmentalist”). Under direct prompting, models readily display sycophancy, mirroring stated constraints (Sharma et al., 2023; Perez et al., 2022), while their expressed principles routinely diverge from concrete actions (Gu et al., 2025). In real-world applications, a user profile is rarely an operational directive. Instead, an agent must independently recognize when an underlying trait (such as budget consciousness, dietary restrictions, or risk aversion) governs a routine action that makes no explicit mention of that trait. APB isolates and benchmarks this implicit behavioral steering. 

B.3 Agentic Environments, Computer Use, and Competence Benchmarks Interactive environments and tool use. Recent agent benchmarks increasingly evaluate models operating within live, interactive environments: executing browser interactions (Yao et al., 2022; Zhou et al., 2024a; Koh et al., 2024; Drouin et al., 2024; De Chezelles et al., 2024; Deng et al., 2023; He et al., 2024a), interacting with operating systems and native desktop interfaces (Xie et al., 2024; Bonatti et al., 2024; Rawles et al., 2024; Trivedi et al., 2024), utilizing tools and external APIs (Qin et al., 2024c; Li et al., 2023; Patil et al., 2025), and solving multi-modal assistant challenges (Mialon et al., 2023; Liu et al., 2024). Specialized suites further benchmark complex real-world workflows, including software engineering (Jimenez et al., 2024; Miserendino et al., 2025; Merrill et al., 2026), machine-learning engineering (Chan et al., 2024), enterprise CRM tasks (Huang et al., 2025), embodied simulation (Shridhar et al., 2021), and adversarial safety boundaries (Andriushchenko et al., 2024; Zhang et al., 2024a). 

Competence vs. adherence: inverting the evaluation paradigm. These benchmarks fundamentally evaluate functional competence : can the model successfully navigate an operating system to configure a server, interact with an e-commerce interface to buy a product, or patch a software bug? In contrast, APB employs similar interactive substrates (web browser environments and desktop GUI applications operated via visual actions), but inverts the evaluation goal. In APB, tasks are engineered to be straightforward to execute from a functional standpoint, ensuring that tool execution is a control variable rather than the principal barrier. The core challenge is whether the model makes choices that conform to its assigned latent persona when multiple functionally valid paths exist. 

Methodological rigor and artifact verification. Recent critical analyses have shown that agent evaluations frequently suffer from environment sensitivity, benchmark contamination, and trivial shortcut paths that inflate perceived capability (Kapoor et al., 2024; Zhu et al., 2025; Kapoor et al., 2025; Raji et al., 2021). APB safeguards against these failure modes through a rigorous two-stage verification framework (§3.3): automated contract audits verify that tasks cannot be solved blindly without persona context, while expert human auditing ensures scenario naturalness and unambiguous ground truth. By recording fine-grained execution traces and isolating interface execution failures from genuine persona violations (§4), APB establishes a reproducible, discriminative standard for measuring persona fidelity in interactive agents. D Reproducible Analysis Protocol 

The evaluation and diagnostic protocols in Section 4 operate on the official APB benchmark release and recorded model execution transcripts. All analyses evaluate recorded runtime artifacts directly, issuing no additional model generation calls and preserving original execution trajectories without altering model outputs. The main leaderboard and category breakdown tables reflect full release evaluations; detailed diagnostic analyses employ strictly matched cohorts to isolate interaction modalities and model architectures. 

Task specification and scoring metrics. Each task is identified by its canonical path, single- or multi-attribute classification, and interaction surface. For a task declaring 𝐾 𝑡 behavioral checks, we compute both full pass, 1[𝑠 𝑡 = 𝐾 𝑡 ]

(requiring all latent behavioral criteria to hold simultaneously), and normalized credit, 𝑠 𝑡 /𝐾 𝑡 , across scored trials. Outcomes are determined strictly by verified verifier findings (rule-based assertions or rubric-guided evaluations), without inferring post-hoc credit. 

Matched cohort construction and uncertainty estimation. To assess cross-surface transfer without confounding from persona sampling or task difficulty, single-attribute scenarios are matched across surfaces by shared scenario template, persona profile hash, and pinned trait specification. The primary four-surface matched cohort contains 140 scenarios across 44 distinct personas evaluated across all four surfaces by the six OpenAI arms. Pointwise 95% confidence intervals are estimated via 10,000 bootstrap resamples clustered by persona hash, capturing behavioral variation across the sampled persona distribution. For multi-attribute analyses, the matched cohort contains 1,172 tasks evaluated across models under identical trait co-exposure conditions. 

Execution error accounting. Reported benchmark adherence rates are conditional on valid completed executions. Infrastructure-level execution errors (such as transient environment timeouts or interface interaction anomalies) are tracked separately. To assess the sensitivity of leaderboard rankings to execution reliability, we conduct an error-inclusive audit where all non-completed attempts are assigned zero credit (§D.2), confirming that relative model rankings and performance separations remain stable. 

D.1 Evaluation Coverage and Standardized Environments 

We evaluate 20 foundation model arms across eight model families (Table 1) across the 2,460 benchmark tasks (502 survey, 565 chat, 707 web, and 686 app). Each model is packaged as a standardized, versioned arm recording its checkpoint, decoding parameters (medium reasoning budget where supported), and interaction wrapper. All reported scores are evaluated against frozen release benchmark environments, ensuring byte-level reproducibility across trials. Due to input modality constraints, models lacking native vision or desktop GUI capabilities (e.g., text-only architectures including the DeepSeek series and glm-5-3 ) participate on the text surfaces (survey, chat, and web), with desktop interaction (app) designated as N/A; more broadly, N/A marks any cell lacking a validated execution run on that interface. For tasks requiring free-text open-ended dialogue evaluation, verifications use a standardized, frozen judge ( gpt-5.6-luna ) with rubric-grounded scoring prompts to guarantee comparability across vendors. 

D.2 Robustness to Execution and Verifier Choice Conditional adherence and end-to-end success. Transient infrastructure errors (such as environment timeouts or interface communication anomalies) are distinguished from behavioral persona violations. On the evaluation set, gpt-5-6-sol ’s chat full-pass rate is 68.2% over 487 scored tasks, and 59.2% when all 561 recorded chat attempts enter the denominator with non-completed trials conservatively scored as zero. For grok-4-6 on app, the corresponding rate shifts from 69.9% over 662 scored attempts to 67.5% over 686 recorded attempts. Across all arms, assigning zero credit to incomplete attempts leaves relative model rankings and tier separations fully intact (§D.2). 

Model separation persists on deterministic checks. The benchmark declares 3,865 checks: 3,033 rule-based (78.5%) and 832 judged. To verify that observed model separations do not rely on LLM judge behavior, we partition tasks by evaluator type and compare models only on shared scored tasks within each subset. For the GPT-6 pair, the full-pass gap is 17.7 points over 1,905 rule-only tasks and 11.4 points over 546 judge-only tasks. For the GPT-5.6 pair, it is 13.0 points over 1,891 rule-only tasks and 19.4 points over 474 judge-only tasks. The direction and magnitude of separation survive the complete removal of LLM judging, confirming that benchmark rankings reflect genuine model steerability rather than evaluator artifacts. 

Failure-overlap cohort and case selection. The eighteen-arm chat/web intersection includes all model arms evaluated under the shared gpt-5.6-luna judge across both chat and web surfaces (1,086 common tasks; Figure 9), fixing the persona profile, pinned traits, and rubric evaluator across models. Directed complementary-success counts are computed from binary full-pass outcomes without post-hoc independence assumptions. The qualitative case in Section 5.1 was inspected post hoc against the complete action trace, environment state, and persona specification to illustrate failure mechanisms where interface competence succeeds but latent constraints are violated.

E Limitations 

We outline several methodological considerations and design trade-offs in APB, distinguishing foundational design choices from operational opportunities for future releases. 

Construct validity and human fidelity. APB prioritizes internal construct validity and causal attribution: tasks evaluate whether an agent acts consistently with declared latent traits under explicit, verified behavioral criteria, rather than predicting unconstrained human population distributions. By testing specific constraints without prompt leaks, the benchmark directly AgentPersonaBench 28 

isolates model steerability from the confounding variance of human open-ended responses. While predicting real-world human behavioral distributions is an important complementary objective (which benefits from empirical telemetry of human users performing matching tasks), deterministic verification serves as a necessary prerequisite. Before evaluating whether an autonomous agent can emulate the nuanced distributional variance of human cohorts, one must first verify that its downstream actions reliably adhere to assigned profile constraints in everyday tasks. 

Trait isolation, multi-attribute evaluation, and verifier precision. To maintain clear attribution, single-attribute tasks isolate individual traits within rich background profiles, treating other background attributes as distracting context. While real-world human behavior often involves subtle trait intersections, isolating specific constraints enables unambiguous error tracing. To assess combined demands, APB includes 1,172 multi-attribute tasks where multiple traits must hold simultaneously. While our primary metric requires full pass across all declared criteria to prevent partial-credit heuristics from masking constraint violations, we also report normalized credit across scored trials. Finally, for open-ended dialogue turns that rely on standardized LLM judges, we anchor evaluations to explicit rubrics and rely on deterministic rule-based predicates for the overwhelming majority of checks (78.5%), with sensitivity audits confirming that model separations remain robust on deterministic checks alone (§D.2). 

Evaluation scale and empirical rank stability. Benchmarking 20 frontier model arms across 2,460 multi-turn tasks spanning four interaction surfaces requires executing over 45,000 full interactive episodes. To balance breadth across diverse model families and realistic desktop environments, the primary leaderboard reports a complete evaluation pass across all 20 arms. To verify that single-pass evaluations provide stable model comparisons, our repeated-sweep audit (§4.6) evaluates independent three-pass evaluations on representative arms, showing minimal run-to-run variation ( 𝜎 ≤ 2.0 points). Furthermore, the 11 to 19 point separations between model tiers persist consistently across repeats, deterministic rule-based subsets, and error-inclusive analyses (§D.2), confirming that aggregate leaderboard rankings capture robust architectural differences rather than runtime stochasticity. 

F Future Directions and Validation Priorities 

Log-grounded human realism: friction, hesitation, and abandonment. In realistic task environments, human behavior involves hesitation, partial disclosure, mid-dialogue correction, and task abandonment alongside compliant execution. Current benchmarks measure trait adherence as a conditioning response, testing whether an assigned characteristic appears in an interaction trace rather than whether the agent reflects authentic human dynamics. A primary direction is evaluating simulated trajectories against matched distributions of real user telemetry. At the population level, simulated turns can be benchmarked against production telemetry on turn length, phrasing variability, volunteered versus withheld context, correction rates, and abandonment frequencies. Distinguishing simulated traces from genuine human interactions with a statistical discriminator offers an interpretable summary metric, where an area under the ROC curve (AUC) near 0 .5 indicates well-calibrated simulation realism. 

Individual-level predictive validation and causal trait intervention. At the individual level, validation requires evaluating whether persona models accurately predict how specific people act under novel tasks. This can be tested by extracting a persona profile from an initial conversation segment, prompting the model to simulate subsequent actions, and scoring simulated choices against historical actions under non-persona and shuffled-persona baselines. In parallel, experimental designs can progress beyond isolated single-trait testing toward counterbalanced factorial interventions (2 × 2 designs). Systematically crossing orthogonal attributes (such as budget constraints with dietary preferences) within identical scenarios can isolate non-linear attribute interactions and quantify continuous adherence levels. 

Long-horizon persona persistence and multi-agent collective dynamics. While APB tests models within bounded, single-session episodes, deployed persona agents often operate across extended horizons spanning days or weeks. Sustained deployment introduces risks of persona drift, forgetting of assigned constraints, and context collapse under accumulated dialogue history. A natural next step involves evaluating long-term memory architectures and parameter-efficient state tracking for continuous persona preservation. Finally, scaling persona evaluation to closed-loop multi-agent sandboxes, where heterogeneous persona agents interact in simulated marketplaces, organizational workflows, or negotiation games, will enable studying whether individual persona conditioning stably aggregates into plausible macro-level collective dynamics. G Responsible Use and Artifact Release 

Simulated personas in APB are diagnostic instruments for evaluating model steerability and user simulation; they do not constitute statistical probability samples of real human populations or replace direct engagement with human stakeholders. Prohibited applications include impersonating named living individuals, generating surveillance or political targeting dossiers, scripting deceptive automated persuasion, or enforcing exclusionary discrimination against protected groups. To ensure reproducible and safe academic auditing, the 2,460 benchmark task definitions and persona attribute schemas are released under Creative Commons Attribution 4.0 International (CC-BY 4.0), while evaluation wrappers and execution harnesses are distributed under the Apache 2.0 license with cryptographic SHA-256 manifests. Practitioners applying APB to evaluate deployed applications should explicitly record the persona backbone model and cross-validate against heterogeneous model families to prevent shared-backbone evaluation bias. 

H Ethical Considerations 

Synthetic personas, impersonation risks, and safety governance. APB simulates latent behavioral profiles to evaluate model steerability and consistency; it makes no normative claim that any real individual would or should behave as a declared persona indicates. A central ethical concern surrounding persona-conditioned models is the risk of deceptive anthropomorphism or malicious impersonation, wherein autonomous agents misrepresent their identity to manipulate users or mimic living individuals. We emphasize that APB does not endorse ungrounded impersonation in public deployments, where autonomous systems should consistently maintain clear, unambiguous disclosure of their artificial nature. Instead, empirical measurement is essential for assessing agentic systems: regulators, developers, and auditors cannot evaluate the behavioral boundaries of deployed agents without reproducible protocols that determine whether conditioning prompts govern real-world actions. 

Stereotype mitigation, data privacy, and execution containment. Evaluating identity-linked attributes risks entrenching societal stereotypes if traits are naively bundled into caricatured personas. To mitigate this risk, APB decouples attributes through modular schema sampling, testing declared traits independently rather than relying on demographic archetypes. In addition, task scenarios focus on benign lifestyle, consumer, and professional settings, avoiding sensitive or high-stakes domains. All evaluated persona profiles are entirely synthetic, containing no personally identifiable information (PII) derived from real individuals. Finally, all interactive environments across web and native application surfaces are executed within isolated, reproducible sandboxes, ensuring that automated browser and desktop GUI operations produce no external network side effects, financial transactions, or interactions with external infrastructure.
