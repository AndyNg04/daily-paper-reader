# Fault-Tolerant Budget Conservation in Distributed Multi-Agent Delegation 

GENLIANG ZHU, Accentrust, Canada and Georgia Institute of Technology, USA 

CHU WANG, Accentrust, Canada and University of Illinois Urbana-Champaign, USA Resource limits are becoming an authorization boundary for AI agents that delegate work across concur-rent and failure-prone workers. Parent-child allocation constraints, affine objects, and distributed escrow do not by themselves prevent overspend when replies are lost, effects complete after timeout, messages re-peat, branches partition, or DAG joins alias one lineage. We formalize fault-tolerant budget conservation for distributed multi-agent delegation. Budgets are quantized resource vectors represented by exclusive es-crow credits that move through a delegation DAG. Before dispatch, a branch converts credit into an oper-ation reservation bound to lineage, epoch, normalized effect, maximum charge, receiver, and idempotency key. It persists a signed dispatch permit with quarantine; the gateway verifies that permit before first ac-ceptance. Uncertain effects remain charged until authenticated settlement, a fenced authoritative no-effect proof, or permanent retirement. We prove ownership partition, ledger and effect conservation, descendant non-amplification, at-most-once settlement, late-completion safety, and partition confinement under explicit mediation, durability, authentication, normalization, and gateway assumptions. An indistinguishability result shows that partition-local availability requires exclusive preallocation. Bounded TLA+ checking, an indepen-dent JavaScript explorer, and crash-injected two-process SQLite experiments exercise the declared scope and detect timeout-refund and historical-certificate-validation mutants. The mechanism preserves the issued bud-get bound across the evaluated crash, retry, duplicate, partition, join, and late-completion schedules. 1 Introduction 

AI agents increasingly act as spenders, callers, schedulers, and delegators. A user may authorize a procurement agent to spend at most $500, an incident agent to issue at most 20 production writes, or a research coordinator to make at most 100 paid API calls. The coordinator can then create workers, workers can create specialists, and multiple branches can converge at a final reviewer. A scalar “remaining budget” is no longer a local counter. It is a consumable authorization resource moving through a distributed, asynchronous delegation graph. Here fault-tolerant means that the safety bound is preserved across declared crash and com-munication faults. It does not mean every isolated branch remains available or every uncertain operation eventually resolves. Recent work makes important parts of this problem explicit. Agent Contracts defines multi-dimensional resource constraints and a conservation law requiring child allocations to remain within the parent contract [48]. Token Budgets documents 63 production budget overruns and uses affine ownership to reject in-process aliasing, double-spending, and use-after-delegation [22]. AIP binds identity, attenuated authorization, provenance, and completion records across the Model Context Protocol (MCP), Agent2Agent (A2A), and HTTP [36]. The settlement gap. Allocation safety is not execution safety. Consider a root grant of two paid effects. The coordinator allocates one unit to each of workers 𝐴 and 𝐵 , so the allocation law is satisfied. Worker 𝐵 first spends its own unit. Worker 𝐴 then reserves its unit and sends a payment request. The response is lost. Its controller times out, declares the worker dead, and refunds the unit to the parent. Worker 𝐵 spends that refunded unit, after which the original 𝐴 payment completes. Every allocation calculation was locally plausible; three effects have nevertheless consumed a two-effect authorization. Figure 1 separates the unsafe trace from the required response. The missing object in this allocation-only trace is an explicit effect obligation : once an external effect may have crossed the durability boundary, its budget remains reserved through uncertainty. Recovery may query, settle, or permanently retire the obligation; elapsed time alone cannot make the right spendable again. 

Why a delegation DAG is harder than a tree. Agent delegation is not necessarily hierarchical. Two branches may ask a shared specialist to synthesize results; a reviewer may join multiple principal-approved inputs; retries may reconstruct a worker under a new process identity. A child with two incoming references does not thereby own the sum of both ancestors. If those references alias one credit generation, adding them creates authority. The budget-ownership relation must therefore preserve disjoint lineage even when the task graph has joins.

Approach. We model each root grant as a vector of quantized resources. The issuer creates ex-clusive escrow credits. Delegation moves a subvector into a fresh child generation and atomically removes it from the parent generation. A branch can spend locally during a partition only from its exclusive escrow. Before dispatch, the branch durably creates a reservation bound to the root, credit lineage, current epoch, normalized effect, maximum charge, receiver, and stable operation key. Dispatch changes the obligation state but does not refund. Authenticated, effect-bound suc-cess receipts are applied at most once; verified no-effect certificates return the reservation; irrecov-erable uncertainty can be permanently forfeited but not silently reused. 

Contributions. This paper makes five contributions: (1) A fault-aware authorization semantics. We define resource vectors, exclusive credits, a del-egation DAG, operation obligations, authenticated terminal evidence, and a transition system that separates allocation, execution, settlement, and effect coverage. (2) A fault-tolerant protocol. We give algorithms for idempotent split/delegation, local reserva-tion, dispatch, terminal-evidence settlement, multi-parent join, recovery, epoch fencing, and safe reclaim, together with refinement obligations for MCP/A2A/HTTP adapters. (3) Conditional safety results. We prove ownership partition, global conservation, descendant non-amplification, at-most-once settlement, effect-instance coverage, an external exposure bound, late-completion safety, and partition confinement. We state an explicit trace-lifting criterion that a concrete runtime must discharge before inheriting those abstract results. (4) A coordination boundary. An indistinguishability argument shows why a shared, unallo-cated unit cannot remain spendable in every partition without risking amplification. Exclusive preallocation buys local availability; reallocation and reclaim may require coordination. (5) An evidence-disciplined reference profile. We specify a provider-free artifact that connects finite model checking, transition-level invariant checks, mutation, fault injection, and descrip-tive local timing to explicit claims. Hosted-model quality and production deployment remain outside the evidence. 

Non-goals. We do not invent escrow, bounded counters, leases, Sagas, idempotency, transac-tional tool use, commit-time authorization, or delegation tokens. We do not guarantee exactly-once network delivery, roll back irreversible effects, infer prices, optimize allocation, tolerate Byzantine infrastructure, or remain globally available through arbitrary partitions. 

2 Background and Closest Work 2.1 Resource-Bounded Agent Execution 

Agent Contracts unifies input/output specifications, skill sets, multi-dimensional resources, tem-poral bounds, success criteria, and termination in one lifecycle [48]. For delegation it requires 

∑𝑖 𝑅 𝑖 ≤ 𝑅 parent , supports recursive subcontracts and returned capacity, and reports zero conser-vation violations in a 50-topic three-agent pipeline. Its hard monitor checks between actions, and the paper explicitly notes that a single call can exceed a budget before usage is known. Our work treats Agent Contracts as the closest allocation-level neighbor. Our question is what happens after allocation when messages duplicate, branches partition, a process crashes, or an external effect is uncertain. Token Budgets establishes that an ordinary copyable number is the wrong abstraction [22]. Its affine Rust object prevents a cost-bearing value from being cloned, double-spent, or used af-ter delegation within the type-system envelope. The paper also quantifies static over-reservation, 4 Zhu and Wang 

sketches distributed reservation using mechanisms such as Sagas, consensus, and leases, and iden-tifies distributed partial failure as a substantial open extension. Our protocol adopts the same move-not-copy intuition but makes that extension explicit across processes, languages, protocols, crash recovery, and DAG joins. CapLease is the closest durable-authorization neighbor: it records consumable authorization state across retries, concurrent use, delegation, and crash recovery, with an idempotent receiver contract [47]. It therefore establishes durable agent authorization as direct prior art. The distinct contribution here is partition-aware transfer of exclusive multi-dimensional credit through a dele-gation DAG, including atomic multi-parent joins, together with one conservation argument span-ning both credit lineage and accepted or uncertain external effects. Among the systems compared here, EpochX is the closest credits-native infrastructure: it locks task bounties, delegates budgets recursively, and settles rewards on acceptance [46]. Its focus is a production marketplace and reusable asset economy. Our focus is the prefix invariant and effect-coverage semantics when transfer, settlement, retry, and recovery traces are duplicated, reordered, lost, partitioned, or crash-interrupted. 

2.2 Delegation Identity and Scope Attenuation 

AIP supplies verifiable identity, attenuated authorization, provenance, and completion records across MCP, A2A, and HTTP [36]. It carries a budget_usd ceiling that attenuates along a token chain, but treats that value as a per-token authorization ceiling rather than a running balance; the runtime remains responsible for aggregate spend enforcement. AATs let a holder derive a signed chain with equal-or-narrower tools, arguments, depth, and lifetime without contacting the root is-suer, while leaving transport binding deployment-specific [32]. Overlaying Governance formalizes recursive delegation and scope composition over existing policy [18]. These mechanisms prevent 

scope amplification . A consumable right adds a different obligation: two children can each hold the same narrow scope while only one may consume the remaining unit. Offline attenuation alone cannot decide exclusive ownership. PAuth derives service-side slices for task-implied concrete operations and binds operand values to provenance-aware authorization envelopes [44]. It strengthens operation-specific authorization, but does not define exclusive ownership of consumable budgets, crash/partition credit transfer, or settlement of an uncertain external effect. Stateful least privilege makes authorization decisions depend on execution history and repre-sents policy state as replicated state machines [9]. This establishes stateful cloud authorization as a deployed security direction. Our state is narrower but structurally different: it tracks exclusive consumable-credit generations and their effect obligations across an agent delegation DAG. MCP standardizes model-to-tool connectivity and profiles OAuth authorization; A2A standard-izes agent discovery and task exchange [1, 30]. MPAC adds multi-principal sessions, intent, opera-tions, conflicts, Lamport-clock watermarks, optimistic concurrency, and governance [37]. We use protocol adapters and causal metadata but do not claim a general coordination protocol. The active individual Internet-Draft on Transaction Tokens for Agents propagates subject, actor, and agentic context through multi-agent call chains [38]; it specifies neither a running consumable balance nor exclusive escrow ownership or effect coverage. 

2.3 Transactional Effects and Commit-Time Authority 

Atomix gives agent tool calls progress-aware transactional semantics. It uses epochs, per-resource frontiers, delayed commit for bufferable effects, and compensation for externalized effects [31]. Commit-time authorization separately requires fresh, causally prior, effect-bound, eligible author-ity at the durability boundary [41]. Context-to-Execution Integrity binds protected fields, exact Fault-Tolerant Budget Conservation in Agent Delegation 5

effects, and invocation authority [40]. These works establish that endpoint success is not enough and that external effects need a boundary protocol. We add conserved consumable rights across distributed delegation and keep uncertain effects charged across recovery. Compensation is useful but weaker than prevention for irreversible payments, messages, disclo-sures, or physical actions. Sagas explicitly accept application-specific compensation rather than atomic rollback [12]. Likewise, “exactly once” can conflate delivery, state consistency, and deter-ministic replay [45]. Our claims are at-most-once settlement and continuous coverage at a mediated gateway, not exactly-once delivery. Two recent results sharpen this boundary. Machine-checked dual-write recovery shows that source-side state alone cannot safely distinguish a missing sink write from a completed one and formalizes recovery around sink acceptance records and fencing [3]. A cross-runtime study of agent tool calls similarly finds that exactly-once behavior depends on the model, harness, and tool contract, and that verification alone does not close late-commit races [28]. Our protocol adopts that receiver-visible boundary and adds the invariant that every accepted or still uncertain effect remains covered by exclusive credit. 

2.4 Escrow, Numeric Invariants, and Coordination 

The Escrow Transactional Method permits concurrent changes while reserving enough rights to preserve numeric bounds [33]. Bounded Counter conflict-free replicated data types (CRDTs) build on convergent replicated-data-type semantics and distribute decrement rights so disconnected replicas can act without violating a lower bound [5, 43]. Just-Right Consistency combines CRDTs, transactional causal consistency, bounded counters, and static analysis to synchronize only where application correctness requires it [42]. These are foundational prior art, not our inventions. Invariant confluence characterizes when valid divergent states can merge without coordina-tion [4]. The recent Complete CALM criterion states a broader history-based boundary between monotone specifications and those requiring coordination [16]. A consumable shared unit is non-monotone: spending in one partition removes an option from another. Our protocol applies the classical answer–exclusive preallocation–and then adds agent-specific delegation lineage, author-ity bindings, normalized effects, and settlement obligations. 

2.5 Leases, Fencing, and Durable Workflow 

Leases provide time-bounded authority under clock and failure assumptions [14]; lock services use monotonically ordered ownership and durable consensus to coordinate clients [8, 34]. Expiry does not undo an already dispatched effect. We use an epoch as a fencing generation, never as evidence that the previous effect did not happen. Recent capability-coherence work maps authorization revocation to memory consistency and reports an execution-count bound on post-revocation operations [35]. That revocation bound is complementary: our epoch fence controls acceptance across ownership generations, while reclaim also requires exclusive credit ownership and terminal effect evidence. Durable distributed applications commonly decompose work into idempotent steps, stable re-quest identifiers, and reconciliation rather than assuming a cross-organization transaction [15]. Stable call identifiers and duplicate suppression trace back to classic RPC semantics [7]; durable systems additionally need the completion record and protected mutation to share the relevant atomicity boundary. Linearizability remains the abstraction for an authoritative local transition [17]; Lamport order supplies causal structure without pretending synchronized clocks determine truth [24]. 3 System, Failure, and Threat Model 3.1 Entities and Trust Boundaries 

The system contains a root issuer, delegation controllers, agent runtimes, durable branch stores, ef-fect gateways, a terminal-evidence authority, a reconciler, and an auditor. The issuer authenticates a task grant and creates the initial budget vector. Controllers split and move rights. Agent models propose work but cannot mint, settle, or refund credit. Each branch store atomically records local ownership, reservations, outbox state, and terminal evidence. The effect gateway is the complete-mediation point for a protected effect. It enforces the canonical operation key and epoch, and returns or exposes an authoritative outcome. In the reference profile, the issuer, controller, branch store, gateway, terminal-evidence author-ity, and reconciler form the declared enforcement trusted computing base (TCB); the controller and reconciler are trusted orchestration but still cannot bypass store/gateway validation. 3.2 Budget and Effect Surfaces 

The protected budget is a finite vector over dimensions 𝒦 : currency quanta, paid API calls, risk units, recipient-specific disclosures, or effect counts. Each dimension has an authenticated unit, quantization, and maximum-charge rule. Cross-dimension exchange is disallowed unless a sepa-rately authorized conversion contract is present; otherwise a dollar cannot silently substitute for a risk unit. Protected effects include any mediated external state change whose authority is consumable: a payment, message, credential issuance, infrastructure write, record mutation, disclosure, or paid call. Pure reads may still consume an API or disclosure budget. The normalizer maps protocol-specific requests to a canonical effect descriptor. Effects outside the declared gateway envelope, including covert channels and direct credentials, are outside the theorem. 

3.3 Failure Model 

The network is asynchronous. Messages can be lost, duplicated, delayed, reordered, or delivered after an arbitrary partition. Processes can crash and restart, losing volatile state while preserving completed durable commits. We do not use wall-clock order to infer whether an effect happened. Clocks are used only for policy expiry under a stated deployment profile; epochs and authoritative terminal evidence determine settlement. The model includes concurrent fan-out and fan-in, duplicate delegation and dispatch, retries, crash before or after each durable write, response loss, timeout followed by late completion, branch 8 Zhu and Wang 

partition and healing, stale epoch messages, repeated terminal evidence, and recovery scans. It excludes Byzantine consensus and arbitrary corruption inside a trusted durable store. 

3.4 Adversary 

The planner, model, tool description, retrieved content, and downstream agent may be malicious or confused. They can request overlapping child budgets, replay messages, alias a reservation across effects, relabel effects, trigger retry storms, construct joins with shared ancestry, withhold com-pletion, and present stale epochs. They cannot forge issuer or terminal-evidence signatures, break collision-resistant digests, bypass the protected gateway, or directly mutate the trusted store under the main theorem.

4 Why Simpler Mechanisms Fail 4.1 Allocation Conservation Is Not Prefix Conservation 

The static condition 

∑

> 𝑖∈ children (𝑝)

𝑏 𝑖 ≤ 𝑏 𝑝 

checks one allocation snapshot. It says nothing about whether an old child still owns 𝑏 𝑖 after a refund, whether the same request was applied twice, or whether an external effect already con-sumed the allocation. The property we require holds for every execution prefix and accounts for in-flight and uncertain obligations. 

4.2 Read–Check–Decrement Replicas Double Spend 

If two partitioned branches read a global remaining value 1, both can locally verify 1 ≥ 1 and decrement. Last-writer-wins convergence can produce the valid-looking final value 0 after two effects. CRDT convergence is not invariant preservation; unrestricted decrement is not invariant-confluent [4]. Exclusive rights make each local decrement monotone with respect to ownership. 

4.3 Static Splitting Is Safe but Can Strand Utility 

Giving each of 𝑛 possible workers 𝐵/𝑛 before execution prevents double-spend, but idle branches strand capacity and dynamic fan-out requires worst-case over-reservation. Token Budgets mea-sures this cost in its setting [22]. Our protocol retains the safety of static escrow while allowing coordinated, idempotent transfer and return. It does not pretend that reallocation remains avail-able through every partition. 

4.4 Timeout Refund Creates a Late-Completion Race 

Let an operation be dispatched before time 𝑡 , let no response arrive by 𝑡 + Δ , and let the controller refund. There are two executions indistinguishable to the controller at 𝑡 + Δ : one in which the gateway never executed and one in which it executed but the response is delayed. A refund is safe in the first and unsafe in the second. No timeout value resolves the information gap. The system needs a fenced, authoritative no-effect proof or must retain/retire the reservation. 

4.5 Idempotency Keys Do Not Allocate Authority 

A stable request key can stop the same receiver from applying one effect twice. It does not stop two different operation keys from spending the same credit, a relabeled path from creating the same normalized effect, or two receivers from interpreting keys in different namespaces. The key must be bound to one credit generation, effect digest, receiver, epoch, and maximum charge, and the gateway must enforce that binding. 

4.6 Scope Attenuation Does Not Consume 

A scope token answers which operations a child may invoke. It can be copied to two children with-out widening the scope set, yet both children may spend the last dollar. Scope non-amplification and resource conservation are orthogonal. We compose with AIP/AAT-style identity and attenua-tion rather than replacing them. 4.7 Compensation Is Not Conservation 

A Saga can schedule a refund after a duplicated purchase, but the duplicate effect occurred and compensation can fail, cost money, or be semantically incomplete. Compensation is a valid re-covery policy for designated effects; it is not evidence that the authorization budget was never exceeded. We record compensation as a further effect with its own authority and accounting. 

4.8 Independent Correct Components Can Compose Incorrectly 

An allocation service, idempotent gateway, lease manager, and receipt verifier can each be locally correct while disagreeing on operation identity or epoch. For example, a gateway may deduplicate on an HTTP key while the ledger retries under a new operation ID; or a lease manager may fence the branch while the receiver accepts the old generation. The protocol therefore requires one joint canonical binding and a concrete-to-abstract refinement contract.

The external bound is not obtained by merely placing familiar components next to one another. It uses four cross-component conditions on the same canonical operation: 

𝒥 = ExclusiveOwnership ∧ InstanceInjectivity ∧ CompleteCharge ∧ FenceOrdering .

Each conjunct has a one-unit counterexample if omitted while the others are retained: (1) without exclusive ownership, two branches reserve the same atom under distinct valid keys and each produces one correctly receipted effect; (2) without instance injectivity, one valid reservation and key produces two distinct receiver in-stances while only one operation remains in the ledger; (3) without complete charge, the gateway enforces a cap of one but a receipt reports zero for a one-unit effect, so internal settlement is conserved while actual cost is not; and (4) without fence ordering, an old request can be accepted after its atom is reissued, then both the old and successor operations complete. Consequently, bounded counters alone do not imply effect conservation, and durable comple-tion records alone do not imply delegated-right conservation. 6 Fault-Tolerant Delegation and Settlement Protocol 6.1 Design Principles 

The protocol follows six rules: (1) Move quantities and attenuate labels. A child receives both an exclusive footprint and a narrower-or-equal authorization label. (2) Persist before exposure. Reservation, obligation, outbox, and audit state commit before a protected dispatch can escape. (3) Bind the real effect. The reservation uses a normalized effect digest and current dependen-cy/authority witness, not a tool name alone. (4) Treat ambiguity as charged. Response loss produces indeterminate , never automatic re-fund. (5) Fence before reuse. Reclaim from an exposed obligation requires a gateway-visible fence and 

authoritative evidence that the exact binding was never accepted and did not occur; a fence alone never releases quarantined rights. (6) Make replay a stutter. Stable transfer, operation, and terminal-evidence IDs return the prior decision without repeating state movement.

6.9 Fan-In and Return 

A task join has three safe choices: (1) retain separate branch credits and select one for each new effect; (2) atomically join disjoint, operation-free footprints under one authoritative store; or (3) transfer each branch’s remainder through sealed handoffs to a join store, then join after ac-knowledgment. It may not add reported numeric balances from branches. Each return uses a stable transfer ID and consumes the child footprint. Duplicate completion or return messages stutter. A canceled branch with an indeterminate operation can return only its unaffected available footprint; the obligation remains charged to the root.

7.9 What the Theorems Permit 

The results intentionally permit conservative outcomes. A right may remain in T𝑔 because a trans-fer cannot be canceled safely, in Q𝑔 because the receiver outcome is unknown, or in F𝑔 because an administrator retires unresolved authority. Such states reduce utilization but preserve the exact equation and keep potential effects covered. The theorems also permit coordination during rebal-ancing, fan-in, and reclaim. Proposition 1 shows why removing that coordination while retaining universal partition availability would change, rather than strengthen, the safety contract. Conversely, a perfectly conserved internal ledger does not prove that a provider reported the true cost, that two protocol encodings normalize to the same effect, or that an agent lacked a by-pass credential. Those are precisely the boundaries isolated by Assumptions 4, 7, and 9. The artifact therefore tests state-machine invariants and terminal-evidence contracts as separate evidence lay-ers. Adapter conformance remains a handler-level trace-lifting obligation; passing either tested layer is not reported as evidence for that obligation. 8.5 Concurrency and Placement 

Credits are the unit of locality. Operations on disjoint credits need no shared counter and can proceed concurrently. A branch serializes transitions for one credit generation, for example with a row-version compare-and-swap or a local transaction lock. Cross-branch transfers serialize only the source seal and destination acceptance; the rights remain unavailable in T between them. A hot root does not imply a hot root row during ordinary spending. Root totals can be recon-structed from credit and operation records or maintained as non-authoritative aggregates. Coordi-nation is required to move unused rights between partitions, join credits at one authority, change resource semantics, or reclaim a lost generation. This is the intended “just-right” boundary: spend-ing preallocated rights is local; changing exclusive ownership is not.

9 Evaluation Methodology 

The evaluation is claim-driven and intentionally performed after the model, protocol, theorem assumptions, and failure semantics are fixed. This order reduces the risk of fitting definitions to favorable measurements. The artifact uses no hosted model or provider. Agent decisions are deter-ministic event generators because the evaluated claims concern distributed ownership and settle-ment, not task quality. We use frozen cases, explicit oracles, and machine-readable denial reasons. The present oracle is a distributed state invariant, and each paired variation is a crash, message, permit, or epoch schedule rather than a natural language authorization context. MAS-FIRE provides a complementary taxonomy and injection framework for semantic, cogni-tive, and inter-agent coordination faults in LLM-based multi-agent systems [19]. Our evaluation targets a different layer: durable crash cuts, duplicated or reordered protocol events, epoch fenc-ing, and preservation of ownership and effect-accounting invariants. It does not measure reasoning robustness.

11 Security Analysis 11.1 Safety Against a Malicious or Confused Agent 

The agent is not trusted to report a balance, choose a credit generation, forge terminal evidence, or decide that a timeout is a refund. It may emit arbitrarily many proposals, retries, delegations, cancellations, and joins. The branch store accepts a proposal only if the current credit exclusively owns the selected footprint, the label and joint witness authorize the normalized effect, and the immutable operation binding either does not yet exist or matches exactly. Consequently, prompt injection can influence the proposed action but cannot by itself duplicate a right. A compromised worker can still waste its own authorized escrow by reserving unproductive work, creating indeterminate obligations, or intentionally withholding completion. Conservation is a safety property, not a defense against budget denial. Deployments need rate limits, per-child reservation caps, bounded delegation depth, admission control, and human escalation for large or long-lived quarantines. 

11.2 Replay, Equivocation, and Identifier Substitution 

Every transfer, operation, and terminal-evidence item has a stable identifier, but identifier unique-ness alone is insufficient. The branch/gateway enforcement verifier , which is inside the safety TCB, recomputes the single canonical operation binding in Equation (6), including the selector schema version 𝜈 sel , and rejects any field mismatch. Transfers use their separate domain-separated certifi-cate and decision binding; they are not silently interpreted through the operation formula. The independent audit verifier in Figure 2 is outside the TCB and can detect divergence but cannot make an unsafe request safe. An exact replay is a stutter. Reuse of an identifier with a different tuple is an equivocation and is rejected. Domain separation prevents an operation ID from being reinterpreted as a transfer or receipt ID. Receiver and effect class are included so two services cannot accidentally share an idempotency namespace. Settlement also binds where unused rights may return. A target must satisfy Equation (7): it has the same root, is a successor of the source lineage that is current in the committed successor state under the settlement authority, and has a label no broader than the source credit. An agent therefore cannot use a valid partial receipt to redirect the remainder into an unrelated, broader, or stale credit. Terminal-evidence equivocation is detected when two authenticated terminal outcomes bind the same operation. Under Assumption 7, at most one can be valid. If a deployed terminal-evidence authority can sign inconsistent outcomes, the main effect theorem no longer applies; the ledger can quarantine the conflict, preserve no-amplification, and expose evidence, but cannot establish receiver truth. 

11.3 Stale Generations and Split Brain 

Epoch fencing protects against a lost controller or worker that later resumes. The critical check is at the last trusted boundary before the effect, not only at the branch database. A gateway accepts a new request for (𝑐𝑖𝑑, 𝜂) only while that generation is current. After an authenticated 𝜂 + > 𝜂 is installed, it may return the already recorded result for an old idempotency key but must reject a new old-generation effect. 50 Zhu and Wang 

If two authorities can independently declare themselves current for the same credit generation, exclusive ownership is already violated. Consensus, single-writer placement, or another correct ownership service is required within that authority. The protocol confines this requirement to ownership changes; it does not solve consensus and does not claim Byzantine split-brain tolerance. 

11.4 External-Effect Ambiguity 

The most consequential attack surface is a receiver that completes work and drops or falsifies the response. The safe response is not to guess. Under the fenced-queryable profile, reconciliation obtains the recorded result. Under the receipt-only profile, missing evidence leaves the right in Q

or eventually F. Under self-report, only the internal ledger claim survives. This tiering follows the same fundamental requirement as systems that provide exactly-once RPC or workflow execution: a completion record must be durably coupled to the mutation or returned by a cooperating transactional endpoint [27, 49, 54]. A client-side retry library cannot manufacture that coupling for an arbitrary third party. 

11.5 Underreporting and Variable Charges 

A maximum reservation u is sound only if the gateway can prevent an actual charge above it. For a paid API, that can mean preauthorization, provider-side hard caps, chunked metering with renewal, or reserving the maximum billable unit. If the provider reveals usage only afterward and may exceed u, no local algorithm can prove a hard bound on the real charge. The appropriate choices are to refuse the hard-bound profile, reserve a conservative worst case, or state a softer monitoring claim. Partial settlement is safe when the receipt authenticates d ⪯ u. The remainder returns only after that receipt. For streaming calls, the gateway can use successive sub-operations with separate reserved chunks; canceling a stream is not itself a zero-charge receipt. 

11.6 Normalization and Path Aliasing 

Two syntactically different requests may cause the same effect, while two similar strings may address different recipients or preconditions. Normalize is therefore part of the trusted policy boundary. Its test obligations include canonical encoding, default parameters, redirects, indirect identifiers, locale and unit conversion, recipient resolution, protocol-specific retries, and relevant domain preconditions. Unknown semantics fail closed. Complete mediation is equally important [39]. An agent with direct cloud credentials can bypass an MCP proxy; a browser can activate an effect through a UI path; a tool can invoke a second tool internally. The theorem covers only credentials and routes that the deployment actually interposes. Network egress policy, workload identity, receiver-side enforcement, and credential isolation are deployment controls, not consequences of the budget data structure. 

11.7 Audit Integrity and Rollback

This strengthens forensic detection; exclusive ownership still depends on the authoritative transition service. Audit records contain sensitive purpose, recipient, spend, and graph metadata. 12 Related Work Beyond the Closest Mechanisms 12.1 Agent Economies and Governance Receipts 

EpochX treats tasks as bounties, supports recursive budgeted delegation, and uses lock and settle operations [46]. It is therefore direct prior art for hierarchical economic allocation; our focus is the failure semantics around duplicate delivery, persistent ownership, quarantine, fencing, and late settlement that are not established by EpochX’s reported model and evaluation. AgentBound cen-tralizes pre-execution governance and emits signed governance receipts [21]. Those receipts attest to a policy decision, while our effect receipt attests to a receiver outcome and actual charge. Human Delegation Provenance (HDP), a lightweight cryptographic protocol, provides human-delegation provenance, while Aegon proposes ledger-bound tokens with attested receipts [6, 10]. We do not claim the first delegation chain, ledger token, or signed receipt. Agent Contracts and Token Budgets are the nearest resource-governance works [22, 48]. Their allocation law and affine ownership discipline motivate our model. The contribution here begins where one process or one allocation snapshot stops being authoritative: ownership must survive messages, durable crash cuts, joins, and uncertain effects. 

12.2 Authorization Delegation 

OAuth token exchange and Rich Authorization Requests provide standardized building blocks for audience-bound exchange and structured authorization [20, 29]. AIP and the current AAT Internet-Draft extend these ideas to agent identity and attenuating chains [32, 36]; Overlaying Governance formalizes policy composition [18]. These answer which actions remain permitted . Our credits add linear consumption: two equally attenuated children must not both own the same last unit. 52 Zhu and Wang 

Resource-bounded temporal logics have long represented strategies constrained by consumable or producible resources [2]. Our problem is operational rather than strategic: it specifies how au-thenticated rights move and settle under crash and partition. The logical connection is useful, but a model-checking semantics does not supply durable message protocols by itself. 

12.3 Escrow, CRDTs, and Mobile Rights 

Escrow transactions reserve rights so concurrent transactions preserve a numeric invariant [33]. Mobile escrow distributes rights to occasionally disconnected clients [23]. Bounded Counter CRDTs provide a particularly strong construction for partition-local decrement and rights trans-fer [5]. Just-Right Consistency combines such mechanisms with analysis that coordinates only invariant-sensitive operations [42]. We adopt, rather than rename, this lineage. The extension is that an agent credit carries an au-thorization label and dynamic delegation lineage, the task graph can join without merging aliased rights, and a spent unit remains coupled to an external-effect obligation through uncertainty. A numeric counter that converges to a valid value can still miss a duplicated payment or disclosure. 

12.4 Transactions, Workflows, and Exactly-Once Semantics 

Sagas decompose long-lived transactions into steps and compensations [12]; Atomix specializes transactional semantics to agent tool use [31]. RIFL couples request IDs with durable completion records [27]. Beldi supplies fault-tolerant transactional serverless workflows, and ExoFlow targets exactly-once DAG execution [49, 54]. These systems demonstrate that replay safety is a storage and endpoint property, not a property of a prompt. Our theorem is narrower in one dimension and broader in another. It does not claim a universal exactly-once workflow. It proves at-most-once settlement and continuous charged coverage for consumable delegated authority, including the safe but unavailable outcome in which an arbitrary effect cannot be authoritatively resolved. 

12.5 Coordination and Impossibility 

CAP rules out simultaneous strong consistency and availability for every request during arbi-trary partitions [13]; FLP limits deterministic consensus progress in an asynchronous system with a crash [11]. Invariant confluence and the Coordination Criterion more directly character-ize when application correctness permits coordination-free execution [4, 16]. Our Proposition 1 is an application-specific consequence: exclusive preallocation permits local work, while creating or reallocating a shared spendable unit requires coordination or blocking. 

13 Discussion 13.1 What Is Actually Conserved? 

The object conserved is authenticated authorization capacity, not money or physical energy. A dollar dimension means “authority to cause up to one dollar of charges under a named settlement rule.” If taxes, exchange rates, tips, later adjustments, or hidden provider usage are outside that rule, they are outside the invariant. A rigorous deployment must define each dimension’s unit, rounding, maximum charge, authority, and reconciliation semantics before issuing it. The exact equality in Equation (8) is stronger and more diagnostic than merely checking C ⪯ B.It distinguishes spendable, moving, not-yet-exposed, uncertain, consumed, and sacrificed capacity. Operators can therefore see whether apparent under-spending is true availability or merely hidden stranding. Fault-Tolerant Budget Conservation in Agent Delegation 53 

13.2 Why Keep Both R and Q?

Combining reserved and indeterminate rights preserves the numeric equation, but loses a crucial recovery fact. A durable R record whose outbox provably never became dispatchable can be locally returned. A Q record may correspond to a real effect and needs external evidence. The separation turns a crash cut into an auditable transition and prevents a generic “release pending reservations” job from creating authority. 

13.3 Safety, Liveness, and Economic Utility 

The protocol’s safety is prefix-closed; its liveness is conditional. A branch can progress during partition while it has suitable local rights and a reachable gateway. Once exhausted, it blocks even if another partition has idle capacity. A quarantined right can remain unavailable forever. These are not incidental implementation shortcomings but consequences of refusing to guess about exclusive ownership or external effects. Allocation policy can reduce the cost: assign escrow proportional to expected demand, keep small emergency reserves in multiple zones, rebalance before known disconnections, and choose queryable endpoints for large effects. Dynamic prediction may improve utilization but must not enter the safety theorem. 

13.4 Vectors and Conversion 

Vectors prevent a worker from exchanging, for example, ten low-risk reads for one high-risk write without authorization. Some applications legitimately convert resources: a dollar cap may pur-chase token calls, or a global risk budget may be divided among effect classes. Such conversion is a new protected effect. It needs a signed rate/rounding contract, consumes source rights, creates target rights once, and preserves a declared value invariant. Without that extra contract, conver-sion is undefined rather than inferred. 

13.5 DAG Tasks, Forest-Owned Rights 

Task dependencies and budget ownership should remain different graphs. A synthesis agent can read results from many parents without owning their remainders. When it needs budget, each input branch may transfer a disjoint credit or one authority may perform an atomic join. This design prevents the common error of summing balances associated with incoming task edges. At large scale, full atom-set proofs are impractical. Range certificates, bounded-counter rights matrices, or cryptographic commitments can compress the footprint. The implementation must nevertheless expose a disjointness witness and consume each input generation exactly once. 

13.6 Standards Integration 

The protocol does not require a new wire protocol. AIP/AAT or OAuth can carry identity and at-tenuated scope; MCP or A2A can carry opaque credit and operation bindings; HTTP idempotency or conditional writes can implement a gateway primitive. Standardization value lies in a common semantic profile: 

• authenticated resource schema and maximum-charge semantics; 

• stable root, logical-slot, transfer, credit-generation, operation, and terminal-evidence IDs; 

• immutable binding digest and the per-slot current epoch/fence; 

• endpoint capability declaration; 

• terminal outcomes including verified no-effect and forfeiture; and 

• machine-readable reason codes for conservative refusal. 54 Zhu and Wang 

Interoperability testing should verify refinement of these meanings, not only JSON shape com-patibility. 

13.7 Deployment Migration 

A practical rollout can proceed in increasing assurance: (1) instrument existing agents with stable operation IDs and observed usage, making no hard-bound claim; (2) move counters into a durable branch ledger and reserve before effects; (3) route high-impact effects through idempotent, queryable gateways; (4) introduce exclusive child credits and disable copyable numeric “remaining” fields as authori-ties; (5) add epoch fencing, disjoint joins, reconciliation, and an independent trace verifier; and (6) publish only claims supported by the endpoint profile actually deployed. Legacy direct paths remain outside complete mediation until credentials and egress controls make bypass impossible. 

14 Limitations and Validity 14.1 Model Limitations 

The base model assumes crash faults, authenticated records, one correct local linearization point per branch, and a finite natural vector. It does not model Byzantine stores, signature compromise, side channels, probabilistic settlement, debt, negative balances, interest, speculative credit, or cross-root netting. Two independently authenticated roots are distinct authorizations; an issuer that accidentally signs overlapping grants can overspend a real-world cap without violating either per-root equation. Issuer-level escrow is required for an aggregate cap. The logical-atom construction is a proof device; a faulty compression or commitment scheme can violate the implementation mapping. Recent work on distributed agent governance under a compromised provider studies Byzantine replication, monitoring, auditing, and hybrid defenses [26]. Its adversary is outside our base theo-rem: we assume correct authoritative stores, gateways, and evidence services and establish safety only for the declared crash, retry, and partition model. The two guarantees are complementary rather than ordered by strength. The end-to-end effect theorem depends on sound normalization, complete mediation, an enforce-able maximum-charge contract, gateway fencing/idempotency, and complete non-equivocating terminal evidence. These are substantial assumptions. The ledger theorem remains useful when some fail, but it must not be presented as conservation of actual external cost. 

14.2 Formal-Method Limitations 

The inductive proofs establish the mathematical transition system. TLA+/TLC and the independent explorer cover only their declared finite constants, fault schedules, and modeled transitions. State-space completion increases confidence in the specification and detects regressions; it is not a proof of database durability, transport authentication, cryptographic libraries, or unmodeled adapters. In particular, the settlement TLA+ and JavaScript encodings assign each logical unit one loca-tion by type. Their exact-sum invariant checks transition bookkeeping but cannot represent the duplicate-tag raw states excluded by the ownership-partition proof. The lineage encoding admits origin-tag multiplicity and detects its compound fan-in mutant, but it is still a bounded model rather than a mechanized proof of the full raw-relation semantics. Model quality, real-provider accounting, Internet-scale latency, multi-region database failover, operator response, production failure frequency, and capacity plan-ning are separate evaluation targets. The most important negative result is structural and does not depend on benchmark scale: with-out endpoint cooperation, a lost response cannot distinguish non-execution from completed exe-cution. Experiments can demonstrate the race but cannot remove it. 

14.4 Construct and External Validity 

“Budget” spans tokens, currency, calls, disclosures, and risk units. These dimensions differ in me-tering authority and reversibility. Results transfer only when the deployment supplies a conser-vative maximum and an authoritative settlement relation. Quantization can over-reserve; coarse units trade utility for simple guarantees. The 2026 agent papers discussed here are recent and several are preprints or an Internet-Draft. Their reported systems and terminology may evolve. We use them to set a conservative novelty boundary, not as proof of deployment prevalence. Classical escrow, invariant-confluence, lease, and workflow literature remains the stronger foundation. 

14.5 Internal Validity 

Independent implementations, mutation tests with expected counterexamples, prefix checks, stable result manifests, and explicit claim/evidence mapping reduce correlated specification risk across the formal model, runtime, checker, and generated tables. Artifact results are regenerated from source; every failed or skipped case is reported. 

15 Ethics and Responsible Deployment 

Budget enforcement can reduce financial loss, excessive API use, unauthorized disclosure, and repeated high-impact effects. It can also create false confidence. A green ledger does not prove that a planner chose a beneficial action, that a price was fair, that a recipient was appropriate, or that complete mediation actually held. Interfaces should display endpoint profile, quarantined capacity, unresolved effects, and assumption failures alongside a remaining balance. Fine-grained receipts and delegation graphs reveal organizational structure, purposes, recipi-ents, spending patterns, and potentially sensitive content. Evidence minimization, separation of duties, access logging, encryption, retention limits, and deletion procedures are necessary. Cryp-tographic commitments can reduce disclosure but do not make metadata harmless. Conservative quarantine can be abused to deny service or trap a competitor’s shared capacity. Issuers should cap the budget one child can reserve, define escalation and forfeiture authority, record human overrides as new authorizations, and avoid automatic punitive decisions based solely on an ambiguous external outcome or unverified assertion. The mechanism allocates authority; it does not decide whose goals deserve resources. 56 Zhu and Wang 

16 Conclusion 

Distributed agent delegation turns a budget from a number into a fault-tolerant authorization object. Parent/child allocation inequalities, affine local types, attenuated scopes, idempotency keys, or convergent counters each solve part of the problem. None alone prevents a refunded timeout from colliding with a late effect, an aliased fan-in from reminting capacity, or a stale worker from spending a reclaimed generation. We introduced a protocol in which exclusive, labeled credit moves through a delegation lineage; a protected effect is reserved before exposure; ambiguity is quarantined; and one idempotent ter-minal decision applies authenticated evidence at most once. A success receipt releases only its authenticated remainder. Full return from an exposed obligation requires a receiver-visible fence together with authoritative proof that the exact binding was never accepted and never occurred. The six-bucket equation keeps spendable, moving, reserved, uncertain, committed, and forfeited rights explicit. Under stated assumptions, distinct effect instances are injectively covered, and set-tled actual charge plus conservative outstanding exposure is bounded by the issued root vector. The construction also preserves ledger conservation, descendant non-amplification, late-completion safety, and partition confinement. The central systems result is precise: local availability comes from rights allocated before a par-tition, while uncertainty must remain charged until evidence resolves it. A system that hides this cost by refunding on timeout does not conserve authority; it merely delays discovery of the over-spend.

G Deployment Assurance Checklist 

(1) Define every resource dimension, unit, rounding rule, maximum-charge rule, and authoritative settlement source. (2) Enumerate every route to each protected effect; remove direct credentials or mark the deploy-ment outside complete mediation. (3) Verify that reserve, obligation, outbox, and audit records share one durable local transaction. (4) Verify that possible gateway exposure occurs only after the durable R → Q transition. (5) At first acceptance, require a signed single-use dispatch permit or an authoritative branch query proving the matching dispatchable marker and Q/F ownership; an offline audit is insufficient. (6) Scope operation keys to receiver and normalized effect; make exact replay a stable decision and conflicting replay an error. (7) Recompute 𝛽 𝑜 and test terminal-evidence signatures and every immutable binding, including root, logical slot, lineage, footprint, epoch, effect, receiver, maximum, and actual charge. (8) Require Equation (7) for every partial remainder or no-effect return; use a separate sealed transfer for cross-store or ancestor return. (9) Before reusing a lost generation, install fences at every protected gateway and obtain terminal settlement or authoritative proof that each returned binding was never accepted and never occurred; a fence or lease expiry alone is not effect-absence evidence. (10) Reject numeric fan-in unless ownership inputs are pairwise disjoint and atomically consumed. (11) Monitor T, Q, F separately from committed spend and available capacity. (12) Bound per-child reservations and define escalation, reconciliation, and forfeiture authorities. (13) Run the conformance suite for each MCP, A2A, HTTP, browser, shell, or framework adapter.
