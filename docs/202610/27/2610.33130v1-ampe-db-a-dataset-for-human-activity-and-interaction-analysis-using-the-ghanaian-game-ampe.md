## 摘要
人体活动识别基准数据集对具有文化情境特征的动作形式与互动关注不足。我们推出 Ampe-DB，这是一个基于加纳传统游戏 Ampe 构建的多模态数据集。该游戏包含协调的跳跃、腿部动作及双人互动。数据集包含来自 15 名参与者的 637 段典型视频片段，提供 RGB 视频、双人姿态表示、参与者及配对元数据、四分类动作标签，以及派生的二分类动作关系标签。我们采用三种评估方案，对四分类动作识别和二分类动作关系分类进行评估：避免重复样本的固定基准、四折配对留出评估，以及针对类别覆盖完整的参与者进行的参与者留出诊断。基线模型包括一个冻结的、在 Kinetics-400 上预训练的 R(2+1)D-18 RGB 编码器、一个用于姿态序列的双层双向 GRU，以及通过验证集选择的 RGB-姿态后期融合模型。对于四分类识别，RGB 的宏平均 F1 分数从固定基准上的 0.955 降至配对留出评估中的 0.418，以及参与者留出评估中的 0.384。姿态表示具有更强的鲁棒性，在相同评估方案下分别达到 0.957、0.811 和 0.839。融合模型在固定基准上达到 0.976，在配对留出评估中达到 0.824，但在参与者变化的情况下，并未持续优于姿态模型。在留出评估中，二分类动作关系识别也变得显著更难，尤其是涉及不同腿部配置的情况。这些结果表明，固定基准上的高分可能掩盖模型无法泛化至不同参与者和配对的缺陷。Ampe-DB 可支持细粒度动作识别、人际交互、多模态学习及身份感知泛化等研究。

## Abstract
Human activity recognition benchmarks underrepresent culturally situated forms of movement and interaction. We introduce Ampe-DB, a multimodal dataset based on Ampe, a traditional Ghanaian game involving coordinated jumping, leg movements, and two-person interaction. It contains 637 canonical clips from 15 participants, with RGB video, two-person pose representations, participant and pair metadata, four-class movement labels, and derived binary movement-relation labels.   We evaluate four-class movement recognition and binary movement-relation classification using three protocols: a duplicate-safe fixed benchmark, four-fold pair-held-out evaluation, and participant-held-out diagnostics for participants with complete class coverage. Baselines comprise a frozen Kinetics-400-pretrained R(2+1)D-18 RGB encoder, a two-layer bidirectional GRU for pose sequences, and validation-selected RGB-pose late fusion.   For four-class recognition, RGB macro-F1 falls from 0.955 on the fixed benchmark to 0.418 under pair-held-out evaluation and 0.384 under participant-held-out evaluation. Pose is more robust, achieving 0.957, 0.811, and 0.839 across the same protocols. Fusion reaches 0.976 on the fixed benchmark and 0.824 under pair-held-out evaluation, but does not consistently improve on pose under participant shift. Binary movement-relation recognition also becomes substantially harder under held-out evaluation, especially for different-leg configurations.   These findings show that strong fixed-benchmark scores can mask failures to generalize across people and pairings. Ampe-DB supports research on fine-grained movement recognition, human-human interaction, multimodal learning, and identity-aware generalization.
