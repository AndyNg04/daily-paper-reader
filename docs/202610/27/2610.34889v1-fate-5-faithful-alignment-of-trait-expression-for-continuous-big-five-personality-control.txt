# ABSTRACT 

Controlling personality in large language models is important for building con-versational agents that can be customized to different users and interaction set-tings. However, existing personality control methods often face a tradeoff between strong trait expression and natural dialogue generation. We introduce FATE-5, Faithful Alignment of Trait Expression for the Big Five, a two-stage frame-work that separates personality encoding from naturalness alignment. FATE-5 first learns continuous soft prompt representations of Big Five profiles using di-alogue supervision and an auxiliary survey objective. It then improves dialogue naturalness through preference alignment based on both dialogue quality and per-sonality agreement. Across three language model backbones, FATE-5 maintains strong trait expression while achieving substantially higher dialogue naturalness than other trait-strong methods. On held-out questionnaires, it achieves mono-tonicity correlations of 0.97–0.99 and recovery RMSE of 8.9–12.4, substantially improving over the evaluated baselines. These results demonstrate effective con-tinuous personality control while maintaining natural conversational behavior. 

# 1 INTRODUCTION 

As large language models increasingly serve as conversational agents, controlling their personality is increasingly important for adapting them to different users and interaction settings (Vu et al., 2026). Such adaptation can be particularly useful in interactive applications, where personality can influ-ence dialogue behavior, task success, and user satisfaction (Guo et al., 2025). The Big Five provides a natural framework for this purpose by organizing personality into five broad and continuous dimen-sions of Openness, Conscientiousness, Extraversion, Agreeableness, and Neuroticism (McCrae & John, 1992). Its continuous structure provides an interpretable way to specify the trait configuration and intensity an agent should express. Recent work has explored several approaches for inducing Big Five personality in LLMs. Prompt-based methods use natural language descriptions or explicit personality profiles to elicit desired traits (Jiang et al., 2024). Training-based approaches instead learn personality conditioned model representations or adaptations (Liu et al., 2024; Li et al., 2025a), while activation-based methods directly manipulate internal directions associated with personality traits (Feng et al., 2026). Prompt-ing tends to preserve natural conversation while providing relatively weak trait expression. Training based methods offer more expressive conditioning mechanisms, although accurately matching the intended continuous trait intensity remains challenging. Activation steering provides flexible and strong control without retraining the underlying model. However, our interactive evaluation shows that strong intervention can substantially degrade dialogue naturalness. These limitations reveal a tradeoff between personality control strength and dialogue naturalness that existing approaches do not fully resolve. To address this tradeoff, we propose FATE-5 , Faithful Alignment of Trait Expression for the Big Five, a framework for faithful and natural continuous personality control. Given a target Big Five 1arXiv:2610.34889v1 [cs.CL] 26 Oct 2026 profile b ∈ [0 , 100] 5, FATE-5 first maps the profile into continuous soft prompt embeddings us-ing a lightweight personality encoder while keeping the pretrained language model frozen, follow-ing the general principle of parameter efficient prompt tuning (Lester et al., 2021). The encoder is trained using two complementary objectives. Dialogue supervision teaches how the target per-sonality should be expressed in conversation, while an auxiliary survey objective strengthens the correspondence between continuous target values and their learned representation. This objective is motivated by psychometric studies showing that standardized personality instruments can provide structured measurements of personality expressed by LLMs (Serapio-Garc´ ıa et al., 2025). We find, however, that stronger trait control can reduce dialogue naturalness. FATE-5 therefore separates personality encoding from naturalness alignment. After learning the personality representation, we freeze the encoder and attach a LoRA adapter to the backbone (Hu et al., 2022). We construct pref-erence pairs that jointly consider dialogue naturalness and agreement with the target personality, and optimize the adapter using Direct Preference Optimization (Rafailov et al., 2023). This two stage design improves conversational quality while preserving strong personality control. We evaluate FATE-5 through complementary interactive dialogue and psychometric protocols, measuring both perceptible trait expression and recovery of the intended continuous personality profile. Our contributions are as follows. • We characterize continuous Big Five personality control along two dimensions: trait ex-pression and dialogue quality. Using a multi-turn dialogue evaluation, we identify a trade-off between strong personality induction and natural conversation in existing approaches. • We introduce FATE-5 , a two-stage framework that separates personality encoding from naturalness alignment. The first stage learns soft prompt representations of continuous Big Five profiles using dialogue and survey supervision. The second stage improves con-versational naturalness through preference alignment based on both dialogue quality and personality agreement. • We evaluate FATE-5 using dialogue and psychometric protocols across multiple backbones and questionnaire formats, with ablations of its key components, assessing both personality expression and control fidelity. 

# 2 RELATED WORK 

2.1 PERSONALITY CONTROL IN LARGE LANGUAGE MODELS 

A growing body of work studies how personality can be imposed on or elicited from language mod-els. The simplest approach specifies personality through natural-language instructions. Big5-Scaler encodes numerical Big Five scores directly into prompts and demonstrates that personality inten-sity can be controlled without parameter updates (Cho & Cheong, 2025). PersonaLLM similarly studies whether prompted LLM personas can express assigned Big Five characteristics across per-sonality questionnaires and open-ended generation (Jiang et al., 2024). Prompt based approaches are inexpensive and model agnostic, but they often provide weaker trait expression than more direct intervention methods, limiting their ability to induce clear and consistent personality differences. Other approaches modify model representations or parameters more directly. DPG uses a hyper-network to generate personality-conditioned low-rank model adaptations (Liu et al., 2024). Psy-chAdapter learns layer-specific transformations from continuous psychological attributes and con-ditions transformer layers on these representations (Vu et al., 2026). Activation-steering approaches instead identify directions associated with personality traits in a pretrained model and intervene di-rectly in its hidden states. PERSONA extracts trait-specific activation directions and supports con-tinuous and compositional personality control through operations in activation space (Feng et al., 2026). More recent activation-space work also studies how layer selection affects the stability of Big Five steering (Bhandari et al., 2026). These approaches provide strong personality control, but their conversational quality is less clear. In our experiments, we find that stronger trait control can reduce dialogue naturalness. FATE-5 is designed to address this tradeoff by maintaining strong personality expression while improving natural dialogue quality. 2Figure 1: Overview of FATE-5. Stage 1 learns a continuous personality representation from a target Big Five profile using a lightweight encoder while keeping the backbone LLM frozen. The encoder is trained jointly with dialogue loss and survey loss to strengthen trait expression. Standardized personality inventories have long been used to measure the Big Five traits. The Big Five Inventory provides a widely used measure of Openness, Conscientiousness, Extraversion, Agreeableness, and Neuroticism (John et al., 1991). The NEO Five Factor Inventory offers another established measure of the five broad dimensions (Costa & Mccrae, 1992), while the IPIP NEO 120 provides a public domain inventory covering both broad traits and their lower level facets (Johnson, 2014). Shorter instruments such as the Ten Item Personality Inventory enable more compact assess-ment (Gosling et al., 2003), and the BFI-2 extends the original BFI with a hierarchical structure that includes additional facet level information (Soto & John, 2017). These psychometric instruments have also been increasingly used to measure personality in LLMs. PersonaLLM evaluates assigned Big Five personas using the BFI together with open ended writing tasks (Jiang et al., 2024). Serapio-Garc´ ıa et al. (2025) develop a broader psychometric framework for administering personality inventories to LLMs and evaluating the reliability and construct validity of their responses. TRAIT further introduces a personality benchmark for LLMs using psycho-metrically grounded questions derived from established personality constructs (Lee et al., 2025). Other work compares Likert and forced choice formats and shows that the response format can substantially affect estimated LLM personality (Li et al., 2025b). Together, these studies establish standardized questionnaires as an important tool for analyzing personality in language models. At the same time, psychometric evaluation of LLMs remains sensitive to how the assessment is con-ducted. Personality measurements can vary with prompt wording and response format, and widely available questionnaire items may also appear in model pretraining data (Serapio-Garc´ ıa et al., 2025; Han et al., 2026). These findings motivate evaluating personality with multiple instruments and question formats rather than relying on a single questionnaire setting. In our experiments, we there-fore use separate inventories for training and evaluation and complement questionnaire based mea-surement with dialogue evaluation. 33 METHOD

# 4 EXPERIMENTS 

4.1 DATA 

The personality encoder and the trained baselines’ conditioning modules are learned from a corpus of examples, each providing a target profile b, a dialogue context x, and a response y that matches 

b. We build this corpus from two complementary sources. 

Big5Chat (Li et al., 2025a) contributes persona grounded dialogues in which a speaker is instructed to a high or low level of a single Big Five trait. We take the utterance as the response y and label it with the corresponding profile, where the conditioned trait is set high or low and the others stay neutral. This source gives clean and strongly expressed trait signals, but its targets are coarse and categorical. 

PANDORA (Gjurkovi´ c et al., 2021) contributes real Reddit conversations. For each comment writ-ten by a user whose Big Five profile is known, we recover its parent comment as the context x

and use the comment itself as the response y, labelled with the author’s continuous Big Five scores. This source provides fine-grained targets drawn from a real population, providing supervision across continuous trait values rather than only high or low settings. In total the mixture contains about 46 k examples, roughly 30 k from Big5Chat and 16 k from PANDORA, with all targets placed on a common 0 to 100 scale. We use this mixture only to train the encoder, which is conditioned on b to produce y under the dialogue loss of §3.3. The survey loss instead draws its items from the training questionnaires Qtr =

{BFI (John et al., 1991) , IPIP-NEO-120 (Johnson, 2014) }. The preference alignment stage of §3.4 uses no external corpus at all. Its pairs come from on policy candidates that the encoder generates over random profiles and dialogue topics, and an external judge scores them. For evaluation we hold out two further questionnaires Qte = {TIPI (Gosling et al., 2003) , BFI-2 (Soto & John, 2017) },which appear in no training stage. 54.2 MODELS AND BASELINES 

Models. We evaluate every method on three instruction-tuned backbones from different model families, Llama-3-8B-Instruct (Grattafiori et al., 2024), Gemma-2-9B-Instruct (Team et al., 2024), and OLMo-2-13B-Instruct (OLMo et al., 2024). Each method is trained and evaluated on each backbone independently, and the backbone weights are frozen for every method. The encoder uses T =64 soft-prompt tokens from a two-layer MLP with hidden size 512 and is trained for 3ksteps, and the offline DPO stage uses β=0 .1, λ=1 , and LoRA with r=16 and α=32 , on preference pairs built from N =5 candidates over 200 random profiles. The candidates are scored for the DPO reward by Llama-3.1-70B-Instruct (Grattafiori et al., 2024), which is distinct from the judges used for evaluation (§4.3). 

Baselines. We compare against four representative families of controllable personality generation, all conditioned on the same target b ∈ [0 , 100] 5:• Prompting (Big5-scaler) (Cho & Cheong, 2025). The target scores are verbalized in the system prompt and no parameters are trained. • Steering vectors (Feng et al., 2026). Per-trait directions extracted from the backbone are added to the residual stream of the middle layers, scaled by the target profile. • PsychAdapter (Vu et al., 2026). A trained module maps b to per-layer additive vectors injected at the middle layers, with the backbone frozen. • DPG (Liu et al., 2024). A hypernetwork maps b to a low-rank weight modulation (LoRA) applied to the attention output projections, with the backbone frozen. The trained baselines use the same personality dialogue corpus as ours but do not use the survey loss. Appendix B gives the full training and decoding configuration. 4.3 EVALUATION 

We assess each method along two complementary axes, how the target personality is expressed in free dialogue and how faithfully it is measured by standardized questionnaires. Also, we ablate our own design. Each part answers one of the research questions. 4.3.1 DIALOGUE EXPRESSION (RQ1). Two agents converse over a shared topic for 10 turns, with each agent assigned an independent random target profile. We use ten conversation topics, two for each Big Five trait, chosen to naturally elicit the trait of interest. Openness is probed by travel and music, Conscientiousness by daily habits and personal goals, Extraversion by making friends and social events, Agreeableness by a disagreement with a friend and helping a stranger in need, and Neuroticism by facing an unexpected setback and dealing with a stressful deadline. Each dialogue is evaluated only on the trait targeted by its topic. For each method and backbone, we generate 100 dialogues balanced across the ten topics. We evaluate dialogue expression using two metrics: • Trait accuracy. Given a dialogue, the evaluator selects which of the two agents expresses more of the target trait. Accuracy is the fraction of responses that match the ground-truth ordering of the two target scores. • Naturalness. The evaluator rates how human-like and fluent the dialogue is on a 1–5 scale, which we rescale to 0–100 .For the main evaluation, we use three external LLM judges (GPT-5-mini, Claude-Haiku-4.5, and DeepSeek-V4-Flash) and average their scores. We report the results on all three backbones. We additionally conduct a human evaluation on 50 Llama-3-8B dialogues using the same persona and topic configurations across methods. We collect 984 dialogue level evaluations from 59 unique workers. Human raters evaluate Trait and Naturalness using the same criteria as the LLM judges, with an additional “I don’t know” option for Trait. # 5 RESULTS 

5.1 RQ1: E XPRESSION 

Table 1 reports dialogue expression across the three backbones. Activation injection methods achieve high trait accuracy but substantially reduce naturalness. For example, Steering reaches high trait accuracy while its naturalness remains low at 2.6, 7.9, and 21 .6 across Llama-3-8B, Gemma-2-9B, and OLMo-2-13B. PsychAdapter improves naturalness compared with Steering but still remains below our method across all three backbones. Prompting generally provides lower trait accuracy and shows mixed naturalness across backbones. Our method maintains high trait accuracy, ranging from 72 .7 to 76 .3 across the three backbones. Its naturalness scores are 43 .0, 39 .1, and 45 .4 on Llama-3-8B, Gemma-2-9B, and OLMo-2-13B, re-spectively. These results indicate that our method preserves perceptible differences in target-trait lev-els while avoiding much of the naturalness degradation observed with activation-based approaches. The human evaluation on Llama-3-8B shows a similar pattern. Steering achieves high trait accuracy but substantially lower naturalness, while prompting achieves the highest naturalness with lower trait accuracy. Our method obtains the highest trait accuracy at 78 .7 while achieving a naturalness score of 67 .8, compared with 74 .4 for prompting. The human evaluation therefore shows that our method maintains high trait accuracy while preserving relatively high dialogue naturalness. 5.2 RQ2: C ONTINUOUS CONTROL 

Table 2 reports monotonicity across all three backbones, measured as the Pearson correlation be-tween the target trait value and the score recovered from held out questionnaires. FATE-5 shows consistently strong directional control under both question formats. It reaches 0.99 under both rigid and natural phrasing on Llama-3-8B and Gemma-2-9B, and 0.97 and 0.98 on OLMo-2-13B. The baselines are less consistent across backbones and response formats. Prompting shows strong monotonicity in several settings but drops to 0.65 under natural phrasing on OLMo-2-13B. The injection based methods also vary substantially across models and formats. Overall, FATE-5 shows consistently high target correlations across the evaluated backbones and questionnaire formats. 5.3 RQ3: Q UESTIONNAIRE GENERALIZATION 

Directional control alone does not guarantee that the recovered personality matches the intended target value. Table 2 therefore also reports recovery RMSE across all three backbones. FATE-5 achieves the lowest error in every setting, with RMSE values of 10 .5, 8.9, and 12 .4 on Llama-3-8B, Gemma-2-9B, and OLMo-2-13B, respectively. Table 3 reports the ablation results for FATE-5 on Llama-3-8B. The two-by-two comparison of the encoder and DPO stage highlights their distinct roles. Removing DPO while retaining the encoder lowers naturalness from 43 .0 to 37 .5, a decrease of 5.5 points. This suggests that DPO contributes to improving dialogue naturalness. Removing the encoder while applying the same DPO procedure to a numeric prompt reduces trait accuracy from 75 .3 to 60 .0, a decrease of 15 .3 points. This shows that the encoder plays a key role in effective trait expression. At the same time, this configuration achieves the highest naturalness score of 48 .8. The plain numeric prompt without either component reaches a trait accuracy of 69 .0 and a naturalness score of 46 .2. These results suggest that the encoder strengthens trait expression at the cost of some dialogue naturalness. This motivates the subsequent DPO stage, which compensates for this loss in naturalness while preserving the trait expression learned by the encoder. These results indicate that the learned personality representation and preference alignment work jointly: DPO improves naturalness when applied to the encoder representation while maintaining, and in this setting slightly improving, trait expression. Removing the survey loss reduces trait accuracy from 75 .3 to 51 .3, supporting its importance for faithful personality encoding. The trait component of the reward is also important. Setting λ = 0 

lowers both trait accuracy and naturalness. As detailed in Appendix E, removing the continuous trait term makes the reward highly discrete, producing many ties and reducing the number of usable preference pairs from 198 to 37. Thus, the trait term not only anchors personality agreement but also provides a denser preference signal. Increasing the weight to λ = 2 raises trait accuracy to 

78 .7 but reduces naturalness to 32 .5, illustrating the tradeoff induced by the reward weight. Finally, replacing DPO with SFT reduces both trait accuracy and naturalness, suggesting that the contrastive preference objective provides additional benefit. 

# 6 CONCLUSION 

We introduced FATE-5, a framework for faithful and natural continuous Big Five personality con-trol that separates personality representation from naturalness alignment. Experiments across three backbones and human evaluation show that FATE-5 maintains strong trait expression while produc-ing substantially more natural dialogue than other trait-strong approaches. Psychometric evaluation further demonstrates reliable continuous control and substantially lower profile recovery error, while ablations support the contributions of survey supervision and preference alignment. These findings suggest that personality fidelity and natural dialogue quality, which are difficult to achieve simulta-neously with existing methods, can be jointly maintained in personality-controllable conversational LLMs. 9AI U SE STATEMENT

# B TRAINING AND REPRODUCTION DETAILS 

All methods are trained and evaluated separately for each backbone while the backbone weights remain frozen. All runs use bfloat16 on a single GPU with seed 42 .

Personality encoder. Stage 1 uses a two layer MLP with GELU activations and hidden size 512 .The encoder produces T =64 soft prompt tokens and is trained for 3k steps. We use AdamW with a learning rate of 10 −3 and weight decay of 0.01 . The learning rate follows a linear schedule with 

100 warmup steps. The batch size is 2 and gradient accumulation is 4, giving an effective batch size of 8. The maximum sequence length is 512 tokens. The first 300 steps use only the dialogue loss as a warmup. After this period, we combine the two losses using λdial =0 .6 and λsurv =0 .4. At each survey step, we rotate through Qtr and sample 

8 items for each target. The targets come from 200 random profiles sampled uniformly over the answer scale. Pseudo answers are constructed deterministically as described in Appendix A. 

Preference alignment. Stage 2 constructs preference pairs from the frozen encoder using 200 

random profiles paired with the ten dialogue topics. For each profile, we sample N =5 candidates. Generation uses temperature 1.0, top p of 0.95 , repetition penalty 1.15 , no repeat 4 grams, and at most 120 new tokens. We score the candidates using Eq. 6 with λ=1 . The candidate with the highest reward is selected as yw and the candidate with the lowest reward is selected as yl. Pairs with a reward gap of zero are discarded. We use Llama-3.1-70B-Instruct as the scoring judge. DPO trains a LoRA adapter with rank 16 , scaling parameter α=32 , and dropout 0.05 . The adapter is applied to all attention and MLP projections. Training runs for 200 optimizer steps with AdamW and a learning rate of 5 × 10 −5. We use β=0 .1, gradient clipping at 1.0, and a maximum sequence length of 1024 tokens. # C HUMAN EVALUATION SETTING 

Sampling. From the 100 dialogues generated per method, we select 10 dialogues using a fixed random seed, with one dialogue selected for each topic. The same ten persona and topic config-urations are used for all five methods. As a result, the 50 dialogues differ only in the generation method. 

Instrument. The 50 dialogues are split across three forms containing 17 , 17 , and 16 dialogues. Each form includes dialogues from every method. Dialogue order is shuffled, method identities are hidden, and the agents are presented only as Speaker A and Speaker B. Each dialogue is rated using the same two criteria as the LLM judges. Trait is evaluated through a forced choice question with an additional “I don’t know” option. Naturalness is rated on a 1 to 5 scale and is rescaled to 0 to 

100 using the same rescaling as the LLM judges. Each item presents only a behavioral description of the target trait, such as “outgoing, talkative and energetic,” and does not include the trait label. 

Participants. We recruit workers through Amazon Mechanical Turk and restrict participation to workers with a lifetime HIT approval rate above 98% . Workers are paid $3.00 per form. Each worker completes exactly one form, so no worker evaluates the same dialogue more than once. 

Exclusions. We use 59 submissions from 59 unique workers and 984 dialogue level evaluations. At the response level, “I don’t know” answers account for 5.2% of Trait judgments. # E WHY REMOVING THE TRAIT ANCHOR ALSO HURTS NATURALNESS 

Setting λ=0 leaves only the naturalness score from the judge in the reward. One might therefore expect naturalness to improve. Instead, it drops sharply as shown in Table 3. This behavior originates from the preference data rather than from the optimization process. The judge evaluates naturalness on a scale from 1 to 5. When λ=0 , the reward can therefore take only five values and every reward gap is a multiple of 25 . If all N =5 candidates for a profile receive the same score, the reward gap becomes zero and the pair is discarded. This occurs for most profiles. Among the 200 sampled profiles, only 37 profiles produce a usable pair, which corresponds to 18 .5% 

of the data. In contrast, λ=1 produces usable pairs for 198 profiles, which corresponds to 99% of the data. Table 4 summarizes these statistics. The trait term provides a finer-grained signal that breaks these ties and allows almost every profile to contribute to preference training. 15
