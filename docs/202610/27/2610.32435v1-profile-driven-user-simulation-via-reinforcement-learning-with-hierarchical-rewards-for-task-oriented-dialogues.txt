## Abstract 

Building Task-Oriented Dialogue (TOD) sys-tems for high-value scenarios such as financial marketing requires large volumes of domain-specific dialogue data, which privacy regula-tions, annotation costs, and the need for ex-pert labor make hard to obtain. User sim-ulation offers a remedy, but existing meth-ods target general-domain dialogue and exhibit two key limitations: insufficient modeling of domain-specific business processes and com-pliance constraints, and inadequate capture of the colloquial, spoken-language character of voice interactions. We propose P ROFILE SIM ,a profile-driven user simulation framework for financial marketing outbound calls. It jointly models user profiles —demographics and Big Five personality traits—and business profiles —intents reverse-engineered from real call logs— enabling fine-grained control over simulated behavior. The base model is fine-tuned on real voice dialogue data and refined by Group Rel-ative Policy Optimization (GRPO) with hier-archical rewards at the dialogue and utterance levels. Paired with a customer service agent in-tegrating Standard Operating Procedure (SOP) constraints and Retrieval-Augmented Genera-tion (RAG), P ROFILE SIM achieves the highest colloquial naturalness, the strongest user profile adherence and the lowest agent score on long-tail intents among all evaluated user generators, while producing substantially shorter user utter-ances at comparable business compliance. The generated dialogue data is designed to support intent recognition training, agent evaluation, and rapid cold-start deployment. 

## 1 Introduction 

Task-oriented dialogue (TOD) systems are central to intelligent customer service and online market-ing, typically using a modular pipeline of natu-ral language understanding, dialogue state track-ing, policy learning, and natural language gener-ation (Young et al., 2013; Chen et al., 2017; Gao et al., 2019; Zhang et al., 2020; Qin et al., 2023). In high-stakes domains such as financial services, these systems must additionally satisfy stringent compliance, accuracy, and professional-rigor re-quirements (Wu et al., 2023; Yang et al., 2023): responses about interest rates, investment returns, or risk disclosures must be grounded in structured knowledge and regulatory guidelines. Adapting general-purpose dialogue systems to such stan-dards remains challenging because they optimize for fluency rather than domain knowledge and com-pliance, and high-quality labeled dialogue data is scarce due to expert annotation, regulatory review, and privacy considerations (Alzubaidi et al., 2023). User simulation—constructing virtual users that interact with dialogue systems to generate synthetic data—has progressed from agenda-based simula-tors (Schatzmann and Young, 2009; Li et al., 2016) through neural approaches (El Asri et al., 2016; Kreyssig et al., 2018; Lin et al., 2022) to LLM-based simulators (Davidson et al., 2023; Sekuli´ cet al., 2024; Luo et al., 2024; Wang et al., 2025b; Ren et al., 2024; Long et al., 2024; Harsha et al., 2025). Two deficiencies persist in vertical domains: (1) insufficient domain constraints —methods trained on general-domain corpora such as Mul-tiWOZ (Budzianowski et al., 2018) lack effective modeling of business processes and compliance rules; (2) inadequate spoken-language model-ing —written-text training produces outputs that diverge from the disfluencies and informality of real voice interactions (Ji et al., 2024; Mayor et al., 2025; Lee et al., 2026). We propose P ROFILE SIM , a user simulation framework for financial marketing outbound calls. The core is a joint modeling mechanism of busi-ness and user profiles : business processes (product attributes, sales workflow, compliance) and user attributes (demographics, Big Five traits) act as explicit conditioning variables. Training on real voice dialogue data mitigates the rigidity of prompt-arXiv:2610.32435v1 [cs.CL] 23 Oct 2026 based generation with closed-source LLMs, pro-ducing dialogues intended to support intent recog-nition training, agent evaluation, and cold-start de-ployment. 

Contributions. (1) A domain-specific user sim-ulation framework that jointly models business and user profiles for financial outbound call-ing. (2) A training recipe that combines super-vised fine-tuning (SFT) with GRPO-based pref-erence optimization under hierarchical dialogue-and utterance-level rewards. (3) Experiments on real outbound-call data showing that the method outperforms strong zero-shot LLM baselines on colloquial naturalness, user profile adherence, and downstream agent stress, and that each component contributes measurable gains. 

## 2 Related Work 

TOD and User Simulation. Classical TOD systems adopt a modular NLU/DST/poli-cy/NLG pipeline (Chen et al., 2017); Mul-tiWOZ (Budzianowski et al., 2018) and its refinements (Eric et al., 2020; Zang et al., 2020; Ye et al., 2022) remain the primary multi-domain benchmark, with a shift toward end-to-end architectures (Qin et al., 2023) pairing pretrained backbones (Radford et al., 2019; Raffel et al., 2020) with LLMs in zero/few-shot settings (Ope-nAI, 2023; Hudeˇ cek and Dušek, 2023). User simulation alleviates data-collection cost: early works used rule- and agenda-based formulations with stack-like user goals (Schatzmann and Young, 2009; Li et al., 2016), while neural network based approaches improved diversity through seq2seq models (El Asri et al., 2016), neural NLG (Kreyssig et al., 2018), hierarchical latent-variable models (Gür et al., 2018), multi-task models (Kim and Lipani, 2022), generative transformers with zero-shot ontology transfer (Lin et al., 2022), and emotion-aware simulation (Lin et al., 2023). Dialogue systems are widely deployed in e-commerce, with industrial systems such as AliMe (Li et al., 2017) integrating intent recognition, knowledge retrieval, and multi-turn management at massive scale; financial dialogue systems impose additional compliance, accuracy, and professional-rigor requirements. 

LLM-Based User Simulation. Prompt-based methods generate diverse user utterances without fine-tuning (Davidson et al., 2023; Terragni et al., 2023; Algherairy and Ahmed, 2025) but suffer from hallucinations, repetition, and incomplete goal fulfillment. Fine-tuning and verification-based approaches address these issues: DAUS fine-tunes on real dialogues for goal consistency (Sekuli´ cet al., 2024); DuetSim uses a generator/veri-fier architecture to mitigate “lost-in-the-middle” failures (Luo et al., 2024); UGRO leverages LLM feedback to optimize smaller models (Hu et al., 2023); utterance-level alignment sharpens response fidelity (Luo et al., 2025). A paral-lel line incorporates profiles or personas (Person-aLLM, 2025): USP infers implicit user profiles and optimizes them via conditional SFT and RL with utterance- and conversation-level cycle con-sistency (Wang et al., 2025a); mTAD composes trait-specific language models at decoding time for trait-conditioned simulation (Ferreira et al., 2024); CSHI emphasizes controllable, human-involved simulation for conversational recommen-dation (Zhu et al., 2025); Zhang et al. (2025) couple an LLM simulator with statistical preference mod-els to train RL-based recommenders; and cognitive-architecture frameworks target general behavior simulation (Wang et al., 2025b; Ren et al., 2024)— see Balog and Zhai (2025) for a comprehensive survey. Abdulhai et al. (2025) show that multi-turn RL reduces persona drift by over 55%, directly mo-tivating our GRPO design. These methods predom-inantly target general written-text scenarios; in con-trast to USP’s implicit profiles, P ROFILE SIM condi-tions on explicit demographic–personality and busi-ness profiles reverse-engineered from in-domain call logs, leaving spoken-language modeling and business-process constraints—largely unaddressed in prior work—as its central focus. Despite the commercial rise of voice AI in out-bound sales, academic work on user simulation for outbound marketing remains sparse, focusing on the system side with little attention to user behav-ior under business-process constraints. We target user simulation specifically for financial outbound calling, combining business-intent and user-profile modeling with reinforcement learning under hierar-chical dialogue- and utterance-level rewards to op-timize spoken-language naturalness and long-range persona coherence; the resulting framework gen-erates dialogues intended to support intent recog-nition training, agent evaluation, and cold-start de-ployment. 3.1 User Simulator Construction 

The construction of the User Simulator proceeds through three stages (Figure 1). First, we clean raw voice dialogue data and extract structured user and business profiles. Second, these pro-files and the cleaned corpus are used to construct SFT training data. Third, the base model under-goes SFT to acquire colloquial language generation and instruction-following capabilities, followed by Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) (Shao et al., 2024) to further improve utterance quality and multi-turn coherence. 3.1.1 User Profile Extraction 

The user profile puser represents personal attributes and personality characteristics, serving as a control signal governing both the informational content and linguistic style of generated responses. We decompose user profiling into two complementary dimensions: 

Objective features encode observable demo-graphic attributes, including gender, age range, oc-cupation, income status, and language style (e.g., “male, 40–55 years old, unstable income, direct and concise language style”). 

Subjective features are grounded in the Big Five personality model (John and Srivastava, 1999), characterizing personality along five dimensions: Extraversion, Agreeableness, Conscientiousness, Emotional Stability, and Openness to Experience. This representation has been widely adopted in computational user modeling and dialogue person-alization (Mairesse and Walker, 2007; Zheng et al., 2019). For instance, a profile might specify “low-to-medium Extraversion, medium Agreeableness, high Emotional Stability, low Openness,” enabling the model to generate behaviorally consistent re-sponses that reflect risk-averse tendencies in a loan consultation scenario.

3.1.2 Business Profile Schema 

To enhance business controllability, we introduce a business profile pbiz that explicitly constrains the model’s topical focus. Rather than constructing business constraints from prior domain knowledge, we adopt a reverse engineering approach grounded in real dialogue data. Starting from historical dialogues, we perform semantic analysis for each utterance and map natu-ral language to structured business intent labels: 

u → yintent (5) where yintent represents the business intent (e.g., “Loan process consultation,” “Repayment method confirmation”). We then reverse-engineer these labels into natural language descriptions: 

yintent → pbiz (6) where pbiz is a natural language prompt describing the user’s potential business concerns. This pipeline of “dialogue corpus → intent ab-straction → profile generation” achieves structured modeling of business knowledge from a data-driven perspective. Compared with using discrete la-bels directly, natural language profiles enhance the model’s contextual understanding. In practice, the business profile is injected as an instruction prompt: 

“Next, you may ask questions about pbiz .For example, you might inquire about the loan application process and pay close attention to the specific entry points and operation methods.” 

This design explicitly guides the model toward specific domain concerns rather than unconstrained exploration, enhancing business relevance and con-trollability. To further model the dynamic nature of user in-tentions in multi-turn conversations, we introduce a business profile transition mechanism that dynam-ically determines the business profile for the next turn. 3.1.4 GRPO-Based Reinforcement Learning 

After supervised fine-tuning, we further optimize the user simulator with Group Relative Policy Op-timization (GRPO) (Shao et al., 2024), which elim-inates the need for a separate value network by es-timating advantages through relative comparisons within groups of sampled responses. GRPO was originally proposed for enhancing mathematical reasoning (Shao et al., 2024) and has since been adopted as the core RL algorithm in DeepSeek-R1 (Guo et al., 2025). The goal is to improve the simulator’s ability to generate responses that are consistent with the target user profile while remain-ing coherent, natural, non-repetitive, and consistent with the evolving business context. During rein-forcement learning, the user simulator is the train-able policy, whereas the customer-service agent is kept fixed and serves as the environment policy.

3.2 Customer Service Agent Construction 

To evaluate the user simulator in realistic settings and complete the simulation loop, we construct a customer service agent providing a structured interactive environment. 

3.2.1 SOP-Constrained Dialogue 

In real financial sales scenarios, conversations are strictly constrained by Standard Operating Proce-dures (SOPs) (Kulkarni, 2025). We model the cus-tomer service dialogue as a decision process gov-erned by procedural constraints rather than free-form generation. We represent the SOP as a directed graph G =(V, E), where each node v ∈ V corresponds to a dialogue stage (e.g., opening remarks, product introduction, channel guidance) and each edge e ∈E represents an allowed transition. At each turn, the agent may only remain at the current stage or transition to a legal subsequent stage, confining the dialogue within a structured procedural space. This graph-based representation ensures adherence to the prescribed business workflow regardless of user input diversity. 

3.2.2 Retrieval-Augmented Generation 

While the SOP graph provides procedural structure, accurate product information is needed for profes-sional responses. We employ Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) (Lewis et al., 2020; Gao et al., 2024) to ground the agent’s responses in domain knowledge. At each turn, the system retrieves three types of information from the knowledge base: (1) the user’s business intent recognition result, (2) can-didate next dialogue stages, and (3) dialogue tem-plates for each candidate stage. These elements are filtered using the current SOP state, retaining only procedurally valid candidates. This retrieval-and-filter mechanism ensures both flexibility in respond-ing to diverse queries and consistency in adhering to business processes. 

3.2.3 Structured Input and Output 

The agent receives as input: historical dialogue, product information, current stage, SOP-filtered candidate stages, and retrieved knowledge. These inputs are organized into a unified prompt, op-tionally augmented with chain-of-thought reason-ing (Wei et al., 2022) for explicit process reasoning. The output is structured JSON containing two fields: the predicted next stage and the correspond-ing response. This format facilitates integration with downstream modules such as dialogue state tracking and execution engines, enhancing engi-neering usability and deployability. 4 Experiments

4.1 Experimental Settings Training details. Our full set of training data con-sists of 72,296 multi-turn dialogues collected from real outbound call scenarios, comprising 432,457 utterances across multiple financial domains, in-cluding insurance and wealth management. We first trained a user profile extraction model to obtain structured user profiles. We sampled 10,000 multi-turn dialogues from the training cor-pus and constructed profile annotations using GPT-5 (OpenAI, 2025). To assess the quality of the generated profiles, we randomly sampled 1,000 dialogue–profile pairs for manual validation. The annotators evaluated whether each profile attribute was supported by the corresponding dialogue and whether the resulting profile was coherent and com-plete. 95.4% of the evaluated profiles were judged valid, suggesting that the automatically constructed profiles provide high-quality supervision for subse-quent user-simulation training. Based on the Qwen3.5-9B base model, we fine-tuned the profile extraction model with LoRA for 3 epochs on 2 NVIDIA A100 GPUs, requiring ap-proximately 4 hours. The trained profile extraction model was then applied to the remaining dialogues. The extracted user profiles were subsequently used to construct the training data for the user simulation model. We used SFT to train the user simulation model based on the Qwen3.5-9B base model by sampling 97,126 utterances from the training dataset. We employed LoRA fine-tuning on 2 NVIDIA A100 GPUs for 3 epochs, with a maximum output se-quence length of 1,024 tokens and a learning rate of 2.0 × 10 −4. The SFT training required approxi-mately 14 hours. For the GRPO-based preference optimization stage (Section 3.1.4), we performed LoRA fine-tuning with a rollout group size of G = 8 . We ran-domly sampled 4,000 user profiles from the 72,296 profiles extracted from the real outbound call dia-logues. Since GRPO was conducted in an online re-inforcement learning setting, only the user profiles were used as the initial conditions for interaction. During training, the user simulation model inter-acted with a separate customer service model to dy-namically generate multi-turn dialogue trajectories, which were used for subsequent policy optimiza-tion. Training was conducted on 8 NVIDIA H200 GPUs for 2 epochs and required approximately 20 hours. 

Customer service simulation. To construct the interactive environment for the user simulator, we employed DeepSeek-R1 (Guo et al., 2025) as the customer service agent backbone. The agent integrates Retrieval-Augmented Genera-tion (RAG) (Lewis et al., 2020) with the SOP-constrained dialogue management framework de-scribed in Section 3.2 to generate contextually grounded and procedurally compliant customer ser-vice responses. 

Baselines. We compare the proposed user simu-lation model against multiple large language mod-els: DeepSeek-R1 (Guo et al., 2025), DeepSeek-V3 (DeepSeek-AI, 2024), GPT-5, and Qwen3-Max (Qwen Team, 2025b). All baselines are evalu-ated in a zero-shot setting without task-specific fine-tuning. Importantly, the baselines and our model are given exactly the same prompt template and in-put information, including the user profile, business profile, product information, and dialogue history. Thus, the only difference is the underlying model and whether it has undergone our task-specific training, providing a controlled comparison of the benefit of the proposed training approach. We also considered direct comparison with existing purpose-built user models, such as UserLM (Naous et al., 2026). However, its train-ing and evaluation setting differs substantially from ours. UserLM is designed to simulate general hu-man users in interactions with AI assistants and is evaluated primarily in open-domain multi-turn scenarios, including coding and mathematical con-versations. In contrast, our model targets Chi-nese outbound business conversations, where the simulator must generate realistic colloquial spo-ken responses while being explicitly conditioned on domain-specific user profiles, business profiles, product information, and dialogue context. As a result, directly applying UserLM would introduce substantial mismatches in language, dialogue do-main, and conditioning setting, making a direct comparison less informative. We therefore use strong general-purpose LLMs under an identical prompting setup as our primary baselines, allowing us to isolate and evaluate the benefit of task-specific training for user simulation in the target scenario.

4.3 Generated Dialogue Dataset 

We generated a multi-turn dialogue dataset through online interaction between the user simulator and the customer service agent in financial product rec-ommendation and sales scenarios. The customer service agent initiates each conversation using a predefined opening template, while the user simu-lator dynamically generates responses conditioned on the user profile and business profile. Across turns, the business profile evolves through the tran-sition mechanism described in Section 3.1.2, with empirical transition probabilities estimated from real outbound call data, allowing the simulator to shift between different business intents while main-taining consistency with the current dialogue state. The resulting dataset contains 3,000 high-quality dialogue sessions and approximately 

20,000 user–agent utterance pairs , with an aver-age of 7 turns per dialogue . Each dialogue ter-minates when either the maximum number of in-teraction turns is reached or the user expresses an intent to end the conversation.The generated dia-logues cover both scenario-general and scenario-specific business intents, with 58% of user utter-ances corresponding to scenario-specific intents. This indicates that the generated data contains a high proportion of business-relevant content and is consistent with the characteristics of real outbound call scenarios. A representative dialogue example is provided in Appendix C. 

4.4 Simulator Fidelity Evaluation 

We evaluate simulator fidelity from three com-plementary perspectives: (1) behavioral realism ,(2) controllability and role consistency , and (3) 

downstream utility . Behavioral realism measures whether generated users resemble real outbound callers in conversational style and interaction pat-terns. Controllability and role consistency evaluate whether the simulator remains faithful to condi-tioned user personas and business guidance. Fi-nally, downstream utility evaluates whether sim-ulated users effectively expose weaknesses in a fixed customer service agent under realistic long-tail business scenarios. Unless otherwise noted, Gemini 3.1 Pro serves as the LLM-as-judge. For metrics whose evalua-tion rubrics are also used as reward functions dur-ing GRPO training, we directly reuse the corre-sponding rubrics defined in Appendix B.2 and Ap-pendix B.3, respectively. The remaining evaluation rubrics are provided in Appendix D. Human spot-checks on 500 stratified samples per metric show substantial agreement with the automated judge, with Cohen’s κ ≥ 0.72 .

4.4.1 Behavioral realism Colloquial naturalness. Colloquial naturalness measures whether generated user utterances re-semble spontaneous spoken telephony conversa-tions rather than written, scripted, or templated text. We directly reuse the same LLM-based evaluation rubric as the rnatural reward used during GRPO training (Appendix B.2). We report the mean score across all evaluated user utterances, with higher scores indicating more natural and human-like con-versational expression. 

Utterance length. Outbound users typically pro-duce short, reactive replies. We report the mean character count (#chars) of user utterances per corpus; lower values are preferred for telephony realism, subject to retaining intelligible content. 

4.4.2 Controllability and role consistency User profile adherence. User profile adherence measures whether the simulated user behavior remains consistent with the predefined persona throughout the entire multi-turn interaction. The target persona is specified by structured attributes such as risk preference, prior product experience, communication style, and cooperation willingness. Concretely, we apply the user profile extraction model to the user’s cumulative responses in each session to recover the profile implied by the gener-ated behavior. We then compute the cosine simi-larity between the extracted profile representation and the target profile, yielding a score in [0 , 1] . We report the mean score averaged over all dialogue sessions; higher values indicate stronger persona consistency throughout the interaction. 

Business profile compliance. Recall that the business profile pbiz is a natural-language instruc-tion derived from real dialogues via intent abstrac-tion ( u → yintent → pbiz ), describing the user’s plausible business concerns and the topical scope that the simulator should follow. It is injected at generation time to improve business controllability 

by constraining the user’s topical focus. To eval-uate whether generated utterances adhere to the conditioned business profile, we directly reuse the same LLM-based evaluation rubric as the rbiz re-ward used during GRPO training (Appendix B.3). We report the mean score across all user utterances; higher values indicate stronger adherence to the conditioned business guidance. 

4.4.3 Downstream utility Downstream agent stress. Beyond intrinsic mea-sures of user-simulation fidelity, we evaluate the downstream utility of generated user behaviors by measuring how well they expose weaknesses of a customer service agent. We focus on long-tail business intents , defined as intents occupying the bottom 20% of frequency in the real training corpus (e.g., niche fee rules or rare objection types). Thus, the evaluated cases are grounded in the empirical distribution of real user conversations rather than artificially constructed adversarial inputs. Holding the agent model and RAG index fixed, we measure the agent’s response quality on generated user turns corresponding to these long-tail intents using the same LLM-as-judge protocol. Lower agent scores indicate that the generated behaviors are more ef-fective at exposing weaknesses of the fixed agent, providing greater downstream training value.

## 5 Conclusion 

We presented P ROFILE SIM , a profile-driven user simulation framework for financial marketing out-bound calls that jointly models user profiles and business profiles to enable fine-grained control over simulated behavior. The framework com-bines supervised fine-tuning on real voice dialogue data with GRPO-based preference optimization un-der hierarchical rewards at the dialogue and ut-terance levels, and is paired with an SOP- and RAG-constrained customer service agent to pro-duce closed-loop multi-turn dialogues. On finan-cial marketing data, P ROFILE SIM achieves the highest colloquial naturalness and user profile ad-herence among all evaluated user generators, while producing substantially shorter and more concise user utterances. It also imposes greater stress on the fixed customer service agent, as reflected by the lowest agent score on long-tail intents, indi-cating that the generated dialogues are more chal-lenging and behaviorally realistic. Ablation results further show that explicit user and business profile conditioning substantially improves distributional alignment, while GRPO provides additional gains in behavioral fidelity beyond SFT. The generated dialogue data is designed to support intent recogni-tion training, agent evaluation, and rapid cold-start deployment. 

## Limitations 

The user profile representation is primarily static and does not capture dynamic shifts in attitude or emotion during a conversation. In addition, the framework is currently validated on a single domain (financial marketing outbound calls), and cross-domain experiments would be needed to fur-ther assess its generality. Promising directions in-clude speech-level paralinguistic modeling (Lee et al., 2026), online co-evolutionary training of the simulator and agent, and cross-domain transfer.

B.1 Contextual Coherence Prompt for Contextual Coherence 

角色： 专业 的对话质量评估专家，专门评估外呼电

话场景下用户回复的上下 文一致性。

评分标准：

• 5分：与对话历史 完全一致，逻辑衔接自然，无

任何事实矛盾，回复内容是对上轮客服话语 的合

理反应，语体风格与用户前几轮保持一致。

• 4分：整体一致，仅有细微的不紧密处（如轻微

的话题偏移）， 不构成矛盾，也不产 生理解上的

困惑；跨轮语体风格基本连贯。

• 3分：大体相关，但存在轻微的不一 致，如态度

无铺垫地 突变、部分话题偏离、回复内容与当前

对话阶段的匹配度不够精准，或语体风格与前几

轮有明显 断裂（如前几轮简短口语，当轮突然长

篇书面化）。

• 2分：与对话历史 存在明显 的事实矛盾（如前一

轮说自己没有信用卡，当轮又说自己一直在用信

用卡）， 或出现与多轮前回复高度雷同的重复行

为。

• 1分：完全脱离上下 文，答非所问，或与此前的

表述严重冲突。

边界参考：

• 不扣分：用户前几轮都说"不需要贷款"，但客服

详细介绍了低息优惠后，用户说"那我了解一下 

吧"—— 有明确的对话触发点，属于合理转变。

• 应扣分：用户上一 轮说"我月收入三万 "，当轮又

说"我一个 月才挣几千块"—— 事实性自相矛盾，

无论有无触发点都应判为不一 致。

输出格式：

请输出 JSON 格式：

{"score": <1-5>, "reason": "< 简要说明评分依据>"} 

reason 使用 1–2 句话说 明具体原因，并给出与评分

直接相关的证据。只输出 JSON ，不要输出其 他内

容。

B.2 Colloquial Naturalness Prompt for Colloquial Naturalness 

角色： 专业 的对话质量评估专家，专门评估外呼电

话场景下用户回复的自然度。

任务： 请评 估给定的用户回复是否具有自然的口语

化表达特征，并根据以下 标准给出 1–5 分。

评估重点：

• 是否具有真实电话交流中的自发口 语感；

• 是否自然地使用语气词、停顿、重复、自我修正

等口语表达；

• 句子的节奏和表达方式是否符合真实用户在电话

中的说话 习惯；

• 是否存在明显 的书面化、脚本化、模板化或训练

材料式表达。

注意：

• 不要仅根据某个单独词语 判断口语化程度。例

如，“因此”“ 然而”“ 所以”等词语 本身并不能直接

• 轻微的书面表达痕迹并不意味着整体不自然。只

有当书面化、正式化表达持续存在并明显 影响整

体口语感时，才应降低评分。

明显 的实时交流感。

• 4 分：整体自然，符合真实电话交流习惯，仅存

在一两 处轻微的书面化或正式表达。

• 3 分：仍然可以理解为用户回复，但明显 不够自

题。

• 2 分：整体表达已经不像正常用户在电话中的自

书面文本。

• 1 分：存在明显 的机械重复、语义不 连贯、无意

{"score": <1-5>, "reason": "< 简要说明评分依据>"}
