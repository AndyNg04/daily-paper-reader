## Abstract 

Long-term personalized tutoring requires language models to translate a learner’s evolving history into appropriate instructional support. Existing LLM-based tu-toring systems use memory and retrieval to personalize responses, but the peda-gogical rationale linking historical evidence to teaching strategies is not always explicit. We introduce RegulaTutor , a theory-informed framework for long-term personalized tutoring that connects historical evidence, learner interpretation, and learner-facing instruction. Guided by self-regulated learning (SRL) research, its tutoring harness operationalizes a cognitive, affective, metacognitive, and moti-vational (CAMM) framework for deriving evidence-supported learner interpreta-tions and teaching policies. The resulting policies retain links to their support-ing evidence and are updated as new observations arrive. This staged workflow also supports systematic refinement through PairRoute , an offline procedure that uses intermediate execution records to distinguish harness, model, and mixed fail-ures. PairRoute proposes and evaluates targeted harness revisions, then constructs reviewed stage-level supervision for confirmed model failures remaining under the selected harness. We focus this refinement on historical evidence acquisi-tion, which underpins personalized instruction and allows outputs to be checked against learning records and reference answers. RegulaTutor improves historical-evidence accuracy, learner diagnosis, and judged teaching quality on average across evaluated model configurations. PairRoute results show an endpoint im-provement in standalone-model historical-evidence accuracy over the refinement trajectory. Together, these results highlight RegulaTutor ’s potential to support life-long personalized education through instructional policies that evolve with learner histories and targeted improvements to the tutoring system. 

## 1 Introduction 

Large language models (LLMs) are increasingly being developed into tutors that explain concepts, provide feedback, and personalize instruction (Zhao et al., 2026; Perczel et al., 2025). Yet learn-ing unfolds across repeated attempts and interactions: a learner’s difficulties may persist, recur, or change as understanding develops. Long-term personalized tutoring must therefore relate the current task to accumulated learner history and adapt as new observations arrive. However, recent evalua-tions highlight the difficulty of translating long-term learning histories into accurate diagnosis and appropriate instructional decisions (Li et al., 2026a). Existing LLM-based tutoring systems use memory, retrieval, and learner profiles to support history-based personalization (Zhao et al., 2026; Wu et al., 2025). However, retrieving relevant records does not by itself specify how those records should guide teaching (Cohn et al., 2026; Gures et al., 2026). Repeated errors, for example, identify a difficulty worth investigating, but do not by themselves determine whether the tutor should reteach a concept, scaffold a procedure, or prompt the learner to inspect their own reasoning. We introduce RegulaTutor , a theory-informed framework for long-term personalized tutoring that connects historical evidence, learner interpretation, and learner-facing instruction (Figure 1). Guided by self-regulated learning (SRL) research, its tutoring harness draws on cognitive, affective, metacognitive, and motivational (CAMM) processes to derive evidence-supported learner interpre-tations (Molenaar et al., 2023; Azevedo et al., 2022). These interpretations inform learner-specific teaching policies that specify the support mode, explanation depth, references to prior learning episodes, and metacognitive or motivational support. The harness translates these policies into in-structional directives while retaining links to the supporting records. Across interactions, the harness maintains learner history and updates these policies as new ob-servations become available. Its shared system prompt also encourages learner goal setting, self-checking, and reflection when appropriate. Together, these mechanisms provide an inspectable path from a learner’s accumulated history to the tutor’s instructional choices. Making these intermediate stages explicit also supports improvement of the tutoring system itself. An incorrect final response alone does not reveal whether the failure comes from the harness’s access to or representation of evidence, the model’s execution of a feasible stage, or both. Regu-laTutor records intermediate model inputs and outputs so that failures can be examined at the stages where they occur. To guide targeted improvements, the framework includes PairRoute , an offline refinement procedure building on trajectory-informed harness optimization and model–harness co-evolution (Lee et al., 2026; Chen et al., 2026a;b). PairRoute uses these execution records to diag-nose harness, model, and mixed failures, evaluate targeted harness revisions, and construct reviewed stage-level supervision for model failures that remain under the selected harness. We focus this refinement study on historical evidence acquisition, which supplies the factual basis for personalized instruction and provides targets that can be checked against learning records and reference answers (Li et al., 2026a). RegulaTutor thus supports adaptation at two timescales: new learner observations inform interpretations and teaching policies during tutoring, while execution failures guide offline refinement of the harness and model. 2Preprint We evaluate RegulaTutor on L ONG TUTOR , a benchmark for tutoring over long learning histories (Li et al., 2026a). Our contributions are threefold: • We introduce RegulaTutor , a theory-informed tutoring framework that connects historical evidence, learner interpretations, and instructional directives, while updating learner history and tutoring policies across interactions. • We develop PairRoute , an offline refinement procedure within RegulaTutor that uses in-termediate execution records to diagnose failures, guide harness revisions, and construct reviewed stage-level supervision for model failures remaining under the selected harness. • We evaluate RegulaTutor on long-history tutoring, examining evidence acquisition, diag-nosis, and teaching. A focused study with Qwen3.5-4B further examines how harness revisions and model updates affect tutoring performance. 

## 2 Method 

RegulaTutor connects historical evidence to learner interpretations and instructional decisions through cognitive, affective, metacognitive, and motivational (CAMM) processes. We further de-velop PairRoute to use execution traces for harness revision and stage-level model supervision. Fig-ure 2 summarizes both workflows. We first describe the tutoring harness, then introduce PairRoute ,which uses its execution records to guide harness revisions and stage-level model supervision. 

2.1 From Learner History to Instructional Decisions 

At interaction i with learner u, RegulaTutor uses the current task qu,i and history Lu,i to derive a learner interpretation, update tutoring policy Pu,i , and generate a response. Model weights and shared harness rules remain unchanged during tutoring; learner history and policy evolve across interactions. 

Learner history. RegulaTutor accumulates observations, dialogue, and policy state during on-going sessions, or accepts a self-contained history for retrospective tasks. From this history, it computes learner statistics Su,i and a bounded retrieval view Bu,i , separating accumulated learner information from the context supplied to individual model calls. 

Historical evidence acquisition. The Evidence Resolution Loop (ERL) identifies the information needed for qu,i . For questions about a particular attempt, it retrieves focused records; for ques-tions spanning multiple records, mapper calls select observations from history chunks for consoli-dation. Resolver calls combine the evidence into answers with source identifiers. Citation checks and bounded retrieval expansion address unresolved information needs. We write 

(Eu,i , A u,i ) = ERL M,H (Bu,i , q u,i ), (1) where M and H denote the language model and tutoring harness, respectively, Eu,i preserves source evidence, and Au,i contains per-need answers and support status. These outputs provide the evidence for the subsequent learner interpretation. 

CAMM-guided learner interpretation. We draw on self-regulated learning (SRL) and its Metacognitive and Affective Model (MASRL) to connect observations with task-specific support (Zimmerman, 2002; Efklides, 2011), using CAMM as the organizing framework (Molenaar et al., 2023). RegulaTutor constructs the current learner interpretation Cu,i from admitted evidence and statistics of the corresponding source events. In the task-verified configuration, this evidence con-sists of records cited by ERL.. Each tentative learner claim retains its supporting evidence, uncer-tainty, instructional implications, and unsupported inferences to avoid. 3Preprint

Evidence-linked teaching policies. Our guidelines map supported interpretations to policy pro-posals Au,i for support mode, explanation depth, history references, and metacognitive or motiva-tional support (Table 6 in Appendix A.2). For example, repeated errors on a concept support an interpretation of local difficulty, leading to stepwise explanation and a focused check grounded in a prior attempt. These errors do not by themselves establish low motivation. RegulaTutor updates the previous policy by screening proposals for evidence support and confidence, penalizing unnecessary switching, and resolving competing assignments to the same field. It retains policy provenance and re-examines learner interpretations as new evidence arrives. Appendix A.2 gives the update rules and representation details.

2.2 PairRoute: Trajectory-Guided Offline System Refinement 

PairRoute uses RegulaTutor’s execution records to guide updates targeting historical evidence ac-quisition. At offline round t, it operates on model-harness pair (Mt, H t) through two reviewing-agent roles: Router1 proposes and evaluates harness revisions; Router2 reviews residual failures and prepares model supervision under the selected harness. Selecting the harness before preparing supervision allows Router2 to assess failures against the inputs and requirements of the selected harness (Chen et al., 2026a;b). 4Preprint 

Trajectory-based failure diagnosis. Both router agents examine the task, reference evaluation, and actual stage inputs and outputs. A harness diagnosis requires evidence of defective access, rep-resentation, or call requirements; a model diagnosis requires sufficient visible support and a feasible local task that the model fails to execute. Mixed cases involve both. The routers separate source or evaluation problems from execution failures and defer unresolved cases. Software validates call identities and evidence pointers supporting each judgment (Appendix B.1). 

Router1: harness revision. Router1 uses training discovery trajectories to propose bounded re-visions addressing harness and mixed failures. It also examines successes to specify behavior to preserve, then evaluates candidates with Mt fixed using static checks, targeted controls, and full-task probes. These evaluations inform selection of Ht+1 under the round’s recorded protocol, which may retain Ht.

Router2: supervision preparation. Router2 examines execution records under (Mt, H t+1 ) and diagnoses failures again, because harness changes can alter the evidence available to a call and in-validate earlier failure assignments. For confirmed model failures, it localizes eligible calls, obtains teacher targets, and reviews them against the actual input, evidence, and output requirements. Only supported corrections enter training; other cases remain outside the correction set. 

Stage-level training and update evaluation. Each stage-level training example consists of a recorded model-call input and a reviewed target. We combine reviewed corrections with reviewed correct current-model outputs, including correct stages within failed tasks, and apply completion-only supervised fine-tuning to obtain Mt+1 . Under the selected harness Ht+1 , we compare Mt+1 

with its parent model Mt on evidence acquisition, diagnosis, and teaching. Appendix B.2 details training, and Appendix B.3 records the round-level update decisions. 

## 3 Experiments 

We use LongTutor to evaluate how RegulaTutor acquires historical evidence, diagnoses learner dif-ficulties, and supports instruction from long learner histories. We first compare each backbone with and without RegulaTutor, then examine the contributions of its components, and finally study Pair-Route through successive model checkpoints and their crossed evaluations with harness versions. 

3.1 Experimental Setup Benchmarks. We evaluate on L ONG TUTOR ’s 1,000-case human-annotated split, which tests his-torical evidence acquisition, learner diagnosis, and teaching using 199-record learner histories (Li et al., 2026a). Appendix C specifies the metrics and judges. 

Implementation details. We instantiate RegulaTutor with Qwen3.5-4B, DeepSeek-v4-pro, Gemini-3.7-flash, GPT-5.6-sol, Kimi-k3, and DeepSeek-v4.1-flash. All fixed-backbone compar-isons use the final harness H1 selected through PairRoute-guided refinement (Table 1). PairRoute starts from the initial Qwen3.5-4B model M0 and harness H0. Three rounds of super-vised refinement on reviewed stage-level trajectories yield model checkpoints M1–M3; the selected harness H1 is retained in later rounds, so H2 = H3 = H1. Full model identities and decoding set-tings are given in Appendix C. Data construction, training settings, and checkpoint identities appear in Appendices B.2 and B.3. 

Baselines. We compare each model and checkpoint with and without RegulaTutor. We further compare with DeepTutor 1.5.1, a tutoring-harness baseline on DeepSeek-v4-pro (Zhao et al., 2026). For GPT-5.6-sol, we also evaluate the native Codex agent as a third harness configuration. To exam-ine PairRoute, we evaluate the crossed combinations of M0–M3 with H0 and H1.

3.4 Trace-Guided Refinement and Compatibility Refinement protocol. PairRoute uses stage-level execution records to distinguish failures in ev-idence access or presentation from failures in the model’s use of available evidence. For harness-related failures, it proposes focused revisions and tests each candidate while holding the model fixed. Under the selected harness, it reviews remaining model failures and correct executions to construct stage-level training examples. We roll out the current model–harness pair on 369 discovery cases and analyze its execution tra-jectories to identify harness failures and model errors. A separate 32-case probe set screens candi-date harness revisions, while a disjoint 100-case development set checks the selected configurations across refinement rounds. Only reviewed stage-level examples from the discovery cases enter model training. Appendix B.2 details the review and training procedure. The present study asks whether this su-pervision improves historical-evidence processing and how the resulting checkpoints behave under different harness versions.

## 4 Related Work 

Intelligent Tutoring Systems Learning theory and learner modeling have long informed intelli-gent tutoring systems. MetaTutor supports metacognitive strategies through assessment and feed-back (Rus et al., 2010), while Wayang Outpost/MathSpring addresses cognition, metacognition, and affect in adaptive mathematics tutoring (Arroyo et al., 2014). More recently, CLASS explicitly con-nects learning-science principles with the design of LLM-based tutoring (Sonkar et al., 2023). These systems provide foundations for connecting observations of learner activity to instructional support. LLM tutoring frameworks extend these ideas with flexible generation, planning, and memory. Deep-Tutor combines grounded tutoring with dynamic learner memory (Zhao et al., 2026). ScaffoldLM maintains a pedagogical plan and updates tutoring memory through assessment of learner progress (Li et al., 2026b). On the model side, Dinucu-Jianu et al. (2025) train tutors with simulated interac-tions and rewards that balance pedagogical support and student problem solving. Complementing these system and model developments, LongTutor evaluates the use of learning histories across ev-idence acquisition, diagnosis, and teaching (Li et al., 2026a). Together, these directions motivate examining how historical evidence informs teaching and how the model executes the resulting sup-port. Building on these foundations, RegulaTutor connects longitudinal evidence management with theory-informed instructional decisions. Its harness combines multi-stage evidence resolution, CAMM-guided interpretation, persistent learner-specific teaching policies, and learner-facing SRL support. Within this workflow, stored observations support learner interpretations, and policy guide-lines translate those interpretations into instructional directives. Incremental session memory allows learner history and policies to be maintained across interactions. 

Model–Harness Co-Evolution A model update and a harness update modify different parts of an agent. The former changes reusable execution capabilities, whereas the latter changes the informa-tion and procedures available during execution. Their effects need not be independent. Lin et al. (2026) distinguish the ability to produce useful harness updates from the ability to benefit from an updated harness, including failures to activate or follow relevant artifacts. This distinction motivates evaluating the executing model and its interface together. HarnessForge represents an agent as a harness–policy pair and combines fault-guided harness tailor-ing with harness-conditioned policy alignment (Chen et al., 2026a). Co-Harness alternates validated local harness updates with model optimization on trajectories produced by the improved harness (Chen et al., 2026b). SIA similarly uses feedback to update both external scaffolding and model weights (Hebbar et al., 2026). Failure-guided adaptation and joint optimization are direct prece-dents for our pipeline; they motivate allocating repairs according to the component responsible for a failure and validating the resulting pair. 8Preprint Within RegulaTutor, PairRoute applies failure-guided offline refinement to historical evidence ac-quisition. It diagnoses failures before harness revision and reassesses them under the selected har-ness, checking the input scope, output requirements, and evidence available at actual stage calls. Confirmed residual model failures supply stage-level correction targets, which are combined with reviewed current-model outputs for retention. This design connects component-level attribution to supervision under the selected harness, while monitoring downstream tutoring performance. 

## 5 Discussion 

Revising learner interpretations over time. A past error remains a valid observation even af-ter a learner has overcome the difficulty, and continuing to remediate it may then be inappropriate. Long-term personalization therefore requires managing the lifetime of an interpretation separately from that of its supporting records. RegulaTutor’s evidence-linked policies make this distinction inspectable. A useful extension is to associate each interpretation with both its supporting evidence and conditions for revision, so that accumulating history enables a tutor to revise outdated interpre-tations. 

Evaluating evidence accuracy and teaching quality jointly. In the ablation batch, removing compiled directives increases Evidence while reducing judged Teaching (Table 2). This exposes a distinction between recovering historical facts and deciding how those facts should shape instruc-tion. A component’s pedagogical value can therefore be missed by an evidence-only evaluation. Future optimization could seek evidence-processing gains subject to explicit Diagnosis and Teach-ing criteria, keeping trade-offs visible when selecting model or harness updates. 

Maintaining personalization across system updates. The crossed checkpoint results show that the effects of model updates depend on the inference configuration (Table 4). In a long-term tutoring service, learner histories and policies may persist after the model or harness changes. This creates a continuity requirement: the updated system should still interpret and execute those policies appro-priately. Future compatibility tests could replay held-out learner histories together with their stored policies before and after an update, checking whether still-supported decisions are preserved in the resulting instruction. The evaluation unit would then include persistent learner state alongside the model and harness. 

## 6 Conclusion 

We presented RegulaTutor , a theory-informed framework connecting learner history, evidence-supported interpretations, and adaptive teaching policies, together with PairRoute for trace-guided offline refinement. LongTutor evaluations show average gains in evidence acquisition and diagnosis, with Teaching gains on several backbones. The refinement study shows development-set gains in evidence processing and characterizes model–harness compatibility. Together, these contributions offer a foundation for exploring lifelong personalized tutoring in which instructional support can evolve alongside learners as their knowledge, goals, and learning contexts change. 

## Limitations 

LongTutor evaluates independent history snapshots and does not measure real learners’ retention, transfer, or progress over time. Teaching scores rely on model judges, with some configurations sharing the evaluator’s model family. The refinement study uses one local backbone, three supervised rounds, and an adaptively reused development set. Crossed model–harness evaluations characterize compatibility, without budget-matched controls for alternative refinement strategies. GRPO and stage-level SFT also differ in su-pervision, objectives, budgets, and checkpoint selection, preventing attribution of score differences to the optimization method alone. Finally, the harness represents learning-related evidence through engineering heuristics; it does not provide calibrated estimates of psychological traits or establish causal effects of instructional inter-ventions. 9Preprint

Ethics statement 

Tutoring systems make inferences about learners that may be incomplete or wrong. Our design separates observed information from episode-specific interpretations and discourages unsupported claims about motivation or affect. These safeguards do not establish fairness or educational safety. Use with real learners requires appropriate data permissions, privacy protections, and review of the consequences of instructional decisions. ## A RegulaTutor Implementation Details 

A.1 History and Evidence Management 

RegulaTutor maintains a learner history Lu,i across interactions and selects evidence from it for the current task. Each learning event contains its content, timestamp, and source identifier, with concept and correctness fields when available. We support two history modes. In snapshot mode , each request supplies the complete history for an independent task, and learner state is reconstructed from that history without importing a stored policy. For example, a LongTutor case supplies past attempts and asks about a separate current ques-tion. In incremental mode , a request supplies a new observation or dialogue turn, which is combined with the history and policy already stored for the same learner and session. This supports an ongo-ing tutoring conversation without resending the entire history. Events are deduplicated and ordered chronologically, and the updated state is saved after a successful interaction. Tutor-generated dia-logue remains separate from scored learner observations, so an unscored exchange does not create a mastery label. 

Evidence acquisition. The Evidence Resolution Loop (ERL) obtains source evidence Eu,i and answers Au,i to the task’s information needs. A question about a particular attempt uses focused retrieval. A question spanning several sessions uses mapper calls to select relevant observations from history chunks, followed by consolidation and resolution. Each resolution includes supporting source identifiers. The harness checks these references and the output structure, and may expand retrieval or repeat resolution when support remains insufficient. This procedure bounds evidence acquisition while leaving interpretation and reasoning to the model. Table 5: Core runtime settings. A.2 From Learner Evidence to Teaching Decisions 

CAMM connects observed learner evidence to instructional support, drawing on SRL and MASRL (Zimmerman, 2002; Efklides, 2011; Molenaar et al., 2023). It represents cognitive and metacog-nitive interpretations separately, with motivational and affective signals sharing an implementation field. Each interpretation records its supporting observations and the limits of the inference. Table 6 summarizes how these interpretations guide teaching. 

Interpretation and policy updates. CAMM uses ERL-cited events and recomputes learner statis-tics over this admitted evidence. Repeated difficulty requires at least two incorrect outcomes on the same concept; prior-success support requires at least two scored events and accuracy of at least 0.7. Confidence values and acceptance thresholds are fixed engineering heuristics, rather than calibrated psychological probabilities. RegulaTutor converts supported interpretations into policy proposals, ranked by confidence, guide-line relevance, and evidence support. A switching cost favors continuity with the previous policy. When proposals conflict, the highest-ranked eligible assignment determines each policy field; the no-inference guard remains active even when other proposals are rejected.
