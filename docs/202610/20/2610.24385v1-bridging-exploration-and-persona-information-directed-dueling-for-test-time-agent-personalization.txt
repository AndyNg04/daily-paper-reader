ABSTRACT 

Effective test-time personalization requires LLM agents to adapt to query-dependent user preferences from lightweight interaction. We study persona selec-tion with binary comparisons between candidate responses as the online signal. We present ID-TAP, a scalable, IDS-inspired neural dueling-bandit framework for large discrete persona spaces. ID-TAP combines a query-conditioned neural util-ity model with a lightweight Bradley–Terry explorer, and transfers comparison evidence across related queries through similarity-weighted warm starts. The neu-ral model shares statistical strength across queries while preserving query-specific scores, and efficient BT updates score hypothetical challengers. Across seven user-conditioned generation benchmarks under reference- and model-based pref-erence simulators, ID-TAP achieves the strongest aggregate performance among the evaluated baselines within a fixed ten-round comparison budget. These re-sults support information-directed challenger selection as an effective design for test-time persona adaptation. 

1 INTRODUCTION 

Large language model (LLM)-based agents support tasks ranging from creative writing to coding as-sistance (Huang et al., 2023; Li et al., 2024a). Their behavior, including response style and persona, is strongly influenced by the instructions supplied at inference time. Prompt design and automatic instruction optimization improve task-level behavior (Wei et al., 2022; Zhou et al., 2022); personal-ization extends this objective toward responses suited to an individual user (Salemi et al., 2024). A natural control interface is the agent’s persona : a prompt-level description of the behavior and style the agent should adopt. Persona selection differs from task-level prompt optimization in three ways. First, personalization is contextual : the persona most useful for one request may be less suitable for another (e.g., a “creative collaborator” for brainstorming versus a “rigorous critic” for editing) (Salemi et al., 2024; Zhang et al., 2025a). Second, user preferences can be implicit and noisy : interaction histories provide partial evidence about relevant tendencies (Richardson et al., 2023). Third, candidate persona descriptions can be semantically dense . Closely related descrip-tions (e.g., “stern tutor” and “strict mentor”) may induce meaningfully different responses despite high embedding similarity. These properties motivate test-time adaptation (TTA) over a fixed persona pool. Prompt-level adaptation preserves a shared backbone while incorporating feedback about the current request. PersonaAgent (Zhang et al., 2025a) uses expressive linguistic feedback, while preference feedback 

reduces an interaction to choosing between two candidate responses, a supervision format also used in RLHF (Ouyang et al., 2022). 1arXiv:2610.24385v1 [cs.AI] 17 Oct 2026 Preprint Recent approaches such as POHF (Lin et al., 2024) and PDO (Wu et al., 2025) apply bandit explo-ration to preference-driven prompt optimization. In the persona pools evaluated here, their UCB-and Thompson-sampling-based components revisit challengers more often and reach lower aggre-gate performance than our information-directed rule (Sec. 4). Building on information-directed and dueling-bandit methods, we develop ID-TAP ( Information-Directed Dueling for Test-Time Agent Personalization), a scalable challenger-selection frame-work that uses pairwise preferences as its online signal. Its technical focus is the joint design required by black-box personalization over large persona pools: query-conditioned utility estima-tion, lightweight hypothetical BT updates, and transfer across related queries. Specifically, a query-conditioned neural utility model estimates utility gaps, while a lightweight statistical explorer evalu-ates hypothetical comparison outcomes through an IDS-inspired entropy-reduction proxy. This sep-aration supports efficient challenger scoring with one neural update per observed comparison, and similarity-weighted warm starts reuse preference evidence across queries. Under a fixed comparison budget, the resulting method attains the strongest aggregate result among the evaluated baselines. Our contributions are summarized as follows: 

Scalable Challenger Acquisition. We operationalize an IDS-inspired acquisition criterion for large discrete persona pools by combining neural utility gaps with a lightweight BT entropy-reduction proxy, enabling efficient scoring across candidate challengers. 

Cross-Query Adaptation. We introduce a similarity-weighted warm start that reuses comparison evidence across related requests while retaining query-conditioned utility prediction. 

Empirical Evaluation. Across seven benchmarks and two offline preference simulators, ID-TAP achieves the strongest aggregate result among the compared methods under the same ten-round budget. On the aggregate generation-task trajectory, it overtakes the strongest compared baseline after approximately 4 simulated-feedback rounds , suggesting favorable feedback-round efficiency in this setting.

Equation (4) evaluates two hypothetical outcomes for each candidate, yielding 2( K − 1) BT re-estimations per interaction round. We therefore decouple global utility learning from local uncertainty estimation: • System 1: Neural Utility Model ( fθ ) This system serves as the shared utility estimator. It maps queries and persona embeddings to utility scores sp and provides the gap estimates 

ˆ∆t(p) from the interaction history D accumulated across queries. It is updated once per interaction round after observing feedback. • System 2: Statistical Explorer (BT-MLE) 

This system acts as the query-local explorer. It maintains a lightweight Bradley–Terry (BT) model using comparison counts {wij , n ij }. Its minorization–maximization (MM) solver can simulate the 2( K − 1) hypothetical outcomes and recompute the normalized strength distribution efficiently. 

Pairwise Probabilities. The BT explorer applies symmetric Beta smoothing to observed pairs and assigns neutral probability Pij = 0 .5 to unobserved pairs. Appendix A gives the count updates, probability estimator, and MM solver.

4.1 EXPERIMENTAL SETUP 

Configuration. We adopt DeepSeek-V3.2 (DeepSeek-AI, 2025) as the backbone LLM for the agent and Qwen3-Embedding-4B (Zhang et al., 2025b) as our embedding model. The main experi-ments use K = 400 candidate personas per user. For each user, every experimental configuration is run independently three times, and the arithmetic mean of those runs is treated as that user’s result. Dataset-level means, standard deviations, and standard errors are computed across these per-user results. Geometric statistics are computed for each user’s persona pool before aggregation. Neval 

denotes query-level evaluation instances, whereas nuser denotes sampled users in the auxiliary sen-sitivity and runtime analyses. 

Datasets. Following PersonaAgent , we evaluate on LaMP (Salemi et al., 2024). We also include 

LongLaMP (Kumar et al., 2024), PrefEval (Zhao et al., 2025), and UltraChat (Ding et al., 2023), covering short- and long-form personalized generation and conversational settings. Dataset details and statistics are provided in Appendix B. 4.2 BASELINES AND SHARED EVALUATION PROTOCOL 

We compare ID-TAP against a generative personalization baseline and two online preference-learning baselines. 6Preprint 

Generative Baseline. PersonaAgent (Zhang et al., 2025a) performs personalization by synthesizing user-specific information into prompts. 

Online Learning Baselines. Since our setting involves iterative preference learning, we compare against two dueling-bandit acquisition rules: POHF , which uses a neural UCB-style rule, and the 

PDO sampling component , i.e., Double Thompson Sampling (D-TS) (Wu & Liu, 2016; Wu et al., 2025). Full PDO combines sampling with prompt mutation and pruning. We instantiate its D-TS sampling component on the shared fixed persona pool, yielding a matched comparison of pair selection under the same search space and feedback budget. 

Shared Evaluation Protocol. PersonaAgent follows its reported configuration. All bandit methods share the same candidate personas, response generator, binary-feedback interface, and ten-round budget for each query. Within this common protocol, PDO Sampling represents its acquisition component and ID-TAP represents the proposed acquisition design. 4.3 PREFERENCE FEEDBACK MECHANISMS 

ID-TAP’s online update consumes one binary outcome for each displayed response pair. In deploy-ment, the user supplies this outcome after viewing the pair. For repeatable offline experiments, the held-out target instantiates a proxy user that returns the same type of outcome after each algorithm-selected comparison. Following POHF’s offline interaction protocol (Lin et al., 2024), persona construction, response generation, and arm selection use the history, query, and observed binary outcomes; after pair selection, the proxy user uses the held-out target to produce the next outcome. This protocol reproduces test-time information flow while standardizing the preference source across methods for T = 10 rounds. For final W.R. evaluation, each method’s final response is paired with a naive response : an answer generated by the same backbone directly from the query and user history or context, without per-sona selection or test-time preference optimization. The pair order is randomized before evaluation, and W.R. is the percentage of comparisons in which the method response is preferred to the naive response. 

ROUGE-L-Based Simulation. For each candidate response rqt,i , the proxy user computes the reference-grounded utility sqt,i = ROUGE -L( rqt,i , r qgt ). Following the offline preference-simulation protocol of POHF (Lin et al., 2024), we sample the binary outcome using the Bradley–Terry–Luce model: 

P (rqt, 1 ≻ rqt, 2) = exp( sqt, 1)exp( sqt, 1) + exp( sqt, 2) . (6) 

Reference-Grounded Holistic Preference Proxy. An LLM judge receives the query, a user-specific reference, and an anonymized response pair. For every comparison, the two responses are randomly assigned to the Response 1 and Response 2 positions, and the judge’s selection is mapped back to the corresponding personas. The judge selects the response that better realizes the intended output as a whole, jointly considering task fulfillment, semantic intent, organization, coherence, and the tone and style expressed by the reference. The reference supplies a task- and user-conditioned example, and the judge balances semantic realization with organization and style. We refer to this signal as a reference-grounded holistic preference proxy. In both settings, the optimizer’s adaptation signal is the resulting binary comparison, while the proxy user retains the reference and its derived score. 4.4 MAIN RESULTS AND ANALYSIS

5 RELATED WORK 

Personalized LLM Agents. Personalized LLMs condition outputs on individual preferences, traits, or context (Li et al., 2024b; Zhu et al., 2024). Retrieval-based methods expose relevant user history at inference time (Richardson et al., 2023; Zhong et al., 2024), while memory and prompt-based approaches maintain or synthesize user-specific context (Zhang et al., 2025a; Liu et al., 2024). LaMP standardizes evaluation across personalized language tasks (Salemi et al., 2024). Recent work increasingly studies lightweight adaptation at inference time (Li et al., 2024b; Qu et al., 2025); ID-TAP contributes a pairwise-feedback method for selecting among fixed persona prompts. 

Feedback Mechanisms and Test-Time Adaptation. Aligning LLM behaviors with user intent relies heavily on feedback-driven optimization. The canonical paradigm, Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF) (Christiano et al., 2017; Ziegler et al., 2019), typically employs supervised fine-tuning, reward modeling (Stiennon et al., 2020), and policy optimization via PPO (Schulman et al., 2017; Ouyang et al., 2022). Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) (Rafailov et al., 2023) and RLAIF (Bai et al., 2022) streamline this process through direct preference learning or AI supervision. Test-Time Adaptation (TTA) further enables online refinement through iterative feedback loops (Madaan et al., 2023; Shinn et al., 2023); reasoning systems use self-correction or tree search (Yao et al., 2023; Ouyang et al., 2025), while personalized prompt- and decoding-level methods adapt behavior with fixed backbone LLM weights (Lin et al., 2024; Qu et al., 2025). 

Exploration in Dueling Bandits. Dueling bandits learn from pairwise preferences (Yue et al., 2012), and contextual variants allow preferences to depend on the query (Dud´ ık et al., 2015; Bengs et al., 2022). Relevant analyses cover structured stochastic preferences and neural utility mod-els (Bengs et al., 2022; Verma et al., 2025); confidence-based linear bandit methods provide ad-ditional background for contextual exploration (Abbasi-Yadkori et al., 2011). Practical strategies include Thompson sampling (Daniel et al., 2018), Double Thompson Sampling (D-TS) (Wu & Liu, 2016), and posterior sampling for LLM exploration (Dwaracherla et al., 2024). For LLM prompt optimization, POHF (Lin et al., 2024) uses neural UCB-style acquisition, and PDO (Wu et al., 2025) combines D-TS with top-performer-guided mutation and pruning. Our PDO Sampling baseline in-stantiates D-TS in the common fixed persona pool and feedback budget. T-POP (Qu et al., 2025) operates during token-level decoding with white-box model access and addresses a complementary setting. IDS balances estimated utility gaps against information gain (Russo & Van Roy, 2018) and has been studied in linear partial monitoring and dueling-feedback settings (Kirschner et al., 2020; Kirschner & Krause, 2021; P´ asztor et al., 2024). These works provide general information-directed objectives or analyses under structured models. ID-TAP builds on these foundations and focuses on the systems question posed by black-box LLM personalization: scoring a large, LLM-generated persona pool, separating query-conditioned neural utility learning from lightweight hypothetical BT updates, and transferring comparison evidence across related queries. The resulting contribution is a scalable, cross-query acquisition architecture for persona-level test-time adaptation. 

6 CONCLUSION 

We presented ID-TAP , an IDS-inspired framework for test-time persona selection. It combines query-conditioned neural utility gaps, a lightweight BT entropy-reduction proxy, and similarity-weighted warm starts across related queries. Across seven user-conditioned tasks with automated proxy feedback, ID-TAP achieves the highest aggregate mean among the evaluated baselines under a fixed comparison budget. B.1 DATASET 

LaMP LaMP evaluates personalization from limited user history across several language tasks. Geometric Structure. K-means silhouette scores at K=20 range from 0.097 (UltraChat) to 0.125 (LaMP-5), indicating weak separation into discrete clusters. Together with the similarity statistics, these scores characterize the candidate pools as dense and weakly clustered; the acquisition experi-ments measure the corresponding method-level behavior.
