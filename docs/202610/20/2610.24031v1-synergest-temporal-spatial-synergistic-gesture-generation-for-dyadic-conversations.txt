to either generic mean-pose regression or unnatural micro-jitters.

Introduction
Nonverbal communication encompassing facial expressions, body
language, and hand gestures is integral to natural human inter-
action, particularly in dyadic (two-person) conversations, where
it enriches the exchange with crucial contextual cues and mutual
emotional depth [28]. Endowing digital avatars with the holistic
capabilities required for natural dyadic interaction has become a
cornerstone of state-of-the-art research [2, 30]. While audio-driven
animation has advanced, mapping acoustic signals directly to para-
metric body models frequently produces kinematically constrained
and uncanny results, lacking the non-verbal subtleties essential
for realistic interaction. Co-speech motion combines continuously
occurring, rhythm-related movement driven by speech cadence [5]
with sparse, semantics-aware gestures [24, 50, 51] that are infre-
quent but critical for conveying specific intent. In dyadic conver-
sation, ensuring the precise semantic alignment of these sparse
gestures is even more demanding than in monologues, as they must
continuously resonate with the conversational context. The listener
is not merely a passive recipient but an active participant who
shapes the narrative flow through continuous non-verbal feedback.
Learning an effective correspondence from latent acoustic features
to this high-dimensional, spatiotemporally coherent interaction
manifold remains a fundamental bottleneck in current generative
frameworks.
Current research remains constrained by significant limitations
regarding temporal consistency and spatial resolution. Broadly, the
vast majority of holistic motion generation frameworks [8, 23, 25,
45] are monadic. They generate one person’s motion without ex-
plicitly conditioning on the behavior or speech of a conversational
partner, thereby omitting the joint dynamics of human communica-
tion. Authentic dyadic conversation constitutes a dynamic feedback
loop, where communication is a jointly-constructed process. The
listener’s reactive behaviors, ranging from subtle postural shifts to
affective facial cues, are integral components of the spatiotemporal
interaction manifold. Authentic communication requires the digital
avatar to not only project its own intent but also continuously adapt
to the evolving behavioral cues of its partner. Monadic approaches
fundamentally struggle to capture this cross-modal responsiveness.
To bridge this gap, research has shifted toward dyadic embodi-
ment, catalyzed by pioneering frameworks and large-scale datasets
such as Audio2Photoreal [30] and Seamless [2]. At a technical level,
Audio2Photoreal combines autoregressive guide-pose prediction
with diffusion-based detail synthesis, whereas Seamless employs a
Diffusion Transformer trained with a flow-matching objective and
multi-step ODE inference. These multi-stage or iterative pipelines
impose substantial computational overhead. Recent monadic work
such as GestureLSM [26] adopts Flow Matching (FM) [20] for ef-
ficiency but relies on a standard conditional FM objective. This
objective learns from matched condition-trajectory pairs without
explicitly contrasting condition mismatches, which can limit sensi-
tivity to ambiguous conversational audio. Additionally, across both
diffusion and FM paradigms, sequence modeling heavily relies on
standard Spatial-Temporal Transformers [3, 43]. This introduces a
computational bottleneck because full self-attention has quadratic
complexity in sequence length [43]. Conventional attention also
does not explicitly distinguish the frequency characteristics of high-
frequency, speech-driven transients and low-frequency kinematic
trajectories, motivating a frequency-aware token mixer.
To address these multifaceted challenges, we propose SynerGest,
a novel framework for dyadic photoreal embodiment (Fig. 1). Specif-
ically, to tackle the tendency of vanilla FM to produce semantically
under-constrained trajectories under ambiguous audio, we adapt
Contrastive Flow Matching (CFM) to dual-audio dyadic con-
ditioning. Building upon foundational FM [20], Contrastive Flow
Matching [40], and its recent use in monadic co-speech gesture
generation [25], our objective compares matched and shuffled au-
dio conditions at the same latent state and maximizes the distance
between their predicted velocity fields. This formulation encour-
ages condition-sensitive trajectories and reduces the tendency to
produce generic, over-smoothed gestures under ambiguous con-
versational contexts while preserving the efficiency of ODE-based
generation.
Second, to overcome the semantic entanglement of standard
Transformers, we propose the Impulse-Trend Synergistic At-
tention (ITSA) mechanism. By replacing quadratic self-attention
with pooling-based gating streams, the front-end of ITSA explicitly
decouples motion into an Impulse Temporal Gating stream (“when
it moves”) and a Trend Feature Gating stream (“what moves”). Cru-
cially, rendering kinematically plausible motion requires more than
local spatiotemporal decoupling; the generated motion must inher-
ently align with the complex spatiotemporal distribution of natural
human motion. To achieve this, the ITSA architecture intrinsically
incorporates a standard Multi-Head Attention (MHA) layer as its
final global refinement stage. While the dual-gating streams pro-
vide efficient local feature disentanglement, this embedded MHA
captures the global contextual dependencies required for long-term
trajectory planning. This unified hybrid design reduces the ten-
dency of the downstream generator to produce out-of-distribution
jitter, supporting the kinematic realism and global stability of the
synthesized conversational gestures.
Our contributions are as follows:
• From Isolated to Interaction-Aware Synthesis. We in-
troduce a dyadic-conditioned framework for conversational
motion generation. Rather than focusing on isolated, unilat-
eral generation driven by a single audio source, our model
explicitly leverages dual-audio context to synthesize highly
responsive behaviors.

Related Work
2.1
From Monadic to Dyadic Co-Speech
Generation
The synthesis of animated characters has progressed from speech-
driven 2D lip synchronization [34] and portrait-avatar animation [44]
to expressive 3D facial and full-body motion. Early data-driven
body-language systems relied on prosody-conditioned Hidden Markov
Models [19], which limited the expressiveness and naturalness of
synthesized motion [31]. Subsequent facial-animation methods use
autoregressive Transformers [13], neural radiance fields [16], or ex-
plicit 3D motion coefficients [49]. For body motion, Text2Gestures
generates emotive gestures from natural-language descriptions [7],
while speech-driven normalizing flows model diverse gesticula-
tion from acoustic input [5]. Supported by large-scale multimodal
motion-capture datasets such as BEAT [21], a growing body of
work targets co-speech gesture generation from multimodal con-
texts [8, 14, 18, 23, 35, 45, 46]. GestureLSM [26] further applies
Flow Matching in a quantized latent space for real-time genera-
tion. Despite this progress, these methods remain monadic and do
not explicitly capture the mutual observations that define dyadic
interaction.
Dyadic embodiment has been significantly advanced by inte-
grated benchmarks such as Audio2Photoreal [30], which provides
both a dataset and a framework, and Seamless [2]. Related work
also models non-deterministic listener facial motion and non-verbal
feedback [29]. Audio2Photoreal combines autoregressive guide-
pose prediction with diffusion-based detail synthesis, whereas Seam-
less uses a flow-matching-trained Diffusion Transformer with multi-
step ODE inference. We reduce the resulting iterative cost with an
eight-step flow-matching backbone tailored to dyadic generation.
2.2
Generative Models and Spatial-Temporal
Modeling
Recent continuous generative modeling includes Denoising Diffu-
sion Probabilistic Models (DDPMs) [17] and score-based stochastic
differential equation models [39]. Motion models such as MDM
and MotionDiffuse demonstrate diverse text-conditioned motion
generation [41, 48]. Their sampling is iterative, however, and MDM
reports roughly 1,000 network evaluations for a generated sam-
ple. Consequently, Flow Matching (FM) and related rectified-flow
formulations [20, 27] have emerged as efficient alternatives. They
train continuous velocity fields without simulating the learned ODE
during training and can map a simple prior to the data distribu-
tion through an ODE [4]. Nevertheless, standard conditional FM
objectives learn from matched condition-trajectory pairs and lack
an explicit mechanism for contrasting condition mismatches. This
can weaken conditional separation when conversational audio is
ambiguous.
Furthermore, across both diffusion and FM paradigms, sequence
modeling typically relies on standard Transformers [13, 45, 47].
For dense 30 fps motion generation, modeling extended conver-
sational contexts creates severe architectural bottlenecks because
full self-attention grows quadratically with sequence length [43].
While efficient sequence models such as linear attention [10] and
state-space models [15] mitigate computational cost, they do not
explicitly distinguish smooth kinematic trends from sharp rhythmic
transients. To address this representational limitation, we augment
global Multi-Head Attention (MHA) with lightweight pooling-based
dual-gating streams that model these frequency characteristics
separately. The gating streams add little overhead, while MHA is
retained for global refinement, as detailed in Sec. 3.4.
2.3
Cross-Modal Alignment and Contrastive
Learning
Precise temporal alignment between speech and facial motion is
central to talking-face synthesis [11, 33], and analogous alignment
is required between speech and body motion in gesture gener-
ation. Because speech-to-gesture mapping is inherently one-to-
many, regression-based objectives can favor averaged motions and
yield over-smoothed results [31]. Recent works emphasize sepa-
rating periodic rhythm from semantic intent to combat this over-
smoothing [6, 22, 50, 51]. Nevertheless, these methods primarily
introduce representation or architectural mechanisms, whereas we
modify the conditional flow objective itself to distinguish matched
and mismatched conversational conditions.
In representation learning, Contrastive Learning [9, 36, 42] is
recognized for building robust, discriminative feature spaces. Its
efficacy extends to audio-language [12] and audio-visual represen-
tation learning [1, 37]. Contrastive Flow Matching separates condi-
tional flows by introducing negative trajectories [40], and SemCon-
Flow applies this principle to monadic holistic gesture generation
using audio-text conditions and mismatched target motions [25].
Our dyadic variant instead conditions on the two conversational
audio streams and compares predicted velocities under matched
and shuffled audio conditions at the same latent state. The result-
ing Audio-Motion Contrastive Loss encourages the ODE trajectory
to remain sensitive to the correct conversational context while
preserving kinematic stability.

3.2
Conditional Feature Generation and Fusion
As illustrated in our overall architecture, the conditional pipeline
follows a structured progression from raw unimodal extraction to
multimodal interactive alignment. First, the raw audio waveforms
of the target listener (a𝑙𝑠𝑛) and the speaker (a𝑠𝑝𝑘) are independently
routed through the frozen AudioRhythm Encoder. These embed-
dings are concatenated to jointly encode the dyadic conversational
context, forming the base acoustic feature F𝑎. Crucially, to accom-
modate their distinct kinetic properties, our framework adopts a
decoupled extraction strategy for body pose and facial expressions.
For body dynamics, a Guide Transformer [30] autoregressively
predicts discrete RVQ tokens from the audio stream, which are
decoded to provide 1 fps initial pose keyframes P as a structural
physical prior. Conversely, initial lip vertex features L are extracted
via a pretrained lip-vertex regressor. These two modality-specific
priors feed into independent generation pipelines: the PoseOnly
generator for body dynamics and the FaceOnly generator for facial
expressions.
These independently extracted preliminary features (P and L) are
subsequently aligned with the dyadic acoustic feature F𝑎within the
Condition Fusion block (Fig. 2c). Specifically, for body dynamics, an
Audio-Pose Cross-Alignment mechanism treats the acoustic feature
F𝑎as the Query and the guide poses P as the Key and Value. Through
this cross-attention operation, we extract the aligned pose seman-
tic feature P′. Concurrently, for facial expressions, an Audio-Lip
Alignment pathway concatenates the acoustic feature F𝑎with the
lip vertex features L to produce the enhanced lip representation L′.
Ultimately, for each independent pipeline, these fused representa-
tions (P′ or L′) are processed through a dedicated lightweight MLP.
The outputs of the MLP serve as the Key and Value for the subse-
quent cross-attention layers within their respective flow estimator
backbones, ensuring dense semantic synchronization between the
modality-specific priors and the generated trajectories.

primary backbone of Fig. 2b, our network effectively integrates the
conditional features into the state trajectory x𝑡through a structured

3.4
Impulse-Trend Synergistic Attention (ITSA)
In dyadic conversations, human gestures physically decouple into
two distinct properties: smooth, long-term kinematic trends and
sparse, instantaneous rhythmic impulses driven by speech beats.
However, relying purely on standard spatial-temporal Transform-
ers incurs prohibitive computational overhead and causes severe
semantic entanglement and over-smoothing.

propose the Impulse-Trend Synergistic Attention (ITSA). As illus-
trated in Fig. 3, ITSA adds two lightweight, complementary 1D-

4
Experiment
4.1
Experimental Settings
Datasets. We evaluate the proposed SynerGest framework on
two extensive dyadic interaction datasets: the Audio2Photoreal
dataset [30] and Seamless Interaction [2]. Audio2Photoreal offers
high-fidelity, studio-captured dyadic conversations paired with pho-
torealistic Codec Avatar assets. The acoustic input comprises 48
kHz dual-channel audio, explicitly capturing the independent wave-
forms of both the speaker and the listener. Additionally, we utilize
the Naturalistic subset of the Seamless Interaction dataset, which
encompasses unscripted, spontaneous recordings. For Seamless,
the release provides a single-channel track for each participant.
Using the interaction-partner mapping, we pair the target and part-
ner tracks, resample them to 48 kHz, align them to their common
duration, and stack them as a dual-channel input.
Implementation Details. Our models are trained on a single
NVIDIA RTX 4090 GPU. During inference, trajectory integration
requires merely 8 ODE sampling steps.
Evaluation Metrics. We adopt four metrics to comprehensively
evaluate our framework: Fréchet Gesture Distance (FGD ↓) for
kinematic realism, Beat Consistency (BC ↑) for audio-motion syn-
chronization, Diversity (Div ↑) for motion variance, and Inference
Efficiency (Tinf ↓), defined as the average wall-clock inference time
per sample.
Baselines. To rigorously demonstrate the superiority of our
framework, we compare SynerGest against four recent state-of-the-
art co-speech gesture generation methods. Specifically, we evaluate
against three advanced monadic frameworks—EMAGE [23], Ges-
tureLSM [26], and TalkSHOW [45]—alongside one dedicated dyadic
baseline, the Audio2Photoreal (A2P) model [30]. To ensure a fair
comparison, all monadic baselines are adapted to our dyadic evalu-
ation protocol under identical data processing conditions.

4.4
Ablation Studies
To verify the effectiveness of our proposed modules, we perform
experiments on the A2P and Seamless Interaction benchmarks.
Following a subtractive ablation protocol, we begin with the full
configuration and progressively disable specific components to
analyze their necessity. Table 2 shows the performance changes
across four key dimensions.
Flow Matching vs. Diffusion. We replace our Flow Matching
backbone with a Denoising Diffusion Probabilistic Model (DDPM)
utilizing the DDIM [38] sampling strategy. To provide a rigorous
and competitive baseline, the diffusion variant is evaluated with
100 sampling steps (DDIM-100), whereas SynerGest requires only 8
ODE steps. As shown in Table 2, despite the 12.5× higher sampling
budget allocated to the diffusion baseline, it still suffers a signifi-
cant degradation in FGD, worsening from 0.741 to 2.170 on A2P,
and exhibits poor beat synchronization (BC: 0.639). On the Seam-
less dataset, its Diversity score drops to 7.026, indicating reduced
coverage of the motion distribution under this experimental setting.

separate sharp speech impulses from smooth motion trends, caus-
ing the network to translate acoustic noise into unnatural struc-
tural jitter, which artificially inflates the Diversity metric without
adding semantically meaningful variation. ITSA explicitly decou-
ples rhythm and trends to preserve physical responsiveness and
stability.
5
Conclusion
In this paper, we present SynerGest, a novel generative frame-
work advancing holistic co-speech gesture synthesis from isolated
monadic generation to dyadic interactive embodiment. To improve
the conditional sensitivity of vanilla Flow Matching under ambigu-
ous conversational audio, we adapt Contrastive Flow Matching
(CFM) to dual-audio conditioning. By explicitly maximizing the 𝐿2
distance between velocity predictions under matched and shuffled
conversational contexts, CFM improves conditional separation and
reduces the tendency to generate generic, over-smoothed gestures.
Furthermore, we propose the Impulse-Trend Synergistic Attention
(ITSA) module to model continuous kinematic trends and sparse,
speech-driven rhythmic impulses via complementary dual-gating
streams and an adaptive soft-mixture mechanism. Extensive evalu-
ations on Audio2Photoreal and Seamless Interaction demonstrate
that SynerGest achieves state-of-the-art performance in both kine-
matic realism and rhythmic synchronization under our protocol. By
reconciling rhythmic impulses with kinematic stability, SynerGest
provides a viable framework for modeling the reactive behavioral
dynamics of human conversation.
