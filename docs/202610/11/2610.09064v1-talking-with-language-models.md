## 摘要
我们与大型语言模型（LLM）互动时，是否是在进行对话？它们的设计旨在引导我们将其视为能够记忆、行动并作出承诺的智能对话者。然而，表象并不可靠。我们提出“人工制品立场”（artifactual stance）这一框架，将人机互动重新理解为由人工制品中介、围绕候选文本展开的交流。LLM 的输出是经过优化以提升效用的候选文本，而非承载意义或言语效力的话语。LLM 是复杂的文本生成器，而不是说话者。会话之间，没有任何程序在运行；轮次之间，也没有任何主体在记忆。持续存在的只是配置和对话记录。所谓“对话”，其实是用户独自进行的表演；这种诠释劳动被界面与人工制品的设计所掩饰。这一视角转变消解了近期的一些哲学难题。关于 AI 交流中“我”与“你”指什么、系统是否能够说谎或应否为承诺负责，以及我们所设想的对话者具有何种同一性等问题，都建立在错误的预设之上。屏幕后面并没有说话者，因此没有可供指称的主体，也没有可追责的对象。那种仿佛在与某人对话的体验，实则是我们在与一种能够以前所未有的规模和适配度生成文本的人工制品互动。摒弃对话框架后，我们便能看清这些系统的本来面目：它们是极其复杂的人工制品，可供人们以多种方式使用。真正重要的哲学问题，关乎设计、采纳、授权以及人类使用实践的规范性基础。

## Abstract
When we interact with large language models (LLMs), are we having a conversation? They are designed to invite us to treat them as intelligent interlocutors who remember, act, and make commitments. But appearances deceive. We introduce the artifactual stance, a framework that reconceives human-AI interaction as artifact-mediated exchanges of candidate texts. LLM outputs are candidate texts optimized for utility, not utterances bearing meaning or force. LLMs are sophisticated text generators, not speakers. Between sessions, nothing runs; between turns, no one remembers. What persists is a configuration and a transcript. The "conversation" is a user's solo performance, interpretive labour disguised by interface and artifact design. This shift dissolves recent philosophical puzzles. Questions about what 'I' and 'you' refer to in AI exchanges, about whether systems can lie or be held to promises, about the identity of our supposed interlocutors all rest on a false presupposition. There is no speaker behind the screen, hence no one to refer to, no one to hold responsible. What feels like dialogue with someone is interaction with an artifact that generates text at unprecedented scale and fit. By abandoning the conversational framing, we see these systems for what they are: immensely sophisticated artifacts that afford varied uses. The philosophical questions that matter are about the normative underpinnings of design, adoption, authorization, and human practices of use.
