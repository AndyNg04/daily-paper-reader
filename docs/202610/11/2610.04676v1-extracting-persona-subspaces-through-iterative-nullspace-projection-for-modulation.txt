ABSTRACT 

Large Language Models (LLMs) can adopt distinct personas to tune their seman-tics, expertise, and perspective to different users and tasks. Precise control over these traits is critical to ensure safety and reliability in model behavior. Exist-ing methods like activation steering and prompt-based persona induction reduce a persona to a single dominant direction, missing the finer, nested traits that emerge only once that dominant signal is factored out. We introduce modulation as a setting where the persona context is already embedded in the content being ma-nipulated, requiring control methods to amplify or suppress a trait already present rather than inject it from scratch. PaSS is an inference-time control paradigm that models personas as multi-dimensional subspaces in a model’s latent space without supervised contrastive examples. The persona subspaces are extracted via itera-tive concept erasure and applied to modulate persona-guided generation without retraining. To extract this subspace, we use Iterative Nullspace Projections (INLP) to linearly and iteratively isolate persona-specific directions. We causally evalu-ate six personas against diverse tasks like MATH-500, TinyAlpaca, GSM8K, and IFEval, showing that discriminative, iterative subspace extraction captures diverse traits underlying a given persona, enabling stronger and larger modulation than single-direction additive methods, while maintaining content fidelity. We further study individual peeled directions within each subspace to uncover the distinct as-pects of persona behavior they encode. Overall, we show that persona subspaces offer a controllable, interpretable, and generalizable framework for modulating LLM behavior without sacrificing task performance. 

1 INTRODUCTION 

Persona is derived from the Latin word for the masks worn by Roman artists to convey varied emo-tions by amplifying or suppressing certain traits, overlaying the actor’s inherent modulated charac-teristics (Mayer, 2008). Large Language Models (LLMs), like this base actor, are general-purpose language generators (Qin et al., 2023), where such persona-like traits can be introduced as behavioral constraints to keep the model safe and aligned to the user’s intent (Xie et al., 2025; Li et al., 2026b), even under unsafe and unrelated contexts. To capture the complexity of the user’s characteristics, preferences, and task-specific context, personas are used as a compressed unity of user informa-tion (Li et al., 2026a), distilled from the user’s traits and intentions. When effectively “layered” over the model’s latent capabilities, they can modulate the target behavior in the model. Conventionally, personas are represented as a natural language instructions in the model’s system or user (Samuel et al., 2025), spanning psychological traits (Pan & Zeng, 2023; La Cava & Tagarelli, 2025), demographic information (Cheng et al., 2023; Malik et al., 2025a) or preferences (Balepur et al., 2025). However, these descriptions are ephemeral (Laban et al., 2025), may drift over long-context and may conflict with the model’s internal knowledge (Xu et al., 2024), leading to a lack of controllability and task inconsistency (Shen et al., 2025). To improve controllability, prior work instead manipulates the model’s internal representations by introducing persona-aligned directions in its activation states (Geiger et al., 2025), attention pat-terns (Zhang et al., 2023; Turner et al., 2023), or KV Cache (Belitsky et al., 2025; Liu et al., 2026). The extraction of the persona-vector collapses many contrastive directions onto a few high-variance axes, capturing dominant behavior but risking conflation with dataset-specific features (Korznikov et al., 2025) discarding other trait-relevant components that add to the persona signal’s diversity. Since the extraction represents the expected behavior from the supervised samples, and not the broader representation of persona, it remains entangled with its source dataset (Wang et al., 2025), causing it to fail upon transfer to new domains or prompt formats (Tan et al., 2024). Beyond extraction and diversity, to enhance controllability, persona methods must also be able to amplify or suppress the persona presence when the persona is already present in the context. We call this task modulation , where the persona presence is modulated relative to how strongly it is currently placed in context, where a weakly implied persona should be steerable with a small nudge, and a strongly applied persona would require a bigger shift. Since additive methods inject fixed increments that are independent of the scale of the persona signal already present, they may be unable to adapt to the signal, restricting modulation capabilities. However, personas are multifaceted constructs, with different layers of behaviors for different tasks (Sheldon et al., 2011), which, when modulated, must be able to amplify and suppress the traits within the model’s context. Thus, to provide more diversity and generalizability within a model’s representation, we model personas as a latent space of constraints that can effectively modulate residual streams along persona-aligned trajectories. We propose PaSS (Persona as a Sub space), an iterative extraction and inference-time modulation method that allows stabilized amplification and suppression of existing behavior. PaSS uses iterative concept isolation of persona from the model’s general stream and layers it as geometric constraints onto the model’s activations (Fig 1), allowing it to capture the persona’s diversity layer-by-layer. In summary, our contributions are as follows: 1. Extraction of the Persona Subspace : We model personas as multi-dimensional subspaces extracted via iterative nullspace projections, generalizing to linear & non-linear probes. 2. Modulation as a Task : We formalize modulation of a persona already present in context as a task, and benchmark it across 6 personas, 4 datasets, and 5 degrees of modulation. 3. Universality within a Model : We show the extracted subspaces generalize across Math, General Knowledge, Task Following, and Adversarial domains within a single model. 2Preprint. Under review. 4. Generalization to Isolation : We show these subspaces generalize to different prompt for-mulations and to modulation in multi-instruction settings such as IFEval. 5. Structure of Persona Subspace : We analyze the nested structure of the persona subspace, both behaviorally and mechanistically, for emoji and refusal personas. 

2 RELATED WORKS 

Personas are defined as a portrait of a group of users focusing on more general features (Zhang et al., 2024) that inform the model of traits and behaviors unique to the group. Personas have been used for a wide range of applications, from personalization(Tseng et al., 2024) to evaluations and mitigation of fairness capabilities, stereotypes, and biases (Malik et al., 2025a; Tan & Lee, 2025; Kamruzzaman et al., 2024; Kamruzzaman & Kim, 2025; Chen et al., 2026). In AI Safety, they are used in understanding sycophantic behavior (Ibrahim et al., 2025), measuring vulnerabilities (Collu et al., 2023; Xu et al., 2025), improving red-teaming capabilities (Deng et al., 2025), and stimulating attacker policies (Zhang et al., 2025). Additionally, these personas are diversely represented across dimensions such as socio-demography (Gupta et al., 2023; Sun et al., 2025), psychology (Pan & Zeng, 2023; Tu et al., 2023), and behavioral tendencies like hallucination (Kose & Uysal, 2025), truthfulness (Joshi et al., 2024), and refusal patterns (Plaza-del Arco et al., 2025). They are in-troduced within the model using prompting (Lutz et al., 2025; De Araujo et al., 2025), in-context learning (Choi & Li, 2024), fine-tuning (Song et al., 2020), and reinforcement learning (Shea & Yu, 2023; Ryan et al., 2025). In this work, we focus on stylistic, identity, and behavioral personas. 

Activation Steering and Latent Geometry. Recent work has leveraged activation steering as a primary mechanism to elicit specific behaviors. Linear Representation Hypothesis (Elhage et al., 2022; Olah et al., 2020) proposes the organization of concepts with the structured latent space of neural networks and LLMs. Additionally, researchers have discovered that stylistic, semantic, and latent properties can be linearly encoded within the model’s latent space (Burns et al., 2022; Gurnee & Tegmark, 2023; Park et al., 2024). Specifically, emotions (Reichman et al., 2025), truth-fulness (Marks & Tegmark, 2023), hallucination (O’Neill et al., 2025), gender bias (Marks et al., 2024), and sycophancy (Vennemeyer et al., 2025) can be extracted from the model’s latent space. Identifying steering functions has enabled a growing body of work to control and introduce persona within models (Zou et al., 2023a; Rimsky et al., 2024; Chen et al., 2025). Beyond vector control, subspace-based behavior modeling like Vu & Nguyen (2025) has recently been explored through rotation of persona direction across layers rather than residual stream manipulation. Further, con-current work in subspace-based behavior modeling (Rocchetti & Ferrara, 2026) has also explored INLP-based refusal steering but not in the context of a generalized framework for modulation.

An autoregressive language model fθ with parameters θ can embed high-level concepts within its activation space (Templeton, 2024; Saglam et al., 2025) that are extractable (Lizzo & Heck, 2025). 6 DISCUSSION 

In this work, we introduce modulation as a special case of steering where the persona context is already embedded in the content being manipulated, thus, reframing controllability as a problem of scaling a trait rather than introducing it into the residual stream. This mirrors realistic deployment settings, where personas are rarely absent but already carry through the system prompt or user history. In light of this, we model personas as subspaces, extracted iteratively through nullspace projections of linearly identified directions in a model’s representation space, without relying on supervised behavioral examples. Further, modulation extends beyond persona control to mitigating harmful biases, as demonstrated in our debiasing case study (Appendix E). We peel and analyze these behavioral subspaces and observe a nested structure within these sub-spaces, where persona-aligned directions behave in a meaningful order. As we see, refusal suppres-sion concentrates early for relatively benign prompts and, however, requires a much larger subset for physical and self-harm categories. On the other hand, emoji persona stabilizes early but continues to build over the previous directions, suggesting that a persona is a composite of unevenly distributed sub-behaviors rather than a monolithic trait. Decomposing persona as a subspace allows for adaptive modulation, which, when compared against additive baselines, provides larger and stable shifts in persona expression, without decaying on the content loss. We see this helps generalize this iterative extraction across different probing methods, across domains like math, general-knowledge, and adversarial tasks, and in multiple-instruction settings. These findings help motivate that subspaces promote more depth and controllability. In its current setting, PaSS scales persona expression linearly along the extracted subspace, and a future direction would be to explore angular rotations as well, which could allow finer control over which specific directions are active rather than scaling all of them together. Further, it would be interesting to extend this study to implicit long-context and multiple active personas interacting when steered at once. 

Limitations. Our study is conducted on only two instruction-tuned models in the 7 − 8B size. Our persona set, ranging across 6 personas, is also much more sanitized and only covers short-context and explicit personas, and does not touch upon long-context or implicit personas. Our evaluation leans heavily on LLM-as-judge for validating persona presence and content fidelity. We do validate this against human judgment and adopt strict scoring guidelines; however, we acknowledge that some implicit features of a persona may be missed. Additionally, as seen in the independence test, with INLP, we don’t achieve exact separation, where a subspace extracted linearly can still recover persona-identity from the content subspace using non-linear probes. 9Preprint. Content Fidelity measures the accuracy of responses in the model with respect to the task in hand. For mathematical tasks, we measure the accuracy as a pairwise comparison between the generated answer and the ground truth, while accounting for aligned reasoning logic Cobbe et al. (2021b). Across all benchmarks, our templates follow a standardized procedure inspired by Liu et al. E CAN WE MITIGATE BIASES USING PERSONA MODULATIONS ?

LLMs demonstrate inherent biases against certain demographic groups (Wen et al., 2025), which can lead to harmful stereotypes (Malik et al., 2025b). While prompting a persona like “an unbiased assistant” can help mitigate stereotypical biases (Furniturewala et al., 2024), the debiasing maybe spurious, underscoring the need to modulate the ”unbiased” persona to control for inference-time biases. To aid this prompt-level debiasing effort, we apply PaSS to measure the effects of modulation on debiasing. F SENSITIVITY ANALYSIS ON EMOJI PERSONA SUBSPACE 

In this section, we conduct additional sensitivity ablations demonstrating that PaSS remains ro-bust across pooling choices, probe designs, initialization prompts, and a range of alternative testing prompts. F.1 POOLING CHOICE 

In Appendix. A.4, we systematically ablate three pooling strategies for constructing persona repre-sentations: mean pooling over all tokens (current baseline), last-token pooling, and mean-pooling over only the persona instructions. As seen in Figure 8, mean pooling is the most consistent across the modulating coefficients, while last-token pooling is weaker and less consistent. Beyond this, theoretically, we see that last-token pooling has been shown to disproportionately weight recent context and can effectively “erase” earlier information in the sequence (Feucht et al., 2024), while mean pooling aggregates both persona and task content information across the full sequence, better capturing a global context (Reichman et al., 2025).
